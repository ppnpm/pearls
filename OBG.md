# Obstetrics & Gynaecology — Revision Pearls (Part 1: Obstetrics)

---

## 1. Puerperal Mastitis

**PEARL:** **Dicloxacillin** is first-line for lactational mastitis, for **10–14 days**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Organism: ***Staphylococcus aureus*** (from the infant's nose/throat via a cracked nipple); MRSA → clindamycin/cotrimoxazole.
- **Continue breastfeeding/emptying the breast** — this is therapeutic, not contraindicated.
- Fluctuant, non-resolving mass after 48–72 h of antibiotics = **breast abscess** → ultrasound-guided aspiration or incision and drainage.

**REMEMBER:** *Keep feeding + dicloxacillin; if it doesn't settle in 48 h, look for an abscess.*

---

## 2. Puerperal Pyrexia

**PEARL:** Temperature **>100.4°F (38°C)** on any 2 of the first 10 days postpartum, **excluding the first 24 hours**.

**MUST KNOW:** Commonest cause = **endometritis** (polymicrobial); then UTI, wound infection, mastitis, DVT. Fever in the first 24 h is usually not infective (atelectasis, dehydration, or normal).

**REMEMBER:** *>100.4°F after day 1 = puerperal pyrexia; think endometritis first.*

---

## 3. Lochia

**PEARL:** The average total duration of lochial discharge is **24–36 days**.

**MUST KNOW:** Sequence — **lochia rubra** (red, days 1–4) → **serosa** (pale, days 5–9) → **alba** (white, days 10 onwards). Offensive lochia = infection; persistent red lochia = retained products/subinvolution.

**REMEMBER:** *Rubra → serosa → alba; total about 3–5 weeks.*

---

## 4. Twin Pregnancy — Timing of Ultrasound

**PEARL:** **Chorionicity must be determined at 11+0 to 13+6 weeks**, when the **CRL is 45–84 mm** — the same window in which twins should be dated. This is also the best time to identify the type of twin pregnancy.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Lambda (twin peak) sign = dichorionic**; **T sign = monochorionic**.
- After 14 weeks the membranes thin and chorionicity becomes unreliable — this is why the window matters.
- Date twins by the **larger** CRL.
- **Most definite proof of monozygosity: DNA fingerprinting (DNA microprobe technique).**

**REMEMBER:** *11–14 weeks: lambda = di, T = mono. Later is too late.*

---

## 5. Nuchal Translucency & NIPT

**PEARL:** **Nuchal translucency** is measured at **11–14 weeks** (CRL 45–84 mm) as part of first-trimester aneuploidy screening. **NIPT (cell-free fetal DNA) is a SCREENING, not a diagnostic, test** — for trisomy **21, 18, 13 and Turner syndrome**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Any positive NIPT/combined screen must be confirmed by **CVS (11–13 wks) or amniocentesis (15+ wks)** — the diagnostic tests.
- Combined test = NT + free β-hCG + PAPP-A + maternal age.
- Increased NT also flags cardiac defects and Noonan syndrome, even with normal karyotype.

**REMEMBER:** *NT and NIPT only screen — karyotype diagnoses.*

---

## 6. Varicella in Pregnancy and the Newborn

**PEARL:**
- **Congenital varicella syndrome** risk is highest at **13–20 weeks**.
- **Perinatal varicella** — maternal rash from **5 days before to 2 days after delivery** → severe, potentially fatal neonatal varicella (transplacental virus without maternal antibody).
- **Neonate:** IV **acyclovir 10 mg/kg 8-hourly** once lesions appear (plus VZIG at birth).
- **Pregnant woman, uncomplicated chickenpox/zoster:** **oral acyclovir 800 mg five times daily × 7 days**.

**UNDERSTAND:** In the −5 to +2 day window the virus crosses the placenta but maternal IgG has not yet been produced or transferred — the baby faces the virus unprotected.

**MUST KNOW:** Congenital varicella syndrome = limb hypoplasia, cicatricial skin scarring in a dermatomal pattern, chorioretinitis, cortical atrophy.

**REMEMBER:** *5 before, 2 after = danger; 13–20 weeks = malformation.*

---

## 7. Anaemia Prophylaxis — Anemia Mukt Bharat

**PEARL:** Iron–folic acid prophylaxis in pregnancy starts at **14 weeks** and continues for ~**6 months (180 days) antenatally and 6 months postpartum** — one **red** sugar-coated tablet daily = **60 mg elemental iron + 0.5 mg folic acid**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- If the woman is already **anaemic**, treatment is **2 tablets/day**, and supplementation begins as early as **8–10 weeks** rather than waiting for 14.
- 14 weeks is chosen to avoid aggravating first-trimester nausea.
- IFA tablet colour coding: **red = pregnant/lactating women & WRA, blue = 6–10 y (weekly), pink = adolescents**.

**REMEMBER:** *14 weeks, 1 red tablet (60 mg + 0.5 mg), 180 + 180 days.*

---

## 8. High-Dose Preconceptional Folic Acid

**PEARL:** Women at **higher risk of neural tube defects** — including those with **poorly controlled diabetes** — need **high-dose (4–5 mg) folic acid preconceptionally**, versus the routine 400 µg.

**MUST KNOW:** Other high-dose indications: previous NTD-affected baby, antiepileptics (valproate, carbamazepine), obesity, malabsorption, and folate antagonists. Start **at least 1 month before conception** and continue through the first trimester (neural tube closes by day 28).

**REMEMBER:** *Routine 400 µg; risky 4–5 mg, started before conception.*

---

## 9. Diabetes in Pregnancy — Overt vs Gestational

**PEARL:** **Overt (pregestational) diabetes** — known diabetes before pregnancy or **fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL** — carries a markedly **higher rate of congenital malformations** than the general population.

**UNDERSTAND:** Malformations arise from **first-trimester hyperglycemia during organogenesis** — a period gestational diabetes (onset in the second half) never touches. Hence GDM causes macrosomia, not malformations.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Classic anomalies: **caudal regression syndrome (most specific)**, cardiac (TGA, VSD), neural tube, renal.
- Risk correlates with **periconceptional HbA1c**.
- **White classification: class A1 = diet-controlled; every other class needs insulin.**

**REMEMBER:** *Overt diabetes malforms the fetus; GDM just makes it big.*

---

## 10. Fetal Growth Restriction — Appearance

**PEARL:** The FGR infant has an **"old man" (wizened) facies** from poor subcutaneous fat, is **alert with normal reflexes**, and has a **weight deficit far greater than the length/head deficit** (asymmetric FGR).

**MUST KNOW:**
- Contrast with the **preterm** baby, who is lethargic with immature reflexes.
- Complications: **hypoglycemia, hypothermia, polycythemia, meconium aspiration**.
- Symmetric FGR (early, all parameters small) → chromosomal/infective causes; asymmetric (late) → placental insufficiency, with **head sparing**.

**REMEMBER:** *Old man's face, bright eyes, thin body = FGR, not prematurity.*

---

## 11. Antiangiogenic Factors in Pre-eclampsia

**PEARL:** The preeclamptic trophoblast overproduces two antiangiogenic peptides, which rise **months before** clinical disease:
- **sFlt-1** — a soluble decoy receptor that mops up **PlGF and VEGF** → endothelial dysfunction.
- **Soluble endoglin (sEng)** — blocks **endothelial NO-dependent vasodilatation** and TGF-β signalling. **sEng is increased in pre-eclampsia.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- Therefore **PlGF is LOW and the sFlt-1:PlGF ratio is HIGH** — used clinically to predict/rule out pre-eclampsia.
- Underlying lesion: failed second-wave trophoblastic invasion of the **spiral arteries** → placental ischemia.
- **Low-dose aspirin from 12–16 weeks** is the proven preventive.

**REMEMBER:** *sFlt-1 and endoglin UP, PlGF and VEGF DOWN.*

---

## 12. Roll-over Test

**PEARL:** A screening test for pre-eclampsia at **28–32 weeks**: BP is taken in the **lateral** position, then the woman **rolls onto her back**; a rise of **≥20 mmHg diastolic** = positive. About **33%** of positives later become hypertensive.

**REMEMBER:** *Side → back, diastolic up 20 = positive roll-over.*

---

## 13. Magnesium Sulphate

**PEARL:** MgSO4 **prevents/controls eclamptic seizures** and **relaxes the uterus**, but it **does NOT lower blood pressure** — antihypertensives (labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine) are still required.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Monitoring before each dose: **knee jerk present, respiratory rate >12–16/min, urine output >30 mL/h (100 mL/4 h)**.
- **Decreased urine output is NOT a feature of magnesium toxicity** — it is a *cause* of it, since Mg is renally excreted; toxicity shows as **loss of deep tendon reflexes → respiratory depression → cardiac arrest**.
- Antidote: **10% calcium gluconate 10 mL IV**.
- Also used for **fetal neuroprotection** in preterm birth <32 weeks.

**REMEMBER:** *MgSO4 stops fits, not BP; oliguria causes toxicity rather than being one of its signs.*

---

## 14. Ritodrine

**PEARL:** Ritodrine, a **β2 agonist** tocolytic, causes **maternal hyperglycemia** by stimulating **glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis**.

**MUST KNOW:** Other β2 effects: tachycardia, hypokalemia, and the feared **pulmonary edema** (worse with steroids and fluid overload). Largely replaced by **nifedipine and atosiban**.

**REMEMBER:** *β2 tocolytic = sugar up, potassium down, lungs wet.*

---

## 15. Fetal Fibronectin

**PEARL:** **Fetal fibronectin in cervicovaginal secretions before membrane rupture** is a marker of impending **preterm labour**.

**MUST KNOW:** Its real value is its **high negative predictive value** — a negative test reliably excludes delivery within 7–14 days, avoiding unnecessary admission and steroids. Valid between 22 and 34 weeks; avoid after intercourse or a vaginal examination.

**REMEMBER:** *Fetal fibronectin is best at telling you she WON'T deliver.*

---

## 16. Nitrazine Test for PROM

**PEARL:** Normal vaginal pH is **4.5–5.5**; with ruptured membranes the amniotic fluid raises it to **6–6.2**, turning nitrazine paper blue.

**MUST KNOW:** False positives: blood, semen, urine, bacterial vaginosis, antiseptics. Confirmatory: **ferning (arborization)** on a dried slide, pooling on sterile speculum, and the newer IGFBP-1/PAMG-1 tests. **Avoid digital examination** in PROM (introduces infection).

**REMEMBER:** *pH >6 + fern = PROM; never do a digital exam.*

---

## 17. First-Trimester Bleeding — Decidual (Cyclic) Bleeding

**PEARL:** Cyclic scanty bleeding can occur **up to 12 weeks** of pregnancy, until the **decidua capsularis fuses with the decidua parietalis** obliterating the uterine cavity. It is scantier and shorter than a normal period.

**REMEMBER:** *Decidual bleeding stops by 12 weeks — when the two deciduas fuse.*

---

## 18. Septic Abortion

**PEARL:** ***Escherichia coli*** is the commonest organism causing septic abortion.

**MUST KNOW:** Usually polymicrobial — anaerobes (**Bacteroides, Peptostreptococcus**), *Clostridium perfringens* (→ hemolysis, renal failure, "gas in tissue"). Management: **triple antibiotics + evacuation** once stabilized; watch for septic shock and renal failure.

**REMEMBER:** *E. coli leads; C. perfringens kills.*

---

## 19. Ectopic Pregnancy — Risk Factors

**PEARL:** **Pelvic inflammatory disease is the commonest cause**; **previous ectopic** is the strongest individual risk factor.

**MUST KNOW:** Others — prior tubal surgery/recanalization after sterilization, infertility and ART, endometriosis, **smoking**, age >40, and contraceptive failure with **IUCD or progesterone-only pills** (which slow tubal motility).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Contraceptives reduce ectopics in absolute terms; but **if pregnancy occurs despite an IUCD or POP, it is more likely to be ectopic**.

**REMEMBER:** *PID = commonest cause; previous ectopic = highest risk.*

---

## 20. Criteria for Unusual Ectopics

**PEARL:**
- **Studdiford's criteria** → primary **abdominal** pregnancy
- **Spiegelberg's criteria** → **ovarian** pregnancy
- **Rubin's criteria** → **cervical** pregnancy
- **Godin's sonographic criteria** → **caesarean scar** pregnancy

**REMEMBER:** *Abdominal = Studdiford, Ovarian = Spiegelberg, Cervical = Rubin, Scar = Godin.*

---

## 21. Anti-D Prophylaxis

**PEARL:** An **Rh-negative, antibody-negative** woman gets **300 µg anti-D** at **28 weeks** and again **within 72 hours** of delivery of an Rh-positive baby.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Also indicated after abortion, ectopic, amniocentesis/CVS, external cephalic version, or antepartum haemorrhage.
- **Useless once the woman is already sensitized** (antibody positive) — hence the "no antibody" wording.
- 300 µg covers ~30 mL fetal whole blood (15 mL fetal RBCs); larger bleeds are quantified by the **Kleihauer–Betke test**.

**REMEMBER:** *28 weeks + within 72 hours, only if not already sensitized.*

---

## 22. Hydrops Fetalis — Buddha Sign

**PEARL:** The **Buddha sign** — the fetus in a Buddha-like posture with a **halo around the head from an edematous scalp** — is seen in hydrops fetalis, the most severe form of **Rh haemolytic disease**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Massive red cell destruction → severe anemia → tissue anoxia, metabolic acidosis and high-output **cardiac failure** → generalized edema and fetal death.

**MUST KNOW:** Fetal anemia is now assessed non-invasively by **MCA peak systolic velocity** on Doppler; treated with intrauterine transfusion.

**REMEMBER:** *Buddha posture + halo scalp = hydrops from Rh disease.*

---

## 23. hCG and Fetal Testosterone

**PEARL:** Placental **hCG stimulates the fetal Leydig cells to produce testosterone** in the male fetus.

**UNDERSTAND:** The fetal pituitary is not yet functional in early gestation; hCG is structurally near-identical to LH (same α subunit) and takes over the job — driving male external genital differentiation in the first trimester.

**REMEMBER:** *hCG is the fetus's LH.*

---

## 24. Vomiting in Pregnancy

**PEARL:** First-line management is **small, frequent meals** (with avoidance of triggers, dry bland carbohydrates, ginger, and pyridoxine ± doxylamine).

**MUST KNOW:** Escalate to antiemetics only if lifestyle fails; **hyperemesis gravidarum** = persistent vomiting with >5% weight loss, ketonuria, and electrolyte disturbance — needs admission, IV fluids and **thiamine** (to prevent Wernicke's).

**REMEMBER:** *Diet first; think hyperemesis when there's weight loss and ketones.*

---

## 25. Leopold's Manoeuvres

**PEARL:**
1. **First (fundal grip)** — which fetal pole occupies the fundus
2. **Second (lateral/umbilical grip)** — fetal orientation (position of back and limbs)
3. **Third (Pawlik's grip)** — confirms the presenting part
4. **Fourth (pelvic grip)** — degree of **descent/engagement**

**MUST KNOW:** The examiner faces the mother's **feet** only for the fourth manoeuvre.

**REMEMBER:** *Fundus → sides → presentation → descent.*

---

## 26. Anthropoid Pelvis

**PEARL:** The **anthropoid** pelvis is most associated with **occipitoposterior** positions and **face-to-pubis** delivery.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Anthropoid = AP diameter > transverse (long oval) → head engages and stays OP.
- **Android** pelvis has the worst prognosis for vaginal delivery (funnel shape, deep transverse arrest).
- **Platypelloid** → transverse arrest at the brim.
- **All combinations of pelvic types occur EXCEPT anthropoid with platypelloid** (geometric opposites).
- **Childbirth trauma is commonest in the gynaecoid pelvis and least in the android** (because the gynaecoid pelvis permits vaginal delivery of larger heads).

**REMEMBER:** *Anthropoid = ape-like, long AP = face-to-pubis OP delivery.*

---

## 27. Uterine Innervation

**PEARL:** The uterus is supplied by the **uterovaginal plexus, also called Frankenhäuser's ganglion**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Sensory pain fibres from the uterus travel with sympathetics to **T10–L1**; the cervix and upper vagina drain to **S2–S4** (hence the paracervical and pudendal blocks).
- **Uterine contractility is not mediated by innervation from T7–T8** — it is primarily **hormonal (oxytocin/prostaglandin)** and myogenic. A completely denervated uterus (as in paraplegia or transplant) still contracts and delivers.

**REMEMBER:** *The uterus contracts by hormones, not by nerves.*

---

## 28. Funic (Fetal) Souffle

**PEARL:** A **soft blowing murmur synchronous with the fetal heart**, due to rush of blood through the **umbilical arteries**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** **Uterine souffle** is synchronous with the **maternal pulse** and arises from the dilated uterine vessels.

**REMEMBER:** *Funic = fetal rate (umbilical artery); uterine souffle = mother's rate.*

---

## 29. Vagitus Uterinus

**PEARL:** The cry of the fetus **inside the uterus** — a rare event requiring **rupture of the membranes with entry of air**.

**REMEMBER:** *No air, no cry — vagitus uterinus needs ruptured membranes.*

---

## 30. Duhrssen's Incision

**PEARL:** Cervical incisions at the **2 and 10 o'clock positions**, made to deliver the head through an **incompletely dilated cervix** (classically the after-coming head in breech).

**MUST KNOW:** Avoid 3 and 9 o'clock — the **descending cervical branches of the uterine vessels** run laterally. Some add a 6 o'clock incision.

**REMEMBER:** *2 and 10 o'clock — dodge the lateral vessels.*

---

## 31. Abdominal Incisions

**PEARL:**
- **Joel-Cohen** — skin incision used for **caesarean section** (straight, 3 cm below the line joining the ASIS; blunt extension; part of the Misgav-Ladach technique).
- **Cherny** — a **gynaecological** transverse muscle-cutting incision (rectus muscles detached from the pubic symphysis) giving wider pelvic access.
- **Pfannenstiel** — the classic curved suprapubic caesarean incision.
- **Maylard** — transverse incision cutting the rectus muscles transversely.

**REMEMBER:** *Joel-Cohen = LSCS; Cherny/Maylard = extra pelvic exposure.*

---

## 32. Caesarean Section — Patient Position

**PEARL:** **Supine with a wedge under the RIGHT hip** (15° left lateral tilt).

**UNDERSTAND:** Tilting left displaces the gravid uterus off the **inferior vena cava**, preventing **supine hypotension (aorto-caval compression) syndrome**.

**REMEMBER:** *Wedge under the right hip → uterus tips left → IVC free.*

---

## 33. Perimortem Caesarean Section

**PEARL:** An **upper segment (classical)** caesarean is performed for **perimortem caesarean section** — the emergency delivery of a woman who has just died or is about to.

**UNDERSTAND:** It is the fastest route to the fetus, needs no bladder dissection, and can be done while CPR continues. Delivery also relieves aortocaval compression and improves maternal resuscitation.

**MUST KNOW:** Ideally started within **4 minutes** of arrest with delivery by **5 minutes** ("4-minute rule"); indicated beyond ~20–24 weeks.

**REMEMBER:** *Perimortem = classical incision, knife by 4 minutes.*

---

## 34. Piper Forceps

**PEARL:** **Piper forceps** are the instrument of choice for the **after-coming head in breech** delivery.

**MUST KNOW:** They have a **backward (reverse) pelvic curve and long shanks** so they can be applied from below while the body is held up. Alternative manoeuvre: **Mauriceau–Smellie–Veit**.

**REMEMBER:** *Piper for the aftercoming head.*

---

## 35. Breech — Commonest Type

**PEARL:** **Frank breech (breech with extended legs)** is the commonest type, especially in **primigravidae** (tight abdominal wall and uterus splint the legs).

**MUST KNOW:** Frank breech is the **safest for vaginal delivery** (best-fitting presenting part, lowest cord prolapse risk); **footling breech has the highest risk of cord prolapse** and is a contraindication to vaginal delivery.

**REMEMBER:** *Frank = commonest and safest; footling = cord prolapse.*

---

## 36. Ventouse — Absolute Contraindications

**PEARL:** **Preterm (<34 weeks), face presentation, after-coming head of breech, and intrauterine death.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The preterm skull is too soft (intracranial/subgaleal haemorrhage), the face and after-coming head offer no proper application point, and traction is pointless in IUD.

**MUST KNOW:** Also avoid in non-vertex presentation and suspected fetal coagulopathy/demineralizing disease. Forceps, not ventouse, are the answer in these situations. Ventouse complications: cephalhaematoma, subgaleal haemorrhage, retinal haemorrhage, chignon.

**REMEMBER:** *No ventouse for the small, the face, the breech head, or the dead.*

---

## 37. Perineal Tears

**PEARL:** A **fourth-degree** tear involves the **anal sphincter complex AND the rectal (anal) mucosa** — the most severe.

**MUST KNOW:** First = skin/vaginal mucosa; second = perineal muscles; **third = sphincter (3a <50% EAS, 3b >50% EAS, 3c IAS involved)**; fourth = through into rectal mucosa. Third and fourth = **obstetric anal sphincter injuries (OASIS)** → repair in theatre, antibiotics, laxatives, no constipating agents.

**REMEMBER:** *Fourth degree = you can see the rectal mucosa.*

---

## 38. Cleidotomy

**PEARL:** Deliberate **fracture/division of the fetal clavicle** to reduce the shoulder girdle diameter.

**MUST KNOW:** Used in shoulder dystocia — but essentially reserved for a **dead fetus** (a destructive operation); in a live baby the sequence is McRoberts → suprapubic pressure → internal rotation manoeuvres → delivery of the posterior arm → Zavanelli/symphysiotomy.

**REMEMBER:** *Cleidotomy shrinks the shoulders — a last resort, usually for a dead fetus.*

---

## 39. Scar Rupture vs Dehiscence

**PEARL:** In **rupture**, the uterine and peritoneal cavities become **continuous** (the serosa is breached, membranes rupture, bleeding and fetal distress). In **dehiscence**, the scar separates but the **serosa/membranes remain intact** — usually asymptomatic and found incidentally.

**REMEMBER:** *Dehiscence = window with intact glass; rupture = the glass is broken.*

---

## 40. Early Cord Clamping

**PEARL:** **Early cord clamping** is indicated for the **non-vigorous baby needing immediate resuscitation**.

**MUST KNOW:** Otherwise **delayed clamping (1–3 minutes)** is standard — it improves iron stores and reduces anaemia; in preterms it lowers IVH and need for transfusion. Other early-clamping situations: severe placental abruption/previa with bleeding, and monochorionic twins with TTTS.

**REMEMBER:** *Delay for everyone except the flat baby.*

---

## 41. Placental Abruption

**PEARL:** Abdominal pain + bleeding per vaginum + loss of fetal movements in a **hypertensive** pregnant woman = **abruptio placentae**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Painful** bleeding with a **tense, tender, "woody" uterus** — versus the **painless** bleeding of placenta previa.
- Revealed bleeding underestimates loss — shock out of proportion to visible blood = **concealed abruption**.
- Complications: **DIC (commonest coagulopathy in obstetrics)**, **Couvelaire uterus**, acute renal failure (cortical necrosis), PPH.
- Commonest risk factor overall = **hypertension**; also trauma, smoking, cocaine, previous abruption.

**REMEMBER:** *Painful bleed + hard uterus + hypertension = abruption; think DIC.*

---

## 42. Septic Pelvic Thrombophlebitis

**PEARL:** Treat with **antibiotics PLUS heparin**.

**MUST KNOW:** Suspect it as a **diagnosis of exclusion** — persistent postpartum "picket-fence" fever despite adequate antibiotics, with a well-looking patient. Response to heparin supports the diagnosis; CT/MRI may show ovarian vein thrombosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Fever that ignores antibiotics after delivery → add heparin.*

---

## 43. Antiphospholipid Syndrome in Pregnancy

**PEARL:** Best treatment = **low-dose aspirin + heparin (LMWH)**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Warfarin is **teratogenic** (fetal warfarin syndrome — nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses) → switch to heparin.
- APS causes recurrent early miscarriage, fetal death >10 weeks, severe early pre-eclampsia and FGR.
- Diagnosis needs a **clinical criterion + a positive test (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2 glycoprotein-I) on two occasions ≥12 weeks apart**.

**REMEMBER:** *APS + pregnancy = aspirin + heparin, never warfarin.*

---

## 44. Homan's Sign

**PEARL:** Calf pain on **dorsiflexion of the foot** — may be present in **deep vein thrombosis**.

**MUST KNOW:** Unreliable (poor sensitivity/specificity) — **compression ultrasound with Doppler** is the investigation of choice. Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state and DVT is commoner in the **left leg** (compression of the left iliac vein by the right iliac artery).

**REMEMBER:** *Homan's sign is a clue, ultrasound is the answer.*

---

## 45. Peripartum Cardiomyopathy

**PEARL:** It is a **LEFT VENTRICULAR SYSTOLIC dysfunction (EF <45%)** — not diastolic dysfunction.

**MUST KNOW:** Defined as heart failure in the **last month of pregnancy to 5 months postpartum** with no other identifiable cause and no pre-existing heart disease. Risk factors: multiparity, age >30, twins, pre-eclampsia, African descent. Prognosis depends on **recovery of EF**; recurrence risk is high in future pregnancy.

**REMEMBER:** *Peripartum cardiomyopathy = dilated, systolic failure, last month to 5 months post.*

---

## 46. Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy

**PEARL:** Occurs most commonly in the **third trimester or early postpartum**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Microvesicular fatty infiltration; linked to fetal **LCHAD deficiency**.
- **Hypoglycemia, prolonged PT/low fibrinogen, high ammonia and marked coagulopathy** distinguish it from HELLP (where platelets fall but glucose and fibrinogen are usually preserved).
- **Swansea criteria** for diagnosis; **treatment is prompt delivery** plus supportive care.

**REMEMBER:** *Third-trimester jaundice + hypoglycemia + coagulopathy = AFLP → deliver.*

---

## 47. Choriocarcinoma — Indicators

**PEARL:** Suggestive features are a **persistently enlarged uterus, a plateau or rise in serum hCG**, and **sub-urethral (vaginal) metastatic nodules** — **not** an increased number of theca lutein cysts by itself.

**MUST KNOW:** Theca lutein cysts merely reflect high hCG and regress as it falls. Choriocarcinoma metastasizes early to **lung (commonest), vagina, brain, liver**; it is highly **chemosensitive** (methotrexate/actinomycin-D; EMA-CO for high risk). Do **not** biopsy a vaginal nodule — torrential bleeding.

**REMEMBER:** *Plateauing hCG + bleeding vaginal nodule = choriocarcinoma; never biopsy the nodule.*

---

## 48. MSAFP in Gestational Trophoblastic Disease

**PEARL:** Maternal serum **alpha-fetoprotein is LOW** in gestational trophoblastic disease (no fetus, hence no fetal AFP source).

**MUST KNOW:** MSAFP is also low in **trisomy 21 and 18**; it is **high** in **neural tube defects, abdominal wall defects (omphalocele/gastroschisis), multiple pregnancy, and wrong (underestimated) dates**.

**REMEMBER:** *No fetus = no AFP; high AFP = open defect.*

---

## 49. MTP Between 20 and 24 Weeks

**PEARL:** Under the amended MTP Act, termination **between 20 and 24 weeks** is permitted for specific categories — **substantial fetal anomalies, risk to maternal health, and pregnancy from sexual assault** (survivors of rape/incest, minors, disabled women, change of marital status, etc.), on the opinion of **two registered medical practitioners**.

**MUST KNOW:** Up to **20 weeks** — the opinion of **one** RMP suffices. **Beyond 24 weeks**, termination requires a **State Medical Board** decision for substantial fetal abnormality.

**REMEMBER:** *≤20 weeks: 1 doctor. 20–24 weeks: 2 doctors, special categories. >24 weeks: Medical Board.*

---

## 50. Clinical Audit

**PEARL:** Clinical audit in obstetrics is done **after analyzing outcomes**, to standardize care and drive continuous evidence-based improvement.

**REMEMBER:** *Audit = compare practice against standard → change → re-audit (the audit cycle).*

# Part 2: Gynaecology & Reproductive Medicine

---

## 51. Halban's Disease

**PEARL:** **Irregular shedding** of the endometrium due to a **persistent corpus luteum** — cycles remain **regular**, flow is **normal**, but bleeding is **prolonged**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Diagnosis: endometrial biopsy on **day 5–6 of bleeding** shows **mixed proliferative and secretory** endometrium.
- Contrast **irregular ripening (Halban's)** with **irregular shedding**; and with **metropathia haemorrhagica** which is anovulatory.

**REMEMBER:** *Corpus luteum won't quit → periods on time but won't stop.*

---

## 52. Metropathia Haemorrhagica

**PEARL:** **Anovulatory** abnormal uterine bleeding — continuous **painless** bleeding at the onset of menses or after **6–8 weeks of amenorrhoea**, typically at **40–45 years**.

**MUST KNOW:** Due to **unopposed estrogen** → **cystic glandular hyperplasia ("Swiss cheese" endometrium)**; ovaries show persistent, unruptured follicles. Bleeding is **painless because it is anovulatory** (no progesterone → no PGF2α → no cramps).

**REMEMBER:** *Amenorrhoea then painless flooding at 40 = metropathia (Swiss cheese endometrium).*

---

## 53. Dysmenorrhoea Mechanism

**PEARL:** Spasmodic (primary) dysmenorrhoea is due to **myometrial contractions driven by increased PGF2α**, whose secretion depends on **progesterone** — hence it occurs only in **ovulatory** cycles.

**MUST KNOW:** Treatment: **NSAIDs (prostaglandin synthesis inhibitors)** first-line; **combined OCPs** work by making cycles anovulatory.

**REMEMBER:** *No ovulation → no progesterone → no PGF2α → no cramps.*

---

## 54. Menometrorrhagia

**PEARL:** **Excessive AND prolonged** bleeding at **irregular, frequent** intervals. Classic causes include **cervical carcinoma** and **granulosa cell tumours**.

**MUST KNOW:** Definitions: menorrhagia = heavy but regular; metrorrhagia = irregular/intermenstrual; **postcoital bleeding = cervical cancer until proven otherwise**. Granulosa cell tumours secrete estrogen → endometrial hyperplasia (and precocious puberty in children); marker = **inhibin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Heavy + irregular → rule out cervix and a granulosa cell tumour.*

---

## 55. Secretory Phase Histology

**PEARL:** **Subnuclear vacuolation** is the histological hallmark of the **early secretory phase** — the **first morphological evidence of ovulation** (appears ~day 16–17, 36–48 h after ovulation).

**MUST KNOW:** Progesterone-driven. Later secretory features: tortuous "saw-tooth" glands, luminal secretions, stromal **predecidual** change. The proliferative phase shows mitoses and pseudostratification.

**REMEMBER:** *Vacuoles under the nuclei = ovulation has happened.*

---

## 56. Gametogenesis Timings

**PEARL:**
- The **secondary oocyte** is formed after the completion of **meiosis I** (at ovulation); it arrests in **metaphase II** and completes meiosis II only on **fertilization**.
- **Sperm capacitation** takes **6–8 hours (~7 h)** in the female genital tract.
- **Spermatogenesis takes ~74 days**, plus ~**14 days** transit → **semen analysis is repeated 80–90 days** after starting hormonal therapy.

**REMEMBER:** *Meiosis I ends at ovulation, meiosis II at fertilization; sperm need 3 months to show a change.*

---

## 57. Male Pseudohermaphroditism & 5α-Reductase Deficiency

**PEARL:** Male pseudohermaphroditism = **male gonads (testes) with female external genitalia**. In **5α-reductase deficiency** the testes fail to descend and the phallus is poorly developed, giving a female phenotype despite male internal structures.

**UNDERSTAND:** Testosterone (needing no conversion) builds the **internal** Wolffian structures — vas, epididymis, seminal vesicle — while **DHT** is required for the **external** genitalia and prostate. Lose 5α-reductase and you lose only the outside.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **46,XY**, testes present, **Müllerian structures absent** (AMH is normal) → no uterus.
- **Virilization at puberty** ("penis at twelve") as testosterone surges — often with gender identity change to male.
- Contrast **complete androgen insensitivity**: female phenotype, **absent uterus**, blind vagina, absent pubic hair, **testes** in the groin, high testosterone and LH.

**REMEMBER:** *Testosterone = internal, DHT = external; 5α-reductase deficiency virilizes at puberty.*

---

## 58. Sertoli Cell-Only Syndrome

**PEARL:** Presents at **20–40 years with primary infertility (azoospermia)**; testicular biopsy shows seminiferous tubules lined **only by Sertoli cells**, with **thickened tubular basement membrane and total absence of germ cells**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **High FSH** (no inhibin from absent germ cells) with **normal testosterone and LH**, normal virilization, normal-to-small testes.
- Associated with **Y-chromosome AZF microdeletions** — test before ICSI (the defect is transmitted to sons).
- Contrast: a **benign testicular neoplasm** presents as a **mass**, with neoplastic cells on histology, not an empty tubule.

**REMEMBER:** *Empty tubules + high FSH = Sertoli cell-only.*

---

## 59. Miller–Kurzrok Test

**PEARL:** An **in-vitro test of sperm–cervical mucus interaction**, used in infertility work-up.

**MUST KNOW:** The **in-vivo** equivalent is the **post-coital (Sims–Huhner) test**; the **Kremer test** measures sperm penetration in a capillary tube. These assess a cervical factor — largely historical now, replaced by IUI/IVF.

**REMEMBER:** *Miller–Kurzrok = sperm meets mucus, in a dish.*

---

## 60. Transverse Vaginal Septum

**PEARL:** A Müllerian anomaly obstructing menstrual outflow → **haematocolpos, haematometra and retrograde flow causing endometriosis**, presenting with **cyclical pain (cryptomenorrhoea)** and chronic pelvic pain.

**MUST KNOW:** Primary amenorrhoea with **cyclical pain + normal secondary sexual characters** = outflow obstruction (imperforate hymen, transverse septum, cervical agenesis). **Imperforate hymen shows a bulging bluish membrane; a transverse septum does not.** Treat by excision/septum resection.

**REMEMBER:** *Cyclical pain + no bleeding = obstructed outflow; retrograde flow gives endometriosis.*

---

## 61. Septate vs Bicornuate Uterus

**PEARL:** On HSG, the **intercornual angle** separates them: **<75° = septate**, **>105° = bicornuate**. HSG cannot see the fundal contour — **MRI is the investigation of choice for Müllerian anomalies**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Septate uterus** is the **commonest** Müllerian anomaly and the one **most associated with recurrent miscarriage** — treated by **hysteroscopic septal resection**.
- Bicornuate uterus has a **fundal cleft >1 cm** (external contour indented) and is generally not resected.
- All Müllerian anomalies warrant **renal tract imaging** (associated renal agenesis).

**REMEMBER:** *Narrow angle = septum (cut it); wide angle + notched fundus = bicornuate (leave it).*

---

## 62. Asherman Syndrome

**PEARL:** **Hysteroscopic adhesiolysis is the treatment of choice.**

**MUST KNOW:** Intrauterine synechiae, usually after **postpartum/post-abortal curettage** or genital TB → secondary amenorrhoea/hypomenorrhoea and infertility with **normal hormones and a negative progesterone withdrawal test**. After adhesiolysis: intrauterine device/balloon or gel + **cyclical estrogen** to prevent re-formation.

**REMEMBER:** *No withdrawal bleed with normal hormones after a D&C = Asherman → hysteroscopy.*

---

## 63. Pipelle Aspirator

**PEARL:** The **Pipelle** is a flexible plastic cannula used to obtain an **endometrial sample for biopsy** in the OPD, without anaesthesia or dilatation.

**MUST KNOW:** First-line for **postmenopausal bleeding/suspected endometrial carcinoma**; high sensitivity for global lesions but can **miss focal lesions (polyps, submucous fibroids)** — a negative Pipelle with persistent bleeding needs **hysteroscopy + D&C**.

**REMEMBER:** *Pipelle samples globally — focal lesions need the hysteroscope.*

---

## 64. Hydrosalpinx — Cogwheel Sign

**PEARL:** The **cogwheel sign** on ultrasound — a cross-section of a **dilated fallopian tube with thickened internal (endosalpingeal) folds** — is characteristic of hydrosalpinx.

**MUST KNOW:** Other signs: "beads on a string" (chronic), incomplete septa, and a tubular rather than round cystic shape (which separates it from an ovarian cyst). Hydrosalpinx **halves IVF success** → **salpingectomy before IVF**.

**REMEMBER:** *Cogwheel = acute hydrosalpinx; remove it before IVF.*

---

## 65. Le Fort's Colpocleisis

**PEARL:** Vaginal flaps are excised from the **anterior and posterior walls** and the raw areas apposed in the midline, obliterating the vagina and preventing prolapse. **A Pap smear and endometrial biopsy are mandatory beforehand**, since the cervix and endometrium become inaccessible afterwards.

**MUST KNOW:** Reserved for **elderly, frail, sexually inactive** women with procidentia. **Coitus is no longer possible**; stress incontinence may be unmasked, so counselling is essential.

**REMEMBER:** *Close the vagina — but clear the cervix and endometrium first.*

---

## 66. Vesicovaginal Fistula Repair

**PEARL:** If a fistula repair fails, a **second attempt is made only after 3 months**.

**MUST KNOW:** Tissues must recover their vascularity and inflammation must subside; the **first attempt has the best chance of success**. Primary repair is usually done **3 months after the injury** (or immediately if recognized intraoperatively). Interposition flaps: **Martius (labial fat pad)**, omentum, gracilis.

**REMEMBER:** *Three months between attempts — and the first try is the best try.*

---

## 67. Stress Incontinence Grading

**PEARL:**
- **Grade 1** — leak on **severe** stress: coughing, sneezing, jogging
- **Grade 2** — leak on **moderate** stress: walking fast, climbing stairs
- **Grade 3** — leak on **mild** stress: standing up (leaks even at rest/lying in severe cases)

**MUST KNOW:** Investigation of choice = **urodynamics**; first-line treatment = **pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises**; surgical gold standard = **mid-urethral sling (TVT/TOT)** or Burch colposuspension. Contrast: **urge incontinence** (detrusor overactivity) → antimuscarinics/mirabegron.

**REMEMBER:** *The milder the stress needed to leak, the higher the grade.*

---

## 68. Space of Retzius

**PEARL:** The **space of Retzius = prevesical = retropubic space**.

**MUST KNOW:** It lies between the pubic symphysis and the bladder; it is entered in **Burch colposuspension**, retropubic sling placement and open bladder-neck surgery. It contains loose areolar tissue and the **veins of Santorini** (bleeding risk).

**REMEMBER:** *Retzius = retropubic = the space you enter for a Burch.*

---

## 69. Ovarian Dermoid (Benign Cystic Teratoma)

**PEARL:** The **commonest benign ovarian tumour in the reproductive age group**. It is also:
- the **commonest germ cell tumour overall**
- **bilateral in 10–15%**
- the **commonest ovarian tumour diagnosed in pregnancy**
- the **commonest tumour to undergo torsion**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Rokitansky's protuberance** — the nodule of skin, sebaceous and sweat glands, teeth and bone (where malignant change, usually **squamous cell carcinoma**, arises).
- **Struma ovarii** = thyroid tissue within a dermoid → can cause thyrotoxicosis.
- Rupture spills sebaceous material → **chemical peritonitis**.
- Ultrasound: "tip of the iceberg" sign, hyperechoic Rokitansky nodule, fat-fluid level; teeth visible on X-ray.

**REMEMBER:** *Dermoid = commonest, most torted, teeth inside, thyroid = struma ovarii.*

---

## 70. Pseudomyxoma Peritonei

**PEARL:** Diagnosed by **jelly-like mucinous deposits filling the peritoneal cavity**, from rupture of a **mucinous tumour of the ovary or appendix**.

**MUST KNOW:** Most cases are now traced to a **ruptured appendiceal mucinous neoplasm** — hence **appendicectomy is mandatory** during surgery for a mucinous ovarian tumour. Treatment: cytoreductive surgery + **HIPEC**. Death is from progressive abdominal compression, not metastasis.

**REMEMBER:** *Jelly belly — always look at the appendix.*

---

## 71. Ovarian Cancer Staging

**PEARL:** **Rectal examination is NOT part of the staging of ovarian malignancy.**

**MUST KNOW:** Ovarian cancer is staged **surgically (FIGO)** — laparotomy with peritoneal washings, omentectomy, and pelvic/para-aortic node sampling. Contrast **cervical carcinoma**, which is staged **clinically**, and where **per rectal examination (parametrial and pelvic side-wall spread) is essential**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ovary = surgical staging; cervix = clinical staging with a PR exam.*

---

## 72. Radical Hysterectomy Types

**PEARL:** In **modified radical (Type 2 / Wertheim) hysterectomy**, the **upper 2 cm (upper third) of the vagina** is removed, with the ureter unroofed and the uterine artery ligated medial to the ureter.

**MUST KNOW:** **Type 3 (Meigs)** removes the upper **third to half** of vagina with the entire parametrium to the pelvic side wall — the standard for stage IB–IIA cervical cancer. Type 1 = extrafascial (simple). Main complication of radical surgery: **bladder dysfunction and ureteric injury/fistula**.

**REMEMBER:** *Type 2 = 2 cm of vagina; Type 3 goes to the pelvic wall.*

---

## 73. Radiotherapy in Cervical Cancer

**PEARL:** Radiotherapy is indicated in **node-positive stage I, node-positive stage II, and all stage III and IV** disease.

**MUST KNOW:** **Concurrent chemoradiation with cisplatin** is standard for locally advanced disease. Surgery and radiotherapy give equal survival in early stage IB; **avoid combining both** (adds morbidity — fistula, stenosis).

**REMEMBER:** *Nodes positive or stage III/IV → radiotherapy (with cisplatin).*

---

## 74. Bethesda System

**PEARL:** **LSIL = CIN 1**; **HSIL = CIN 2 + CIN 3/carcinoma in situ**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The Bethesda system also requires a statement on **specimen adequacy** and reports **ASC-US/ASC-H** and **AGC**.
- LSIL reflects **productive HPV infection** and mostly regresses; **HSIL is the true precancer** → colposcopy + LEEP/excision.
- Two-tier terminology now: **LSIL (CIN1) and HSIL (CIN2–3)**.

**REMEMBER:** *L = 1; H = 2 and 3.*

---

## 75. Scar Endometriosis

**PEARL:** Suspect it with **cyclical pain and a nodular swelling at an episiotomy or caesarean scar**, worsening with menstruation.

**MUST KNOW:** Caused by intraoperative implantation of endometrial tissue. Treatment is **wide local excision** (medical therapy alone usually fails). May be mistaken for a suture granuloma, hernia or desmoid — the **cyclicity** is the clue.

**REMEMBER:** *Painful scar lump that swells with periods = scar endometriosis → excise.*

---

## 76. Gonorrhoea — Presentation

**PEARL:**
- **Gynaecological:** excessive vaginal discharge with pruritus and inflammation, **acute unilateral painful swelling of Bartholin's gland**, intermenstrual bleeding.
- **Non-gynaecological:** pharyngitis, dysuria, and rectal discomfort from **proctitis**.

**MUST KNOW:** Commonest cause of **Bartholin abscess**; ascending infection → **PID, tubal damage, Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome** (perihepatic adhesions). Most women are **asymptomatic** — the reason for silent tubal infertility. Treatment: **ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose** (+ doxycycline if chlamydia not excluded).

**REMEMBER:** *Gonorrhoea = Bartholin's + silent tubes + ceftriaxone once.*

---

## 77. Precocious Puberty

**PEARL:** In a girl: **any secondary sexual characteristic before 8 years**, or **menarche before 10 years** (= precocious menstruation).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Central (GnRH-dependent, true)** — commonest is **idiopathic** in girls; **GnRH agonists** are the treatment.
- **Peripheral (GnRH-independent)** — **McCune–Albright**, granulosa cell tumour, adrenal tumour, CAH, exogenous estrogen.
- **McCune–Albright syndrome** = **café-au-lait macules ("coast of Maine" borders) + precocious puberty + polyostotic fibrous dysplasia** with recurrent fractures; due to a somatic **GNAS** activating mutation (mosaic).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Neurofibromatosis 1 café-au-lait spots have **smooth "coast of California" borders**; **acoustic neuromas belong to NF2, not NF1**. NF1 criteria = café-au-lait macules, axillary/inguinal freckling, Lisch nodules, neurofibromas, sphenoid dysplasia, optic glioma, first-degree relative.

**REMEMBER:** *Before 8 (signs) or 10 (menarche) = precocious; McCune–Albright = spots + bones + puberty.*

---

## 78. Climacteric

**PEARL:** The phase of **waning ovarian activity** — beginning **2–3 years before** menopause and continuing **2–5 years after** it.

**MUST KNOW:** **Menopause** = permanent cessation of menstruation, diagnosed retrospectively after **12 months of amenorrhoea** (mean age ~50 in India, 51 worldwide); **perimenopause** = climacteric up to 1 year post-menopause; **premature ovarian insufficiency** = before 40 years. Hormonal picture: **FSH >40 IU/L, low estradiol** (FSH rises before LH).

**REMEMBER:** *Climacteric is the whole transition; menopause is one day in it, named a year later.*

---

## 79. Intrauterine Devices

**PEARL:**
- **Mirena** (32 × 32 mm): **52 mg levonorgestrel**, releasing **20 µg/day**, retained **5 years**.
- **CuT 380A (ParaGard)**: **10 years** — the longest-acting.
- **Nova T** = copper wire with a **silver core**, which prevents copper fragmentation and extends life from **3 to 5 years**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Mirena is the **treatment of choice for menorrhagia** and gives endometrial protection with HRT; it causes amenorrhoea/oligomenorrhoea.
- Copper IUCDs work by a **sterile inflammatory/spermicidal** effect; the main complaint is **increased bleeding and cramps** — hence **iron supplementation for post-insertion bleeding**, with removal if bleeding persists.
- Copper IUCD is the most effective **emergency contraceptive** (up to 5 days).

**REMEMBER:** *Mirena 5 years and less bleeding; CuT 380A 10 years and more bleeding; silver makes Nova T last 5.*

---

## 80. Progesterone-Only Pills

**PEARL:** POPs act by **thickening cervical mucus** (the main mechanism for all POPs **except desogestrel/Cerazette**, which chiefly inhibits ovulation), **inhibiting tubal motility**, and **suppressing ovulation**. **They do NOT kill sperm** — they are not spermicidal.

**MUST KNOW:** Ideal in **lactation** (no effect on milk) and where estrogen is contraindicated. Must be taken at the **same time daily** (3-hour window; 12 h for desogestrel). Main problem: **irregular bleeding**; increases the proportion of pregnancies that are **ectopic** if it fails.

**REMEMBER:** *POP blocks sperm entry, doesn't kill sperm; Cerazette blocks ovulation.*

---

## 81. Oral Contraceptive Pills and GnRH

**PEARL:** OCPs **inhibit GnRH release** (negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary) — they do **not** increase it.

**MUST KNOW:** Estrogen suppresses **FSH** (no dominant follicle); progesterone suppresses the **LH surge** (no ovulation) and thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium. Non-contraceptive benefits: reduced **ovarian and endometrial cancer**, dysmenorrhoea, and functional cysts.

**REMEMBER:** *OCP = brake on the hypothalamus, not a stimulus.*

---

## 82. Gossypol

**PEARL:** **Hypokalemic paralysis** is a side effect of **gossypol**, the male contraceptive from cottonseed oil.

**MUST KNOW:** It inhibits spermatogenesis (acts on sperm and Sertoli cells) but was abandoned because of hypokalemia and **irreversible infertility** in a proportion of users.

**REMEMBER:** *Gossypol = cotton seed male pill = low potassium paralysis.*

---

## 83. Rubin's Cannula

**PEARL:** Used to instil dye into the uterine cavity for **hysterosalpingography** — its cone seals the cervix and **prevents retrograde spill into the vagina**.

**MUST KNOW:** Don't confuse with **Rubin's test (tubal insufflation with CO₂)** or **Rubin's manoeuvre** for shoulder dystocia. HSG is done in the **proliferative phase (day 6–11)**, after menstruation ends and before ovulation.

**REMEMBER:** *Rubin's cannula seals the cervix for HSG dye.*

---

## 84. Female Genital Mutilation — WHO Classification

**PEARL:** **Infibulation is Type III.**

**MUST KNOW:** Type I = clitoridectomy; Type II = excision (clitoris ± labia minora); **Type III = infibulation — narrowing the vaginal orifice by cutting and apposing the labia**; Type IV = all other harmful procedures (pricking, piercing, cauterization). Type III causes obstructed labour, urinary retention, haematocolpos and infertility; requires **deinfibulation**.

**REMEMBER:** *III = Infibulation (three letters of "sew it shut").*

## 85. Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in Pregnancy

**PEARL:** If untreated, about **25% of women with asymptomatic bacteriuria develop symptomatic infection during pregnancy**; eradication with antibiotics prevents most of these.

**MUST KNOW:** ASB = **≥10⁵ CFU/mL on a clean-catch specimen with no symptoms** — **screen ALL pregnant women** (this is one of the few settings where ASB is treated, along with pre-urological surgery). Untreated ASB → **pyelonephritis (~20–30%), preterm labour and low birth weight**. Commonest organism **E. coli**; treat 3–7 days with **nitrofurantoin, amoxicillin or cephalexin** and do a **test of cure**.

**REMEMBER:** *Screen everyone, treat everyone — 1 in 4 untreated becomes symptomatic.*

---

## 86. Glucose Challenge Test

**PEARL:** The GCT is a **NON-FASTING initial screening test** for gestational diabetes, done at **24–28 weeks**; a positive GCT requires confirmation with a **glucose tolerance test**. Lowering the threshold raises sensitivity at the cost of specificity.

**MUST KNOW:** The classic two-step approach: **50 g GCT** (no fasting) → if ≥140 mg/dL at 1 hour, proceed to a **100 g 3-hour OGTT (Carpenter–Coustan)**. The **one-step 75 g 2-hour OGTT** (fasting ≥92, 1 h ≥180, 2 h ≥153 — **any one** abnormal) is the IADPSG/WHO alternative. India's **DIPSI test** uses **75 g non-fasting with a 2-hour value ≥140 mg/dL** — designed so women need not come fasting.

**REMEMBER:** *GCT screens (non-fasting), OGTT diagnoses; DIPSI does both in one non-fasting step.*

---

## 87. GDM vs Pregestational Diabetes — Fetal Effects

**PEARL:** **GDM is NOT associated with congenital malformations** — it causes **macrosomia, polyhydramnios, birth trauma and neonatal hypoglycaemia**. **Pregestational (overt) diabetes causes malformations**, of which **CARDIAC anomalies are the commonest — VSD most often**; **sirenomelia (mermaid syndrome) and caudal regression are the most specific**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Malformations require hyperglycaemia during **organogenesis (first trimester)** — GDM begins in the second half of pregnancy, long after the organs are formed.

**MUST KNOW:** Risk factors for GDM: **obesity, previous macrosomic baby, previous GDM, family history, PCOS, age, prior stillbirth**. Malformation risk correlates with **periconceptional HbA1c**.

**REMEMBER:** *GDM makes the baby big; overt diabetes makes it malformed (heart commonest, sirenomelia most specific).*

---

## 88. Drugs for Diabetes in Pregnancy

**PEARL:** **Insulin is the preferred agent** (does not cross the placenta). **Metformin and glyburide may be used with caution and monitoring. Pioglitazone is NOT recommended** in pregnancy.

**MUST KNOW:** Insulin requirement **rises through pregnancy** (placental anti-insulin hormones) and **falls abruptly after delivery** — reduce the dose immediately postpartum to avoid hypoglycaemia. Target: **fasting <95, 1 h <140, 2 h <120 mg/dL**. Screen with an OGTT at **6–12 weeks postpartum** — half will develop type 2 diabetes within 10 years.

**REMEMBER:** *Insulin first; metformin/glyburide acceptable; never a glitazone.*

---

## 89. Pre-eclampsia Without Proteinuria

**PEARL:** New-onset hypertension **without proteinuria** still diagnoses pre-eclampsia if **ANY** of the following appear:
- **Thrombocytopenia** — platelets **<100 × 10⁹/L**
- **Renal insufficiency** — creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** or **doubling** of baseline
- **Impaired liver function** — transaminases **≥2× normal**
- **Pulmonary oedema**
- **New-onset headache unresponsive to medication, or visual symptoms**

**UNDERSTAND:** Pre-eclampsia is a **systemic endothelial disease**, not a kidney disease — proteinuria is just one manifestation, and waiting for it delays diagnosis in women who are already deteriorating.

**MUST KNOW:** **The amount of proteinuria does NOT determine severity** and is no longer part of the severe-features list. Any of these features = **pre-eclampsia with severe features** → delivery is the definitive treatment.

**REMEMBER:** *No protein needed — platelets, creatinine, transaminases, lungs, or head will do.*

---

## 90. MgSO4 Regimens in Eclampsia

**PEARL:** The recognized regimens are **Pritchard, Zuspan, Sibai and Dhaka**. **"Magpie regimen" does NOT exist** — the **Magpie Trial** was the landmark study that proved magnesium sulphate's effectiveness.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Pritchard (IM + IV)**: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading, then **5 g IM 4-hourly** — suited to settings without infusion pumps.
- **Zuspan (IV)**: 4 g IV loading then **1–2 g/hour infusion**.
- **Dhaka/low-dose regimens** are used where women have lower body weight.
- Continue for **24 hours after delivery or after the last fit**, whichever is later.

**REMEMBER:** *Pritchard, Zuspan, Sibai, Dhaka are regimens; Magpie is a trial.*

---

## 91. Antihypertensives Safe in Breastfeeding

**PEARL:** Agents with **no known adverse effect on the breastfed infant** — mnemonic **"ECLAM-N"**: **E**nalapril, **C**aptopril, **L**abetalol, **A**tenolol, **M**etoprolol, **N**ifedipine.

**MUST KNOW:** Note the contrast with **pregnancy**, where **ACE inhibitors are absolutely contraindicated** (fetal renal failure, oligohydramnios, skull hypoplasia) but become acceptable **after delivery**. In pregnancy the safe drugs are **labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa and hydralazine**.

**REMEMBER:** *ACE inhibitors: forbidden in pregnancy, permitted in lactation.*
