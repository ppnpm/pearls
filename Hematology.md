## 1. MDS with Isolated del(5q)

**PEARL:** A distinct subtype of myelodysplastic syndrome with a **relatively good prognosis**; **treatment of choice = LENALIDOMIDE**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Classic patient: an **elderly woman** with **macrocytic anaemia, a normal or HIGH platelet count**, and **hypolobated (mononuclear) megakaryocytes** — the "**5q− syndrome**".
- Lenalidomide produces **transfusion independence** in ~two-thirds and can cause **cytogenetic remission**.
- Low risk of transformation to AML; the **IPSS-R** score guides overall MDS management (azacitidine/decitabine for high risk, allogeneic HSCT for the fit young).

**REMEMBER:** *5q− = old lady, big red cells, high platelets, funny megakaryocytes → lenalidomide.*

## 2. Targeted Agents — Quick Grid

**PEARL:**
- **Vemurafenib** → **BRAF inhibitor** (BRAF V600E **melanoma**)
- **Alemtuzumab** → **anti-CD52** monoclonal antibody
- **Lonafarnib** → **farnesyl transferase inhibitor**
- **Vorinostat** → **HDAC (histone deacetylase) inhibitor** — used in **cutaneous T-cell lymphoma**, including **mycosis fungoides and Sézary syndrome**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Alemtuzumab** (CD52 is on both B and T lymphocytes) is used in **CLL and in multiple sclerosis**, causing profound, prolonged lymphopenia → **opportunistic infection (CMV reactivation)**.
- **Lonafarnib** is approved for **Hutchinson–Gilford progeria** — it blocks farnesylation of the abnormal **progerin**.
- **Vemurafenib must not be used in BRAF wild-type tumours** (paradoxical MAPK activation); it causes **cutaneous squamous cell carcinomas/keratoacanthomas** — reduced by adding a MEK inhibitor.
- Other CTCL options: bexarotene, romidepsin (also an HDAC inhibitor), PUVA, mogamulizumab.

**REMEMBER:** *Vemurafenib–BRAF, Alemtuzumab–CD52, Lonafarnib–farnesyl (progeria), Vorinostat–HDAC (CTCL).*

## 3. Emperipolesis

**PEARL:** **Intact lymphocytes or other cells lying WITHIN the cytoplasm of a histiocyte/plasma cell — without being destroyed.** Classically seen in:
- **Rosai–Dorfman disease** — the **hallmark** feature
- **Autoimmune hepatitis** — plasma cells with emperipolesis
- **Hodgkin lymphoma** — occasionally in Reed–Sternberg cells

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction from **phagocytosis** is the whole point — in emperipolesis the engulfed cell is **alive, intact and can walk out again**; in phagocytosis it is digested.

**MUST KNOW:** Rosai–Dorfman disease = **sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy** — painless bilateral cervical nodes in a young person, with **S100-positive, CD1a-NEGATIVE** histiocytes (contrast **Langerhans cell histiocytosis: S100+ AND CD1a+/langerin+**).

**REMEMBER:** *Cells alive inside a histiocyte = emperipolesis = Rosai–Dorfman (S100+, CD1a−).*

## 4. Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis — Classic Presentation

**PEARL:** **Seborrhoeic dermatitis-like rash + "punched-out" lytic skull lesions** are the classic LCH combination.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The rash is **scaly, crusted, petechial, in the scalp and flexures** — often mistaken for cradle cap or nappy rash that "won't clear".
- Skeletal signs: punched-out skull lesions **without a sclerotic rim**, **beveled edge / hole-within-a-hole**, **button sequestrum**, **floating teeth** (loss of lamina dura), and **vertebra plana** (LCH is the commonest cause in children).
- **Diabetes insipidus** from pituitary stalk involvement is the commonest endocrine complication.
- Diagnosis: **Birbeck granules** on EM; **CD1a, S100 and langerin (CD207) positive**. Many carry the **BRAF V600E** mutation — hence a role for vemurafenib.

**REMEMBER:** *Refractory "cradle cap" + punched-out skull holes = LCH; CD1a positive.*

## 5. B-cell Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia — Markers

**PEARL:** Blasts expressing **CD19, CD20 and CD79a** are of **B-cell lineage** → **B-ALL**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **B-lineage:** CD19, CD20, CD79a, CD22, **CD10 (CALLA — good prognosis)**, TdT.
- **T-lineage:** CD3 (most specific), CD2, CD5, CD7 — T-ALL classically presents in an **adolescent boy with a mediastinal mass**.
- **TdT is positive in both** and marks a **lymphoblast** (absent in AML); **myeloperoxidase and Auer rods** indicate AML.
- Prognosis in B-ALL: **hyperdiploidy and t(12;21) are good; t(9;22) (Philadelphia) and hypodiploidy are poor**.

**REMEMBER:** *CD19/20/79a = B; CD3 = T; TdT = lymphoblast either way.*

## 6. Childhood ALL — Presentation

**PEARL:** Presents with **bone pain, pallor, fever, bruising and hepatosplenomegaly** from marrow infiltration by lymphoblasts.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Bone pain and a limp/refusal to walk** are highly characteristic in children — ALL is a classic mimic of **juvenile idiopathic arthritis** and "growing pains". The clue is **cytopenias with bone pain**.
- Peak age **2–5 years**; the commonest childhood malignancy, with **>85% cure**.
- **CNS and testes are sanctuary sites** → intrathecal prophylaxis is mandatory.
- Watch for **tumour lysis syndrome** on starting therapy (hydration ± rasburicase).

**REMEMBER:** *Child + bone pain + pancytopenia = ALL until the marrow says otherwise.*

## 7. Pseudo-Gaucher Cells

**PEARL:** A **large macrophage engorged with ingested haematopoietic cells** (a **pseudo-Gaucher / sea-blue histiocyte-like** appearance) is classically associated with **high cell turnover states — especially chronic myeloid leukaemia**.

**UNDERSTAND:** These macrophages are **not** enzyme-deficient — they are simply **overwhelmed by the volume of cell membrane (glucocerebroside) they must digest**. True Gaucher disease is a **glucocerebrosidase deficiency**.

**MUST KNOW:** Also seen in **thalassaemia, sickle cell disease, and other myeloproliferative disorders**. True Gaucher cells have a "**crumpled tissue paper**" cytoplasm; sea-blue histiocytes stain blue with Giemsa/Wright.

**REMEMBER:** *Too much turnover, not too little enzyme = pseudo-Gaucher (think CML).*
