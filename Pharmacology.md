# Pharmacology — Revision Pearls (Part 1)

---

## 1. Pharmacogenetics

**PEARL:** Pharmacogenetic reactions are ADRs caused by genetic variation in individuals.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Classic examples: G6PD deficiency (primaquine/dapsone hemolysis), slow acetylators (INH neuropathy, hydralazine lupus), pseudocholinesterase deficiency (succinylcholine apnoea), malignant hyperthermia (RYR1 + halothane/suxamethonium), HLA-B*1502 + carbamazepine → SJS.
- In the Rawlins–Thompson classification these are **Type B (bizarre)** — unpredictable, not dose-related.

**REMEMBER:** *Genes decide the reaction → Type B, unpredictable ADR.*

---

## 2. New Drug Application Timing

**PEARL:** The NDA is filed **after phase 3**. Phase 3 is also called the **multisite/multicentric trial**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Phase 1: healthy volunteers (20–100), safety/PK. Phase 2: patients (100–300), efficacy + dose. Phase 3: large multicentre RCT (500–3000). Phase 4: post-marketing surveillance (pharmacovigilance).
- Phase 0 = microdosing, subtherapeutic, human PK only.

**REMEMBER:** *NDA after Phase 3; Phase 4 needs no NDA (it's post-marketing).*

---

## 3. Pharmacokinetic Formulas

**PEARL:**

- **Loading dose = Vd × desired steady-state plasma concentration**
- **Maintenance dose = Clearance × desired steady-state plasma concentration**

**MUST KNOW:**

- Loading dose depends only on **Vd** — unchanged in renal/hepatic failure; maintenance dose depends on **clearance** — must be reduced in organ failure.
- Divide by bioavailability (F) for oral dosing.
- Steady state is reached in **4–5 half-lives** regardless of dose.

**REMEMBER:** *Load by volume, maintain by clearance.*

---

## 4. Drug–Protein Binding

**PEARL:** **Albumin** binds **acidic** drugs (phenytoin, NSAIDs, warfarin, sulfonamides). **α1-acid glycoprotein** binds **basic** drugs (lignocaine, disopyramide, quinidine, propranolol, imipramine).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Only the **free (unbound)** fraction is pharmacologically active and available for metabolism/excretion.
- Hypoalbuminemia (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis) → raised free phenytoin with a normal total level → toxicity at "therapeutic" levels.
- α1-acid glycoprotein is an **acute phase reactant** — rises in MI/inflammation, lowering free lignocaine.

**REMEMBER:** *Albumin = Acidic; α1-Acid Glycoprotein = Basic (name is the opposite of its cargo).*

---

## 5. P-glycoprotein and Digoxin Toxicity

**PEARL:** P-gp (MDR1, **CD243**) is the efflux pump chiefly responsible for digoxin elimination. Inhibiting P-gp → digoxin toxicity. **Clarithromycin/erythromycin** are classic culprits.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Other P-gp inhibitors: **amiodarone, quinidine, verapamil, diltiazem, cyclosporine, itraconazole, propafenone, flecainide**.
- P-gp inducer: **rifampicin** (lowers digoxin levels).
- P-gp is MDR**1**, not MDR2; it also limits drug entry at the blood–brain barrier and gut.
- Digoxin toxicity: nausea, **yellow-green vision (xanthopsia)**, arrhythmia with hypokalemia; antidote = digoxin-specific Fab.

**REMEMBER:** *P-gp inhibitor + digoxin = toxicity; rifampicin does the opposite.*

---

## 6. CYP Interactions Worth Memorizing

**PEARL:**

- **Verapamil (CYP3A4 inhibitor) + statins → myopathy/rhabdomyolysis** (atorvastatin, simvastatin, lovastatin are 3A4-metabolized).
- **Erythromycin (CYP1A2 inhibitor) + theophylline → theophylline toxicity.**
- **Ritonavir/lopinavir (potent CYP3A4 inhibitors) + ergotamine → ergotism** (limb ischemia, gangrene).
- **CYP2C9 inhibitors:** amiodarone, azoles, cimetidine, clopidogrel, cotrimoxazole, disulfiram, isoniazid, metronidazole (→ raise warfarin/phenytoin levels).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Statin myopathy is the key toxicity — check CPK; rosuvastatin/pravastatin are less 3A4-dependent.
- **Clarithromycin also inhibits OATP1B1/1B3**, raising **rosuvastatin and pravastatin** — so no statin is entirely safe with it.

**REMEMBER:** *3A4 inhibitors: statins → muscle, ergot → gangrene.*

---

## 7. Fenofibrate

**PEARL:** Fibrates act via **PPAR-α**, stimulating **lipoprotein lipase** → drug of choice for severe hypertriglyceridemia (**>1000 mg/dL**, to prevent pancreatitis).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Best drug class for lowering **triglycerides**; statins are best for LDL.
- ADRs: myopathy (much worse **with statins**), gallstones (cholesterol supersaturation), raised transaminases, raised creatinine.
- **Fenofibrate** is the safer fibrate to combine with a statin (gemfibrozil most increases myopathy risk).

**REMEMBER:** *Fibrate = PPAR-α = TG drug; gemfibrozil + statin = danger.*

---

## 8. Isosorbide Dinitrate + Hydralazine

**PEARL:** ISDN with hydralazine is indicated in CHF — particularly in African-American patients and in those who cannot tolerate ACE inhibitors/ARBs.

**UNDERSTAND:** Nitrate = venodilator (↓preload); hydralazine = arteriolar dilator (↓afterload) and it also limits nitrate tolerance.

**REMEMBER:** *Nitrate + hydralazine = preload + afterload; the CHF option when ACEi can't be used.*

---

## 9. Ranolazine and Trimetazidine — Metabolic Antianginals

**PEARL:**

- **Ranolazine:** inhibits the **late sodium current (I-Na)** → less Ca²⁺ entry via Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange → ↓diastolic tension and wall stress.
- **Trimetazidine:** **pFOX inhibitor** — partially blocks myocardial fatty-acid oxidation, shifting to more oxygen-efficient glucose oxidation.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Both act **without lowering heart rate or blood pressure** — ideal add-ons in hypotensive/bradycardic angina patients.
- Ranolazine prolongs QT but is not proarrhythmic; it also lowers HbA1c.

**REMEMBER:** *Ranolazine = late Na current; trimetazidine = fatty acid oxidation. No effect on HR/BP.*

---

## 10. Sumatriptan Worsens Angina

**PEARL:** Sumatriptan is a **5-HT1B/1D agonist** — it constricts cranial vessels to abort migraine, but also causes **coronary vasoconstriction** → contraindicated in ischemic heart disease, Prinzmetal angina, uncontrolled hypertension.

**MUST KNOW:** Also contraindicated with ergot alkaloids or MAO inhibitors (within 24 h/2 weeks respectively).

**REMEMBER:** *Triptans constrict everywhere — never in CAD.*

---

## 11. Ergotamine

**PEARL:** Ergotamine is a **partial agonist/antagonist at α-adrenergic, 5-HT1 and 5-HT2 receptors** → sustained vasoconstriction, vasomotor centre depression, visceral smooth muscle contraction.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Ergotism = peripheral ischemia/gangrene, "St. Anthony's fire".
- Precipitated by CYP3A4 inhibitors (HIV protease inhibitors, macrolides, azoles).
- Contraindicated in pregnancy (uterine contraction), PVD, CAD, sepsis.

**REMEMBER:** *Ergot + CYP3A4 inhibitor = gangrene.*

---

## 12. Amiodarone — Two Key Toxicities

**PEARL:**

- **Pulmonary fibrosis** is the most serious chronic toxicity → amiodarone is **contraindicated in interstitial lung disease**. Risk factors: pre-existing lung disease, ≥400 mg/day, recent pneumonia.
- **Slate grey-blue facial pigmentation** in sun-exposed skin — UV light drives deposition of amiodarone/metabolites; elderly men most affected.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Other ADRs: corneal microdeposits (almost universal, benign), thyroid dysfunction (both hypo and hyper — it is 37% iodine by weight), hepatotoxicity, optic neuropathy.
- Class III antiarrhythmic but has all four Vaughan–Williams class actions; huge Vd, t½ ~ 25–100 days.

**REMEMBER:** *Amiodarone = Lung fibrosis + blue face + thyroid + cornea.*

---

## 13. Dofetilide

**PEARL:** Dofetilide is a **class III** antiarrhythmic (pure IKr potassium channel blocker) — used for AF conversion/maintenance; requires in-hospital initiation because of **torsades de pointes** risk.

**REMEMBER:** *Class III = K⁺ blockers = QT prolongation (amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide, dronedarone).*

---

## 14. Vorapaxar

**PEARL:** Vorapaxar is a competitive antagonist of **PAR-1**, the platelet **thrombin** receptor — it blocks thrombin-induced platelet aggregation.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Used with aspirin and/or clopidogrel to reduce thrombotic events post-MI or in PAD.
- **Contraindicated with any history of stroke/TIA/ICH** — high intracranial bleed risk.

**REMEMBER:** *Vorapaxar = PAR-1 (thrombin receptor) blocker; never after a stroke.*

---

## 15. Antiplatelet Mechanisms — Quick Grid

**PEARL:**

- **Aspirin** irreversibly inhibits **COX-1** → no **TXA2** (a platelet aggregator and vasoconstrictor) → bleeding tendency for the platelet's 7–10 day life.
- **Clopidogrel/prasugrel/ticagrelor** = **P2Y12 (ADP)** blockers.
- **Prasugrel** is a **prodrug**, irreversible, activated by **CYP3A4/2B6** — hence **omeprazole (a CYP2C19 inhibitor) does not blunt it**, unlike clopidogrel. It is ~10× more potent than clopidogrel with faster, more predictable onset.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Clopidogrel needs **CYP2C19** → poor metabolizers and omeprazole reduce its effect. Ticagrelor is reversible and not a prodrug.

**REMEMBER:** *Clopidogrel–2C19–omeprazole problem; prasugrel escapes it.*

---

## 16. Anticoagulant Reversal Agents

**PEARL:**

- **Idarucizumab** → reverses **dabigatran** (monoclonal antibody, 350× higher affinity for dabigatran than thrombin; renally excreted complex).
- **Andexanet alfa** → reverses **factor Xa inhibitors** (rivaroxaban, apixaban).
- **Ciraparantag** → reverses **all direct anticoagulants** — but **not fondaparinux**, an *indirect* Xa inhibitor.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Dabigatran is the only oral direct thrombin inhibitor** in clinical use; parenteral DTIs = bivalirudin, argatroban, **lepirudin**.
- **Lepirudin** is an irreversible direct thrombin inhibitor; main ADR is **bleeding** — needs aPTT monitoring and dose adjustment.
- Warfarin reversal = vitamin K + PCC/FFP; heparin reversal = protamine.

**REMEMBER:** *Ida→Dabi, Andexanet→Xa, Ciraparantag→all direct (not fondaparinux).*

---

## 17. Apixaban — New Indication

**PEARL:** Apixaban is approved to reduce stroke and systemic embolism in **non-valvular atrial fibrillation**.

**MUST KNOW:** DOACs are **contraindicated in valvular AF (mechanical valves, moderate–severe mitral stenosis)** — warfarin is the only option there.

**REMEMBER:** *DOACs for non-valvular AF only.*

---

## 18. Tranexamic Acid & ε-Aminocaproic Acid

**PEARL:** Both are **lysine analogues** that competitively occupy the lysine-binding sites on plasminogen/plasmin, preventing binding to fibrin → **inhibit fibrinolysis**. They are the **antidote to fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) therapy**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- TXA is given IV in **trauma resuscitation (CRASH-2: within 3 h)**, massive hemorrhage, and **postpartum hemorrhage (WOMAN trial)**.
- TXA is ~10× more potent than EACA.
- Also used in menorrhagia, hereditary angioedema prophylaxis, and dental extraction in hemophilia.

**REMEMBER:** *Lysine analogue = anti-fibrinolytic = thrombolytic antidote.*

---

## 19. Timolol in Glaucoma

**PEARL:** Timolol is a **non-selective β-blocker** that lowers IOP by **reducing aqueous humour production** at the ciliary epithelium.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Ocular ADRs: allergy, **decreased corneal sensation**, punctate keratitis, dry eye.
- Systemic absorption via nasolacrimal duct → **bronchospasm and bradycardia** — avoid in asthma/COPD and heart block (press on the punctum after instilling).
- It does **not** cause miosis or accommodative spasm (unlike pilocarpine) — no dim vision/brow ache.

**REMEMBER:** *Timolol reduces production; asthma is the trap.*

---

## 20. Prostaglandin Analogues in Glaucoma

**PEARL:** PG analogues (latanoprost etc.) are best avoided/last-line when **macular edema** is a concern — cystoid macular oedema is a known side effect.

**MUST KNOW:** They lower IOP most effectively, by increasing **uveoscleral outflow**; ADRs: iris/periocular hyperpigmentation, eyelash growth (used as bimatoprost for hypotrichosis), uveitis reactivation, and CME in aphakic/pseudophakic eyes.

**REMEMBER:** *Latanoprost = best pressure drop, but macular edema + brown iris.*

---

## 21. Brimonidine in Infants

**PEARL:** Brimonidine (α2 agonist) **crosses the blood–brain barrier** → CNS depression and **apnoea** in neonates. **Contraindicated below 2 years of age.**

**REMEMBER:** *Brimonidine = baby apnoea; banned under 2.*

---

## 22. Phenylephrine vs Naloxone in Opioid Miosis

**PEARL:** Phenylephrine (α1 agonist) causes mydriasis by contracting the dilator pupillae, but it will **not reverse opioid-induced miosis**, which is centrally driven (Edinger–Westphal nucleus disinhibition).

**MUST KNOW:** Reverse it with an **opioid antagonist** — naloxone (or levallorphan).

**REMEMBER:** *Opioid miosis is central — you need naloxone, not a mydriatic.*

---

## 23. Levallorphan

**PEARL:** Levallorphan is an opioid antagonist similar to naloxone — reverses severe opioid-induced **respiratory depression**.

**MUST KNOW:** Naloxone is the drug of choice (pure antagonist, IV, t½ ~1 h — repeat doses/infusion needed as it is shorter-acting than most opioids). Naltrexone = oral, long-acting, for maintenance/de-addiction.

**REMEMBER:** *Naloxone = acute overdose; naltrexone = long-term abstinence.*

---

## 24. Levopropoxyphene

**PEARL:** Levopropoxyphene is a **centrally acting opioid antitussive** with **no analgesic activity**; dose 50–100 mg every 4 h; may cause sedation.

**MUST KNOW:** Opioid antitussives: codeine (standard), pholcodine, dextromethorphan (non-opioid sigma/NMDA action, no addiction), noscapine.

**REMEMBER:** *Levopropoxyphene = cough only, no pain relief.*

---

## 25. Tramadol

**PEARL:** Tramadol acts at the **µ-opioid receptor AND inhibits serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Hence risks: **serotonin syndrome** (with SSRIs/MAOIs) and **seizures** (lowers seizure threshold).
- Naloxone reverses only the opioid component.
- It is a **preferred analgesic in CKD** along with fentanyl (see next pearl).

**REMEMBER:** *Tramadol = opioid + SNRI → serotonin syndrome and fits.*

---

## 26. Pentazocine

**PEARL:** Pentazocine is a **partial agonist at µ** and a **full agonist at κ** receptors.

**MUST KNOW:**

- κ agonism → analgesia + **dysphoria/hallucinations**; µ partial agonism → can **precipitate withdrawal** in a morphine-dependent patient and has a **ceiling effect**.
- Raises catecholamines → ↑BP and cardiac work — avoid in MI (unlike morphine).

**REMEMBER:** *Pentazocine = kappa full, mu partial → dysphoria + withdrawal in addicts.*

---

## 27. Analgesia in CKD

**PEARL:** **Paracetamol (acetaminophen)** is the safest non-narcotic analgesic in CKD; for severe pain use **tramadol or fentanyl**.

**MUST KNOW:** Avoid NSAIDs (reduce afferent arteriolar flow, AKI, interstitial nephritis) and **morphine/pethidine** (active metabolites morphine-6-glucuronide and norpethidine accumulate → sedation/seizures). Fentanyl has no active metabolites.

**REMEMBER:** *CKD pain ladder: paracetamol → tramadol/fentanyl. Never NSAIDs or morphine.*

---

## 28. Arachidonic Acid Pathway Inhibitors

**PEARL:**

- **Corticosteroids** → inhibit **phospholipase A2** (via lipocortin/annexin) → block **both** prostaglandins and leukotrienes.
- **NSAIDs** → inhibit **cyclooxygenase** → block prostaglandins/thromboxanes only.
- **Zileuton** → inhibits **5-lipoxygenase** → blocks leukotrienes. (Montelukast/zafirlukast block the CysLT1 **receptor**.)

**REMEMBER:** *Steroid blocks the tap (PLA2); NSAID blocks one pipe (COX); zileuton blocks the other (LOX).*

---

## 29. Eicosanoids

**PEARL:** Eicosanoids (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes) are the most **universally distributed autacoids** — nearly every cell makes one or more. **Platelets mainly make thromboxane (TXA2)**; **endothelium mainly makes PGI2 (prostacyclin)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Platelet = TXA2 (aggregate, vasoconstrict); Endothelium = PGI2 (opposite).*

---

## 30. Disulfiram

**PEARL:** Disulfiram is an **irreversible inhibitor of aldehyde dehydrogenase** (both cytosolic and mitochondrial) → acetaldehyde accumulates. It works by **aversive conditioning**, **not** anti-craving.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Reaction: flushing, throbbing headache, vomiting, hypotension, palpitations, dread.
- **Anti-craving** drugs: **naltrexone, acamprosate** (NMDA modulator), topiramate, baclofen.
- Disulfiram-like reaction also from **metronidazole, tinidazole, cefoperazone, chlorpropamide, griseofulvin**.
- It also inhibits dopamine β-hydroxylase → can precipitate psychosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Disulfiram = aversion (ALDH); naltrexone/acamprosate = craving.*

---

## 31. Oxazepam in Liver Disease

**PEARL:** **Oxazepam** (with lorazepam and temazepam) is safe in hepatic impairment — short-acting and cleared by direct **glucuronidation only**, with no active metabolites and no CYP oxidation.

**REMEMBER:** *"LOT" (Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam) = liver-friendly benzodiazepines.*

---

## 32. Oxcarbazepine and Hyponatremia

**PEARL:** **Hyponatremia occurs more often with oxcarbazepine than carbamazepine.**

**MUST KNOW:** Mechanism = SIADH-like effect; risk rises with age, diuretics, high dose. Oxcarbazepine otherwise has fewer interactions (no epoxide metabolite, weaker enzyme induction) and no need for blood-count monitoring.

**REMEMBER:** *Oxcarbazepine drops sodium more than carbamazepine.*

---

## 33. Carbamazepine — Idiosyncratic Reactions

**PEARL:** Idiosyncratic blood dyscrasias include fatal **aplastic anaemia and agranulocytosis**; the **commonest** idiosyncratic reaction is an **erythematous rash**, and rarely **SJS/TEN**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **HLA-B*1502** (Han Chinese/South-East Asian) predicts SJS/TEN → test before starting.
- Also causes hyponatremia (SIADH), diplopia/ataxia (dose-related), and is a potent **CYP3A4 inducer** with **auto-induction**.
- DOC for trigeminal neuralgia; can worsen absence and myoclonic seizures.

**REMEMBER:** *Carbamazepine: rash common, aplastic anaemia rare but fatal, HLA-B*1502 screen.*

---

## 34. Retigabine (Ezogabine)

**PEARL:** A **K⁺ channel opener** (enhances **KCNQ** transmembrane potassium currents) approved as add-on for focal-onset seizures in adults >18 y; **withdrawn** because of **retinal abnormalities and blue-grey pigmentation** of skin/nails/retina.

**MUST KNOW:** Metabolized by **glucuronidation and acetylation**; t½ 7–11 h → thrice-daily dosing. **Phenytoin/carbamazepine lower its plasma level** → increase the retigabine dose when co-administered.

**REMEMBER:** *Only antiepileptic that opens K⁺ channels — killed by blue retina.*

---

## 35. Sodium Valproate for Refractory Rheumatic Chorea

**PEARL:** **Sodium valproate** is the drug used for refractory/intractable rheumatic (Sydenham's) chorea — preferred because it lacks **extrapyramidal** side effects.

**MUST KNOW:** Alternatives: carbamazepine; haloperidol/antipsychotics work but cause EPS. Most Sydenham chorea is self-limiting — treat only if disabling.

**REMEMBER:** *Sydenham chorea → valproate (no EPS).*

---

## 36. Memantine

**PEARL:** Memantine is a **non-competitive (open-channel, low-affinity) NMDA antagonist** that limits glutamate excitotoxicity and slows progression in **moderate-to-severe** Alzheimer's disease.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Can be given alone or **with cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine — used from mild disease onward).
- Titrate 5 mg OD up to 10 mg BD; give for ≥6 months.
- **Renally excreted** → dose-adjust in renal failure; ADRs are dizziness, headache, confusion.

**REMEMBER:** *Mild AD = cholinesterase inhibitor; moderate-severe = add memantine (renal drug).*

---

## 37. Piracetam

**PEARL:** Piracetam is a **nootropic** (cognition enhancer) used off-label for memory and, importantly, for **myoclonus**.

**REMEMBER:** *Piracetam = nootropic + myoclonus.*

---

## 38. Edaravone

**PEARL:** Edaravone (a free-radical scavenger) is FDA-approved for **amyotrophic lateral sclerosis**.

**MUST KNOW:** The other ALS drug is **riluzole** (inhibits glutamate release) — the only one shown to prolong survival. Both slow, neither cures.

**REMEMBER:** *ALS = Riluzole + Edaravone.*

---

## 39. Trihexyphenidyl

**PEARL:** **Trihexyphenidyl**, a central anticholinergic, is the **drug of choice for drug-induced parkinsonism**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Antipsychotics block D2 → relative cholinergic excess in the striatum; restoring the dopamine–acetylcholine balance requires an anticholinergic, not levodopa (which would worsen psychosis and is ineffective while receptors are blocked).

**MUST KNOW:** Anticholinergics help tremor and rigidity, **not bradykinesia**; avoid in the elderly (confusion) and in **tardive dyskinesia** (they worsen it).

**REMEMBER:** *Drug-induced parkinsonism → anticholinergic, never levodopa.*

---

## 40. Muscarinic Receptor Subtypes

**PEARL:** M1–M5. Broad effects:

- **M1** (CNS, gastric parietal): cognition, gastric acid, **increased seizure activity**
- **M2** (heart, presynaptic): **bradycardia**, tremors, bronchoconstriction, abdominal cramps
- **M3** (smooth muscle/glands): **bladder contraction, miosis, salivary and bronchial secretion**
- **M4/M5**: CNS — M4 cataplexy-like muscle weakness; M5 dopamine release

**MUST KNOW:**

- Odd receptors (M1, M3, M5) → **Gq → IP3/DAG**; even (M2, M4) → **Gi → ↓cAMP + GIRK opening**.
- M3 blockade explains antimuscarinic side effects: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision.

**REMEMBER:** *Odd = Gq (excitatory), Even = Gi (inhibitory).*

---

## 41. GIRK-Coupled Receptors

**PEARL:** These Gi/Go receptors open **G-protein–coupled inwardly rectifying K⁺ channels (GIRK)** → K⁺ efflux → **hyperpolarization/inhibition**: **M2 muscarinic, A1 adenosine, α2 adrenergic, D2 dopamine, 5-HT1, somatostatin, GABA-B, opioid**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The **AT1 (angiotensin 1)** receptor is **Gq** — it acts through IP3/DAG and Ca²⁺, **not** through K⁺ channels. Classic "which does NOT" answer.

**REMEMBER:** *Inhibitory GPCRs open K⁺ (GIRK); AT1 is Gq/Ca²⁺.*

---

## 42. Presynaptic K⁺ Channel Blockade

**PEARL:** Blocking presynaptic **potassium** channels prolongs depolarization → more **voltage-gated Ca²⁺** channel opening → **increased acetylcholine release** at the NMJ.

**MUST KNOW:** This is the basis of **3,4-diaminopyridine/amifampridine** in **Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome**, and of 4-aminopyridine (dalfampridine) in multiple sclerosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Block K⁺ → Ca²⁺ enters → ACh released (LEMS drug).*

---

## 43. Drugs to Avoid in Myasthenia Gravis

**PEARL:** **Aminoglycosides** block Ca²⁺-mediated presynaptic ACh exocytosis and also directly inhibit post-synaptic ACh receptors → worsen MG.

**MUST KNOW:** Other aggravators: **quinolones, macrolides, β-blockers, botulinum toxin, local anaesthetics, magnesium, non-depolarizing muscle relaxants, penicillamine, quinine, phenytoin, chloroquine**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** **Penicillamine** can actually *induce* MG (antibody-mediated); **magnesium** (as in eclampsia therapy) can precipitate a crisis.

**REMEMBER:** *MG mnemonic: "A-B-C" — Aminoglycosides/Antiarrhythmics, Beta-blockers/Botulinum, Ca-blockers/Chloroquine + Magnesium & Penicillamine.*

---

## 44. Adrenaline — Doses

**PEARL:** For anaphylactic shock give **adrenaline 1:1000, 0.5 mL IM** (= 0.5 mg) into the **anterolateral thigh**, repeatable every 5–15 minutes.

**MUST KNOW:**

- 1:1000 = 1 mg/mL (IM); **1:10,000 = 0.1 mg/mL is the IV/cardiac arrest strength**.
- Paediatric dose: 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.5 mg).
- IM thigh gives faster peak levels than subcutaneous or deltoid.

**REMEMBER:** *Anaphylaxis = 1:1000, 0.5 mL, IM thigh. Arrest = 1:10,000 IV.*

---

## 45. Ipratropium — Paradoxical Bronchoconstriction

**PEARL:** Paradoxical bronchoconstriction, especially by **nebulizer**, due to: **hypotonic nebulizer solution**, **preservatives (benzalkonium chloride, EDTA)**, and blockade of **prejunctional M2 autoreceptors** (which normally brake ACh release) → more ACh released.

**REMEMBER:** *M2 autoreceptor block = more ACh = wheeze; blame benzalkonium too.*

---

## 46. Salbutamol Causes Hyperglycemia

**PEARL:** Salbutamol causes **hyperglycemia**, not hypoglycemia (β2 → glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis).

**MUST KNOW:** Other β2 effects: **hypokalemia** (K⁺ driven intracellularly — used therapeutically in hyperkalemia), tremor, tachycardia, lactic acidosis.

**REMEMBER:** *β2 agonist: sugar up, potassium down.*

---

## 47. Theophylline

**PEARL:** Metabolized mainly by **CYP1A2**; **erythromycin (and ciprofloxacin, cimetidine)** inhibit CYP1A2 → theophylline toxicity (arrhythmias, seizures).

**MUST KNOW:** **Smoking induces CYP1A2** → smokers need higher doses; toxicity may appear on quitting. Narrow therapeutic index (10–20 µg/mL).

**REMEMBER:** *Erythromycin raises, smoking lowers, theophylline.*

---

## 48. SGLT2 Inhibitors — Adverse Effects

**PEARL:** Main ADRs are **genital mycotic infections and lower urinary tract infections** from glycosuria. FDA has also warned of an **increased fracture risk**, with effects on **PTH and 1,25-(OH)2 vitamin D** disturbing bone mineral balance.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis** — the high-yield emergency; stop before surgery/fasting.
- Rare but classic: **Fournier's gangrene**, volume depletion/hypotension.
- Benefits: proven cardiovascular and **renal** protection, weight and BP reduction.

**REMEMBER:** *Sugar in urine = yeast + UTI; watch euglycemic DKA and bones.*

---

## 49. Linagliptin in Renal Failure

**PEARL:** **Linagliptin needs no dose adjustment in renal impairment** — it is the DPP-4 inhibitor with predominant **biliary/faecal excretion**.

**MUST KNOW:** Sitagliptin, saxagliptin and vildagliptin are renally excreted and require dose reduction. Class ADRs: pancreatitis, arthralgia, urticaria/angioedema (with ACE inhibitors); weight-neutral, low hypoglycemia risk.

**REMEMBER:** *Linagliptin = "liver-gliptin" = safe kidney.*

---

## 50. 5α-Reductase Inhibitors

**PEARL:** Finasteride/dutasteride competitively inhibit **5α-reductase**, which converts testosterone to the more potent **DHT** → ↓DHT, **↓PSA (roughly halved)**, ↓prostate volume — while **plasma testosterone and LH remain unchanged**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Because PSA halves, **double the measured PSA** when screening a patient on finasteride.
- Takes **6 months** for maximal benefit; ADRs: decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, gynecomastia.
- Also reduces prostate bleeding/hematuria; finasteride 1 mg for androgenetic alopecia. **Category X — pregnant women must not handle crushed tablets.**

**REMEMBER:** *DHT down, PSA halves, testosterone unchanged.*

---

## 51. Tamsulosin for Postoperative Urinary Retention

**PEARL:** **Tamsulosin** is used for postoperative urinary retention, and is the α-blocker of choice in a **COPD** patient (α1A-selective — no bronchial or significant cardiovascular effect).

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **retrograde ejaculation**, dizziness/postural hypotension, and **intraoperative floppy iris syndrome** — always ask before cataract surgery.

**REMEMBER:** *Tamsulosin = uroselective; think floppy iris.*

---

## 52. Erection is Muscarinic (Cholinergic)

**PEARL:** Erection is mediated by **muscarinic (parasympathetic)** transmission → NO release; ejaculation is sympathetic.

**REMEMBER:** *"Point (Parasympathetic) and Shoot (Sympathetic)."*

---

## 53. Sucralfate

**PEARL:** In an acidic environment (**pH < 4**) sucralfate polymerizes into a viscous sticky gel that adheres to the ulcer crater for ~6 hours.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Therefore give **on an empty stomach** and **do not combine with antacids/PPIs** which raise pH and abolish its action.
- It **blocks absorption of other drugs** — phenytoin, digoxin, cimetidine, ketoconazole, fluoroquinolones, levothyroxine → separate doses by **≥2 hours**.
- **Constipation** is the commonest ADR; contains **aluminium** → avoid in renal failure (aluminium toxicity/encephalopathy); can cause **bezoars**.

**REMEMBER:** *Sucralfate needs acid, sticks to ulcers, steals other drugs — space by 2 hours.*

---

## 54. Prokinetics

**PEARL:** Prokinetics increase GI motility:

- **D2 antagonists** — metoclopramide, domperidone
- **5-HT4 agonists** — cisapride, prucalopride, velusetrag, naronapride
- **Motilin/motilin-receptor agonists** — erythromycin, camicinal
- **Others** — sincalide (CCK octapeptide), relamorelin (ghrelin agonist)

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Prucalopride** is a selective 5-HT4 agonist for chronic constipation — safe because it lacks cisapride's hERG/QT toxicity (cisapride was withdrawn).
- **Domperidone** does not cross the BBB → no extrapyramidal effects, unlike metoclopramide (dystonia, tardive dyskinesia).
- **Diphenylmethane is NOT a prokinetic** — its derivative **bisacodyl** is a stimulant **laxative**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prokinetic ≠ laxative; bisacodyl (diphenylmethane) is a laxative.*

---

## 55. Acute Diarrhea Management

**PEARL:** Acute diarrhea in an **afebrile, stable** patient is managed with **ORS alone** — no antibiotics.

**MUST KNOW:** Add **zinc for 14 days in children** (10 mg <6 months, 20 mg >6 months). Antibiotics only for cholera, dysentery (bloody), severe/systemic illness, or immunocompromised.

**REMEMBER:** *No fever, no blood → ORS (+ zinc in kids).*

---

## 56. Antibiotic-Associated Colitis

**PEARL:** *Clostridioides difficile* colitis follows destruction of normal gut flora. **Clindamycin (lincosamides)** carries the highest risk per exposure; **fluoroquinolones, broad-spectrum penicillins and cephalosporins** cause the largest number of cases.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Pseudomembranous colitis; toxins A and B; diagnosis by **GDH + toxin EIA/NAAT**.
- Treatment: **oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin** (metronidazole now second-line); recurrent → faecal microbiota transplant, bezlotoxumab.
- Do **not** give antimotility drugs (risk of toxic megacolon).

**REMEMBER:** *Clindamycin = highest risk; treat with oral vancomycin, not IV.*

---

## 57. Ceftriaxone for Gonorrhea

**PEARL:** **Single dose ceftriaxone 500 mg IM** is the current treatment for gonorrhea. Dual therapy with azithromycin is **no longer routinely recommended**.

**MUST KNOW:** Add doxycycline 100 mg BD × 7 days only if **chlamydial co-infection** is not excluded. Rising azithromycin resistance drove the change; test of cure for pharyngeal infection.

**REMEMBER:** *Gonorrhea = ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once; azithromycin dropped.*

---

## 58. Doxycycline for Scrub Typhus

**PEARL:** **Doxycycline** is the drug of choice for scrub typhus (*Orientia tsutsugamushi*).

**MUST KNOW:** Alternatives: azithromycin (**preferred in pregnancy and children**), chloramphenicol; rifampicin for doxycycline-resistant areas. Clue: **eschar** + fever + lymphadenopathy; dramatic defervescence within 48 h supports diagnosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Scrub typhus = doxycycline; pregnancy = azithromycin.*

---

## 59. Chloramphenicol — Gray Baby Syndrome

**PEARL:** Occurs in neonates (especially **premature**) given high doses (~100 mg/kg), often prophylactically.

**UNDERSTAND:** Immature **UDP-glucuronyl transferase** cannot conjugate the drug, and immature renal excretion of the conjugate → accumulation → mitochondrial toxicity.

**MUST KNOW:** Features: vomiting, refusal to feed, abdominal distension, **ashen-grey cyanosis**, hypothermia, flaccidity, circulatory collapse. Chloramphenicol also causes **dose-related reversible marrow suppression** and **idiosyncratic irreversible aplastic anaemia**.

**REMEMBER:** *No glucuronidation → grey baby.*

---

## 60. Linezolid and Tedizolid

**PEARL:**

- **Linezolid** causes **myelosuppression** — most commonly **thrombocytopenia**, appearing **7–10 days** after starting, and reversible on stopping.
- **Tedizolid** is a newer oxazolidinone, active against **gram-positives** only (poor against gram-negatives and anaerobes), binding the **P site of the 50S** subunit to block protein synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Other linezolid ADRs: peripheral and **optic neuropathy** (prolonged use), lactic acidosis, and **serotonin syndrome** (it is a weak MAO inhibitor — caution with SSRIs).
- Excellent oral bioavailability (~100%) — IV to oral switch without dose change; covers MRSA and VRE.

**REMEMBER:** *Linezolid: platelets down at day 7–10, MAOI effect, 100% oral.*

---

## 61. Rifampicin

**PEARL:** Rifampicin binds the **β subunit of DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (rpoB)** → suppresses RNA chain formation.

**MUST KNOW:** Resistance = **rpoB mutation** (the target of **GeneXpert/CBNAAT**, used as a surrogate marker for MDR-TB). Potent **CYP3A4 inducer** → OCP failure, reduced warfarin/protease inhibitor levels. Orange-red urine/secretions; flu-like syndrome with intermittent dosing.

**REMEMBER:** *rpoB = rifampicin resistance = GeneXpert.*

---

## 62. Isoniazid

**PEARL:** INH is a prodrug activated by mycobacterial **KatG**; its reactive metabolite forms an adduct with **NAD** that inhibits **InhA and KasA**, blocking **mycolic acid** synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:** Resistance: **katG** (high-level) and **inhA** (low-level, cross-resistance with ethionamide). ADRs: **peripheral neuropathy** (pyridoxine-responsive, worse in slow acetylators), hepatitis (age-related), lupus-like syndrome, and it is a **CYP inhibitor**.

**REMEMBER:** *KatG activates it, InhA/KasA are the targets, mycolic acid the casualty.*

---

## 63. Ethambutol

**PEARL:** Inhibits **arabinosyl transferase III** → ↓arabinogalactan → defective mycobacterial cell wall. Key toxicity: **optic (retrobulbar) neuritis** with reduced acuity and **loss of red–green discrimination**.

**MUST KNOW:** Dose-related and largely reversible on stopping; test colour vision before and during treatment; **avoid in young children** who cannot report visual change. It is **bacteriostatic**, added to prevent resistance.

**REMEMBER:** *Ethambutol = Eye (red-green).*

---

## 64. Pyrazinamide

**PEARL:** A **prodrug** converted by pyrazinamidase (**pncA**) to **pyrazinoic acid**, which acidifies the intracellular environment and **collapses the proton motive force** — killing dormant intracellular bacilli in acidic caseous foci.

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **hyperuricemia/gout** (inhibits urate excretion) and **hepatotoxicity** (the most hepatotoxic first-line drug). Its sterilizing action is why short-course therapy is 6 months.

**REMEMBER:** *PZA works only in acid, gives hyperuricemia and hepatitis.*

---

## 65. Dapsone

**PEARL:** Dapsone inhibits **folic acid synthesis** (dihydropteroate synthase — a sulfone acting like a sulfonamide).

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **hemolysis (dose-related, severe in G6PD deficiency)**, **methemoglobinemia**, agranulocytosis, and the **dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome**. Uses beyond leprosy: dermatitis herpetiformis, PCP prophylaxis, linear IgA disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Dapsone = folate blocker → hemolysis + methemoglobinemia; check G6PD.*

---

## 66. Flucytosine

**PEARL:** Flucytosine inhibits **fungal DNA synthesis** (converted by cytosine deaminase into 5-FU → thymidylate synthase inhibition). Clinically useful in the **"3 C's": Cryptococcus, Candida, Chromoblastomycosis**.

**MUST KNOW:** Never used alone (rapid resistance) — combined with **amphotericin B for cryptococcal meningitis** induction. Main toxicity: **bone marrow suppression** (worse in renal impairment, as it is renally excreted).

**REMEMBER:** *Flucytosine = 3 C's, always with amphotericin, marrow toxic.*

---

## 67. Quinolone Metabolism

**PEARL:**

- **Hepatic:** moxifloxacin, pefloxacin, trovafloxacin (withdrawn for hepatotoxicity) → avoid in liver disease; **not** useful for UTI.
- **Renal:** ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, norfloxacin, lomefloxacin, ofloxacin → dose-adjust in renal failure.

**MUST KNOW:** Class ADRs: **tendinitis/tendon rupture** (Achilles, elderly + steroids), **QT prolongation** (moxifloxacin most), aortic aneurysm/dissection, peripheral neuropathy, dysglycemia, cartilage concerns in children.

**REMEMBER:** *Moxifloxacin = liver = no UTI; the rest = kidney.*

---

## 68. Indinavir

**PEARL:** Indinavir, a protease inhibitor, classically causes **nephrolithiasis (crystalluria)**, plus the class effects — **central obesity, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance** (lipodystrophy syndrome).

**MUST KNOW:** Prevent stones with high fluid intake. Also causes indirect **hyperbilirubinemia**. All PIs are **CYP3A4 inhibitors** (ritonavir used deliberately as a "booster") → the ergotamine interaction above.

**REMEMBER:** *Indinavir = Indian stones; PIs = fat redistribution + CYP3A4 block.*

## 69. Favipiravir

**PEARL:** Favipiravir (**viral RNA polymerase inhibitor**) was the first **oral** drug approved in India under restricted emergency use for **mild-to-moderate COVID-19** — **not** for severe disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Favipiravir = oral, RNA polymerase, mild-moderate only.*

## 70. Bebtelovimab

**PEARL:** A monoclonal antibody for COVID-19 that binds the **spike protein**, reducing hospitalization/death — notable because it **retains activity against the Omicron variant** (unlike earlier mAb cocktails).

**REMEMBER:** *Bebtelovimab = the anti-spike mAb that survived Omicron.*

## 71. Cyclophosphamide

**PEARL:** An **alkylating agent** with antitumour and immunosuppressant use; key toxicities are **hemorrhagic cystitis due to the metabolite acrolein** and later **bladder carcinoma**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Prevent with hydration and **MESNA** (binds acrolein in urine).
- Activated in the **liver by CYP** (a prodrug); also causes **SIADH/hyponatremia**, and is the alkylating agent **least likely to cause thrombocytopenia**.
- Immunosuppressive uses: lupus nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, nephrotic syndrome.

**REMEMBER:** *Acrolein burns the bladder — give MESNA.*

---

## 72. Methotrexate

**PEARL:** As a DMARD, methotrexate works mainly by **increasing extracellular adenosine** (anti-inflammatory); its antiproliferative action is via inhibition of **AICAR transformylase** and **thymidylate synthetase** (and dihydrofolate reductase).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Anchor DMARD in RA; weekly dosing + **folic acid** to reduce toxicity.
- ADRs: mucositis, marrow suppression, hepatic fibrosis, pneumonitis; **teratogenic** (contraindicated in pregnancy).
- Rescue for high-dose therapy = **leucovorin (folinic acid)**; avoid with NSAIDs/sulfonamides (displacement + reduced renal clearance).

**REMEMBER:** *Adenosine = anti-inflammatory action; folate block = toxicity.*

---

## 73. Colchicine

**PEARL:** Colchicine binds **tubulin** and prevents **microtubule polymerization** → impaired neutrophil chemotaxis, recruitment and release of chemotactic factors.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Used in acute gout (and prophylaxis when starting allopurinol), familial Mediterranean fever, pericarditis.
- Dose-limiting ADR: **diarrhea/GI upset**; overdose → multiorgan failure, myelosuppression, myopathy.
- It is **not** uricosuric and does not lower urate.

**REMEMBER:** *Colchicine paralyses the neutrophil, not the urate.*

---

## 74. Drugs Causing Hyperkalemia

**PEARL:** **Triamterene, atenolol (β-blockers), and potassium chloride** cause hyperkalemia.

**MUST KNOW:** Full list: **K⁺-sparing diuretics** (amiloride, triamterene, spironolactone, eplerenone), **ACE inhibitors/ARBs**, NSAIDs, heparin, digoxin toxicity, **succinylcholine**, trimethoprim, cyclosporine/tacrolimus, non-selective β-blockers (block β2-driven cellular K⁺ uptake).

**REMEMBER:** *Anything that blocks aldosterone or β2 raises potassium.*

---

## 75. ACE Inhibitors/ARBs in Proteinuria

**PEARL:** ACE inhibitors or ARBs are **first-line** in proteinuric renal disease — they lower BP, reduce proteinuria, and slow GFR decline; **CCBs may be added** if BP is still uncontrolled.

**UNDERSTAND:** They dilate the **efferent** arteriole → lower intraglomerular pressure → less protein filtration. Expect a **rise in creatinine up to ~30%** — this is acceptable, not a reason to stop.

**MUST KNOW:** Avoid combining ACEi **with** ARB (hyperkalemia and AKI without added benefit); contraindicated in **bilateral renal artery stenosis** and **pregnancy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Efferent dilation = less proteinuria; 30% creatinine rise is allowed.*

---

## 76. Tolvaptan

**PEARL:** Tolvaptan is a **V2 receptor antagonist**: blocking vasopressin at the collecting duct lowers adenyl cyclase → ↓cAMP → **aquaporin-2 vesicles are not inserted** into the membrane → increased **free water clearance** and reduced urine osmolality.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Because it is an **aquaretic**, patients get **thirst, dry mouth, polyuria and dehydration** — sodium rises without sodium loss.
- Uses: **euvolemic/hypervolemic hyponatremia (SIADH)**, and slowing cyst growth in **ADPKD**.
- Correct sodium **slowly** (risk of osmotic demyelination); hepatotoxicity limits ADPKD dosing.

**REMEMBER:** *Tolvaptan = water out, sodium up, patient thirsty.*

---

## 77. Teriparatide

**PEARL:** Recombinant **PTH (1-34) analogue** — the **anabolic** osteoporosis drug; **intermittent** dosing stimulates **osteoblasts**, raising BMD and cutting fracture risk in postmenopausal women.

**UNDERSTAND:** Continuous PTH elevation resorbs bone (as in hyperparathyroidism); once-daily pulses favour bone formation.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Approved for a maximum of **2 years**; indicated for prior osteoporotic fracture, multiple risk factors, or failure/intolerance of other therapy.
- **Give teriparatide before (not after) bisphosphonates** — prior bisphosphonate blunts the anabolic response.
- Must be **followed by an antiresorptive** or gains are lost; contraindicated with Paget's disease, prior skeletal radiation, hypercalcemia.

**REMEMBER:** *Pulse PTH builds bone — 2 years only, then a bisphosphonate.*

---

## 78. Bisphosphonates

**PEARL:** **Pyrophosphate analogues** that bind hydroxyapatite and directly inhibit **osteoclasts** — causing osteoclast apoptosis and preventing hydroxyapatite dissolution.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Duration **3–5 years**, then consider a **drug holiday** because of **osteonecrosis of the jaw** and **atypical subtrochanteric femur fractures**.
- Nitrogen-containing ones (alendronate, zoledronate) inhibit **farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase**; take oral doses fasting with water, **stay upright 30 min** (esophagitis).
- Correct **vitamin D and calcium first** — otherwise hypocalcemia. Avoid if CrCl <30–35.

**REMEMBER:** *Osteoclast killers — 3–5 years, then holiday (jaw + femur).*

---

## 79. Hydrocortisone Replacement in Chronic Adrenal Insufficiency

**PEARL:** Chronic (non-crisis) adrenal insufficiency needs **oral hydrocortisone 15–25 mg/day**, typically **20 mg in 2–3 divided doses (10 + 5 + 5)**, with **half the daily dose in the morning** to mimic the circadian rhythm.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Add **fludrocortisone** (0.05–0.2 mg) in **primary** (Addison's) disease only — secondary insufficiency preserves aldosterone.
- **Double or triple the dose during illness/stress ("sick day rules")**; parenteral hydrocortisone 100 mg for crisis.
- Crisis management: IV fluids + hydrocortisone; do not delay for test results.

**REMEMBER:** *15–25 mg/day, most of it on waking; fludrocortisone only for Addison's.*

---

## 80. Metyrapone

**PEARL:** Metyrapone **reversibly inhibits steroid 11β-hydroxylase**, blocking cortisol synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Causes accumulation of **11-deoxycortisol** — the basis of the **metyrapone test** for HPA axis integrity (ACTH and 11-deoxycortisol should rise).
- Other steroidogenesis inhibitors: **ketoconazole** (multiple enzymes), **osilodrostat**, **mitotane** (adrenolytic), etomidate (IV, rapid).
- Mineralocorticoid precursors accumulate → hypertension and hypokalemia.

**REMEMBER:** *Metyrapone blocks the last step (11β) → 11-deoxycortisol piles up.*

## 81. Letrozole

**PEARL:** A **non-steroidal, reversible aromatase inhibitor** — blocks conversion of androgens to estrogen. Oral bioavailability ~100%, **t½ ≈ 41 hours**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- ADRs are all estrogen-deprivation effects: **hot flushes, vaginal dryness, accelerated bone loss/osteoporosis, arthralgia, hair thinning, dyslipidemia, GI upset**.
- Used for hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in **postmenopausal** women (aromatase is the only estrogen source), and off-label as a **first-line ovulation inducer in PCOS** (now preferred over clomiphene for live-birth rates).
- Exemestane is the **steroidal, irreversible** aromatase inhibitor.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Tamoxifen (SERM) causes **endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma and thrombosis but protects bone**; aromatase inhibitors do the opposite — **bone loss but no endometrial cancer**.

**REMEMBER:** *Letrozole = no estrogen anywhere → hot flushes + thin bones.*

## 82. Clomiphene

**PEARL:** Clomiphene is a mixture of **enclomiphene and zuclomiphene** — it blocks the **negative feedback of estrogen on the hypothalamus**, causing a surge of **FSH and LH** and hence ovulation; continued FSH drive commonly causes **multiple follicular development**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Consequence: **multiple pregnancy (~8%)** and risk of ovarian hyperstimulation.
- Its **anti-estrogenic effect on cervical mucus and endometrium** is why pregnancy rates lag ovulation rates.
- Requires an intact HPO axis — useless in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (give gonadotropins) or premature ovarian failure.

**REMEMBER:** *Clomiphene fools the hypothalamus into thinking estrogen is low.*

---

## 83. Tocolytics — Best Outcomes

**PEARL:** Meta-analysis favours **calcium channel blockers (nifedipine)** and **atosiban** for the best maternal and fetal outcomes among tocolytics.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Atosiban** = oxytocin receptor antagonist — fewest maternal side effects. **Nifedipine** = cheap, oral, first-line in India.
- Tocolysis buys **48 hours** for antenatal **corticosteroids** and transfer — it does not improve perinatal mortality by itself.
- **Ritodrine/isoxsuprine (β2 agonists)** are largely abandoned — pulmonary edema, tachycardia, hyperglycemia. **MgSO4** is used for **fetal neuroprotection**, not as a primary tocolytic.

**REMEMBER:** *Nifedipine or atosiban; tocolysis just buys steroid time.*

## 84. Sickle Cell Drugs

**PEARL:** Four drugs, four mechanisms:

- **Hydroxyurea** — raises **HbF** (primary drug); also inhibits **ribonucleotide reductase** (hence its use in CML, polycythemia vera, thrombocythemia).
- **L-glutamine** — antioxidant; increases reduced **glutathione/NAD** in sickle RBCs, lessening oxidative damage.
- **Voxelotor** — **HbS polymerization inhibitor**, stabilizes the oxygenated (R) state of hemoglobin.
- **Crizanlizumab** — anti-P-selectin antibody, reduces vaso-occlusive crises.

**MUST KNOW:** Hydroxyurea reduces crises, acute chest syndrome and transfusion need; monitor for myelosuppression.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** **Pegfilgrastim/filgrastim must be avoided in sickle cell disease — G-CSF can trigger a severe sickle crisis.**

**REMEMBER:** *Hydroxyurea = HbF; voxelotor = stops polymerization; glutamine = antioxidant.*

## 85. Filgrastim

**PEARL:** Filgrastim is recombinant human **G-CSF**; **pegfilgrastim** and **lipegfilgrastim** are the long-acting pegylated forms. Given **subcutaneously or by slow IV infusion only**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Used for chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and stem-cell mobilization; classic ADR is **bone pain**.
- **Contraindicated in sickle cell disease** (precipitates crisis).
- GM-CSF equivalent = sargramostim.

**REMEMBER:** *G-CSF = filgrastim; pegylated = weekly; never in sickle cell.*

## 86. Checkpoint Inhibitors — PD-1

**PEARL:** **Nivolumab** (fully human **IgG4** monoclonal antibody) and **pembrolizumab** both block **PD-1** binding to its ligands, restoring T-cell anti-tumour activity.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Pembrolizumab** is approved **tissue-agnostically** for any solid tumour with **MMR deficiency / MSI-high** — the first site-independent cancer approval.
- **Nivolumab** is approved as adjuvant therapy in completely resected **esophageal/gastroesophageal junction cancer** with residual disease after neoadjuvant chemoradiation.
- Class toxicity = **immune-related adverse events**: colitis, hypophysitis/thyroiditis, pneumonitis, hepatitis — treated with **corticosteroids**.
- **Ipilimumab** targets **CTLA-4**; atezolizumab/durvalumab target **PD-L1**.

**REMEMBER:** *PD-1 = nivo/pembro; CTLA-4 = ipilimumab; irAEs = give steroids.*

## 87. Dabrafenib + Trametinib

**PEARL:** **Dabrafenib (BRAF inhibitor)** and **trametinib (MEK inhibitor)** are each active as monotherapy in **BRAF V600E/K melanoma**, and the **combination** is approved for unresectable/metastatic **anaplastic thyroid carcinoma**.

**MUST KNOW:** Combining BRAF + MEK inhibition increases efficacy and **reduces the paradoxical MAPK activation** that causes squamous cell carcinomas with BRAF inhibitor monotherapy. **BRAF inhibitors must not be used in BRAF wild-type tumours** (paradoxical tumour growth).

**REMEMBER:** *BRAF + MEK together: melanoma and anaplastic thyroid cancer.*

## 88. Basiliximab

**PEARL:** Basiliximab is an **anti-CD25 (IL-2 receptor α chain)** monoclonal antibody with high affinity for the IL-2 receptor — used for **induction immunosuppression** in renal transplantation.

**MUST KNOW:** It is non-depleting and non-lymphotoxic → **no cytokine release syndrome** (unlike muromonab/ATG); given as two doses around transplant. Daclizumab was the related agent (withdrawn).

**REMEMBER:** *Basiliximab = blocks IL-2 receptor (CD25) at induction.*

## 89. Vilazodone

**PEARL:** Vilazodone is a **SPARI** — **S**erotonin **PA**rtial agonist (**5-HT1A**) and **R**euptake **I**nhibitor. It selectively blocks the serotonin transporter (no NE/DA effect) **and** is a 5-HT1A partial agonist.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Never combine with an **MAO inhibitor**, or use within **14 days** of starting/stopping one — serotonin syndrome.
- Compare: **vortioxetine** (multimodal), **buspirone** (pure 5-HT1A partial agonist, anxiolytic), **trazodone** (SARI).

**REMEMBER:** *Vilazodone = SSRI + buspirone in one molecule.*

## 90. Mirtazapine

**PEARL:** Mirtazapine and mianserin block **H1** receptors and are potent antagonists at **5-HT2A, 5-HT2C and 5-HT3**. **Sexual dysfunction is NOT a side effect** of mirtazapine.

**UNDERSTAND:** SSRIs cause sexual dysfunction and nausea by stimulating 5-HT2 and 5-HT3; mirtazapine **blocks** those receptors — so it avoids both, and its 5-HT3 blockade is antiemetic.

**MUST KNOW:**

- It is a **NaSSA** — blocks presynaptic **α2** autoreceptors, increasing NE and 5-HT release.
- Its H1 blockade gives **sedation, increased appetite and weight gain** — ideal for depressed patients with insomnia and poor appetite/cachexia.

**REMEMBER:** *Mirtazapine: sleep + appetite up, nausea + sexual dysfunction absent.*

## 91. Lithium

**PEARL:**

- Serum lithium must be sampled **10–12 hours after the previous dose** (standard **12-hour trough**).
- Lithium is a mood stabilizer with a **proven antisuicidal effect** — unique among psychotropics.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Therapeutic range 0.6–1.2 mEq/L; narrow index — toxicity with dehydration, **NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides**; excretion is **purely renal** and it is reabsorbed with sodium (low Na = high lithium).
- Chronic: nephrogenic **diabetes insipidus**, hypothyroidism, tremor, weight gain; **Ebstein anomaly** in first-trimester exposure.
- Severe toxicity → **hemodialysis**.

**REMEMBER:** *12-hour trough; lithium is the antisuicide drug; thiazides and NSAIDs push it up.*

## 92. Propranolol for Infantile Hemangioma

**PEARL:** **Propranolol** is the drug of choice for infantile hemangioma that is **ulcerated, symptomatic, or function-threatening** — acting by **vasoconstriction, induction of apoptosis, and inhibition of angiogenesis** (downregulating VEGF/bFGF).

**MUST KNOW:** Watch for **hypoglycemia, bradycardia, hypotension and bronchospasm** in infants — give with feeds. Topical **timolol** for small superficial lesions. Most untreated hemangiomas involute spontaneously — treat only when complicated.

**REMEMBER:** *Ulcerated hemangioma → propranolol (watch the sugar).*

## 93. Acitretin and Pregnancy

**PEARL:** Women must avoid conception for **3 years** after stopping **acitretin** (retinoid embryopathy).

**UNDERSTAND:** Acitretin is re-esterified to **etretinate**, which is highly lipophilic and stored in fat with a very long half-life — especially if alcohol is consumed. Hence the extraordinarily long wait.

**MUST KNOW:** Contrast: **isotretinoin — 1 month** before pregnancy is safe. Also avoid **blood donation** during and for a period after therapy.

**REMEMBER:** *Isotretinoin = 1 month; acitretin = 3 years (alcohol makes etretinate).*

## 94. Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis — Skeletal Signs

**PEARL:** LCH bone lesions:

- **Skull:** punched-out lytic lesions **without a sclerotic rim**, **beveled edge / "hole within a hole"** (double contour), **button sequestrum**, and **geographic skull**.
- **Mandible:** **"floating teeth"** — loss of the lamina dura.
- **Spine:** **vertebra plana** — LCH is the **commonest cause of vertebra plana in children**, mostly thoracic.

**MUST KNOW:** Birbeck granules on EM; **CD1a and langerin (CD207)** positive. Vertebra plana in LCH typically shows **preserved disc spaces** and can reconstitute with growth.

**REMEMBER:** *Beveled skull hole + floating teeth + vertebra plana = LCH.*
