## 1. Pharmacokinetic Calculations

**PEARL:** ***LOADING DOSE = VOLUME OF DISTRIBUTION × DESIRED PLASMA CONCENTRATION*** (so the loading dose is governed by Vd). ***MAINTENANCE DOSE (rate) = CLEARANCE × DESIRED STEADY-STATE CONCENTRATION.*** Hence **Css = infusion rate ÷ clearance** — an infusion of 1.6 mg/min with a clearance of 640 mL/min gives **1.6 ÷ 640 = 0.0025 ≈ 0.002 mg/mL.** ***HALF-LIFE determines the DOSING FREQUENCY and the TIME to steady state, and BIOAVAILABILITY determines the route/size of an oral dose — neither determines the loading dose.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The two formulas answer two different questions. A **loading dose FILLS A SPACE** — you must fill the whole volume the drug distributes into before the plasma concentration reaches target, so only Vd matters and **clearance is irrelevant (it is the same in renal failure).** A **maintenance dose REPLACES LOSSES** — at steady state, in equals out, so only clearance matters, and ***MAINTENANCE DOSE MUST BE REDUCED IN RENAL OR HEPATIC IMPAIRMENT WHILE THE LOADING DOSE IS UNCHANGED.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***STEADY STATE IS REACHED AFTER ABOUT 4–5 HALF-LIVES REGARDLESS OF THE DOSE***; the dose sets the LEVEL, the half-life sets the TIME. Similarly, ~4–5 half-lives are needed for the drug to wash out. This is why a loading dose is used for drugs with long half-lives (amiodarone, digoxin, phenytoin, vancomycin).
- **FIRST-ORDER kinetics — a constant FRACTION eliminated per unit time, so half-life is constant. ***ZERO-ORDER (saturation) kinetics — a constant AMOUNT per unit time, so small dose increases cause disproportionate, dangerous rises: PHENYTOIN, ETHANOL, ASPIRIN (in overdose), THEOPHYLLINE, WARFARIN and OMEPRAZOLE ("PEA-WT").***
- **A LARGE volume of distribution (>>body water) means extensive tissue binding — such drugs are ***NOT REMOVED BY DIALYSIS*** (digoxin, tricyclics, amiodarone), whereas small-Vd, water-soluble, poorly protein-bound drugs are (lithium, salicylate, methanol, ethylene glycol, theophylline — "I STUMBLED").**
- **Bedside dose arithmetic (asked directly): a vial of 500 mg/5 mL contains 100 mg/mL, so 180 mg = 1.8 mL; with 10 divisions per mL on the syringe that is ***18 DIVISIONS.*** Always convert to concentration per mL first.
- **Clearance is additive across organs (CL total = renal + hepatic + other); ***EXTRACTION RATIO determines whether hepatic clearance is FLOW-limited (propranolol, lidocaine, morphine — high extraction, big first-pass effect) or CAPACITY-limited (warfarin, phenytoin, diazepam).***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Loading dose is governed by → **volume of distribution**; steady-state concentration → **infusion rate ÷ clearance**.

**REMEMBER:** *Loading fills the tank (Vd); maintenance replaces the leak (clearance).*

## 2. Choosing Drugs in Renal Impairment — the Gliptins

**PEARL:** ***LINAGLIPTIN IS THE ONE DPP-4 INHIBITOR THAT NEEDS NO DOSE ADJUSTMENT IN ANY DEGREE OF RENAL IMPAIRMENT, INCLUDING CKD STAGE 5*** — because it is eliminated **HEPATOBILIARY (enterohepatic), largely unchanged in the faeces.** Sitagliptin, vildagliptin, saxagliptin and alogliptin are all **RENALLY excreted and must be dose-reduced.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The rule generalises far beyond gliptins: **a drug's safety in renal failure is decided by its ROUTE OF ELIMINATION, not by its class.** Whenever an exam offers four drugs of one class and asks which is safe in CKD, the answer is **the one with a non-renal escape route** — the same logic gives you **linagliptin among gliptins, atorvastatin/fluvastatin among statins, and the hepatically metabolised agents in every other class.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Diabetes drugs in CKD: ***METFORMIN — stop below eGFR 30 (risk of LACTIC ACIDOSIS), reduce dose at 30–45; SULFONYLUREAS — glibenclamide/glimepiride accumulate and cause PROLONGED HYPOGLYCAEMIA (prefer GLICLAZIDE or glipizide); INSULIN requirements FALL in renal failure (insulin is degraded by the kidney); PIOGLITAZONE needs no renal adjustment but causes fluid retention, heart failure, fractures and bladder cancer risk.***
- ***SGLT2 INHIBITORS AND GLP-1 AGONISTS NOW HAVE THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE IN DIABETIC KIDNEY DISEASE*** — SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) slow CKD progression and reduce heart-failure admissions independently of glucose lowering (watch genital mycotic infection, volume depletion and EUGLYCAEMIC KETOACIDOSIS); GLP-1 agonists give weight loss and cardiovascular benefit.
- **Common drugs needing renal dose reduction: aminoglycosides, vancomycin, most beta-lactams, fluconazole, aciclovir, ***DIGOXIN, LITHIUM, METHOTREXATE, LMWH (enoxaparin), gabapentin/pregabalin, allopurinol and dabigatran.*** Drugs to AVOID: NSAIDs, gadolinium (nephrogenic systemic fibrosis), and metformin below eGFR 30.
- **Drugs safe/unchanged in renal failure: linagliptin, atorvastatin, rifampicin, isoniazid (with pyridoxine), doxycycline, azithromycin, clindamycin, ceftriaxone, warfarin, amiodarone, prednisolone.**
- **In HEPATIC impairment the opposite logic applies — avoid or reduce hepatically cleared drugs (paracetamol dose limits, benzodiazepines other than lorazepam/oxazepam/temazepam, statins, sodium valproate).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** DPP-4 inhibitor safe without dose change in CKD 5 → **linagliptin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ask how the drug leaves before you ask what class it is.*

## 3. Opioid Receptor Pharmacology

**PEARL:** ***PENTAZOCINE = PARTIAL AGONIST (or weak antagonist) at MU + FULL AGONIST at KAPPA*** — its analgesia is kappa-mediated. ***BUPRENORPHINE = PARTIAL AGONIST at MU + ANTAGONIST at KAPPA***, with very high mu affinity and a long duration. ***TRAMADOL = weak MU AGONIST + INHIBITOR OF SEROTONIN AND NORADRENALINE REUPTAKE.*** ***FENTANYL, morphine and methadone are FULL MU AGONISTS; NALOXONE and naltrexone are pure ANTAGONISTS.***

**UNDERSTAND:** These profiles predict every clinical consequence. A **partial mu agonist has a CEILING EFFECT** — hence buprenorphine's relative safety in respiratory depression and its use in opioid substitution — but it also means **giving it to a patient on a full agonist PRECIPITATES WITHDRAWAL, because it displaces morphine from mu receptors while producing less effect.** Its high affinity also makes ***BUPRENORPHINE OVERDOSE RELATIVELY RESISTANT TO NALOXONE***, requiring much higher doses. Kappa agonism (pentazocine) explains **dysphoria, hallucinations and psychotomimetic effects**, plus a rise in blood pressure and cardiac work that makes it unsuitable in myocardial infarction.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Receptor effects: ***MU — analgesia (supraspinal), euphoria, RESPIRATORY DEPRESSION, miosis, constipation, physical dependence. KAPPA — spinal analgesia, DYSPHORIA and sedation, miosis, diuresis. DELTA — analgesia, mood modulation.***
- ***TRAMADOL'S NON-OPIOID ACTIONS CAUSE ITS CHARACTERISTIC HAZARDS: SEIZURES (lowered threshold — avoid in epilepsy and with other proconvulsants) and SEROTONIN SYNDROME with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors and linezolid.*** Its analgesia is only partly naloxone-reversible for the same reason. It is a prodrug activated by CYP2D6 — poor metabolisers get little relief.
- **Morphine metabolites matter: ***MORPHINE-6-GLUCURONIDE IS AN ACTIVE ANALGESIC THAT ACCUMULATES IN RENAL FAILURE***, causing delayed narcosis — use fentanyl, buprenorphine or oxycodone instead. ***PETHIDINE'S metabolite NORPETHIDINE IS PROCONVULSANT***, and pethidine with MAO inhibitors causes a severe excitatory reaction.
- **Codeine is a prodrug requiring CYP2D6 → morphine; ***ULTRA-RAPID METABOLISERS AND NURSING MOTHERS HAVE CAUSED INFANT DEATHS***, and codeine is now contraindicated under 12 years and after tonsillectomy.
- **Opioid overdose: pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression and coma → ***NALOXONE (short half-life — repeat doses or infusion needed, especially after methadone or long-acting agents).*** Constipation is the one effect to which tolerance does NOT develop, so laxatives are prescribed alongside.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Partial mu agonist and full kappa agonist → **pentazocine**; tramadol's second mechanism → **serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibition**.

**REMEMBER:** *Buprenorphine hogs the mu receptor and does little with it; pentazocine works through kappa and makes you dysphoric.*

## 4. Adrenaline in Anaphylaxis

**PEARL:** ***ANAPHYLAXIS: ADRENALINE 1:1000, 0.5 mL (0.5 mg) INTRAMUSCULARLY into the ANTEROLATERAL THIGH*** (0.01 mg/kg in children), repeated every 5–15 minutes as needed. ***THE IV ROUTE IS RESERVED FOR REFRACTORY SHOCK OR CARDIAC ARREST AND MUST USE THE 1:10,000 DILUTION*** — in cardiac arrest, **1 mg = 10 mL of 1:10,000 IV every 3–5 minutes.** ***IM IS PREFERRED BECAUSE IT IS FASTER IN PRACTICE AND FAR SAFER*** — IV bolus risks severe hypertension, myocardial ischaemia and ventricular arrhythmia.

**UNDERSTAND:** Adrenaline is the only drug that reverses **every arm of anaphylaxis simultaneously: ALPHA-1 vasoconstriction restores blood pressure and reduces mucosal oedema, BETA-1 supports the heart, and BETA-2 bronchodilates AND STABILISES MAST CELLS to stop further mediator release.** Antihistamines and steroids touch only parts of that and act far too slowly — which is why **delay in giving adrenaline is the commonest factor in fatal anaphylaxis** and why the anterolateral thigh (large muscle, rich blood supply) is specified over the deltoid.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Concentration arithmetic worth being fluent in: ***1:1000 = 1 mg/mL; 1:10,000 = 0.1 mg/mL; 1:100,000 = 0.01 mg/mL.*** A 1% solution = 10 mg/mL.
- **Adjuncts AFTER adrenaline: high-flow oxygen, ***AGGRESSIVE IV FLUID RESUSCITATION (massive capillary leak)***, positioning supine with legs raised (NOT sitting or standing — sudden upright posture has caused deaths), nebulised salbutamol for bronchospasm, and antihistamines and steroids for skin symptoms and the ***BIPHASIC REACTION that can occur up to 72 hours later*** — hence a period of observation.
- ***PATIENTS ON BETA-BLOCKERS MAY BE RESISTANT TO ADRENALINE — GIVE GLUCAGON*** (which bypasses the beta receptor to raise cyclic AMP).
- **Discharge care: an ***ADRENALINE AUTO-INJECTOR WITH TRAINING***, a written emergency plan, allergen identification and referral; measure serum TRYPTASE to confirm the diagnosis retrospectively.
- **Other adrenaline uses and their concentrations: cardiac arrest (1:10,000 IV), croup and post-extubation stridor (nebulised), with local anaesthetics for vasoconstriction (1:200,000), and topical haemostasis.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Dose in anaphylactic shock → **1:1000, 0.5 mL IM**.

**REMEMBER:** *Thigh, intramuscular, one-in-a-thousand — everything else is an adjunct.*

## 5. Heparins, Antithrombin and Antifibrinolytics

**PEARL:** ***HEPARIN HAS NO ANTICOAGULANT ACTIVITY OF ITS OWN — IT REQUIRES ANTITHROMBIN III***, whose inhibition of thrombin and factor Xa it accelerates about 1000-fold. **Low ATIII is the commonest cause of "HEPARIN RESISTANCE" (failure to reach a therapeutic aPTT despite ≥35,000 units/day).** ***LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT HEPARIN IS THE ANTICOAGULANT OF CHOICE IN CANCER-ASSOCIATED THROMBOEMBOLISM.*** ***TRANEXAMIC ACID IS THE ANTIDOTE TO FIBRINOLYTIC THERAPY*** — a lysine analogue that blocks plasminogen/plasmin binding to fibrin.

**UNDERSTAND:** Chain length explains the whole heparin family. **Inhibiting THROMBIN requires heparin to bridge antithrombin AND thrombin simultaneously, which needs at least 18 saccharide units; inhibiting FACTOR Xa needs only the pentasaccharide.** So **UNFRACTIONATED heparin inhibits both (anti-Xa:anti-IIa ≈ 1:1, monitored by aPTT), LMWH inhibits mainly Xa (≈3:1, predictable, monitored only by anti-Xa levels when needed), and FONDAPARINUX is the pure pentasaccharide (anti-Xa only).** Everything about monitoring, dosing and reversal follows from that single geometric fact.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Causes of low antithrombin: hereditary deficiency, hepatic failure (reduced synthesis), DIC and cardiopulmonary bypass (consumption), and ***NEPHROTIC SYNDROME (urinary loss)*** — the last being a classic cause of both thrombosis and heparin resistance.
- ***PROTAMINE FULLY REVERSES UNFRACTIONATED HEPARIN BUT ONLY PARTIALLY (~60%) REVERSES LMWH, AND NOT FONDAPARINUX AT ALL.*** Andexanet alfa reverses factor Xa inhibitors; ***IDARUCIZUMAB reverses DABIGATRAN***; vitamin K plus 4-factor PCC reverses warfarin.
- ***HEPARIN-INDUCED THROMBOCYTOPENIA (HIT)***: IgG against the heparin–PF4 complex causing a platelet fall of >50% at days 5–10 with paradoxical ***THROMBOSIS, not bleeding***; commoner with UFH than LMWH. ***STOP ALL HEPARIN AND START A NON-HEPARIN ANTICOAGULANT (argatroban, bivalirudin, fondaparinux) — NEVER give platelets or warfarin alone (risk of venous limb gangrene).***
- **Why LMWH in cancer: better bioavailability, predictable response without monitoring, fewer drug and diet interactions than warfarin, and superior efficacy in the CLOT trial; ***use UFH in severe renal failure and fondaparinux where there is a history of HIT***. DOACs are now an accepted alternative except in GI/GU tumours (bleeding).
- **Tranexamic acid is also used in trauma (CRASH-2 — within 3 hours), postpartum haemorrhage (WOMAN trial), menorrhagia, hereditary angioedema and dental extraction in haemophilia — ***but is AVOIDED with prothrombin complex concentrate and in haematuria (clot colic/obstruction).***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Substance mandatory for heparin's action → **antithrombin III**; anticoagulant of choice in cancer thrombosis → **LMWH**; antidote to fibrinolytics → **tranexamic acid**.

**REMEMBER:** *Heparin is only a catalyst — no antithrombin, no effect.*

## 6. Methotrexate and the DMARDs

**PEARL:** ***METHOTREXATE'S ANTI-INFLAMMATORY ACTION IS MEDIATED BY INCREASED EXTRACELLULAR ADENOSINE.*** By inhibiting **AICAR TRANSFORMYLASE** (as well as dihydrofolate reductase and thymidylate synthase), it causes AICAR to accumulate, which inhibits AMP deaminase; the resulting AMP is released and converted extracellularly to **ADENOSINE — a potent endogenous suppressor of neutrophil, macrophage, dendritic-cell and lymphocyte function.**

**UNDERSTAND:** This is why methotrexate works in rheumatoid arthritis at **weekly low doses that are far below any meaningful antiproliferative effect** — the mechanism in inflammation is **adenosine-mediated, not antifolate cytotoxicity.** It also explains two clinical observations: **caffeine (an adenosine receptor antagonist) may blunt its efficacy**, and its benefit takes 6–8 weeks to appear.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***METHOTREXATE IS GIVEN ONCE WEEKLY — DAILY DOSING HAS KILLED PATIENTS*** (pancytopenia, mucositis). ***ALWAYS PRESCRIBE FOLIC ACID SUPPLEMENTATION*** (on a different day) to reduce toxicity without losing efficacy. Toxicities: myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity and fibrosis, mucositis, ***HYPERSENSITIVITY PNEUMONITIS***, nephrotoxicity at high dose, and ***TERATOGENICITY (contraindicated in pregnancy — it is also used to end ectopic pregnancy).*** Avoid with trimethoprim/cotrimoxazole (additive antifolate) and NSAIDs at high dose (reduced clearance). ***FOLINIC ACID (LEUCOVORIN) IS THE RESCUE AGENT.***
- **Other DMARD mechanisms: ***LEFLUNOMIDE — dihydro-orotate dehydrogenase inhibition (pyrimidine synthesis); very long half-life, needs cholestyramine washout; teratogenic. SULFASALAZINE — cleaved to 5-ASA and sulfapyridine; safe in pregnancy. HYDROXYCHLOROQUINE — raises lysosomal pH and impairs antigen presentation; the mildest DMARD, with RETINAL TOXICITY requiring annual screening; also used in SLE. AZATHIOPRINE — purine antimetabolite; ***CHECK TPMT/NUDT15 STATUS***, and NEVER combine with ALLOPURINOL without a large dose reduction (xanthine oxidase blockade causes fatal myelosuppression).**
- **Biologics: TNF inhibitors (infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab) — ***SCREEN FOR LATENT TB AND HEPATITIS B BEFORE STARTING***; rituximab (anti-CD20); tocilizumab (anti-IL-6); abatacept (CTLA4-Ig); and JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib — thrombosis and herpes zoster risk).**
- **In rheumatoid arthritis, ***METHOTREXATE REMAINS FIRST-LINE AND THE ANCHOR DRUG***, started early to prevent erosions; steroids are a bridge, not a strategy.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** DMARD that increases extracellular adenosine → **methotrexate**.

**REMEMBER:** *Low-dose weekly methotrexate treats inflammation with adenosine, not with antifolate killing.*

## 7. Beta-2 Agonist Adverse Effects

**PEARL:** **Salbutamol's systemic effects come from extrapulmonary beta-2 stimulation: ***MUSCLE TREMOR (the commonest), TACHYCARDIA, HYPOKALAEMIA, restlessness, transient HYPOXAEMIA, lactic acidosis — and HYPERGLYCAEMIA, NOT hypoglycaemia.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Beta-2 receptors are metabolic as well as bronchial. Stimulation **drives potassium INTO cells by activating the Na+/K+-ATPase — hence hypokalaemia (and hence salbutamol's use in treating HYPERKALAEMIA)** — and promotes **glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, lipolysis and insulin release, raising glucose, free fatty acids, lactate and pyruvate.** The transient FALL in oxygen saturation after a nebuliser is also mechanistic: **beta-2 vasodilatation reverses hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction and worsens ventilation–perfusion matching for a few minutes.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Tremor is a beta-2 effect on skeletal muscle spindles; it is dose-related and diminishes with continued use. Tachycardia is partly reflex (vasodilatation) and partly direct beta-1 cross-reactivity at higher doses.**
- ***MONITOR POTASSIUM DURING REPEATED NEBULISED SALBUTAMOL IN ACUTE SEVERE ASTHMA***, especially with concurrent steroids, diuretics or theophylline — the combination can produce dangerous hypokalaemia and arrhythmia.
- **Long-acting beta-2 agonists (salmeterol, formoterol) must ***NEVER BE USED AS MONOTHERAPY IN ASTHMA*** (increased asthma deaths) — always combined with an inhaled corticosteroid. Formoterol has a rapid onset, allowing its use in the maintenance-and-reliever regimen.**
- **Other beta-2 uses: ***TOCOLYSIS in preterm labour (ritodrine, isoxsuprine — watch pulmonary oedema and maternal tachycardia)*** and hyperkalaemia.
- **In children, high-dose beta-2 agonists can cause lactic acidosis mimicking clinical deterioration; and the reliever inhaler use rate is itself the best marker of asthma control (>2 canisters/month signals poor control and mortality risk).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** NOT a side effect of salbutamol → **hypoglycaemia** (it causes hyperglycaemia).

**REMEMBER:** *Beta-2 pushes potassium in and glucose out.*

## 8. Thalidomide

**PEARL:** **Thalidomide is ***NOT an antimetabolite*** — it is an IMMUNOMODULATORY AND ANTIANGIOGENIC agent that inhibits **TNF-alpha and IL-6, blocks NF-κB signalling, reduces angiogenesis and enhances T-cell IL-2 and interferon-gamma production.** It is used for ***ERYTHEMA NODOSUM LEPROSUM (type 2 lepra reaction) and for RELAPSED/REFRACTORY MULTIPLE MYELOMA***, and is ***SEVERELY TERATOGENIC (PHOCOMELIA/dysmelia).***

**UNDERSTAND:** The molecular explanation for both the benefit and the birth defects came only decades later: **thalidomide binds CEREBLON, a component of an E3 ubiquitin ligase, redirecting it to degrade specific transcription factors (IKZF1/IKZF3 in myeloma cells — hence its anti-myeloma action; SALL4 — hence limb malformation).** It is the founding "molecular glue" degrader, and its history is the reason modern drug regulation, pregnancy testing programmes and pharmacovigilance exist.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***THALIDOMIDE IS ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED IN PREGNANCY; PRESCRIPTION REQUIRES A STRICT PREGNANCY-PREVENTION PROGRAMME*** (two forms of contraception, regular pregnancy testing) in men and women alike. Other major toxicities: ***VENOUS THROMBOEMBOLISM (thromboprophylaxis is mandatory in myeloma), PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY (often irreversible), sedation and constipation.***
- **In LEPROSY, thalidomide treats ***TYPE 2 (ENL) reactions*** — but ***CORTICOSTEROIDS remain first-line for TYPE 1 (reversal) reactions and for any reaction with NEURITIS***, and clofazimine is the alternative in ENL. It is ***NOT an antimycobacterial drug***; multidrug therapy (rifampicin, dapsone, clofazimine) continues alongside.
- **Successors LENALIDOMIDE and POMALIDOMIDE are more potent with less neuropathy (but more myelosuppression) and are backbone drugs in myeloma and in del(5q) myelodysplastic syndrome.**
- **Multiple myeloma treatment classes to pair with it: proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib — peripheral neuropathy), anti-CD38 antibodies (daratumumab), steroids and autologous transplant.**
- **Historical note worth carrying: it was marketed for morning sickness and withdrawn in 1961 — the disaster that created modern teratogenicity testing and the "pregnancy category" system.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about thalidomide → **used in refractory myeloma, used in erythema nodosum leprosum, and teratogenic** (NOT an antimetabolite).

**REMEMBER:** *A drug that glues a ubiquitin ligase to the wrong protein — it destroys myeloma cells and fetal limbs alike.*

## 9. Antiretroviral Toxicities

**PEARL:** **A patient on antiretroviral therapy who develops ***NEPHROLITHIASIS plus CENTRAL OBESITY, HYPERLIPIDAEMIA AND INSULIN RESISTANCE has PROTEASE-INHIBITOR toxicity — classically INDINAVIR***, whose crystalluria causes stones and whose class effect is **HIV/HAART-ASSOCIATED LIPODYSTROPHY**, typically after 6–12 months.

**UNDERSTAND:** Protease inhibitors interfere with **adipocyte differentiation and lipid metabolism (in part by inhibiting SREBP-1 and GLUT4 signalling)**, producing a striking redistribution of fat — **peripheral and facial WASTING with CENTRAL/VISCERAL and dorsocervical ("buffalo hump") ACCUMULATION** — along with insulin resistance and dyslipidaemia. Indinavir's stones are simply a **solubility problem: the drug crystallises in urine**, which is why generous hydration was mandated with it.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Class-defining toxicities: ***ZIDOVUDINE (NRTI) — ANAEMIA AND MYELOSUPPRESSION, myopathy. STAVUDINE/DIDANOSINE — LACTIC ACIDOSIS AND PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY from MITOCHONDRIAL TOXICITY, pancreatitis, lipoatrophy. TENOFOVIR (TDF) — RENAL TUBULAR DYSFUNCTION (FANCONI SYNDROME) AND REDUCED BONE DENSITY. ABACAVIR — HYPERSENSITIVITY REACTION, requiring HLA-B*57:01 TESTING BEFORE USE. EFAVIRENZ (NNRTI) — VIVID DREAMS, dizziness, depression and false-positive cannabis screens. NEVIRAPINE — HEPATOTOXICITY AND STEVENS–JOHNSON SYNDROME. PROTEASE INHIBITORS — lipodystrophy, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, and MANY CYP3A4 INTERACTIONS (ritonavir is used deliberately as a "booster"). INTEGRASE INHIBITORS (dolutegravir) — weight gain, insomnia; now first-line worldwide.***
- **Current first-line ART in most programmes is ***TENOFOVIR + LAMIVUDINE + DOLUTEGRAVIR (TLD)*** — fewer toxicities, high barrier to resistance and a single daily tablet.
- ***IMMUNE RECONSTITUTION INFLAMMATORY SYNDROME (IRIS)*** occurs weeks after starting ART as CD4 counts recover, most dangerously with tuberculosis and cryptococcal meningitis — which is why ***ART IS DELAYED (2–8 WEEKS) AFTER STARTING TREATMENT FOR THOSE INFECTIONS, AND LONGER IN CRYPTOCOCCAL MENINGITIS.***
- **Drug interactions worth knowing: ***RIFAMPICIN induces CYP3A4 and slashes protease-inhibitor levels — use RIFABUTIN or adjusted dolutegravir dosing***; and PPIs impair atazanavir absorption.
- **Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (for *Pneumocystis* and toxoplasmosis) is given at CD4 <200 and to all HIV-exposed infants.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Nephrolithiasis with central obesity, hyperlipidaemia and insulin resistance on ART → **indinavir**.

**REMEMBER:** *Protease inhibitors move the fat and break the sugar; indinavir also grows stones.*

## 10. Antiglaucoma Drugs

**PEARL:** ***PROSTAGLANDIN ANALOGUES (latanoprost, bimatoprost, travoprost) SHOULD BE THE LAST CHOICE IN A PATIENT WITH MACULAR OEDEMA (or uveitis, or aphakia) — THEY CAUSE AND WORSEN CYSTOID MACULAR OEDEMA.*** Their other effects: **IRIS AND PERIOCULAR SKIN HYPERPIGMENTATION (heterochromia), EYELASH HYPERTRICHOSIS, PERIORBITAL FAT ATROPHY (deepening of the upper lid sulcus) and conjunctival hyperaemia** — cosmetic, but the reason they are otherwise the first-line agent is a **powerful once-daily pressure reduction with no systemic effects.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Glaucoma drugs divide by **whether they cut aqueous PRODUCTION or increase OUTFLOW**, and their systemic side effects follow from the receptor, not the eye. **Prostaglandin analogues increase UVEOSCLERAL outflow — a local prostaglandin effect, which is exactly why they also cause inflammation-related macular oedema.** Beta-blockers and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors reduce production; pilocarpine opens the trabecular meshwork by contracting the ciliary muscle.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Class-by-class: ***TIMOLOL (beta-blocker) — reduces production; CONTRAINDICATED IN ASTHMA/COPD, bradycardia and heart block (systemic absorption through the nasolacrimal duct — teach punctal occlusion). BRIMONIDINE (alpha-2 agonist) — reduces production and increases uveoscleral outflow; causes allergic conjunctivitis and, IN INFANTS, APNOEA AND CNS DEPRESSION (contraindicated under 2 years). DORZOLAMIDE/acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitors) — reduce production; systemic acetazolamide causes paraesthesiae, hypokalaemia, metabolic acidosis, renal stones and is a SULFONAMIDE. PILOCARPINE (muscarinic agonist) — increases trabecular outflow; causes MIOSIS, brow ache, dim vision and accommodative spasm — most useful in ANGLE-CLOSURE.***
- ***IN ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA: IV/oral ACETAZOLAMIDE, topical beta-blocker, pilocarpine (after the pressure begins to fall), hyperosmotics (mannitol), steroids for inflammation — followed by DEFINITIVE LASER PERIPHERAL IRIDOTOMY IN BOTH EYES.***
- ***AVOID MYDRIATICS AND ANTICHOLINERGICS IN NARROW ANGLES*** (atropine, antihistamines, tricyclics, nebulised ipratropium sprayed into the eye) — they can precipitate angle closure.
- **Latanoprost must be refrigerated; all topical drops are more effective if the patient occludes the punctum for 2 minutes, which also reduces systemic absorption.**
- **Adherence is the greatest practical problem in glaucoma — a chronic, asymptomatic disease treated with irritating drops.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Glaucoma drug to give LAST in diabetic macular oedema → **prostaglandin analogues**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prostaglandins open the outflow and swell the macula — save them for the eye with a healthy retina.*

## 11. Lithium and the Mood Stabilisers

**PEARL:** ***LITHIUM IS THE MOOD STABILISER WITH A PROVEN ANTI-SUICIDAL EFFECT*** — it reduces suicide and all-cause mortality in mood disorders, an effect **NOT shared by valproate, carbamazepine or lamotrigine** and apparently independent of its mood-stabilising action.

**UNDERSTAND:** Lithium's therapeutic index is narrow (0.6–1.2 mmol/L), and its handling is entirely **renal — it is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule IN PLACE OF SODIUM.** Therefore ***ANYTHING THAT DEPLETES SODIUM OR REDUCES RENAL PERFUSION RAISES LITHIUM TO TOXIC LEVELS***: dehydration, vomiting, diarrhoea, a low-salt diet, ***THIAZIDES, NSAIDs and ACE INHIBITORS/ARBs.*** That single mechanism generates most lithium questions and most real-world lithium toxicity.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Adverse effects: fine tremor, polyuria and polydipsia from ***NEPHROGENIC DIABETES INSIPIDUS***, weight gain, acne and psoriasis, ***HYPOTHYROIDISM (and goitre) — check TSH*** — hyperparathyroidism with hypercalcaemia, leucocytosis, chronic interstitial nephritis, and ***EBSTEIN ANOMALY with first-trimester exposure.***
- ***TOXICITY (>1.5 mmol/L): coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, vomiting and diarrhoea, confusion, seizures and coma — TREAT WITH FLUIDS AND, IN SEVERE CASES, HAEMODIALYSIS*** (lithium's small volume of distribution makes it highly dialysable). Monitor levels 12 hours post-dose, and check renal and thyroid function periodically.
- **Alternatives and their niches: ***VALPROATE — effective in mania, but TERATOGENIC (neural tube defects, reduced IQ) — AVOID IN WOMEN OF CHILDBEARING POTENTIAL; also causes hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, weight gain, tremor, hair loss and hyperammonaemia. CARBAMAZEPINE — enzyme INDUCER with many interactions, hyponatraemia/SIADH, agranulocytosis, and SJS/TEN with HLA-B*1502 in Asians. LAMOTRIGINE — best for BIPOLAR DEPRESSION; must be titrated SLOWLY because of rash/SJS risk (and doses halve when combined with valproate).***
- **Antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone, aripiprazole) are effective mood stabilisers in acute mania and maintenance, at the cost of metabolic syndrome.**
- ***ABRUPT DISCONTINUATION OF LITHIUM CAUSES REBOUND MANIA*** — always taper.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mood stabiliser with an anti-suicidal role → **lithium**.

**REMEMBER:** *The kidney treats lithium as sodium — anything that saves salt poisons the patient.*

## 12. Antidepressants and Sexual Dysfunction

**PEARL:** ***MIRTAZAPINE CAUSES THE LEAST SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION*** of the common antidepressants, because it does not block serotonin reuptake — instead it is a **presynaptic alpha-2 ANTAGONIST that increases noradrenergic and serotonergic transmission while BLOCKING 5-HT2A, 5-HT2C, 5-HT3 and H1 receptors.** SSRIs (fluoxetine), SNRIs (venlafaxine) and tricyclics (imipramine) all commonly cause it. **BUPROPION, VILAZODONE and VORTIOXETINE are the other sexually sparing options.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Sexual dysfunction from SSRIs is a ***5-HT2A-mediated effect*** — the very receptor that mirtazapine blocks. Blocking 5-HT2A and 5-HT3 while enhancing transmission gives mirtazapine its whole profile: **no sexual dysfunction, no nausea (5-HT3 block, so it is useful in patients who cannot tolerate SSRIs), and — through H1 blockade — SEDATION AND APPETITE STIMULATION WITH WEIGHT GAIN.** That makes it the drug of choice for **depression with insomnia, anorexia and weight loss**, especially in the elderly and in cancer patients, and a poor choice for an obese patient.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Antidepressant side-effect signatures: ***FLUOXETINE — long half-life (no discontinuation syndrome; useful in poor adherence), activating, many CYP interactions. PAROXETINE — most anticholinergic, most weight gain, worst discontinuation syndrome, and the most teratogenic concern (cardiac defects). SERTRALINE — safest in cardiac disease and preferred in pregnancy/lactation. CITALOPRAM/ESCITALOPRAM — QT PROLONGATION (dose limits). VENLAFAXINE — dose-dependent HYPERTENSION and dangerous in overdose. BUPROPION — LOWERS SEIZURE THRESHOLD (avoid in eating disorders and alcohol withdrawal) but no sexual or weight effects; also used for smoking cessation. TRAZODONE — sedation and PRIAPISM. TCAs — anticholinergic effects, postural hypotension, and CARDIOTOXICITY IN OVERDOSE (wide QRS — treat with SODIUM BICARBONATE).***
- ***SSRIs INCREASE BLEEDING RISK (platelet serotonin depletion), CAUSE HYPONATRAEMIA/SIADH (especially in the elderly), and carry a black-box warning for suicidal ideation in those under 25 — FLUOXETINE IS THE ONLY SSRI APPROVED FOR CHILDHOOD DEPRESSION.***
- **SEROTONIN SYNDROME (SSRI + MAOI, tramadol, linezolid, triptans): ***HYPERTHERMIA, agitation, CLONUS AND HYPERREFLEXIA (lower limbs > upper) with dilated pupils and diarrhoea*** — rapid onset; treat with withdrawal, benzodiazepines and cyproheptadine. Contrast NEUROLEPTIC MALIGNANT SYNDROME (antipsychotics): slower onset, ***LEAD-PIPE RIGIDITY, hyporeflexia, very high creatine kinase*** — treat with dantrolene and bromocriptine.
- **A washout of ***2 WEEKS (5 WEEKS FOR FLUOXETINE)*** is required before starting an MAO inhibitor.
- **All antidepressants take 2–4 weeks to work and should be continued at least 6–9 months after remission of a first episode.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Antidepressant with least sexual dysfunction → **mirtazapine**.

**REMEMBER:** *Blocking 5-HT2A spares the libido and blocking H1 fattens the patient — that is mirtazapine in one line.*

## 13. Disulfiram and Alcohol De-addiction

**PEARL:** **DISULFIRAM is an ***IRREVERSIBLE INHIBITOR OF ALDEHYDE DEHYDROGENASE***, used for AVERSIVE CONDITIONING — it is ***NOT an anticraving drug.*** Drinking on disulfiram raises acetaldehyde and causes **flushing, throbbing headache, nausea and vomiting, chest discomfort, respiratory difficulty, hypotension, drowsiness and even collapse.** ***IT MUST ONLY BE GIVEN TO A HIGHLY MOTIVATED PATIENT, ONLY AFTER AT LEAST 12–24 HOURS OF ABSTINENCE — AND ITS EFFECT PERSISTS UP TO 14 DAYS AFTER THE LAST DOSE*** (not 2–3 days), because the enzyme must be resynthesised.

**UNDERSTAND:** The 14-day tail is a direct consequence of ***IRREVERSIBLE (covalent) enzyme inhibition***: recovery requires new enzyme synthesis, not drug elimination. This makes disulfiram categorically different from the anticraving drugs, which act on reward pathways: ***NALTREXONE (opioid antagonist — reduces the pleasure of drinking and the "priming" effect) and ACAMPROSATE (NMDA/GABA modulator — reduces the protracted withdrawal-related craving; safe in liver disease and preferred there).***

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***DISULFIRAM-LIKE REACTIONS also occur with METRONIDAZOLE and tinidazole, CHLORPROPAMIDE and other sulfonylureas, CEPHALOSPORINS with a methylthiotetrazole side chain (cefoperazone, cefotetan), griseofulvin, ketoconazole and procarbazine*** — warn patients about alcohol.
- **Disulfiram is contraindicated in severe cardiac disease, psychosis and pregnancy, and can cause hepatotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy and psychosis; it also inhibits dopamine beta-hydroxylase.**
- **Alcohol withdrawal management: ***BENZODIAZEPINES (chlordiazepoxide or diazepam; LORAZEPAM in liver disease as it avoids hepatic oxidation) are the drugs of choice***, with symptom-triggered dosing by CIWA-Ar. ***GIVE PARENTERAL THIAMINE BEFORE ANY GLUCOSE*** to prevent WERNICKE ENCEPHALOPATHY (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia — progressing to KORSAKOFF psychosis with anterograde amnesia and confabulation).
- **Timeline of withdrawal: tremulousness 6–12 h, hallucinosis 12–24 h, ***WITHDRAWAL SEIZURES 24–48 h***, ***DELIRIUM TREMENS 48–96 h (clouded consciousness, autonomic storm, visual hallucinations — a medical emergency with real mortality).***
- **Brief interventions, motivational interviewing, Alcoholics Anonymous and treatment of comorbid depression are as important as any drug; pharmacotherapy without psychosocial support fails.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about disulfiram → **the reaction with alcohol causes drowsiness, chest discomfort and respiratory difficulty** (it is NOT anticraving, needs a motivated patient, and lasts up to 14 days).

**REMEMBER:** *Disulfiram punishes, naltrexone and acamprosate persuade.*

## 14. Antiepileptic Drugs — Mechanisms and Choices

**PEARL:** ***ABSENCE SEIZURES ARE TREATED WITH ETHOSUXIMIDE (first-line for uncomplicated typical absence) OR VALPROATE*** — and ***CARBAMAZEPINE, PHENYTOIN, GABAPENTIN, PREGABALIN, VIGABATRIN AND TIAGABINE CAN ALL WORSEN ABSENCE AND MYOCLONIC SEIZURES.*** Ethosuximide works by blocking ***T-TYPE CALCIUM CHANNELS IN THALAMIC NEURONES***, the pacemaker of the 3 Hz spike-and-wave rhythm.

**UNDERSTAND:** Absence epilepsy is generated by an **oscillating thalamocortical circuit that depends on low-threshold T-type calcium currents** — a completely different mechanism from the focal, sodium-channel-driven propagation of partial seizures. That is why **sodium-channel blockers not only fail but AGGRAVATE absence and myoclonus**, and why an exam stem naming carbamazepine for a "staring child" is always wrong. ***MATCHING THE MECHANISM TO THE SEIZURE TYPE IS THE ENTIRE LOGIC OF ANTIEPILEPTIC PRESCRIBING.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Mechanistic families: ***SODIUM-CHANNEL BLOCKERS — phenytoin, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, lacosamide (and partly valproate and topiramate). T-TYPE CALCIUM BLOCKERS — ETHOSUXIMIDE, valproate, zonisamide. GABA ENHANCERS — benzodiazepines (increase FREQUENCY of channel opening), barbiturates (increase DURATION), vigabatrin (irreversible GABA transaminase inhibitor — visual field loss), tiagabine (GAT-1 uptake inhibitor). GLUTAMATE/AMPA BLOCK — perampanel, topiramate, felbamate. SV2A BINDING — LEVETIRACETAM and brivaracetam. ALPHA-2-DELTA CALCIUM SUBUNIT — gabapentin, pregabalin.***
- **First-line choices: focal seizures — ***LAMOTRIGINE or LEVETIRACETAM (carbamazepine historically)***; generalised tonic–clonic — valproate (levetiracetam or lamotrigine in women of childbearing age); ***JUVENILE MYOCLONIC EPILEPSY — VALPROATE (levetiracetam in women), and it needs LIFELONG treatment***; absence — ethosuximide; infantile spasms — ACTH/vigabatrin; Lennox–Gastaut — valproate, lamotrigine, rufinamide, cannabidiol.
- ***VALPROATE IS TERATOGENIC (neural tube defects and impaired neurodevelopment) AND MUST BE AVOIDED IN GIRLS AND WOMEN OF CHILDBEARING POTENTIAL UNLESS THERE IS NO ALTERNATIVE***, with pregnancy prevention. All women on antiepileptics need ***5 mg FOLIC ACID PRECONCEPTIONALLY.***
- **Toxicity signatures: ***PHENYTOIN — gum hypertrophy, hirsutism, coarse facies, cerebellar ataxia and nystagmus, megaloblastic anaemia, ZERO-ORDER kinetics; CARBAMAZEPINE — hyponatraemia, agranulocytosis, enzyme induction, HLA-B*1502 and SJS; LAMOTRIGINE — rash/SJS on rapid titration; LEVETIRACETAM — irritability and behavioural change; TOPIRAMATE — weight loss, renal stones, word-finding difficulty, acute glaucoma; PHENOBARBITONE — sedation and, in children, hyperactivity.***
- **Monotherapy at the lowest effective dose is the aim; measure levels only for phenytoin, toxicity or adherence questions.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drugs used for absence seizures → **valproate and ethosuximide only**.

**REMEMBER:** *Thalamic T-channels make the 3 Hz rhythm — block them, and never reach for a sodium-channel blocker.*

## 15. Drugs in Gout, in Myasthenia, and Two Signalling Facts

**PEARL:** In GOUT, ***ASPIRIN IS AVOIDED BECAUSE LOW DOSES CAUSE RENAL RETENTION OF URATE*** (it is uricosuric only at high, poorly tolerated doses); **INDOMETHACIN is favoured because it also inhibits urate crystal phagocytosis in addition to COX**, and **DICLOFENAC concentrates in synovial fluid.** In MYASTHENIA GRAVIS, ***AVOID AMINOGLYCOSIDES, QUINOLONES AND MACROLIDES, BETA-BLOCKERS, LOCAL ANAESTHETICS (lidocaine), MAGNESIUM (including magnesium-containing antacids), QUININE, PENICILLAMINE, BOTULINUM TOXIN AND NON-DEPOLARISING MUSCLE RELAXANTS.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Every myasthenia-aggravating drug interferes with **transmission at the already-marginal neuromuscular junction: MAGNESIUM AND AMINOGLYCOSIDES BLOCK PRESYNAPTIC CALCIUM-DEPENDENT ACETYLCHOLINE RELEASE**, local anaesthetics and quinine stabilise the postsynaptic membrane, and penicillamine actually **induces anti-acetylcholine-receptor antibodies.** Since the myasthenic patient has only a fraction of the normal receptor reserve, a drug that would be harmless in anyone else precipitates a crisis.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Acute gout: NSAIDs (indomethacin, naproxen), ***COLCHICINE (inhibits microtubule polymerisation and neutrophil chemotaxis — dose-limited by DIARRHOEA)***, or intra-articular/oral steroids; ***NEVER START OR STOP URATE-LOWERING THERAPY DURING AN ACUTE ATTACK*** (but continue it if already established), and always cover initiation with colchicine or an NSAID for months.
- **Urate-lowering: ***ALLOPURINOL (xanthine oxidase inhibitor — beware the HYPERSENSITIVITY SYNDROME with HLA-B*5801 in Han Chinese and the fatal interaction with AZATHIOPRINE/6-MP), FEBUXOSTAT (no dose change in mild renal impairment, cardiovascular caution), PROBENECID (uricosuric — avoid if stones or overproduction), and RASBURICASE for tumour lysis.***
- **Myasthenia treatment: ***PYRIDOSTIGMINE for symptoms, steroids and steroid-sparing immunosuppression, thymectomy (especially with thymoma), and IVIG or PLASMA EXCHANGE for CRISIS.*** In a crisis, the priority is ***AIRWAY AND VITAL CAPACITY MONITORING***, not more anticholinesterase (cholinergic crisis is the differential).
- **Two mechanistic facts commonly asked: ***ACETYLCHOLINE SLOWS THE HEART BY DELAYING DIASTOLIC (phase 4) DEPOLARISATION IN THE SA NODE*** — vagal M2 receptors open GIRK potassium channels (I-KACh) via the G-protein beta-gamma subunit, hyperpolarising the cell. And ***POTASSIUM-CHANNEL BLOCKERS (AMINOPYRIDINES) INCREASE PRESYNAPTIC ACETYLCHOLINE RELEASE*** by prolonging the action potential and admitting more calcium — hence 3,4-diaminopyridine in ***LAMBERT–EATON MYASTHENIC SYNDROME.***
- ***GPCRs THAT OPEN GIRK POTASSIUM CHANNELS: M2 MUSCARINIC, A1 ADENOSINE, ALPHA-2 ADRENERGIC, D2 DOPAMINE, 5-HT1 AND SOMATOSTATIN RECEPTORS — all Gi-coupled. THE ANGIOTENSIN AT1 RECEPTOR IS Gq-COUPLED AND ACTS THROUGH IP3/CALCIUM, NOT POTASSIUM.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Correct statements about gout drugs → **aspirin decreases urate excretion, diclofenac concentrates in the joint, indomethacin acts by multiple pathways**; drugs to avoid in myasthenia → **lidocaine and magnesium-containing antacids**; GPCR NOT acting via K+ channels → **angiotensin AT1**.

**REMEMBER:** *Low-dose aspirin hoards urate; magnesium and aminoglycosides silence a junction that has nothing to spare.*

## 16. Spurious, Adulterated and Misbranded Drugs

**PEARL:** Under the **DRUGS AND COSMETICS ACT (1940, amended)**, a **MISBRANDED drug is one whose LABEL OR CONTAINER MAKES A FALSE OR MISLEADING CLAIM, is not labelled in the prescribed manner, or whose appearance has been altered to conceal damage or imply greater value** — so **a tablet labelled 500 mg of paracetamol that contains only 200 mg is MISBRANDED.** A **SPURIOUS drug is IMPORTED/SOLD UNDER ANOTHER DRUG'S NAME, IMITATES another drug with intent to deceive, or has a FALSELY CLAIMED MANUFACTURER (counterfeit).** An **ADULTERATED drug CONTAINS FILTHY, PUTRID OR DECOMPOSED SUBSTANCE, is prepared or stored in insanitary conditions, or contains a harmful contaminant or unsafe colour.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The three categories are separated by **WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE PRODUCT: MISBRANDED = a labelling/claim problem (the identity is honest but the description is not). SPURIOUS = an IDENTITY problem (it pretends to be another product or maker). ADULTERATED = a QUALITY/CONTAMINATION problem (something foul or unsafe is in it).** Match the defect to the noun and these questions become trivial.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **NOT OF STANDARD QUALITY ("substandard") is a fourth category — the drug fails to meet pharmacopoeial specifications (potency, dissolution, sterility) without necessarily being mislabelled.**
- **Regulatory framework in India: ***CDSCO under the Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)*** is the central authority, with state licensing authorities and Drug Inspectors; the ***DRUGS AND MAGIC REMEDIES (OBJECTIONABLE ADVERTISEMENTS) ACT 1954*** bars advertising cures for listed diseases; the ***NDPS ACT 1985*** governs narcotics; the ***DRUG PRICE CONTROL ORDER*** under NPPA caps prices of essential medicines.
- **Prescription schedules: ***SCHEDULE H and H1 — prescription-only (H1 covering third-generation antibiotics, antitubercular and anti-anxiety drugs, with a separate register kept for 3 years — an antimicrobial-stewardship measure); SCHEDULE X — narcotic and psychotropic drugs needing a duplicate prescription retained for 2 years; SCHEDULE M — Good Manufacturing Practice; SCHEDULE Y — clinical trial and new drug requirements.***
- **Clinical trial phases: ***PHASE 0/I — safety and pharmacokinetics in a few healthy volunteers; PHASE II — efficacy and dose-finding in patients; PHASE III — comparison with standard therapy in large numbers (the basis of approval); PHASE IV — post-marketing surveillance/PHARMACOVIGILANCE, which detects RARE and DELAYED adverse effects that trials cannot.***
- **PHARMACOVIGILANCE PROGRAMME OF INDIA collects ADR reports; ***the WHO-UMC and Naranjo scales grade causality***, and adverse events are classified as Type A (augmented, dose-related, predictable) and Type B (bizarre, idiosyncratic) among others.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Tablet labelled 500 mg containing 200 mg → **misbranded drug**.

**REMEMBER:** *Wrong label = misbranded; wrong identity = spurious; wrong contents = adulterated.*

## 17. Statin Interactions

**PEARL:** ***DO NOT COMBINE STATINS WITH CLARITHROMYCIN.*** Macrolides inhibit **CYP3A4 and the hepatic uptake transporters OATP1B1/1B3**, so statin levels rise — the combination increases hospitalisation for **ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY, HYPERKALAEMIA, RHABDOMYOLYSIS and all-cause mortality.** (Even rosuvastatin and pravastatin, which are only minimally CYP3A4-metabolised, are affected — because they are **OATP substrates.**)

**UNDERSTAND:** Statin myopathy is **concentration-dependent**, so anything that raises plasma levels raises risk. Two separate mechanisms do this: **BLOCKING METABOLISM (CYP3A4 — clarithromycin/erythromycin, azole antifungals, protease inhibitors, ciclosporin, verapamil/diltiazem, amiodarone, grapefruit juice) and BLOCKING HEPATIC UPTAKE (OATP1B1 — macrolides, ciclosporin, gemfibrozil).** Recognising that the second mechanism exists explains why "non-CYP3A4" statins are not automatically safe.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***AZITHROMYCIN DOES NOT INHIBIT CYP3A4 AND IS THE SAFE MACROLIDE***; ***PRAVASTATIN, ROSUVASTATIN AND FLUVASTATIN AVOID CYP3A4***, and pravastatin is the least interaction-prone overall. When a strong interacting drug is unavoidable, hold the statin for the course.
- ***GEMFIBROZIL + STATIN CARRIES THE HIGHEST RHABDOMYOLYSIS RISK OF ANY FIBRATE COMBINATION — USE FENOFIBRATE INSTEAD.***
- **Statin adverse effects: myalgia (common, usually benign), ***MYOSITIS with raised creatine kinase, and RHABDOMYOLYSIS with myoglobinuric renal failure***; transaminitis; a small increase in new-onset diabetes; and rare immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (anti-HMGCR antibodies) that persists after stopping the drug. ***CHECK CK AND THYROID FUNCTION IN MUSCLE SYMPTOMS — HYPOTHYROIDISM IS A COMMON UNRECOGNISED CONTRIBUTOR.***
- **Statins are ***CONTRAINDICATED IN PREGNANCY AND LACTATION***; they act by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, upregulating hepatic LDL receptors, and have pleiotropic plaque-stabilising and anti-inflammatory effects that explain benefit beyond LDL lowering.
- **Add-on agents when statins are insufficient or not tolerated: ***EZETIMIBE (NPC1L1 cholesterol absorption inhibitor), PCSK9 INHIBITORS (evolocumab, alirocumab) and INCLISIRAN (siRNA), BEMPEDOIC ACID***; fibrates and omega-3 for hypertriglyceridaemia (and fibrates are the drug of choice when triglycerides threaten pancreatitis).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Rosuvastatin should not be combined with → **clarithromycin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Block the enzyme or block the transporter — either way the statin piles up and the muscle dissolves.*

## 18. Antiplatelet Drugs

**PEARL:** ***CLOPIDOGREL IS A PRODRUG ACTIVATED BY CYP2C19 — LOSS-OF-FUNCTION VARIANTS OR CYP2C19 INHIBITION (especially by OMEPRAZOLE/esomeprazole) CAUSE TREATMENT FAILURE AND RECURRENT THROMBOTIC EVENTS.*** ***PRASUGREL IS ALSO A PRODRUG, but is activated by CYP3A4/CYP2B6 — so it is UNAFFECTED by omeprazole, is about 10 TIMES MORE POTENT than clopidogrel and acts faster.*** Both are **IRREVERSIBLE P2Y12 ANTAGONISTS** (not agonists).

**UNDERSTAND:** The pharmacogenetic problem is entirely a **PRODRUG problem** — a drug that must be converted before it works can be defeated at the conversion step. The solutions therefore all bypass that step: **use a different activation pathway (prasugrel), use a drug that needs no activation at all (TICAGRELOR and cangrelor are DIRECT, REVERSIBLE P2Y12 blockers), or use a PPI that does not inhibit CYP2C19 (pantoprazole).**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Antiplatelet mechanisms: ***ASPIRIN — irreversible COX-1 acetylation, abolishing platelet thromboxane A2 for the platelet's 7–10-day life (hence the low dose and the need to stop it a week before major surgery). CLOPIDOGREL/PRASUGREL/TICAGRELOR — P2Y12 ADP receptor blockade. ABCIXIMAB/eptifibatide/tirofiban — GLYCOPROTEIN IIb/IIIa blockade (the final common pathway of aggregation). DIPYRIDAMOLE — phosphodiesterase inhibition and adenosine uptake block. CILOSTAZOL — PDE3 inhibitor, used in intermittent claudication.***
- ***PRASUGREL IS CONTRAINDICATED AFTER STROKE OR TIA*** (intracranial bleeding) and avoided over 75 years or under 60 kg; ***TICAGRELOR causes DYSPNOEA and ventricular pauses (adenosine-mediated) and requires TWICE-DAILY dosing***, and its effect wears off faster after stopping — an advantage before surgery.
- ***CLOPIDOGREL AND TICLOPIDINE CAN CAUSE THROMBOTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIC PURPURA***; ticlopidine also causes neutropenia and is obsolete.
- **Dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + a P2Y12 inhibitor) is standard after acute coronary syndrome and stenting, with duration balanced against bleeding risk; ***"TRIPLE THERAPY" with an anticoagulant should be as short as possible.***
- **In secondary stroke prevention, clopidogrel or aspirin+dipyridamole are used; ***short-course DUAL therapy after minor stroke/high-risk TIA, then monotherapy.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about prasugrel → **it is a prodrug** (10× MORE potent, unaffected by omeprazole); clopidogrel failure → **downregulation/inhibition of CYP2C19**.

**REMEMBER:** *A prodrug is only as good as its activating enzyme.*

## 19. Direct Oral Anticoagulants and Monitoring

**PEARL:** ***APIXABAN, RIVAROXABAN, EDOXABAN and BETRIXABAN are DIRECT FACTOR Xa INHIBITORS ("-XABANS"); DABIGATRAN IS THE ONLY ORAL DIRECT THROMBIN INHIBITOR; BIVALIRUDIN, ARGATROBAN, LEPIRUDIN and DESIRUDIN are PARENTERAL direct thrombin inhibitors.*** ***ROUTINE COAGULATION MONITORING IS NEEDED FOR LEPIRUDIN (aPTT), BUT NOT FOR ENOXAPARIN, FONDAPARINUX OR DABIGATRAN***, whose response is predictable because they bind neither endothelium nor plasma proteins.

**UNDERSTAND:** The whole appeal of the DOACs is **PREDICTABLE PHARMACOKINETICS**: unlike warfarin (vitamin-K-dependent, diet- and CYP-sensitive, with a narrow index) and unlike unfractionated heparin (which binds plasma proteins, macrophages and endothelium, producing an erratic dose–response), they hit a single factor directly with high bioavailability. **No variability means no monitoring** — but it also means **no INR to warn you of accumulation**, which is why renal function must be checked instead.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***DABIGATRAN IS ~80% RENALLY CLEARED — AVOID IN SIGNIFICANT RENAL IMPAIRMENT***; the -xabans are less renally dependent but still need dose adjustment. ***ALL DOACs ARE CONTRAINDICATED IN MECHANICAL HEART VALVES AND MODERATE-TO-SEVERE MITRAL STENOSIS — WARFARIN IS MANDATORY THERE***, as it is in antiphospholipid syndrome (triple-positive).
- **Reversal: ***IDARUCIZUMAB for dabigatran; ANDEXANET ALFA for the -xabans; PROTAMINE for unfractionated heparin (partial for LMWH); VITAMIN K PLUS 4-FACTOR PCC for warfarin.*** Dabigatran is also dialysable.
- **Warfarin points that persist in exams: it inhibits ***VITAMIN K EPOXIDE REDUCTASE***, depleting factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S; the ***EARLY PROCOAGULANT STATE from the short half-life of PROTEIN C causes WARFARIN-INDUCED SKIN NECROSIS***, hence heparin bridging; it is ***TERATOGENIC (nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses — avoid in the first trimester and near delivery)***; and it interacts with everything (potentiated by metronidazole, cotrimoxazole, fluconazole, amiodarone; antagonised by rifampicin, carbamazepine and vitamin-K-rich foods).
- **Indications: DOACs are first-line for ***NON-VALVULAR ATRIAL FIBRILLATION (CHA2DS2-VASc scoring, balanced against HAS-BLED) and for VTE treatment and prophylaxis after major orthopaedic surgery.***
- ***ARGATROBAN AND BIVALIRUDIN ARE THE ANTICOAGULANTS OF CHOICE IN HEPARIN-INDUCED THROMBOCYTOPENIA*** (argatroban is hepatically cleared — useful in renal failure).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Apixaban class → **factor Xa inhibitor**; drug needing coagulation monitoring → **lepirudin**.

**REMEMBER:** *"-xaban" blocks ten, "-gatran" blocks thrombin — and predictable drugs need no monitoring.*

## 20. Antiarrhythmics, QT Prolongation and Anti-anginals

**PEARL:** ***QUINIDINE (class Ia) PROLONGS THE QT AND CAUSES TORSADES DE POINTES.*** ***AMIODARONE IS CONTRAINDICATED IN INTERSTITIAL LUNG DISEASE*** — its most feared chronic toxicity is **PULMONARY FIBROSIS**, which can be rapidly progressive and fatal. ***RANOLAZINE, an antianginal, works by INHIBITING THE LATE SODIUM CURRENT***, thereby reducing sodium–calcium exchange, intracellular calcium, diastolic wall tension and oxygen demand.

**UNDERSTAND:** The **VAUGHAN WILLIAMS classes map directly onto the action potential: I = SODIUM channel block (phase 0 upstroke) — Ia (quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide) also blocks potassium so it PROLONGS repolarisation and the QT; Ib (LIGNOCAINE, mexiletine) SHORTENS it and is ischaemia-selective; Ic (flecainide, propafenone) markedly slows conduction with little repolarisation change. II = BETA-BLOCKERS. III = POTASSIUM channel block (amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide) — prolong repolarisation, hence QT. IV = CALCIUM channel block (verapamil, diltiazem).** Once you know the class you can predict both the ECG change and the danger.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***MAGNESIUM SULPHATE IS THE TREATMENT FOR TORSADES DE POINTES***, not a cause of QT prolongation; other causes of a long QT are ***hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia, bradycardia, congenital long-QT syndromes, and drugs — class Ia and III antiarrhythmics, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics (haloperidol), methadone, ondansetron, citalopram, antifungals and antimalarials.***
- **AMIODARONE toxicity list (very examinable): ***PULMONARY FIBROSIS, THYROID dysfunction (both hypo- and hyperthyroidism — it is 37% iodine by weight), CORNEAL MICRODEPOSITS (almost universal, usually harmless), HEPATOTOXICITY, PHOTOSENSITIVITY WITH SLATE-GREY SKIN, peripheral neuropathy, bradycardia and torsades.*** It has an enormous volume of distribution and a half-life of ~50 days, and it raises digoxin and warfarin levels. ***BASELINE AND SERIAL LUNG, THYROID, LIVER AND EYE ASSESSMENT ARE MANDATORY.***
- ***FLECAINIDE AND OTHER Ic AGENTS ARE CONTRAINDICATED AFTER MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION AND IN STRUCTURAL HEART DISEASE (the CAST trial — INCREASED MORTALITY)*** — one of cardiology's foundational safety lessons.
- **Other antianginals worth pairing: ***IVABRADINE — funny-current (If) inhibitor in the SA node, slowing rate WITHOUT affecting contractility (side effect: luminous phosphenes); TRIMETAZIDINE — metabolic modulator; NICORANDIL — potassium channel opener plus nitrate (causes ulceration).***
- ***ISOSORBIDE DINITRATE PLUS HYDRALAZINE IS AN EVIDENCE-BASED HEART FAILURE COMBINATION (mortality benefit, particularly in African-American patients and in those intolerant of ACE inhibitors)*** — hydralazine is an arterial dilator that also reduces NITRATE TOLERANCE, while the nitrate provides venodilatation. ***NITRATE-FREE INTERVALS PREVENT TOLERANCE***, and nitrates are ***ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED WITH PDE5 INHIBITORS (sildenafil) — catastrophic hypotension.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug causing QT prolongation → **quinidine**; antiarrhythmic contraindicated in interstitial lung disease → **amiodarone**; late sodium current inhibitor → **ranolazine**; combined with isosorbide dinitrate in heart failure → **hydralazine**.

**REMEMBER:** *Class III and Ia stretch repolarisation and invite torsades — and magnesium is the cure, not the cause.*

## 21. Vaptans

**PEARL:** ***TOLVAPTAN, a SELECTIVE V2 RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST, IS THE BEST APPROACH FOR CHRONIC SIADH*** — it blocks vasopressin's action in the collecting duct, lowering intracellular cAMP so that AQUAPORIN-2 vesicles are not inserted into the apical membrane. The result is ***INCREASED FREE WATER CLEARANCE with dilute urine*** — and hence the characteristic side effects of **THIRST, DRY MOUTH, dehydration and HYPERNATRAEMIA.** Tolvaptan is also used to **slow progression in ADPKD.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Vaptans produce an **"aquaresis" — loss of water WITHOUT electrolytes** — which is exactly what a hyponatraemic, water-overloaded SIADH patient needs and exactly why fluid restriction (the traditional treatment) fails: **thirst is almost impossible to suppress long-term, so adherence collapses.** In ADPKD the logic is different: **vasopressin-driven cAMP promotes cyst-cell proliferation and fluid secretion**, so blocking V2 slows cyst growth and eGFR decline.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***CORRECT CHRONIC HYPONATRAEMIA SLOWLY — NO MORE THAN 8–10 mmol/L IN 24 HOURS*** — or risk ***OSMOTIC DEMYELINATION SYNDROME (central pontine myelinolysis)***, with locked-in syndrome days later. Vaptans must therefore be started in hospital with frequent sodium monitoring, and fluid restriction is relaxed while they are given.
- ***TOLVAPTAN CAUSES DOSE-DEPENDENT HEPATOTOXICITY (a black-box warning, particularly at ADPKD doses) — MONITOR LIVER FUNCTION.***
- **Other SIADH options: fluid restriction (<500–1000 mL/day), oral salt/urea, loop diuretics, and ***DEMECLOCYCLINE (which induces a nephrogenic diabetes insipidus — slow, nephrotoxic and photosensitising).*** Treat the underlying cause (small cell lung cancer, CNS disease, pneumonia, drugs — carbamazepine, SSRIs, cyclophosphamide, ecstasy).
- **Diagnosis of SIADH: ***HYPONATRAEMIA with LOW plasma osmolality, INAPPROPRIATELY CONCENTRATED URINE (>100 mOsm/kg), URINE SODIUM >30 mmol/L, EUVOLAEMIA, and NORMAL thyroid, adrenal and renal function.***
- **The mirror image, DIABETES INSIPIDUS, is treated with ***DESMOPRESSIN (a V2 AGONIST)*** in the central form and with thiazides, amiloride and low-solute diet in the nephrogenic form (classically lithium-induced).
- ***CONIVAPTAN is the intravenous, non-selective (V1a/V2) vaptan.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Best treatment for chronic SIADH → **tolvaptan**; cause of thirst and dry mouth on tolvaptan → **increased free water clearance**.

**REMEMBER:** *Vaptans lose water and keep the salt — which is precisely why the patient gets thirsty.*

## 22. Drugs Acting on Steroid Synthesis and Receptors

**PEARL:** ***METYRAPONE INHIBITS 11-BETA-HYDROXYLASE***, blocking the conversion of 11-deoxycortisol to cortisol — hence it lowers cortisol and is used both to **TREAT Cushing syndrome** and to **TEST the hypothalamic–pituitary axis** (a normal axis responds to the fall in cortisol with a surge of ACTH and a rise in 11-deoxycortisol). By contrast, ***MIFEPRISTONE is a GLUCOCORTICOID (and PROGESTERONE) RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST; FLUTAMIDE is an ANDROGEN RECEPTOR antagonist; FINASTERIDE is a 5-ALPHA-REDUCTASE inhibitor.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction the question tests is ***SYNTHESIS INHIBITION versus RECEPTOR BLOCKADE.*** Enzyme inhibitors (metyrapone, ketoconazole, osilodrostat, etomidate, aminoglutethimide, mitotane) reduce hormone PRODUCTION, so precursors ACCUMULATE upstream — which is diagnostically useful and can cause side effects from the precursors (metyrapone raises 11-deoxycorticosterone, causing **hypertension and hypokalaemia**, and androgens, causing **hirsutism**). Receptor blockers leave hormone levels high or rising, so **hormone assays become uninterpretable and feedback escape can occur.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Medical therapy of Cushing syndrome: ***STEROIDOGENESIS INHIBITORS — ketoconazole, metyrapone, osilodrostat, etomidate (the only IV option, for crisis), MITOTANE (adrenolytic, used in adrenocortical carcinoma); PITUITARY-DIRECTED — pasireotide, cabergoline; RECEPTOR BLOCKADE — MIFEPRISTONE (useful when hyperglycaemia dominates).*** Definitive treatment is surgical.
- ***KETOCONAZOLE'S INHIBITION OF STEROID SYNTHESIS EXPLAINS ITS SIDE EFFECTS — GYNAECOMASTIA AND ADRENAL INSUFFICIENCY*** — and is the reason it is no longer used systemically as an antifungal.
- **Antiandrogens: ***FLUTAMIDE/BICALUTAMIDE (receptor blockers — a "FLARE" occurs if a GnRH agonist is given without antiandrogen cover in prostate cancer), FINASTERIDE/DUTASTERIDE (5-alpha-reductase inhibitors for BPH and androgenetic alopecia — they LOWER PSA by about half, which must be corrected for in screening), SPIRONOLACTONE and CYPROTERONE (used in hirsutism and PCOS), and ABIRATERONE (CYP17 inhibitor — needs steroid cover).***
- **MIFEPRISTONE plus MISOPROSTOL is the standard medical abortion regimen; mifepristone alone is also used in Cushing syndrome and, at low dose, as emergency contraception.**
- **Adrenal suppression from exogenous steroids is the commonest cause of adrenal insufficiency; ***NEVER STOP LONG-TERM STEROIDS ABRUPTLY, AND INCREASE THE DOSE DURING ILLNESS OR SURGERY.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug inhibiting glucocorticoid SYNTHESIS → **metyrapone**.

**REMEMBER:** *Metyrapone stops the last step and lets the precursor pile up — that is both the treatment and the test.*

## 23. Biological Immunosuppressants

**PEARL:** ***BASILIXIMAB IS AN ANTI-CD25 MONOCLONAL ANTIBODY — AN IL-2 RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST*** used for **INDUCTION at transplantation to prevent acute rejection**, with a plasma half-life of about a week; its risks are anaphylaxis and opportunistic infection.

**UNDERSTAND:** **IL-2 is the growth factor that drives ACTIVATED T-cell clonal expansion**, and CD25 (the high-affinity IL-2 receptor alpha chain) is expressed almost exclusively on **RECENTLY ACTIVATED T cells.** Blocking it therefore suppresses **precisely the cells responding to the graft while sparing resting lymphocytes** — a far more selective strategy than the broad cytotoxicity of older agents, which is why basiliximab causes no myelosuppression and no cytokine-release syndrome.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Match the biologic to its target: ***BASILIXIMAB — CD25/IL-2R. MUROMONAB (OKT3) — CD3 (obsolete; caused cytokine storm). RITUXIMAB — CD20 (B cells). ANTI-THYMOCYTE GLOBULIN — polyclonal lymphocyte depletion. ANAKINRA — IL-1 receptor antagonist. TOCILIZUMAB — IL-6 receptor. INFLIXIMAB/adalimumab/etanercept — TNF-alpha. USTEKINUMAB — IL-12/23. SECUKINUMAB — IL-17. VEDOLIZUMAB — alpha-4-beta-7 integrin (gut-selective). NATALIZUMAB — alpha-4 integrin (risk of PROGRESSIVE MULTIFOCAL LEUKOENCEPHALOPATHY with JC virus). ABATACEPT — CTLA4-Ig (costimulation blockade). ECULIZUMAB — C5 (needs MENINGOCOCCAL VACCINATION). OMALIZUMAB — IgE. DENOSUMAB — RANKL.***
- **Small-molecule immunosuppressants: ***CICLOSPORIN and TACROLIMUS — CALCINEURIN inhibitors blocking IL-2 TRANSCRIPTION (nephrotoxicity, hypertension, tremor; ciclosporin causes GUM HYPERTROPHY and HIRSUTISM, tacrolimus causes DIABETES and alopecia). SIROLIMUS/EVEROLIMUS — mTOR inhibitors (impaired wound healing, hyperlipidaemia, pneumonitis). MYCOPHENOLATE — IMPDH inhibitor, lymphocyte-selective (GI toxicity, TERATOGENIC). AZATHIOPRINE — purine antimetabolite (TPMT testing; fatal interaction with allopurinol).***
- **Naming convention worth knowing: ***"-ximab" chimeric, "-zumab" humanised, "-umab" fully human, "-cept" receptor fusion protein.***
- **All potent immunosuppression demands ***SCREENING FOR LATENT TB, HEPATITIS B AND C AND HIV, VACCINATION BEFORE STARTING (live vaccines are then contraindicated), and vigilance for opportunistic infection and malignancy.***
- **Checkpoint INHIBITORS work in the opposite direction — ***IPILIMUMAB (anti-CTLA-4) and NIVOLUMAB/PEMBROLIZUMAB (anti-PD-1) RELEASE the immune brake to treat cancer, causing autoimmune "IMMUNE-RELATED ADVERSE EVENTS" (colitis, hypophysitis, thyroiditis, pneumonitis, hepatitis) treated with STEROIDS.*** ***THE 2018 NOBEL PRIZE (Allison and Honjo) WAS AWARDED FOR EXACTLY THIS — "cancer therapy by inhibition of negative immune regulation".***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Basiliximab → **IL-2 receptor (anti-CD25) antagonist**; 2018 Nobel Prize → **checkpoint inhibition in cancer therapy**.

**REMEMBER:** *CD25 appears only on the T cells that have just woken up — block it and you block only them.*

## 24. Granulocyte Colony-Stimulating Factor

**PEARL:** **FILGRASTIM is recombinant human G-CSF; PEGFILGRASTIM is the pegylated, ***LONGER-ACTING*** form (one injection per chemotherapy cycle instead of daily). It is given **subcutaneously or by slow IV infusion — never orally** — for **severe neutropenia after chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation, severe congenital neutropenia and zidovudine-related neutropenia.** ***IT SHOULD BE AVOIDED IN SICKLE CELL DISEASE — IT CAN PRECIPITATE A SEVERE VASO-OCCLUSIVE CRISIS.***

**UNDERSTAND:** G-CSF works by **mobilising and expanding the neutrophil pool and speeding maturation**, which is exactly the problem in sickle cell disease: **a sudden flood of leucocytes increases blood viscosity and adhesion to endothelium**, and leucocyte–endothelial interaction is central to sickle vaso-occlusion. The same mobilising property is exploited deliberately in ***PERIPHERAL BLOOD STEM CELL HARVESTING FOR TRANSPLANTATION.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Common adverse effects: ***BONE PAIN (from marrow expansion — the most characteristic), fever, splenic enlargement and rare SPLENIC RUPTURE, capillary leak and acute respiratory distress syndrome, and cutaneous vasculitis/SWEET SYNDROME.***
- **Indications for PRIMARY prophylaxis: chemotherapy regimens with a ***≥20% RISK OF FEBRILE NEUTROPENIA***, or 10–20% risk with patient risk factors (age, comorbidity, prior neutropenia). ***G-CSF IS NOT ROUTINELY USED TO TREAT ESTABLISHED FEBRILE NEUTROPENIA*** — the priority there is ***EMPIRICAL BROAD-SPECTRUM ANTIBIOTICS WITHIN AN HOUR (piperacillin-tazobactam or a carbapenem, with vancomycin if a line infection or MRSA is suspected).***
- **Related growth factors: ***ERYTHROPOIETIN/darbepoetin for anaemia of CKD (target Hb 10–11.5 g/dL — over-correction increases stroke and death; ensure iron sufficiency first); ROMIPLOSTIM and ELTROMBOPAG (thrombopoietin receptor agonists) for ITP and aplastic anaemia; GM-CSF (sargramostim).***
- ***PLERIXAFOR (a CXCR4 antagonist) IS ADDED TO G-CSF FOR STEM CELL MOBILISATION*** when G-CSF alone fails.
- **Neutropenia definition: absolute neutrophil count <1500/µL; ***SEVERE below 500, with profound infection risk below 100.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about pegfilgrastim → **it should not be given in sickle cell disease** (its duration is LONGER, it is not oral, and it is effective in severe neutropenia).

**REMEMBER:** *G-CSF fills the blood with sticky white cells — a gift in neutropenia and a hazard in sickle cell.*

## 25. Prostaglandins in Obstetrics

**PEARL:** ***CARBOPROST is the 15-METHYL SYNTHETIC PGF2-ALPHA analogue*** used as an ABORTIFACIENT and for ***REFRACTORY POSTPARTUM HAEMORRHAGE.*** For contrast: ***DINOPROST = natural PGF2-alpha; DINOPROSTONE = natural PGE2 (cervical ripening); MISOPROSTOL and GEMEPROST = synthetic PGE1 analogues.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The prostaglandin chosen follows what the uterus and cervix must do. **PGE2 (dinoprostone) SOFTENS AND RIPENS THE CERVIX** and is therefore the induction agent; **PGF2-alpha (carboprost) is the most powerful MYOMETRIAL CONTRACTOR** and is therefore reserved for stopping haemorrhage. ***BUT PGF2-alpha ALSO CONSTRICTS BRONCHIAL SMOOTH MUSCLE — SO CARBOPROST IS CONTRAINDICATED IN ASTHMA***, one of the highest-yield contraindications in obstetric practice.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***THE STEPWISE MANAGEMENT OF PPH: OXYTOCIN first-line → ERGOMETRINE (CONTRAINDICATED IN HYPERTENSION AND PRE-ECLAMPSIA) → CARBOPROST (CONTRAINDICATED IN ASTHMA) → MISOPROSTOL (rectal/sublingual; causes fever and shivering) → TRANEXAMIC ACID → mechanical measures (bimanual compression, balloon tamponade, B-Lynch suture, uterine/internal iliac artery ligation) → hysterectomy.*** ***ACTIVE MANAGEMENT OF THE THIRD STAGE WITH PROPHYLACTIC OXYTOCIN PREVENTS MOST PPH.***
- **MISOPROSTOL (PGE1) also protects against NSAID-induced peptic ulcers (it replaces the mucosal prostaglandins the NSAID removes) — and is therefore ***CONTRAINDICATED IN PREGNANCY, where it causes abortion and MÖBIUS SEQUENCE if the pregnancy continues.***
- **Medical abortion: ***MIFEPRISTONE followed 24–48 hours later by MISOPROSTOL***, legal in India under the MTP Act up to 24 weeks in defined circumstances.
- **Other therapeutic prostaglandins: ***ALPROSTADIL (PGE1) to keep the ductus arteriosus open and for erectile dysfunction; EPOPROSTENOL/iloprost (PGI2) for pulmonary arterial hypertension; LATANOPROST (PGF2-alpha analogue) for glaucoma.***
- **NSAIDs cause their entire adverse-effect profile by removing physiological prostaglandins: gastric mucosal injury (PGE2), renal vasoconstriction and sodium retention (PGE2/PGI2), delayed labour and ***CLOSURE OF THE DUCTUS ARTERIOSUS (which is why INDOMETHACIN/ibuprofen is used to close a PDA).***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Synthetic PGF2-alpha derivative used as an abortifacient → **carboprost**.

**REMEMBER:** *E ripens the cervix, F squeezes the uterus — and F also squeezes the airway.*

## 26. Antimicrobial Resistance and Combination Therapy

**PEARL:** ***BETA-LACTAMASES CAUSE RESISTANCE BY DEGRADING (HYDROLYSING) THE BETA-LACTAM RING*** — not by altering the target. The four mechanisms of beta-lactam resistance are: ***(1) ENZYMATIC INACTIVATION by beta-lactamases; (2) MODIFICATION OF THE TARGET PENICILLIN-BINDING PROTEINS (MRSA's mecA/PBP2a, penicillin-resistant pneumococcus); (3) IMPAIRED PENETRATION (porin loss in gram-negatives); (4) EFFLUX PUMPS.*** Antimicrobial COMBINATIONS are standard in **tuberculosis, malaria, HIV and polymicrobial intra-abdominal infection — but ***GONORRHOEA IS NOW TREATED WITH SINGLE-AGENT CEFTRIAXONE*** (500 mg IM), with doxycycline added only as presumptive treatment for chlamydia.

**UNDERSTAND:** Combinations are used for **four legitimate reasons: to PREVENT RESISTANCE emerging in a large bacterial population (TB, HIV, malaria), to cover a POLYMICROBIAL infection empirically, to achieve SYNERGY (beta-lactam + aminoglycoside in enterococcal endocarditis), and to broaden empirical cover in a critically ill patient.** Anything outside those reasons is unnecessary and drives resistance — which is precisely why the CDC dropped dual therapy for gonorrhoea once azithromycin resistance began to rise.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Fluoroquinolone elimination determines dose adjustment: ***HEPATICALLY metabolised — MOXIFLOXACIN, PEFLOXACIN and trovafloxacin (AVOID PEFLOXACIN IN LIVER DISEASE; moxifloxacin needs NO renal adjustment and is therefore useless for UTI); RENALLY excreted — CIPROFLOXACIN, LEVOFLOXACIN, OFLOXACIN, NORFLOXACIN and lomefloxacin.***
- **Quinolone class toxicities: ***TENDINITIS AND TENDON RUPTURE (especially with steroids and in the elderly), QT prolongation, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects and seizures, AORTIC ANEURYSM/DISSECTION, dysglycaemia, C. DIFFICILE colitis, and cartilage concerns in children (relative contraindication).***
- **Beta-lactamase inhibitors extend the spectrum: ***CLAVULANIC ACID, SULBACTAM, TAZOBACTAM, and the newer AVIBACTAM, VABORBACTAM and RELEBACTAM (active against carbapenemases).*** ***ESBL producers are treated with CARBAPENEMS; carbapenem-resistant organisms need colistin, ceftazidime-avibactam or newer agents.***
- ***DOXYCYCLINE IS THE DRUG OF CHOICE FOR SCRUB TYPHUS*** (and for rickettsial infections generally), with azithromycin the alternative in pregnancy and children — a favourite Indian exam question in the "acute febrile illness with an ESCHAR" stem.
- **Antimicrobial stewardship principles: culture before antibiotics, de-escalate once sensitivities are known, use the shortest effective course, and prefer the narrowest agent; ***SCHEDULE H1 IN INDIA EXISTS FOR EXACTLY THIS PURPOSE.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mechanism of beta-lactamase resistance → **degradation of the antibiotic**; combination therapy used in all EXCEPT → **gonorrhoea**; quinolone to avoid in liver disease → **pefloxacin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Destroy the drug, change the target, keep it out, or pump it back out — those are the only four options a bacterium has.*

## 27. Drug Interactions Worth Memorising

**PEARL:** ***ERYTHROMYCIN MUST NOT BE GIVEN WITH THEOPHYLLINE*** — it inhibits **CYP1A2**, reducing theophylline clearance and precipitating toxicity in a drug with a narrow therapeutic index. ***SUCRALFATE MUST BE SEPARATED FROM PHENYTOIN (and digoxin, ketoconazole, cimetidine and fluoroquinolones) BY AT LEAST 2 HOURS (120 MINUTES)***, because its viscous polymer coats the mucosa and blocks their absorption. And ***PHENYTOIN, AS AN ENZYME INDUCER, LOWERS THE LEVELS OF RETIGABINE (ezogabine) — SO THE RETIGABINE DOSE MUST BE INCREASED.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Nearly every interaction question reduces to **one of three mechanisms: ABSORPTION interference (chelation or a physical barrier — sucralfate, antacids, calcium, iron, cholestyramine), METABOLIC induction or inhibition (the CYP system), or EXCRETION/transport competition (probenecid, OATP inhibitors).** Absorption problems are solved by **spacing the doses**; metabolic problems require **dose adjustment or drug substitution**, since spacing achieves nothing.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***ENZYME INDUCERS ("CRAP GPS"): CARBAMAZEPINE, RIFAMPICIN, ALCOHOL (chronic), PHENYTOIN, GRISEOFULVIN, PHENOBARBITONE, SULPHONYLUREAS — and ST JOHN'S WORT and smoking (CYP1A2).*** They cause ***ORAL CONTRACEPTIVE FAILURE, warfarin resistance and transplant rejection***, and take days to develop.
- ***ENZYME INHIBITORS ("SICKFACES.COM"): SODIUM VALPROATE, ISONIAZID, CIMETIDINE, KETOCONAZOLE (and azoles), FLUCONAZOLE, ALCOHOL (acute), CHLORAMPHENICOL, ERYTHROMYCIN/clarithromycin, SULFONAMIDES, CIPROFLOXACIN, OMEPRAZOLE, METRONIDAZOLE, and GRAPEFRUIT JUICE.*** They act within hours.
- **Theophylline clearance is also ***REDUCED by cimetidine, ciprofloxacin, allopurinol, fluvoxamine, zileuton, heart failure, liver disease, pneumonia, viral infection and old age — and INCREASED by SMOKING, rifampicin, phenytoin and a high-protein diet.*** Toxicity causes nausea, vomiting, tremor, tachyarrhythmias, hypokalaemia and seizures.
- **Sucralfate's other points: it works only at ***pH <4 (so do NOT combine with acid suppression)***, causes CONSTIPATION, forms bezoars, and is ***AVOIDED IN RENAL FAILURE (aluminium accumulation).***
- **Classic pharmacodynamic interactions to carry: NSAIDs + ACE inhibitor + diuretic ("TRIPLE WHAMMY" renal failure); potassium-sparing diuretic + ACE inhibitor (hyperkalaemia); MAO inhibitor + tyramine (hypertensive crisis) or + pethidine/SSRIs (serotonin syndrome); allopurinol + azathioprine (marrow failure); and QT-prolonging drugs in combination.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Not to be prescribed with theophylline → **erythromycin**; gap between phenytoin and sucralfate → **120 minutes**; adding phenytoin to retigabine → **increase the retigabine dose**.

**REMEMBER:** *Absorption problems you can space apart; metabolic problems you must dose around.*

## 28. Inhaled Anticholinergics and Prokinetics

**PEARL:** **PARADOXICAL BRONCHOCONSTRICTION with nebulised IPRATROPIUM is caused by a ***HYPOTONIC nebuliser solution (NOT hypertonic saline)***, by preservatives — ***BENZALKONIUM CHLORIDE and EDTA*** — and by ***PREJUNCTIONAL M2 RECEPTOR BLOCKADE***. Among GI drugs, prokinetics are ***5-HT4 AGONISTS (prucalopride, cisapride), DOPAMINE D2 ANTAGONISTS (metoclopramide, domperidone), MOTILIN and motilin-mimetics (ERYTHROMYCIN)*** — while ***DIPHENYLMETHANE derivatives (BISACODYL) are LAXATIVES, not prokinetics.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The ipratropium paradox is elegant: **prejunctional M2 autoreceptors on airway cholinergic nerves normally provide NEGATIVE FEEDBACK, inhibiting further acetylcholine release. A non-selective antimuscarinic blocks these too, so acetylcholine release INCREASES and can overwhelm the postjunctional M3 blockade.** ***TIOTROPIUM AND THE OTHER LONG-ACTING ANTIMUSCARINICS ARE KINETICALLY SELECTIVE FOR M3 AND M1 AND DO NOT CAUSE THIS.*** Understanding autoreceptors explains the entire phenomenon.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other inhaled antimuscarinic effects: bitter taste, dry mouth, urinary retention in men with prostatic enlargement, and ***PRECIPITATION OF ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA IF THE NEBULISED DRUG REACHES THE EYES — USE A MOUTHPIECE RATHER THAN A MASK IN SUSCEPTIBLE PATIENTS.***
- **METOCLOPRAMIDE crosses the blood–brain barrier and causes ***EXTRAPYRAMIDAL REACTIONS, ACUTE DYSTONIA (especially in the young), TARDIVE DYSKINESIA AND HYPERPROLACTINAEMIA***; ***DOMPERIDONE does NOT cross appreciably but PROLONGS THE QT***; ***CISAPRIDE WAS WITHDRAWN FOR TORSADES DE POINTES.*** PRUCALOPRIDE is a selective 5-HT4 agonist for refractory chronic constipation.
- **Laxative classes: ***BULK (ispaghula), OSMOTIC (lactulose, PEG, magnesium salts), STIMULANT (bisacodyl, senna), SOFTENERS (docusate, liquid paraffin), and newer secretagogues (LUBIPROSTONE, LINACLOTIDE) and the peripheral opioid antagonists (METHYLNALTREXONE, naloxegol) for opioid-induced constipation.*** ***LACTULOSE IS ALSO THE TREATMENT OF HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY*** (acidifies the colon and traps ammonium).
- **Antitussives: ***CENTRALLY ACTING OPIOID — CODEINE, PHOLCODINE and LEVOPROPOXYPHENE (an opioid antitussive WITHOUT analgesic activity); CENTRAL NON-OPIOID — DEXTROMETHORPHAN (NMDA antagonist; abuse potential), noscapine; PERIPHERAL — demulcents, benzonatate.*** Contrast MUCOLYTICS/expectorants — ***AMBROXOL and BROMHEXINE, acetylcysteine, guaifenesin*** — which loosen secretions in a PRODUCTIVE cough, where a cough suppressant would be harmful. ***DIPHENOXYLATE is an ANTIDIARRHOEAL opioid (with atropine, to deter abuse), not an antitussive.***
- ***COUGH AND COLD PREPARATIONS ARE CONTRAINDICATED IN CHILDREN UNDER 4–6 YEARS***, and codeine is contraindicated under 12.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Paradoxical bronchoconstriction with ipratropium is due to all EXCEPT → **hypertonic saline** (it is a HYPOTONIC solution); NOT a prokinetic → **diphenylmethane**; centrally acting opioid antitussive → **levopropoxyphene**.

**REMEMBER:** *Block the autoreceptor and the nerve shouts louder — that is the ipratropium paradox.*

## 29. Antiepileptics for Neuropathic Pain

**PEARL:** ***PREGABALIN, GABAPENTIN and CARBAMAZEPINE are the antiepileptics established for NEUROPATHIC PAIN; LAMOTRIGINE is NOT.*** The gabapentinoids bind the ***ALPHA-2-DELTA SUBUNIT OF VOLTAGE-GATED CALCIUM CHANNELS***, reducing presynaptic calcium entry and glutamate release; ***CARBAMAZEPINE IS THE DRUG OF CHOICE FOR TRIGEMINAL NEURALGIA.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Neuropathic pain arises from **spontaneous, ectopic firing of damaged neurones and central sensitisation of the dorsal horn** — not from nociceptor stimulation. That is why ***NSAIDs AND PARACETAMOL WORK POORLY AND OPIOIDS ARE ONLY PARTIALLY EFFECTIVE***, while drugs that **stabilise membranes (sodium-channel blockers), damp transmitter release (gabapentinoids) or enhance descending inhibition (tricyclics, SNRIs)** are effective. Choosing by mechanism rather than by "painkiller strength" is the whole point.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **First-line agents for painful diabetic neuropathy and postherpetic neuralgia: ***AMITRIPTYLINE (or nortriptyline), DULOXETINE, PREGABALIN or GABAPENTIN*** — with topical lidocaine or capsaicin as adjuncts and tramadol/opioids reserved. In ***TRIGEMINAL NEURALGIA, CARBAMAZEPINE (or oxcarbazepine) is first-line***, with microvascular decompression for refractory cases.
- **Gabapentinoid practicalities: ***PREGABALIN has LINEAR, predictable absorption while GABAPENTIN's is SATURABLE***; both are ***RENALLY CLEARED (dose-reduce in CKD)***, cause sedation, dizziness, weight gain and peripheral oedema, and are increasingly recognised as ***DRUGS OF MISUSE*** (now controlled in many countries), with respiratory depression when combined with opioids.
- **Duloxetine (an SNRI) also treats depression, generalised anxiety, fibromyalgia and stress incontinence — a useful single agent when depression coexists with neuropathic pain.**
- **Assessment matters: ***BURNING, SHOOTING, ELECTRIC-SHOCK-LIKE PAIN WITH ALLODYNIA AND NUMBNESS IN A NEUROANATOMICAL DISTRIBUTION*** identifies neuropathic pain; screening tools (DN4, LANSS) formalise it. ***ALWAYS TREAT THE UNDERLYING CAUSE — glycaemic control, B12 replacement, antiviral treatment, decompression.***
- **The WHO analgesic ladder applies to nociceptive pain; ***adjuvants (antidepressants, anticonvulsants, steroids, bisphosphonates) can be introduced at ANY step***, and are the mainstay in neuropathic and cancer pain.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Antiepileptics also used for neuropathic pain → **pregabalin, carbamazepine and gabapentin** (NOT lamotrigine).

**REMEMBER:** *Neuropathic pain is a firing problem, not an inflammation problem — quieten the membrane, not the prostaglandins.*

## 30. Clomiphene Citrate

**PEARL:** ***CLOMIPHENE CAUSES MULTI-FOLLICULAR, NOT MONOFOLLICULAR, DEVELOPMENT*** (hence a ~8–10% twin rate). It is a **SELECTIVE OESTROGEN RECEPTOR MODULATOR that BLOCKS OESTROGEN'S NEGATIVE FEEDBACK at the hypothalamus**, producing a surge of GnRH → FSH and LH → ovulation. Its **ANTI-OESTROGENIC peripheral actions THICKEN CERVICAL MUCUS AND THIN THE ENDOMETRIUM**, and it can be combined with gonadotropins.

**UNDERSTAND:** The central paradox of clomiphene is that **the same receptor blockade that triggers ovulation also harms implantation.** Centrally, blocking oestrogen receptors is *desirable* — it tricks the hypothalamus into thinking oestrogen is low. Peripherally it is *undesirable* — the endometrium needs oestrogen to proliferate and the cervical mucus needs it to thin. That is why **ovulation rates with clomiphene (~80%) far exceed pregnancy rates (~40%)**, and why letrozole has displaced it in many protocols.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***LETROZOLE (an AROMATASE INHIBITOR) IS NOW FIRST-LINE FOR OVULATION INDUCTION IN PCOS*** — higher live-birth rates than clomiphene, with no anti-oestrogenic effect on the endometrium and a lower multiple-pregnancy rate.
- ***CLOMIPHENE WORKS ONLY IF THE HYPOTHALAMIC–PITUITARY–OVARIAN AXIS IS INTACT AND OESTROGEN LEVELS ARE NORMAL (WHO GROUP II — mainly PCOS). IT IS USELESS IN OVARIAN FAILURE (group III, high FSH) OR HYPOGONADOTROPIC HYPOGONADISM (group I, which needs pulsatile GnRH or gonadotropins).***
- **Given days 2–5 of the cycle for 5 days; monitor with follicular tracking. ***OVARIAN HYPERSTIMULATION SYNDROME (ascites, pleural effusion, haemoconcentration, thrombosis) is rare with clomiphene but a real risk with gonadotropins***, especially in PCOS.
- **In PCOS, weight loss of 5–10% alone can restore ovulation; metformin helps insulin resistance and is an adjunct; laparoscopic ovarian drilling is a second-line surgical option.**
- **Related SERMs: ***TAMOXIFEN — antagonist in breast, AGONIST in ENDOMETRIUM (endometrial carcinoma risk) and bone; RALOXIFENE — agonist in bone (osteoporosis), antagonist in breast and neutral in endometrium; OSPEMIFENE for dyspareunia.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Incorrect about clomiphene → **that it causes monofollicular development**.

**REMEMBER:** *It fools the hypothalamus and starves the endometrium — many eggs, fewer pregnancies.*

## 31. Enzyme Inhibition and the Edrophonium Test

**PEARL:** ***A SUICIDE (MECHANISM-BASED) INHIBITOR BINDS ONLY AT THE ACTIVE SITE*** — it is a substrate analogue that is chemically inert until the enzyme's own catalytic machinery converts it into a reactive species, which then binds ***IRREVERSIBLY (covalently).*** Hence such inhibitors are **highly enzyme-specific and are a mainstay of rational drug design.** Separately, ***EDROPHONIUM — a very short-acting anticholinesterase — DISTINGUISHES A MYASTHENIC CRISIS (weakness IMPROVES) FROM A CHOLINERGIC CRISIS (weakness worsens or is unchanged).***

**UNDERSTAND:** The elegance of suicide inhibition is that **the enzyme kills itself** — the drug is harmless everywhere else in the body because only the target enzyme can activate it, which is why side effects are few. Classic examples: ***ASPIRIN (acetylates COX), OMEPRAZOLE (activated by gastric acid to bind the H+/K+ ATPase), ALLOPURINOL's metabolite OXYPURINOL on xanthine oxidase, 5-FLUOROURACIL on thymidylate synthase, CLAVULANIC ACID on beta-lactamase, SELEGILINE on MAO-B, VIGABATRIN on GABA transaminase, ALPHA-DIFLUOROMETHYLORNITHINE on ornithine decarboxylase, and DISULFIRAM on aldehyde dehydrogenase.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Inhibition types: ***COMPETITIVE — binds the active site, Km INCREASES, Vmax UNCHANGED, overcome by more substrate. NON-COMPETITIVE — binds an allosteric site, Vmax DECREASES, Km unchanged. UNCOMPETITIVE — binds only the enzyme–substrate complex, BOTH Km and Vmax decrease. IRREVERSIBLE/suicide — covalent, and activity returns only with NEW ENZYME SYNTHESIS.***
- **The therapeutic consequence of irreversibility is duration: ***ASPIRIN'S ANTIPLATELET EFFECT LASTS THE PLATELET'S LIFETIME (7–10 days) AND DISULFIRAM'S EFFECT LASTS UP TO 14 DAYS***, regardless of the drug's own half-life.
- ***THE EDROPHONIUM (TENSILON) TEST: 2 mg IV, then 8 mg more if no response within 45 seconds; improvement is positive for myasthenia gravis.*** ***HAVE ATROPINE AND RESUSCITATION FACILITIES READY — bradycardia and bronchospasm can occur.*** The test has largely been replaced by antibody testing (anti-AChR, anti-MuSK) and repetitive nerve stimulation/single-fibre EMG, but remains a favourite exam item.
- **Distinguishing the crises clinically: ***CHOLINERGIC crisis has MUSCARINIC EXCESS — miosis, salivation, sweating, bradycardia, diarrhoea and fasciculations; MYASTHENIC crisis has none of these.*** Both need ***AIRWAY ASSESSMENT AND VENTILATORY SUPPORT FIRST.***
- **Anticholinesterases by duration: ***EDROPHONIUM (minutes, diagnostic), NEOSTIGMINE and PYRIDOSTIGMINE (hours, therapeutic — pyridostigmine longer-acting and used orally), PHYSOSTIGMINE (crosses the blood–brain barrier — used for central anticholinergic syndrome), and ORGANOPHOSPHATES (irreversible after "ageing").***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** False about suicide inhibition → **that the inhibitor can bind at any site**; drug distinguishing myasthenic from cholinergic crisis → **edrophonium**.

**REMEMBER:** *The enzyme activates its own assassin — and only at the active site.*

## 32. Linezolid and the Oxazolidinones

**PEARL:** ***LINEZOLID MUST NOT BE USED EMPIRICALLY IN POST-CHEMOTHERAPY FEBRILE NEUTROPENIA*** — it causes **MYELOSUPPRESSION (especially THROMBOCYTOPENIA at 7–10 days, plus anaemia and leucopenia)** and would deepen the existing marrow failure. It is an **oxazolidinone that binds the 50S subunit (P site) and blocks initiation of protein synthesis**, active almost exclusively against ***GRAM-POSITIVE organisms — MRSA, VRE, penicillin-resistant pneumococci*** — with **essentially 100% ORAL BIOAVAILABILITY.** (TEDIZOLID is the newer congener with the same spectrum, poor activity against gram-negatives and anaerobes.)

**UNDERSTAND:** Linezolid's other toxicities all come from a single off-target effect: **it also inhibits MITOCHONDRIAL protein synthesis** (mitochondrial ribosomes resemble bacterial ones). That explains **LACTIC ACIDOSIS, PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY and OPTIC NEURITIS with prolonged use, and the marrow suppression** — a set of adverse effects that appears only after 2 weeks, which is why courses are kept short. Separately, it is a ***WEAK NON-SELECTIVE MAO INHIBITOR — hence SEROTONIN SYNDROME with SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol and pethidine, and hypertensive reactions with tyramine and sympathomimetics.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Empirical therapy for FEBRILE NEUTROPENIA is an ***ANTIPSEUDOMONAL BETA-LACTAM — PIPERACILLIN-TAZOBACTAM, CEFEPIME or a CARBAPENEM (meropenem) — within 1 HOUR***; glycopeptide cover (vancomycin) is added for suspected line infection, skin/soft tissue infection, haemodynamic instability or known MRSA colonisation.
- **Its excellent oral bioavailability and lung penetration make linezolid useful for ***MRSA PNEUMONIA and skin/soft-tissue infection, and it is a core drug in MDR/XDR TUBERCULOSIS regimens (BPaL)*** — where neuropathy and marrow toxicity are dose-limiting.
- **Other antibiotics with characteristic haematological toxicity: ***CHLORAMPHENICOL (dose-related marrow suppression AND idiosyncratic APLASTIC ANAEMIA; GREY BABY SYNDROME in neonates), COTRIMOXAZOLE (megaloblastic change, neutropenia), and beta-lactams at high dose (neutropenia).***
- **Gram-positive alternatives: ***VANCOMYCIN (nephro/ototoxicity, red-man syndrome — an infusion-rate histamine reaction, not allergy), DAPTOMYCIN (INACTIVATED BY PULMONARY SURFACTANT — never for pneumonia; causes myopathy, monitor CK), TIGECYCLINE, and CEFTAROLINE (the anti-MRSA cephalosporin).***
- ***VANCOMYCIN-RESISTANT ENTEROCOCCUS: linezolid and daptomycin are the mainstays.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Antibiotic NOT to use empirically in post-chemotherapy neutropenia → **linezolid**.

**REMEMBER:** *It poisons bacterial and mitochondrial ribosomes alike — marrow, nerve and optic nerve pay the price.*

## 33. Ergotism and CYP3A4 Catastrophes

**PEARL:** **A patient on ANTIRETROVIRALS (ritonavir/lopinavir — potent CYP3A4 INHIBITORS) who takes ERGOTAMINE for migraine can develop ***ERGOTISM: severe, diffuse, symmetrical vasospasm of the limb arteries with pain, pallor, paraesthesiae and threatened gangrene***, shown as long smooth luminal narrowing on angiography. Ergotamine is a **partial agonist/antagonist at ALPHA-ADRENERGIC, 5-HT1 and 5-HT2 receptors**, producing sustained vasoconstriction.

**UNDERSTAND:** Ergot alkaloids have a **steep, narrow therapeutic window and are cleared almost entirely by CYP3A4.** Any potent CYP3A4 inhibitor — ***protease inhibitors, MACROLIDES (clarithromycin, erythromycin), AZOLE ANTIFUNGALS, grapefruit juice*** — can multiply their levels several-fold, converting a therapeutic dose into a limb-threatening one. This is the archetype of the "***CONTRAINDICATED COMBINATION***" category of interaction: not a dose adjustment, but an absolute prohibition.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***ERGOT PREPARATIONS ARE CONTRAINDICATED IN: pregnancy, peripheral vascular disease, ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, sepsis, hepatic or renal impairment, and with CYP3A4 inhibitors or triptans (within 24 hours).*** Treatment of ergotism is stopping the drug plus vasodilators (nitroprusside, nifedipine) and anticoagulation.
- **Migraine pharmacology: ***ACUTE — NSAIDs/paracetamol, TRIPTANS (5-HT1B/1D agonists — contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, and in hemiplegic/basilar migraine), antiemetics, and the newer GEPANTS (CGRP antagonists — rimegepant, ubrogepant) and LASMIDITAN (5-HT1F agonist, safe in vascular disease). PROPHYLAXIS — PROPRANOLOL, TOPIRAMATE, amitriptyline, flunarizine, valproate, and CGRP MONOCLONAL ANTIBODIES (erenumab, fremanezumab); BOTULINUM TOXIN for chronic migraine.***
- ***MEDICATION-OVERUSE HEADACHE*** is the commonest reason a migraine becomes chronic — especially with ergots, opioids and combination analgesics.
- **Other ergot uses: ***ERGOMETRINE/METHYLERGOMETRINE for PPH (contraindicated in hypertension and pre-eclampsia), BROMOCRIPTINE and CABERGOLINE (dopamine agonists — hyperprolactinaemia, acromegaly, Parkinson disease; the ergot-derived ones cause CARDIAC VALVE FIBROSIS and retroperitoneal fibrosis).***
- **Classic CYP3A4 substrate casualties to remember: ***ergots, statins, ciclosporin/tacrolimus, warfarin, midazolam, calcium channel blockers, sildenafil, carbamazepine and many antiarrhythmics.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Limb ischaemia after ergotamine in a patient on ART → **ergotism**.

**REMEMBER:** *Block CYP3A4 and an ordinary ergot dose becomes a tourniquet.*

## 34. Topiramate

**PEARL:** **TOPIRAMATE is used for ***focal and generalised tonic–clonic seizures, for TONIC-CLONIC SEIZURES IN LENNOX–GASTAUT SYNDROME, and for MIGRAINE PROPHYLAXIS (not acute treatment)*** — with a broad, multi-target mechanism: **blocks voltage-gated sodium currents, activates a hyperpolarising potassium current, enhances GABA-A currents, antagonises AMPA/kainate glutamate receptors, and weakly inhibits CARBONIC ANHYDRASE.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The **carbonic anhydrase inhibition, though weak, explains its most examinable adverse effects**: ***RENAL STONES, metabolic acidosis, paraesthesiae, and ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA WITH MYOPIA (an idiosyncratic ciliochoroidal effusion in the first weeks — an ophthalmic emergency, and NOT treated by iridotomy, but by stopping the drug).*** The multi-target mechanism also explains its breadth of use — and its cognitive burden.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other adverse effects: ***WEIGHT LOSS AND ANOREXIA (exploited in the phentermine–topiramate obesity combination), COGNITIVE SLOWING AND WORD-FINDING DIFFICULTY ("dopamax"), paraesthesiae, hypohidrosis with hyperthermia in children, and TERATOGENICITY (oral clefts) — so contraception counselling is needed.***
- **Pharmacokinetics: only 10–20% protein-bound, largely excreted UNCHANGED IN THE URINE (dose-reduce in renal impairment), half-life ~1 day allowing once/twice daily dosing.**
- **Lennox–Gastaut syndrome (multiple seizure types, slow spike-and-wave, intellectual disability) is treated with ***VALPROATE, LAMOTRIGINE, TOPIRAMATE, RUFINAMIDE, clobazam, felbamate and CANNABIDIOL***; the ketogenic diet and vagal nerve stimulation are non-pharmacological options.
- **Migraine prophylaxis is considered when attacks are ***frequent (≥4/month), disabling or poorly responsive to acute treatment***, and is judged over 2–3 months.
- **Compare ZONISAMIDE (also a broad-spectrum agent with carbonic anhydrase activity, stones and weight loss) and ACETAZOLAMIDE (a pure carbonic anhydrase inhibitor used in glaucoma, altitude sickness, idiopathic intracranial hypertension and periodic paralysis).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Topiramate is used for → **Lennox–Gastaut syndrome** (and migraine PROPHYLAXIS, not treatment).

**REMEMBER:** *A little carbonic anhydrase inhibition buys stones, tingling and a glaucoma emergency.*

## 35. Digoxin and P-Glycoprotein

**PEARL:** ***CLARITHROMYCIN PRECIPITATES DIGOXIN TOXICITY BY INHIBITING P-GLYCOPROTEIN***, the efflux transporter responsible for much of digoxin's elimination. Other P-gp inhibitors that raise digoxin levels: ***AMIODARONE, QUINIDINE, VERAPAMIL, DILTIAZEM, CICLOSPORIN, ITRACONAZOLE, PROPAFENONE and FLECAINIDE.*** Digoxin acts by **inhibiting Na+/K+-ATPase → raised intracellular sodium → reduced calcium extrusion by the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger → increased intracellular calcium and contractility.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Digoxin's toxicity is potentiated by **HYPOKALAEMIA** because **potassium and digoxin COMPETE FOR THE SAME BINDING SITE on the Na+/K+-ATPase** — so a low potassium leaves the site free and effectively increases digoxin binding at any given level. That is why ***DIURETIC-INDUCED HYPOKALAEMIA IS THE COMMONEST PRECIPITANT OF DIGOXIN TOXICITY***, and why hypomagnesaemia and hypercalcaemia do the same. Conversely, in ACUTE massive overdose the ATPase blockade drives potassium OUT of cells, so ***HYPERKALAEMIA IS THE HALLMARK OF ACUTE POISONING AND THE BEST PREDICTOR OF MORTALITY.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Toxicity features: anorexia, nausea and vomiting, ***XANTHOPSIA (yellow-green vision) and haloes***, confusion, and arrhythmias — classically ***"PAROXYSMAL ATRIAL TACHYCARDIA WITH BLOCK", bidirectional ventricular tachycardia, and ATRIAL FIBRILLATION WITH A REGULARISED/SLOW VENTRICULAR RESPONSE (complete heart block)***. The ***"REVERSE TICK" (sagging ST depression) is a sign of digoxin EFFECT, not toxicity.***
- **Management: stop the drug, correct potassium and magnesium, treat bradyarrhythmia with atropine, and give ***DIGOXIN-SPECIFIC FAB FRAGMENTS*** for life-threatening arrhythmia, hyperkalaemia >5.5 or massive ingestion. ***AVOID CALCIUM IN DIGOXIN TOXICITY ("stone heart") AND AVOID CARDIOVERSION IF POSSIBLE.***
- **Digoxin is ***RENALLY EXCRETED with a narrow therapeutic range (0.5–0.9 ng/mL in heart failure)***, has a large volume of distribution (NOT dialysable), and is best used for ***RATE CONTROL IN ATRIAL FIBRILLATION (particularly in sedentary patients and heart failure) — it reduces hospitalisation but NOT mortality in heart failure.***
- ***DIGOXIN CONTROLS RATE AT REST BUT POORLY DURING EXERCISE***, because its effect is largely vagally mediated.
- **P-glycoprotein also determines the levels of ***DABIGATRAN, the -xabans, ciclosporin and many chemotherapy drugs*** — and its overexpression in tumours is a mechanism of multidrug resistance.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug raising digoxin levels → **clarithromycin (P-glycoprotein inhibition)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Potassium and digoxin fight for the same seat on the pump — lose potassium and digoxin wins.*

## 36. Prescribing in Chronic Kidney Disease

**PEARL:** ***PARACETAMOL (ACETAMINOPHEN) IS THE ANALGESIC OF CHOICE IN CKD*** — ***NSAIDs (naproxen, indomethacin, diclofenac) ARE AVOIDED*** because prostaglandin-dependent afferent arteriolar vasodilatation is what maintains GFR when the renin–angiotensin system is activated. For hypertension: ***ACE INHIBITORS/ARBs ARE FIRST-LINE WHEN THERE IS PROTEINURIA; WITHOUT PROTEINURIA there is no established first-line, and a THIAZIDE-LIKE DIURETIC (CHLORTHALIDONE) is appropriate — particularly with fluid overload*** (switching to a LOOP diuretic once eGFR falls below ~30).

**UNDERSTAND:** In a healthy person, prostaglandins contribute little to renal blood flow. But in ***hypovolaemia, heart failure, cirrhosis or CKD — states of high angiotensin II and sympathetic tone — efferent constriction is intense and only PGE2/PGI2-mediated AFFERENT DILATATION preserves filtration.*** An NSAID removes that support and GFR collapses. Combine it with an ACE inhibitor (which removes efferent tone) and a diuretic (which removes volume) and you have the ***"TRIPLE WHAMMY" of acute kidney injury.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Chlorthalidone is ***LONGER-ACTING AND MORE POTENT THAN HYDROCHLOROTHIAZIDE***, with better outcome data, and ***RETAINS EFFICACY IN ADVANCED CKD (the CLICK trial)*** — a point that has changed practice.
- **For severe pain in CKD prefer ***FENTANYL, BUPRENORPHINE or (with caution) tramadol; AVOID MORPHINE, CODEINE AND PETHIDINE*** — their active metabolites (morphine-6-glucuronide, norpethidine) accumulate and cause narcosis or seizures.
- **Other drugs to avoid or adjust in CKD: metformin below eGFR 30, gadolinium contrast (nephrogenic systemic fibrosis), aminoglycosides, ***iodinated CONTRAST (hydrate beforehand)***, and dose-adjust digoxin, LMWH, gabapentinoids and most antimicrobials.
- **The complete CKD package: ***BP <130/80, ACE inhibitor/ARB for proteinuria, an SGLT2 INHIBITOR (now standard for CKD with albuminuria, diabetic or not), statin, phosphate control and vitamin D analogues for mineral–bone disease, erythropoietin with iron for anaemia, sodium bicarbonate for acidosis, dietary salt and protein moderation, and vaccination (hepatitis B, pneumococcal, influenza).***
- ***HYPERKALAEMIA RISK GOVERNS THE RAS-BLOCKER DOSE*** — use potassium binders (patiromer, sodium zirconium) rather than abandoning a prognostically important drug.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Postoperative analgesic in CKD → **acetaminophen**; antihypertensive for renal insufficiency with fluid overload and no proteinuria → **chlorthalidone**.

**REMEMBER:** *In a stressed kidney, prostaglandins hold the front door open — never shut it with an NSAID.*

## 37. Glaucoma Drugs by Mechanism of Outflow

**PEARL:** ***NETARSUDIL, a RHO-KINASE INHIBITOR, INCREASES TRABECULAR OUTFLOW*** by relaxing the trabecular meshwork (and also reduces aqueous production and episcleral venous pressure); its side effects are **conjunctival hyperaemia and CORNEAL VERTICILLATA.** ***LATANOPROST (a PGF2-alpha analogue) IS THE DRUG OF CHOICE IN OPEN-ANGLE GLAUCOMA WITH ASTHMA*** — it increases UVEOSCLERAL outflow and has **no systemic effects**, whereas ***TIMOLOL CAN CAUSE LIFE-THREATENING BRONCHOSPASM.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Sorting the drugs by ***PRODUCTION versus OUTFLOW, and by WHICH outflow pathway***, makes the whole class memorable: **REDUCE PRODUCTION — beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, alpha-2 agonists. INCREASE UVEOSCLERAL OUTFLOW — prostaglandin analogues, alpha-2 agonists. INCREASE TRABECULAR OUTFLOW — pilocarpine (by ciliary muscle traction on the scleral spur) and NETARSUDIL (by cytoskeletal relaxation of the meshwork itself).** The trabecular meshwork is the site of the actual pathology in open-angle glaucoma, which is why drugs acting there are conceptually attractive.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***PROSTAGLANDIN ANALOGUES ARE FIRST-LINE FOR PRIMARY OPEN-ANGLE GLAUCOMA*** — best efficacy, once daily, no systemic effects — but AVOID in uveitis, aphakia and macular oedema (they can cause cystoid macular oedema), and warn about iris darkening, lash growth and periorbital fat atrophy.
- **Systemic contraindications to remember: ***TIMOLOL — asthma, COPD, bradycardia, heart block, and it masks hypoglycaemia in diabetics; BRIMONIDINE — infants and young children (apnoea, CNS depression) and with MAO inhibitors; ACETAZOLAMIDE — sulfa allergy, renal stones, hypokalaemia, metabolic acidosis, and it is a teratogen.***
- **New fixed combinations (netarsudil–latanoprost) and ***LATANOPROSTENE BUNOD (a nitric-oxide-donating prostaglandin acting on BOTH pathways)*** reflect the same production/outflow logic.
- ***DEFINITIVE MANAGEMENT OPTIONS: SELECTIVE LASER TRABECULOPLASTY (now often used as FIRST-LINE), trabeculectomy with antimetabolites, glaucoma drainage devices and MIGS.***
- **Glaucoma is an ***OPTIC NEUROPATHY — intraocular pressure is the only modifiable risk factor, and NORMAL-TENSION GLAUCOMA exists***; management is judged by visual fields and OCT of the retinal nerve fibre layer, not pressure alone.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug increasing trabecular outflow → **netarsudil**; glaucoma drug for a patient with asthma → **latanoprost**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prostaglandins push aqueous out the back door, netarsudil unclogs the front one.*

## 38. Antiplatelet Targets and the Eicosanoid Pathway

**PEARL:** ***ASPIRIN'S BLEEDING (occult blood in stool) IS DUE TO INHIBITION OF THROMBOXANE A2*** — irreversible acetylation of platelet COX-1 abolishing TXA2-driven aggregation and vasoconstriction (in addition to loss of gastroprotective PGE2). The other antiplatelet targets: ***EPTIFIBATIDE/tirofiban/abciximab — GLYCOPROTEIN IIb/IIIa; VORAPAXAR — PROTEASE-ACTIVATED RECEPTOR-1 (PAR-1), the thrombin receptor; clopidogrel/prasugrel/ticagrelor — P2Y12.*** ***EICOSANOIDS ARE SYNTHESISED FROM ARACHIDONIC ACID (NOT arginine)***, released from membrane phospholipids by **PHOSPHOLIPASE A2.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The arachidonic acid cascade is worth holding as a single diagram because so many drugs act on it: ***CORTICOSTEROIDS inhibit PHOSPHOLIPASE A2 (via lipocortin/annexin-1) — hence they block BOTH prostaglandins AND leukotrienes, which is why they are more powerful anti-inflammatories than NSAIDs. NSAIDs inhibit only CYCLOOXYGENASE, so leukotriene synthesis continues and can even be shunted upwards — the mechanism of ASPIRIN-EXACERBATED RESPIRATORY DISEASE (SAMTER'S TRIAD: asthma, nasal polyps and aspirin sensitivity). ZILEUTON inhibits 5-LIPOXYGENASE and MONTELUKAST/zafirlukast block the CysLT1 receptor.***
**Platelets make mainly THROMBOXANE A2 (aggregation, vasoconstriction); endothelium makes mainly PROSTACYCLIN (PGI2 — the opposite).** Low-dose aspirin exploits the fact that ***PLATELETS CANNOT SYNTHESISE NEW COX (no nucleus) WHILE ENDOTHELIUM CAN***, so TXA2 is suppressed far longer than PGI2.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Prostaglandins contain a ***5-CARBON (cyclopentane) RING***; leukotrienes do not. Prostacyclin and thromboxane are the vascular counterweights.
- **Aspirin dose-dependent effects: ***75–150 mg — antiplatelet; 0.5–2 g — analgesic/antipyretic (with occult GI blood loss); 3–5 g — anti-inflammatory, producing SALICYLISM (tinnitus, vertigo, hyperventilation, reversible hearing loss); overdose — a MIXED RESPIRATORY ALKALOSIS AND HIGH-ANION-GAP METABOLIC ACIDOSIS, treated with URINARY ALKALINISATION and dialysis.*** ***REYE SYNDROME (encephalopathy with fatty liver) contraindicates aspirin in children with viral illness — except in KAWASAKI DISEASE.***
- **COX-2 selective inhibitors (celecoxib, etoricoxib) spare the stomach but ***REMOVE ENDOTHELIAL PROSTACYCLIN WITHOUT AFFECTING PLATELET THROMBOXANE — hence a PROTHROMBOTIC tendency*** (rofecoxib was withdrawn). Use the lowest dose for the shortest time and avoid in cardiovascular disease.
- **Aspirin also has an important obstetric role: ***LOW-DOSE ASPIRIN FROM 12–16 WEEKS PREVENTS PRE-ECLAMPSIA IN HIGH-RISK WOMEN.***
- **Vorapaxar (PAR-1 antagonist) is added to aspirin ± clopidogrel in prior MI or peripheral arterial disease, but is ***CONTRAINDICATED AFTER STROKE, TIA OR INTRACRANIAL HAEMORRHAGE.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Occult GI bleeding on aspirin → **thromboxane inhibition**; false about eicosanoids → **that they are synthesised from arginine**; identifying the antiplatelet targets → **aspirin (COX), eptifibatide (GpIIb/IIIa), vorapaxar (PAR-1)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Steroids stop the cascade at its source; NSAIDs only close one branch and divert traffic down the other.*

## 39. Pharmacotherapy of Osteoporosis

**PEARL:** **A postmenopausal woman with a fragility (Colles) fracture and a ***T-SCORE ≤ −2.5 has OSTEOPOROSIS***. ***BISPHOSPHONATES ARE GIVEN FOR 3–5 YEARS (NOT "no more than a year")***, followed by consideration of a drug holiday; ***TERIPARATIDE (an ANABOLIC agent) IS APPROPRIATE BEFORE bisphosphonates in severe disease with an existing fragility fracture***, and must be FOLLOWED by an antiresorptive to preserve the gain. Calcium requirement is **1000–1200 mg/day with vitamin D**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Bone drugs split into ***ANTIRESORPTIVE (bisphosphonates, denosumab, raloxifene, oestrogen, calcitonin) and ANABOLIC (TERIPARATIDE/abaloparatide, ROMOSOZUMAB).*** The sequence matters because ***THE ANABOLIC WINDOW IS SHORT AND IS BLUNTED BY PRIOR POTENT ANTIRESORPTIVE THERAPY***, whereas gains made by an anabolic agent are LOST WITHIN MONTHS unless an antiresorptive follows. Hence: in severe osteoporosis, ***ANABOLIC FIRST, ANTIRESORPTIVE SECOND.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Bisphosphonates are pyrophosphate analogues that bind hydroxyapatite and induce ***OSTEOCLAST APOPTOSIS***; oral forms must be taken ***FASTING, WITH PLAIN WATER, SITTING UPRIGHT FOR 30 MINUTES*** (oesophagitis). Adverse effects: ***OSTEONECROSIS OF THE JAW (dental clearance first) and ATYPICAL SUBTROCHANTERIC FEMORAL FRACTURES***, acute-phase reaction with IV zoledronate, hypocalcaemia and renal impairment.
- ***CORRECT VITAMIN D AND CALCIUM DEFICIENCY BEFORE STARTING ANY POTENT ANTIRESORPTIVE*** or severe hypocalcaemia may follow (especially with denosumab and zoledronate).
- **TERIPARATIDE (recombinant PTH 1-34): given daily by injection for up to 2 years; ***INTERMITTENT PTH IS ANABOLIC WHILE CONTINUOUS PTH IS CATABOLIC*** — the elegant paradox of the drug. Avoid in Paget disease, prior skeletal irradiation, hypercalcaemia and unexplained raised alkaline phosphatase.
- **DENOSUMAB (anti-RANKL) is given 6-monthly; ***IT MUST NEVER SIMPLY BE STOPPED — REBOUND VERTEBRAL FRACTURES OCCUR***, so transition to a bisphosphonate. ROMOSOZUMAB (anti-sclerostin) both builds and blocks resorption, but carries a cardiovascular warning.
- **Assessment: DEXA T-score (−1 to −2.5 = osteopenia; ≤−2.5 = osteoporosis; with a fragility fracture = severe/established), ***FRAX for 10-year fracture risk***, plus exclusion of secondary causes (steroids, hyperparathyroidism, thyrotoxicosis, myeloma, coeliac disease, hypogonadism). Weight-bearing exercise, smoking and alcohol cessation and falls prevention are integral.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** False statement about osteoporosis management → **that bisphosphonates are not given for more than a year**.

**REMEMBER:** *Build first, then protect — and never stop denosumab into thin air.*

## 40. SGLT2 Inhibitors

**PEARL:** ***CANAGLIFLOZIN CARRIES AN FDA WARNING FOR INCREASED FRACTURE RISK*** (and, historically, amputation), an effect linked to altered PTH and 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D handling and to volume depletion/falls. SGLT2 inhibitors block glucose (and sodium) reabsorption in the **PROXIMAL CONVOLUTED TUBULE**, producing **GLYCOSURIA with 2–4 kg weight loss, lower blood pressure, and — most importantly — ***REDUCTION IN CARDIOVASCULAR EVENTS, HEART FAILURE HOSPITALISATION AND CKD PROGRESSION.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Their benefit is **only partly glycaemic.** Delivering sodium to the macula densa **restores TUBULOGLOMERULAR FEEDBACK, constricting the afferent arteriole and relieving glomerular HYPERFILTRATION** — hence a small early dip in eGFR that is *desirable* and predicts long-term protection. They also improve cardiac energetics and reduce preload. This is why they are now prescribed in ***HEART FAILURE AND CKD REGARDLESS OF WHETHER THE PATIENT HAS DIABETES.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Adverse effects: ***GENITAL MYCOTIC INFECTIONS AND URINARY TRACT INFECTION (from glycosuria), volume depletion and hypotension, and EUGLYCAEMIC DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS*** — a diagnostic trap, since the glucose may be near-normal. ***HOLD SGLT2 INHIBITORS BEFORE SURGERY, DURING ACUTE ILLNESS AND WHILE FASTING ("SICK DAY RULES").*** Rare FOURNIER'S GANGRENE.
- **They do NOT cause hypoglycaemia alone (insulin-independent mechanism) but do when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.**
- **Other antidiabetic drug hazards worth pairing: ***PIOGLITAZONE (rosiglitazone) — fluid retention, HEART FAILURE, FRACTURES in women, bladder cancer concern; METFORMIN — GI upset, B12 deficiency, lactic acidosis; SULFONYLUREAS — hypoglycaemia and weight gain; GLP-1 AGONISTS — nausea, pancreatitis concern, MEDULLARY THYROID CARCINOMA warning; DPP-4 INHIBITORS — bullous pemphigoid, joint pain; ACARBOSE/VOGLIBOSE — flatulence, and hypoglycaemia must be treated with GLUCOSE, not sucrose.***
- **Choice logic in type 2 diabetes: ***METFORMIN first; then add an SGLT2 INHIBITOR or GLP-1 AGONIST if there is atherosclerotic disease, heart failure or CKD — regardless of HbA1c.***
- **Empagliflozin and canagliflozin have the strongest cardiovascular outcome data; dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are approved across the heart failure spectrum.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Antidiabetic associated with increased fracture risk → **canagliflozin**.

**REMEMBER:** *They dump sugar and sodium — the kidney stops hyperfiltering and the heart stops failing.*

## 41. Drugs for Alzheimer Disease

**PEARL:** **MEMANTINE is a ***NON-COMPETITIVE NMDA RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST*** that reduces glutamate excitotoxicity and is ***RESERVED FOR MODERATE-TO-SEVERE DISEASE.*** ***IT CAN BE COMBINED WITH CHOLINESTERASE INHIBITORS (it is not restricted to monotherapy), and the usual dose is 5 mg daily titrated to 10 mg TWICE daily (20 mg/day), not 15–30 mg.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Memantine's therapeutic trick is that it is a ***LOW-AFFINITY, VOLTAGE-DEPENDENT, RAPIDLY DISSOCIATING channel blocker.*** It occupies the NMDA channel during the sustained low-level pathological activation that causes excitotoxicity, but is **displaced by the strong, transient depolarisations of normal synaptic transmission** — so it blocks the noise without blocking the signal. Older, high-affinity NMDA blockers (ketamine, phencyclidine) do the opposite and cause psychosis.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The cholinergic arm: ***DONEPEZIL, RIVASTIGMINE and GALANTAMINE*** are cholinesterase inhibitors for MILD-TO-MODERATE disease; adverse effects are cholinergic (nausea, diarrhoea, bradycardia and syncope, vivid dreams, urinary urgency) — ***CAUTION IN BRADYCARDIA, HEART BLOCK AND PEPTIC ULCER.*** ***RIVASTIGMINE IS THE AGENT WITH BEST EVIDENCE IN PARKINSON DISEASE DEMENTIA AND LEWY BODY DEMENTIA.***
- ***NEITHER CLASS MODIFIES DISEASE — THEY PRODUCE MODEST SYMPTOMATIC BENEFIT.*** The newer anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) slow decline slightly at the cost of ***ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities — oedema and microhaemorrhage)***, and require APOE genotyping and MRI monitoring.
- ***ANTIPSYCHOTICS FOR BEHAVIOURAL AND PSYCHOLOGICAL SYMPTOMS OF DEMENTIA INCREASE STROKE AND MORTALITY*** and must be a last resort at the lowest dose for the shortest time — with ***EXTREME CAUTION (NEUROLEPTIC SENSITIVITY) IN LEWY BODY DEMENTIA***, where they can cause severe, even fatal, extrapyramidal reactions.
- **Memantine is ***RENALLY EXCRETED — reduce the dose in renal impairment***; its main side effects are dizziness, headache, confusion and constipation.
- **Always look for ***REVERSIBLE contributors to cognitive decline: B12 and thyroid deficiency, depression ("pseudodementia"), normal-pressure hydrocephalus, subdural haematoma, neurosyphilis, HIV and — very commonly — ANTICHOLINERGIC DRUG BURDEN.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about memantine → **it acts on NMDA receptors and is reserved for moderate-to-severe disease** (it CAN be combined with cholinesterase inhibitors; the dose is 20 mg/day).

**REMEMBER:** *It plugs the leak without blocking the signal — that is why it is tolerated and ketamine is not.*

## 42. Choosing a Benzodiazepine in Liver Disease

**PEARL:** ***OXAZEPAM, LORAZEPAM AND TEMAZEPAM ("LOT") ARE THE BENZODIAZEPINES OF CHOICE IN LIVER DISEASE AND IN THE ELDERLY*** — they undergo ***ONLY GLUCURONIDATION (phase II conjugation)***, which is well preserved when hepatic oxidation fails, and they have **NO ACTIVE METABOLITES.** ***DIAZEPAM, CHLORDIAZEPOXIDE, MIDAZOLAM AND ALPRAZOLAM require CYP OXIDATION (phase I) and produce long-acting active metabolites (nordazepam)*** that accumulate and precipitate encephalopathy.

**UNDERSTAND:** ***PHASE I (oxidative, CYP-dependent) METABOLISM IS IMPAIRED EARLY IN LIVER DISEASE AND WITH AGEING; PHASE II (conjugation) IS RELATIVELY SPARED.*** This one principle generalises far beyond benzodiazepines and explains many geriatric prescribing rules. Combine it with the fact that **hepatic encephalopathy is itself a state of enhanced GABAergic tone**, and the danger of a long-acting benzodiazepine in cirrhosis becomes obvious.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Benzodiazepines act at a distinct site on the ***GABA-A receptor and INCREASE THE FREQUENCY of chloride channel opening; BARBITURATES INCREASE THE DURATION*** (and at high doses open the channel directly, which is why they have no ceiling and are far more lethal in overdose).
- ***FLUMAZENIL reverses benzodiazepines but CAN PRECIPITATE SEIZURES*** in chronic users and in mixed overdoses (especially with tricyclics) — use cautiously.
- **Clinical niches: ***LORAZEPAM — status epilepticus and alcohol withdrawal in liver disease; MIDAZOLAM — procedural sedation and buccal/IM seizure control (short-acting, water-soluble); DIAZEPAM — muscle spasm, febrile seizures (rectal); CLONAZEPAM — myoclonus; ALPRAZOLAM — panic disorder (but high dependence potential).***
- ***ALL BENZODIAZEPINES CAUSE TOLERANCE, DEPENDENCE, ANTEROGRADE AMNESIA, FALLS AND FRACTURES IN THE ELDERLY, AND RESPIRATORY DEPRESSION WITH OPIOIDS OR ALCOHOL*** — prescribe for the shortest possible period and taper to stop. Z-drugs (zolpidem, zopiclone) act at the same receptor with similar problems.
- **In hepatic encephalopathy the therapeutic priorities are ***LACTULOSE and RIFAXIMIN, correction of precipitants (GI bleed, infection, constipation, electrolyte disturbance, sedatives) — and avoiding sedation altogether where possible.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Benzodiazepine for a patient with altered sensorium, seizures and raised transaminases → **oxazepam**.

**REMEMBER:** *"LOT" needs only conjugation — the sick liver can still manage that.*

## 43. Fibrates and the Lipid-Lowering Classes

**PEARL:** ***FIBRATES ACT BY ACTIVATING PPAR-ALPHA***, which upregulates LIPOPROTEIN LIPASE and fatty acid oxidation and suppresses apo C-III — lowering triglycerides and raising HDL. ***ALL FIBRATES INCREASE THE LITHOGENICITY OF BILE AND HENCE CAUSE GALLSTONES.*** For contrast: **STATINS inhibit HMG-CoA reductase; BILE ACID SEQUESTRANTS bind bile acids in the gut; NIACIN inhibits lipolysis in adipose tissue and hepatic VLDL secretion; EZETIMIBE blocks NPC1L1.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The PPAR family is worth holding as a set because it recurs: ***PPAR-ALPHA (fibrates) — lowers TRIGLYCERIDES; PPAR-GAMMA (thiazolidinediones) — INSULIN SENSITISATION; PPAR-DELTA — fatty acid oxidation.*** These are **nuclear receptors that alter gene transcription**, which is why their effects take weeks and why they have broad metabolic consequences rather than a single clean action.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Fibrate adverse effects: ***MYOPATHY (greatly increased with statins — GEMFIBROZIL IS THE WORST OFFENDER; use FENOFIBRATE if combination is needed), GALLSTONES, raised transaminases, and a reversible RISE IN CREATININE.***
- **Which drug for which lipid problem: ***STATIN for high LDL and for cardiovascular risk reduction in general; FIBRATES or high-dose omega-3 (icosapent ethyl) for TRIGLYCERIDES >500 mg/dL to PREVENT PANCREATITIS; ezetimibe/PCSK9 inhibitors as LDL add-ons; niacin is now little used (flushing — reduced by aspirin — hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia, and no outcome benefit).***
- **Statin facts commonly examined: ***THEY SHOULD BE CONTINUED PERIOPERATIVELY AND DURING ACUTE ILLNESS (withdrawal increases events); they slightly RAISE Lp(a) and slightly increase new-onset TYPE 2 DIABETES; and they must NOT be combined with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as VERAPAMIL, clarithromycin or azoles.***
- **BILE ACID SEQUESTRANTS (cholestyramine, colesevelam) are the safest in pregnancy but ***BIND OTHER DRUGS AND FAT-SOLUBLE VITAMINS (separate dosing) and RAISE TRIGLYCERIDES.***
- **Monitoring: baseline lipids, liver enzymes and CK if symptomatic; lifestyle change (diet, exercise, weight, smoking) underlies all pharmacotherapy.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Lipid drug causing gallstones → **fibrate (PPAR-alpha activation)**; false about statins → **that they can be given with verapamil and other enzyme inhibitors**.

**REMEMBER:** *Alpha for triglycerides, gamma for glucose — and fibrates thicken the bile.*

## 44. Dose–Response Curves: Potency, Efficacy and Antagonism

**PEARL:** On a set of dose–response curves, ***AGONISTS are the drugs that reach the FULL maximal response — a curve further LEFT means GREATER POTENCY (less drug needed for the same effect), while a LOWER PLATEAU means LOWER EFFICACY.*** A drug that **never reaches the maximum at any concentration is a PARTIAL AGONIST**, and ***IN THE PRESENCE OF A FULL AGONIST A PARTIAL AGONIST BEHAVES AS A COMPETITIVE ANTAGONIST.***

**UNDERSTAND:** ***POTENCY (position on the x-axis, EC50) AND EFFICACY (height of the plateau, Emax) ARE INDEPENDENT PROPERTIES***, and confusing them is the commonest error. Potency depends largely on **receptor affinity** and is clinically almost irrelevant (it only sets the milligram dose), whereas efficacy — the **intrinsic activity of the drug–receptor complex** — determines the maximum achievable effect and therefore matters enormously. *A more potent drug is not a better drug.*

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Antagonist recognition on a graph: ***COMPETITIVE (reversible) ANTAGONIST — the agonist curve shifts RIGHT (higher EC50) with the SAME maximum (surmountable). NON-COMPETITIVE or IRREVERSIBLE ANTAGONIST — the MAXIMUM IS DEPRESSED (insurmountable).***
- **Types of antagonism: ***PHARMACOLOGICAL (same receptor — naloxone vs morphine), PHYSIOLOGICAL/functional (different receptors, opposite effects — adrenaline vs histamine in anaphylaxis), CHEMICAL (chelation — desferrioxamine and iron, protamine and heparin) and PHARMACOKINETIC (enzyme induction).***
- **Related definitions: ***INVERSE AGONIST (produces the OPPOSITE effect by reducing constitutive receptor activity), PARTIAL AGONIST (buprenorphine, pindolol, aripiprazole), THERAPEUTIC INDEX = TD50/ED50 (or LD50/ED50 — the higher the safer), and the CEILING EFFECT of partial agonists.***
- **The quantal dose–response curve gives ED50, TD50 and LD50 and describes the population; the graded curve describes the individual.**
- **Clinical illustration: ***FUROSEMIDE has LOWER POTENCY but HIGHER EFFICACY than a thiazide*** — no dose of a thiazide will achieve the diuresis a loop diuretic can. That is the entire practical meaning of the distinction.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** From the dose–response curves → **drugs A and B are agonists** (equal efficacy at different potencies; the drug that never reaches maximum is a partial agonist).

**REMEMBER:** *Left–right is potency, up–down is efficacy — and only the second one matters at the bedside.*

## 45. Beta-Blocker Subclasses and Antihypertensive Side Effects

**PEARL:** ***PINDOLOL — NON-SELECTIVE with INTRINSIC SYMPATHOMIMETIC ACTIVITY (partial agonist). CARVEDILOL — NON-SELECTIVE with alpha-1 blockade and ANTIOXIDANT/anti-inflammatory properties (a heart-failure drug). BETAXOLOL — BETA-1 SELECTIVE with MEMBRANE-STABILISING activity. ACEBUTOLOL — BETA-1 SELECTIVE with INTRINSIC SYMPATHOMIMETIC ACTIVITY.*** Among antihypertensive adverse effects: ***VERAPAMIL — CONSTIPATION (and bradycardia); HYDRALAZINE — DRUG-INDUCED LUPUS (not heart failure — it TREATS heart failure); ALISKIREN — HYPERkalaemia (not hypo).***

**UNDERSTAND:** **INTRINSIC SYMPATHOMIMETIC ACTIVITY means the blocker is itself a weak partial agonist**, so it blocks catecholamines during exercise but provides mild stimulation at rest — **less bradycardia, less lipid derangement and less cold-extremity discomfort.** But it also means ***ISA BETA-BLOCKERS ARE NOT USED POST-MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION OR IN HEART FAILURE***, where the entire benefit derives from sustained blockade of sympathetic drive. Knowing what ISA does explains both its niche and its prohibition.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***THE FOUR BETA-BLOCKERS WITH MORTALITY BENEFIT IN HEART FAILURE ARE BISOPROLOL, CARVEDILOL, METOPROLOL SUCCINATE (extended-release) AND NEBIVOLOL*** (the last also releasing nitric oxide) — start low, go slow, and never during acute decompensation.
- **Beta-blocker cautions: ***ASTHMA (non-selective agents), severe peripheral vascular disease, second/third-degree heart block, and MASKING OF HYPOGLYCAEMIA IN DIABETICS (sweating is preserved). NEVER STOP ABRUPTLY — REBOUND TACHYCARDIA, HYPERTENSION AND ANGINA/INFARCTION.*** ***IN PHAEOCHROMOCYTOMA AND COCAINE TOXICITY, NEVER BETA-BLOCK BEFORE ALPHA-BLOCKING*** (unopposed alpha stimulation).
- **Other antihypertensive signatures: ***ACE INHIBITORS — dry COUGH (bradykinin) and ANGIO-OEDEMA, hyperkalaemia, FETAL TOXICITY; ARBs — same without the cough; AMLODIPINE — ANKLE OEDEMA, flushing, gum hypertrophy; THIAZIDES — hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia/gout, hyperglycaemia, hypercalcaemia, erectile dysfunction; SPIRONOLACTONE — hyperkalaemia and GYNAECOMASTIA (use eplerenone); CLONIDINE and methyldopa — sedation and REBOUND HYPERTENSION/positive Coombs test respectively; MINOXIDIL — HIRSUTISM and pericardial effusion.***
- ***DIURETIC-INDUCED HYPOKALAEMIA IS GREATEST WITH ACETAZOLAMIDE AT EQUINATRIURETIC DOSES*** (it delivers a large bicarbonate-rich sodium load distally, maximising K+ secretion), followed by thiazides then loop diuretics; potassium-sparing agents (triamterene, amiloride, spironolactone) cause hyperkalaemia instead.
- **In pregnancy the safe antihypertensives are ***LABETALOL, NIFEDIPINE, METHYLDOPA and hydralazine***; ACE inhibitors, ARBs and aliskiren are contraindicated.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Matching → **pindolol (non-selective + ISA), carvedilol (non-selective + antioxidant), betaxolol (beta-1 + membrane stabilising), acebutolol (beta-1 + ISA)**; correct drug–adverse effect pair → **verapamil–constipation**.

**REMEMBER:** *Intrinsic sympathomimetic activity is a gentler block — and exactly the wrong thing after a heart attack.*

## 46. Disease-Modifying Drugs in Sickle Cell Disease

**PEARL:** **Drugs used in SICKLE CELL DISEASE: ***HYDROXYUREA (raises HbF — the primary disease-modifying drug, reducing crises, acute chest syndrome, transfusions and mortality), VOXELOTOR (an HbS polymerisation inhibitor that stabilises the oxygenated form), L-GLUTAMINE (reduces oxidative stress) and CRIZANLIZUMAB (anti-P-selectin, reducing vaso-occlusive crises).*** ***BEBTELOVIMAB IS A COVID-19 MONOCLONAL ANTIBODY AND HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH SICKLE CELL DISEASE.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Each drug attacks a different step of the same pathway. **Sickling requires DEOXYGENATED HbS to POLYMERISE; polymerised cells become rigid, adhere to endothelium via P-SELECTIN and occlude microvessels.** So: ***HYDROXYUREA dilutes HbS with HbF (which cannot enter the polymer); VOXELOTOR holds haemoglobin in the oxygenated, non-polymerising state; CRIZANLIZUMAB blocks the adhesion step; L-GLUTAMINE reduces the oxidative injury that stiffens membranes.*** Understanding the cascade makes the drug list self-explanatory.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Hydroxyurea also inhibits ***RIBONUCLEOTIDE REDUCTASE*** (hence its use in CML, polycythaemia vera and essential thrombocythaemia); its main toxicity is ***MYELOSUPPRESSION (monitor counts)***, plus leg ulcers, hyperpigmentation and possible teratogenicity.
- **Crisis management: ***HYDRATION, ANALGESIA (opioids — undertreatment of sickle pain is a well-documented failure of care), oxygen if hypoxic, WARMTH, and treatment of the precipitant (infection, dehydration, cold, hypoxia, acidosis).*** ***ACUTE CHEST SYNDROME (fever, chest pain, hypoxia, new infiltrate) NEEDS ANTIBIOTICS, INCENTIVE SPIROMETRY AND URGENT EXCHANGE TRANSFUSION*** — it is the leading cause of death.
- **Preventive care: ***PENICILLIN PROPHYLAXIS from infancy and pneumococcal/Hib/meningococcal vaccination (functional ASPLENIA from autosplenectomy), FOLIC ACID, TRANSCRANIAL DOPPLER SCREENING IN CHILDREN with chronic transfusion to prevent STROKE, and retinal, renal and pulmonary surveillance.***
- **Complications to recognise: ***vaso-occlusive/painful crisis, aplastic crisis (PARVOVIRUS B19), splenic sequestration, hyperhaemolytic crisis, dactylitis in infants, AVASCULAR NECROSIS of the femoral head, priapism, leg ulcers, gallstones (pigment), stroke and renal papillary necrosis with hyposthenuria.***
- ***ALLOGENEIC STEM CELL TRANSPLANTATION IS CURATIVE, AND GENE THERAPY/GENE EDITING (exagamglogene, lovotibeglogene) IS NOW APPROVED*** — the newest exam-worthy development.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug NOT used in sickle cell disease → **bebtelovimab**.

**REMEMBER:** *Dilute the HbS, keep it oxygenated, stop it sticking — three drugs, three steps of one polymer.*

## 47. Dose-Dependent Effects of Dopamine

**PEARL:** ***DOPAMINE'S RECEPTOR ACTION CHANGES WITH DOSE: <2 µg/kg/min — DOPAMINERGIC (D1/D2), dilating renal and mesenteric vessels; 2–10 µg/kg/min — BETA-1, increasing contractility and heart rate; >10 µg/kg/min — ALPHA-1, causing vasoconstriction and a rise in blood pressure.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The sequence reflects **descending receptor affinity: D1 receptors are the most sensitive, beta-1 next, alpha-1 least.** So as the infusion rises, each receptor class is recruited in turn — and the clinical effect changes character entirely. This also warns against the fallacy of "renal-dose dopamine": ***LOW-DOSE DOPAMINE INCREASES URINE OUTPUT WITHOUT IMPROVING RENAL OUTCOMES OR SURVIVAL, AND IS NO LONGER RECOMMENDED FOR RENAL PROTECTION.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***NORADRENALINE IS THE FIRST-LINE VASOPRESSOR IN SEPTIC SHOCK*** (dopamine causes more tachyarrhythmias and higher mortality in some subgroups); ***DOBUTAMINE (a beta-1 agonist) is the inotrope for cardiogenic shock and low-output states***; ***ADRENALINE for anaphylaxis and cardiac arrest***; ***VASOPRESSIN as an adjunct to reduce noradrenaline requirement***; ***PHENYLEPHRINE (pure alpha-1) where tachycardia must be avoided.***
- **Adverse effects of dopamine: tachyarrhythmias, ***SEVERE HYPERTENSION IF GIVEN WITH A NON-SELECTIVE BETA-BLOCKER (unopposed alpha)***, and TISSUE NECROSIS ON EXTRAVASATION — treated with local ***PHENTOLAMINE*** infiltration. Give vasopressors through a central line where possible.
- **Inotrope mechanisms worth pairing: ***DOBUTAMINE and dopamine act via beta-1/cAMP; MILRINONE is a PHOSPHODIESTERASE-3 INHIBITOR ("inodilator" — raises cAMP without a receptor, so it works even with beta-blockade, but causes hypotension); LEVOSIMENDAN is a CALCIUM SENSITISER; DIGOXIN inhibits the Na+/K+-ATPase.***
- **In cardiogenic shock, ***AN INOTROPE RAISES CARDIAC OUTPUT AT THE COST OF MYOCARDIAL OXYGEN DEMAND AND ARRHYTHMIA*** — hence use the lowest dose for the shortest time and address the cause (revascularisation, mechanical support).
- **Fenoldopam is a selective D1 agonist used in hypertensive emergency with renal impairment.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Dose at which dopamine acts on beta-1 receptors → **2–10 µg/kg/min**.

**REMEMBER:** *Dopamine climbs the receptor ladder — dopaminergic, then beta, then alpha.*

## 48. Glucocorticoid Replacement and Equivalence

**PEARL:** **The physiological replacement dose in chronic adrenal insufficiency is ***HYDROCORTISONE 15–25 mg/day (typically 20 mg), given in 2–3 DIVIDED DOSES with HALF OR MORE IN THE MORNING*** to mimic the circadian rhythm — plus ***FLUDROCORTISONE 50–200 µg/day in PRIMARY (Addison) disease***, which is NOT needed in secondary (pituitary) insufficiency.

**UNDERSTAND:** The dosing schedule is dictated by **cortisol's natural circadian pattern — a peak at waking and a trough at midnight.** Reversing that (large evening doses) causes insomnia and metabolic harm, while a single daily dose leaves the patient fatigued by evening. And the ***MINERALOCORTICOID DISTINCTION IS PURELY ANATOMICAL***: aldosterone is driven by the renin–angiotensin system and the adrenal cortex itself, so it fails only when the ADRENAL is destroyed — pituitary disease leaves it intact.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***"SICK DAY RULES": DOUBLE THE GLUCOCORTICOID DOSE DURING FEVER OR INTERCURRENT ILLNESS, USE PARENTERAL HYDROCORTISONE FOR VOMITING, AND GIVE STRESS DOSES FOR SURGERY.*** Every patient needs a ***STEROID EMERGENCY CARD, A MEDIC-ALERT BRACELET AND AN EMERGENCY HYDROCORTISONE INJECTION KIT.***
- ***ADRENAL CRISIS: hypotension/shock, vomiting, abdominal pain, HYPONATRAEMIA, HYPERKALAEMIA, HYPOGLYCAEMIA and eosinophilia — TREAT IMMEDIATELY WITH IV HYDROCORTISONE 100 mg AND FLUID RESUSCITATION WITH NORMAL SALINE; DO NOT WAIT FOR THE CORTISOL RESULT*** (take the sample, then treat). Dexamethasone can be used if a short synacthen test is planned, as it does not cross-react in the cortisol assay.
- **Glucocorticoid equivalence: ***HYDROCORTISONE 20 mg = PREDNISOLONE 5 mg = METHYLPREDNISOLONE 4 mg = DEXAMETHASONE 0.75 mg = betamethasone 0.6 mg.*** Relative mineralocorticoid activity: ***FLUDROCORTISONE >> hydrocortisone > prednisolone > methylprednisolone; DEXAMETHASONE AND BETAMETHASONE HAVE NONE*** — which is why dexamethasone is used for cerebral oedema and betamethasone for fetal lung maturity, where sodium retention is undesirable.
- **Adverse effects of pharmacological doses: Cushingoid habitus, hyperglycaemia, hypertension, ***OSTEOPOROSIS (give calcium, vitamin D and consider a bisphosphonate if ≥3 months of ≥7.5 mg prednisolone)***, AVASCULAR NECROSIS, myopathy, cataract and glaucoma, peptic ulceration (with NSAIDs), infection and reactivation of TB, poor wound healing, psychosis and growth suppression in children.
- ***NEVER STOP LONG-TERM STEROIDS ABRUPTLY — TAPER***; the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis can take months to recover.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Replacement dose of hydrocortisone in chronic adrenal insufficiency → **20 mg/day**.

**REMEMBER:** *Twenty milligrams, mostly in the morning — and double it the day the patient falls ill.*

## 49. Insulin Preparations

**PEARL:** ***LENTE INSULIN = 70% CRYSTALLINE (Ultralente, long-acting) + 30% AMORPHOUS (Semilente, short-acting) — a 7:3 mixture giving an INTERMEDIATE-acting insulin zinc suspension.*** It is ***NOT the same as NPH (isophane)***, which is insulin complexed with PROTAMINE rather than excess zinc.

**UNDERSTAND:** Every insulin formulation manipulates one variable — ***HOW FAST THE HEXAMER DISSOCIATES INTO ABSORBABLE MONOMERS.*** Zinc and protamine slow that dissociation (hence intermediate/long action and a cloudy suspension), while the ***RAPID-ACTING ANALOGUES (LISPRO, ASPART, GLULISINE) carry amino-acid substitutions that PREVENT self-association***, so they are absorbed almost immediately. ***GLARGINE precipitates at neutral tissue pH*** for slow release, and ***DETEMIR and DEGLUDEC bind albumin.*** One principle explains the entire chart.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Onset/peak/duration: ***RAPID analogues — 5–15 min / 1 h / 3–5 h (given AT the meal). REGULAR (soluble) — 30 min / 2–3 h / 6–8 h (given 30 MIN BEFORE the meal; the ONLY insulin that can be given INTRAVENOUSLY, and hence the one used in DKA). NPH — 2 h / 6–10 h / 12–18 h. GLARGINE/DETEMIR — peakless, ~24 h. DEGLUDEC — >40 h.***
- **Regimens: ***BASAL-BOLUS (basal analogue plus rapid analogue with meals) is the physiological standard; PREMIXED 30/70 twice daily is cheaper and commonly used in India; INSULIN PUMPS and closed-loop systems for selected patients.***
- ***HYPOGLYCAEMIA IS THE LIMITING TOXICITY*** — teach recognition, the "rule of 15", glucagon for severe episodes, and beware ***HYPOGLYCAEMIA UNAWARENESS*** in long-standing diabetes and with beta-blockers. Also: ***LIPOHYPERTROPHY at repeatedly used injection sites causes erratic absorption — ROTATE SITES***; weight gain; and insulin oedema.
- **Absorption is fastest from the ***ABDOMEN > arm > thigh > buttock***, and is accelerated by exercise, heat and massage.
- ***SOMOGYI PHENOMENON (nocturnal hypoglycaemia with rebound morning hyperglycaemia — reduce the evening dose) versus the DAWN PHENOMENON (early-morning growth hormone/cortisol surge — increase or shift the evening basal insulin)*** — a classic examined pair.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Composition of Lente insulin → **70% crystalline, 30% amorphous**.

**REMEMBER:** *Everything in insulin design is about how quickly the hexamer falls apart.*

## 50. Local Anaesthetics and Methaemoglobinaemia

**PEARL:** ***PRILOCAINE (and BENZOCAINE) CAUSE METHAEMOGLOBINAEMIA*** — prilocaine is metabolised to ***o-TOLUIDINE***, an oxidising agent that converts haemoglobin iron from Fe2+ to Fe3+; the risk rises above about 8 mg/kg. ***TREATMENT IS INTRAVENOUS METHYLENE BLUE.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Methaemoglobin **cannot bind oxygen and shifts the dissociation curve LEFT**, so tissue delivery fails twice over. The clinical signature is a **"CHOCOLATE-BROWN" BLOOD SAMPLE AND CYANOSIS THAT DOES NOT IMPROVE WITH OXYGEN**, with a ***PULSE OXIMETER STUCK AROUND 85% AND A "SATURATION GAP" (normal PaO2 on blood gas but low SpO2)*** — because methaemoglobin absorbs both oximeter wavelengths equally. ***CO-OXIMETRY IS THE DIAGNOSTIC TEST.*** Methylene blue works by donating electrons via NADPH methaemoglobin reductase — ***AND IS THEREFORE INEFFECTIVE AND HAZARDOUS IN G6PD DEFICIENCY***, where ascorbic acid or exchange transfusion is used instead.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other causes of methaemoglobinaemia: ***nitrates/nitrites (including well water in infants), dapsone, primaquine, chloroquine, metoclopramide, sulfonamides, phenazopyridine and aniline dyes.***
- **Local anaesthetic pharmacology: ***AMIDES (lignocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine, prilocaine — "two i's in the name") are HEPATICALLY metabolised; ESTERS (procaine, cocaine, tetracaine, benzocaine, chloroprocaine) are hydrolysed by PLASMA CHOLINESTERASE and cause more ALLERGY (PABA metabolite).*** They block ***VOLTAGE-GATED SODIUM CHANNELS from the INSIDE, preferentially in rapidly firing ("use-dependent") fibres***, blocking small unmyelinated pain fibres before motor fibres.
- ***THEY WORK POORLY IN INFECTED, ACIDIC TISSUE***, because the ionised fraction rises and less non-ionised drug crosses the membrane — the reason an abscess is drained under a block or general anaesthesia rather than local infiltration.
- ***LOCAL ANAESTHETIC SYSTEMIC TOXICITY: perioral tingling and metallic taste → tinnitus and visual disturbance → tremor and SEIZURES → coma → CARDIOVASCULAR COLLAPSE. BUPIVACAINE IS THE MOST CARDIOTOXIC (refractory ventricular arrhythmias); ROPIVACAINE and levobupivacaine are safer. TREATMENT IS AIRWAY SUPPORT, BENZODIAZEPINES AND INTRAVENOUS 20% LIPID EMULSION ("lipid rescue").***
- **Maximum safe doses to know: ***LIGNOCAINE 3–5 mg/kg (7 mg/kg with adrenaline), BUPIVACAINE 2 mg/kg (3 with adrenaline)***; adrenaline prolongs the block and reduces systemic absorption but is ***avoided in end-arteries (classically fingers, toes, nose, penis) in traditional teaching.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Local anaesthetic causing methaemoglobinaemia → **prilocaine**.

**REMEMBER:** *Chocolate blood, cyanosis unresponsive to oxygen, and a pulse oximeter frozen at 85%.*

## 51. Diuretic Sites of Action and Liddle Syndrome

**PEARL:** **Young-onset HYPERTENSION with HYPOKALAEMIA, METABOLIC ALKALOSIS, ***LOW renin and LOW aldosterone*** and a family history, caused by a ***GAIN-OF-FUNCTION MUTATION IN ENaC = LIDDLE SYNDROME — treated with AMILORIDE (or triamterene), which directly BLOCKS the epithelial sodium channel.*** ***SPIRONOLACTONE DOES NOT WORK*** — the channel is constitutively open and does not need aldosterone.

**UNDERSTAND:** This is the cleanest illustration in pharmacology of ***MATCHING THE DRUG TO THE MOLECULAR LESION.*** Both amiloride and spironolactone are "potassium-sparing diuretics", but they act at different points: **spironolactone blocks the ALDOSTERONE RECEPTOR (upstream), amiloride blocks the CHANNEL ITSELF (downstream).** When the channel is autonomously overactive, only the downstream blocker helps. The same logic distinguishes the causes of hypokalaemic hypertension.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Diuretic sites: ***ACETAZOLAMIDE — proximal tubule (carbonic anhydrase); MANNITOL — osmotic, thin descending limb; LOOP DIURETICS — NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb (the most potent, "high-ceiling"); THIAZIDES — NCC in the distal convoluted tubule; AMILORIDE/TRIAMTERENE — ENaC in the collecting duct; SPIRONOLACTONE/EPLERENONE — mineralocorticoid receptor; VAPTANS — V2 receptor.***
- ***HYPOKALAEMIA AT EQUINATRIURETIC DOSES IS GREATEST WITH ACETAZOLAMIDE***, then thiazides, then loop diuretics — because acetazolamide delivers a large, poorly reabsorbable bicarbonate-rich sodium load to the collecting duct, maximising potassium secretion.
- **Electrolyte signatures: ***LOOP — hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia (used in hypercalcaemia), hypomagnesaemia, metabolic alkalosis, ototoxicity. THIAZIDE — hypokalaemia, HYPOnatraemia, HYPERcalcaemia (used in calcium stones and nephrogenic DI), hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperlipidaemia. ACETAZOLAMIDE — hyperchloraemic METABOLIC ACIDOSIS. POTASSIUM-SPARING — HYPERkalaemic metabolic acidosis and (spironolactone) gynaecomastia.***
- **The genetic mimics worth pairing: ***BARTTER SYNDROME resembles a LOOP DIURETIC (NKCC2 defect — hypokalaemic alkalosis, NORMOTENSIVE, hypercalciuria); GITELMAN SYNDROME resembles a THIAZIDE (NCC defect — hypokalaemic alkalosis, hypomagnesaemia, HYPOcalciuria); LIDDLE resembles ALDOSTERONE EXCESS but with low aldosterone.***
- **In HYPERTENSION with hypokalaemia, check renin and aldosterone: ***BOTH HIGH — renovascular disease or a renin-secreting tumour; ALDOSTERONE HIGH, RENIN LOW — PRIMARY HYPERALDOSTERONISM (Conn); BOTH LOW — Liddle syndrome, apparent mineralocorticoid excess (liquorice), CAH (11-beta or 17-alpha) or exogenous steroid.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** ENaC mutation with hypertension and hypokalaemic alkalosis → **amiloride**.

**REMEMBER:** *Block the receptor or block the channel — when the channel is stuck open, only the channel blocker works.*

## 52. Levodopa Interactions

**PEARL:** ***PYRIDOXINE (VITAMIN B6) ABOLISHES THE EFFECT OF PLAIN LEVODOPA*** by acting as a cofactor for DOPA DECARBOXYLASE, accelerating its **PERIPHERAL conversion to dopamine so that less crosses the blood–brain barrier.** (This does NOT matter when levodopa is combined with ***CARBIDOPA or BENSERAZIDE***, which block that enzyme peripherally — which is why all modern preparations are combinations.)

**UNDERSTAND:** Only about **1–3% of plain levodopa reaches the brain**; the rest is decarboxylated in the gut wall, liver and circulation, producing peripheral dopamine — hence **nausea, vomiting, postural hypotension and arrhythmias, and no benefit.** ***CARBIDOPA CANNOT CROSS THE BLOOD–BRAIN BARRIER***, so it blocks the peripheral enzyme only, raising brain delivery about tenfold, cutting the required dose by ~75% and abolishing most peripheral side effects. Once carbidopa is present, pyridoxine is irrelevant.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other levodopa interactions: ***PHENOTHIAZINES, HALOPERIDOL and METOCLOPRAMIDE block dopamine receptors and reverse its effect (use DOMPERIDONE, which does not cross the barrier, for nausea); NON-SELECTIVE MAO INHIBITORS cause a HYPERTENSIVE CRISIS; antihypertensives worsen postural hypotension; ANTICHOLINERGICS delay gastric emptying and can reduce absorption; and a HIGH-PROTEIN MEAL COMPETES FOR THE INTESTINAL AND BLOOD–BRAIN LARGE NEUTRAL AMINO ACID TRANSPORTER — so take levodopa 30–60 minutes before food.***
- **Long-term levodopa problems: ***"WEARING-OFF" AND ON–OFF FLUCTUATIONS, PEAK-DOSE DYSKINESIAS, and dose failures*** — managed with more frequent dosing, COMT inhibitors (entacapone, opicapone), MAO-B inhibitors (selegiline, rasagiline), dopamine agonists, amantadine (for dyskinesia), apomorphine rescue, intestinal levodopa gel or deep brain stimulation.
- **Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) cause ***IMPULSE-CONTROL DISORDERS (gambling, hypersexuality, compulsive shopping), sleep attacks, hallucinations and ankle oedema*** — ask about these explicitly at every visit.
- ***NEVER STOP DOPAMINERGIC DRUGS ABRUPTLY*** — risk of a neuroleptic-malignant-like syndrome (parkinsonism–hyperpyrexia).
- **Drug-induced parkinsonism (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, flunarizine) is a common and reversible mimic — always review the drug chart before diagnosing Parkinson disease.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug NOT to be given with levodopa → **pyridoxine**.

**REMEMBER:** *B6 helps the enzyme you are trying to block — unless carbidopa has already blocked it.*

## 53. Antibiotic Pharmacodynamics

**PEARL:** ***TIME-DEPENDENT KILLING — the effect depends on the TIME the concentration stays above the MIC (T>MIC): BETA-LACTAMS (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems), MACROLIDES, clindamycin, linezolid and VANCOMYCIN. Give them FREQUENTLY or by EXTENDED/CONTINUOUS INFUSION.*** ***CONCENTRATION-DEPENDENT KILLING — the effect depends on the PEAK:MIC ratio: AMINOGLYCOSIDES and FLUOROQUINOLONES (AUC:MIC). Give them as LARGE, INFREQUENT DOSES.***

**UNDERSTAND:** This single distinction explains the two great dosing conventions of antimicrobial therapy. ***ONCE-DAILY AMINOGLYCOSIDE DOSING is not a convenience — it is pharmacologically superior***: it maximises the peak (efficacy), exploits the ***POST-ANTIBIOTIC EFFECT*** (continued suppression after levels fall), and allows a drug-free trough that reduces the ***SATURABLE UPTAKE into renal tubular and cochlear cells that causes nephro- and ototoxicity.*** Conversely, splitting a beta-lactam dose into more frequent administrations increases the time above MIC and improves outcomes.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***MONITOR AMINOGLYCOSIDE TROUGHS (for toxicity) AND VANCOMYCIN AUC/TROUGH (for both efficacy and nephrotoxicity).***
- **Bactericidal versus bacteriostatic: ***CIDAL — beta-lactams, aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, vancomycin, metronidazole, rifampicin, isoniazid, daptomycin. STATIC — tetracyclines, macrolides (mostly), clindamycin, sulfonamides, chloramphenicol, linezolid, ethambutol.*** ***BACTERICIDAL AGENTS ARE PREFERRED IN ENDOCARDITIS, MENINGITIS, OSTEOMYELITIS AND NEUTROPENIA*** — sites and hosts where the immune system cannot finish the job.
- ***HETERORESISTANCE*** — a subpopulation is resistant while the population as a whole tests SUSCEPTIBLE, because the resistant subset lies below the assay detection limit; described in ***STAPHYLOCOCCI, *M. TUBERCULOSIS*, *Candida albicans* and *Cryptococcus* (fluconazole)*** and a recognised cause of unexplained treatment failure. The viral equivalent is the resistant ***QUASI-SPECIES*** that defeats antiretroviral therapy.
- **Other resistance concepts: ***INTRINSIC vs ACQUIRED, CROSS-RESISTANCE (within a class), MULTIDRUG resistance, TOLERANCE (killed slowly but still inhibited), PERSISTERS (metabolically dormant survivors — the basis of relapse), and BIOFILM-associated resistance on prosthetic material.***
- **Malaria chemoprophylaxis (a directly asked application): ***travel <6 WEEKS — DOXYCYCLINE 100 mg daily, started 2 DAYS before and continued 4 WEEKS after; travel >6 WEEKS — MEFLOQUINE 250 mg weekly, started 2 WEEKS before and continued 4 weeks after*** (mefloquine is avoided in psychiatric illness and epilepsy). ***PRIMAQUINE/tafenoquine is needed for radical cure of the liver hypnozoites of *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* — check G6PD first.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Graph showing time-dependent killing → **penicillin**; subset resistance undetected on testing → **heteroresistance**; prophylaxis for a 5-week trip to a malaria-endemic area → **doxycycline**.

**REMEMBER:** *Beta-lactams need TIME above the MIC; aminoglycosides need a big PEAK and a clean trough.*

## 54. Receptor Antagonists Worth Naming

**PEARL:** ***ICATIBANT IS A SELECTIVE BRADYKININ B2 RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST***, licensed for acute attacks of ***HEREDITARY ANGIOEDEMA.*** For contrast: **RIMONABANT — cannabinoid CB1 (withdrawn for depression and suicidality); MOEXIPRIL — an ACE inhibitor; EPROSARTAN — an angiotensin receptor blocker.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Hereditary angioedema is a ***BRADYKININ-mediated, NOT histamine-mediated, swelling*** — caused by C1-ESTERASE INHIBITOR deficiency, which unleashes the contact/kallikrein system and floods the tissue with bradykinin. That single fact explains the most important clinical rule in the condition: ***ADRENALINE, ANTIHISTAMINES AND CORTICOSTEROIDS DO NOT WORK*** (though adrenaline is still given if the diagnosis is uncertain and the airway is threatened). Treatment must target bradykinin: ***C1-INHIBITOR CONCENTRATE, ICATIBANT (B2 blocker) or ECALLANTIDE (kallikrein inhibitor)***, with ***LANADELUMAB and berotralstat for prophylaxis.***
It also explains ***ACE-INHIBITOR ANGIO-OEDEMA***, which is bradykinin-mediated (ACE degrades bradykinin) — hence its resistance to antihistamines, its unpredictable timing (often years after starting the drug) and the absolute need to stop the drug permanently and avoid the whole class.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Hereditary angioedema features: ***RECURRENT, NON-PITTING, NON-PRURITIC, NON-URTICARIAL swelling of face, limbs, larynx and GUT (causing severe abdominal pain and vomiting that mimics an acute abdomen)***; attacks are triggered by trauma, dental work, infection, oestrogens and ACE inhibitors. ***LOW C4 IS THE BEST SCREENING TEST*** (low between attacks as well), with C1-INH level and function confirming the type.
- ***SHORT-TERM PROPHYLAXIS BEFORE SURGERY OR DENTAL PROCEDURES*** is essential; danazol/tranexamic acid were the older long-term options.
- **A useful contrast: ***HISTAMINE-mediated angioedema (allergic) comes with URTICARIA AND ITCH and responds to adrenaline, antihistamines and steroids; BRADYKININ-mediated angioedema does not itch, has no weals and does not respond.***
- **Bradykinin also explains the ***ACE-INHIBITOR COUGH*** (accumulation in the airway) and part of the hypotensive effect of ACE inhibitors — icatibant blunts captopril's hypotensive action, the experimental proof of that mechanism.
- **Other "-bant/-mab/-tide" antagonists sometimes asked: ***APREPITANT — NK1 (substance P) for chemotherapy-induced vomiting; BOSENTAN — endothelin receptor; TOLVAPTAN — V2; MIFEPRISTONE — progesterone/glucocorticoid.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Bradykinin receptor antagonist → **icatibant**.

**REMEMBER:** *If the swelling doesn't itch and adrenaline doesn't help, it is bradykinin.*

## 55. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

**PEARL:** ***LIRAGLUTIDE ACTS BY STIMULATING GLUCOSE-DEPENDENT INSULIN RELEASE*** (a GLP-1 receptor agonist — an INCRETIN MIMETIC, ***NOT an amylin analogue***, which is pramlintide). It is ***INJECTED, NOT ORALLY EFFECTIVE (except the oral semaglutide formulation with an absorption enhancer)***, and is used in ***TYPE 2 DIABETES AND OBESITY ONLY — NOT in type 1 diabetes***, since it requires functioning beta cells.

**UNDERSTAND:** The whole class exploits the ***INCRETIN EFFECT*** — oral glucose releases far more insulin than the same glucose given intravenously, because gut L-cells release GLP-1. Because the insulin release is ***GLUCOSE-DEPENDENT, GLP-1 AGONISTS ALONE DO NOT CAUSE HYPOGLYCAEMIA.*** They additionally **suppress glucagon, SLOW GASTRIC EMPTYING and act centrally to reduce appetite** — the last two producing both the weight loss and the nausea that limit the dose.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Adverse effects: ***NAUSEA, VOMITING AND DIARRHOEA (dose-related, improve with slow titration), pancreatitis (rare), gallstones with rapid weight loss, injection-site reactions, and a rodent-derived warning for MEDULLARY THYROID CARCINOMA — CONTRAINDICATED IN PERSONAL/FAMILY HISTORY OF MEDULLARY THYROID CANCER OR MEN2.*** ***DELAYED GASTRIC EMPTYING RAISES ASPIRATION RISK AT ANAESTHESIA — hold before elective surgery.***
- **Members: ***exenatide, LIRAGLUTIDE, dulaglutide, SEMAGLUTIDE (weekly; oral form available; the most effective for weight loss) and TIRZEPATIDE (a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist).***
- ***CARDIOVASCULAR AND RENAL OUTCOME BENEFIT (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide) MAKES THEM A PREFERRED ADD-ON IN TYPE 2 DIABETES WITH ATHEROSCLEROTIC DISEASE OR OBESITY***, alongside SGLT2 inhibitors (which are preferred where heart failure or CKD dominates).
- **Contrast the DPP-4 INHIBITORS, which raise endogenous incretin levels — weight-neutral, weaker, oral; ***SAXAGLIPTIN uniquely requires DOSE REDUCTION WITH STRONG CYP3A4 INHIBITORS such as KETOCONAZOLE***, since it alone is extensively CYP3A4-metabolised, while LINAGLIPTIN needs no adjustment in renal impairment.
- **PRAMLINTIDE (an amylin analogue) is given with insulin in type 1 and type 2 diabetes, slows gastric emptying and suppresses glucagon — ***it CAN cause severe hypoglycaemia with insulin.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True about liraglutide → **it acts by releasing insulin** (it is not an amylin analogue, not oral, and not for type 1 diabetes); DPP-4 inhibitor needing dose reduction with ketoconazole → **saxagliptin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Glucose-dependent insulin release — that is why it works without causing hypoglycaemia.*

## 56. Antiarrhythmic Choices in Toxicity and Transporters

**PEARL:** ***LIGNOCAINE IS THE DRUG OF CHOICE FOR DIGOXIN-INDUCED VENTRICULAR TACHYARRHYTHMIAS*** (phenytoin is the alternative) — ***QUINIDINE AND AMIODARONE ARE CONTRAINDICATED because they RAISE digoxin levels further.*** ***DOFETILIDE is a CLASS III (potassium channel blocker) antiarrhythmic.*** And ***CICLOSPORIN CAUSES CHOLESTASIS BY INHIBITING THE MDR1 (P-glycoprotein) CANALICULAR EFFLUX TRANSPORTER.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Lignocaine is ideal in digoxin toxicity because it is a ***CLASS Ib AGENT: it SHORTENS the action potential, SUPPRESSES the abnormal automaticity and delayed afterdepolarisations that digoxin generates, and — critically — HAS LITTLE EFFECT ON AV NODAL CONDUCTION***, which digoxin has already depressed. A class Ia or III drug would further slow conduction and prolong repolarisation in a heart already primed for block and torsades.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Digoxin toxicity management recap: ***correct potassium and magnesium, atropine for bradyarrhythmia, LIGNOCAINE or PHENYTOIN for ventricular arrhythmia, DIGOXIN-SPECIFIC FAB FRAGMENTS for life-threatening toxicity — and AVOID CALCIUM AND, WHERE POSSIBLE, CARDIOVERSION.***
- **Hepatobiliary transporters worth naming: ***MDR1/P-glycoprotein (drugs), MRP2 (conjugated bilirubin — defective in DUBIN–JOHNSON SYNDROME), BSEP (bile salts — defective in PFIC2), MDR3 (phospholipids — PFIC3) and OATP1B1/1B3 (hepatic UPTAKE of statins).*** ***DRUG-INDUCED CHOLESTASIS GENERALLY REFLECTS INHIBITION OF THESE CANALICULAR PUMPS*** — the mechanism for ciclosporin, oestrogens, rifampicin and bosentan.
- **Ciclosporin's other toxicities: ***NEPHROTOXICITY (dose-limiting), hypertension, hyperkalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hyperuricaemia and gout, tremor, GUM HYPERTROPHY, HIRSUTISM*** — and a long list of CYP3A4 interactions.
- **Class III agents (amiodarone, sotalol, DOFETILIDE, ibutilide, dronedarone) all prolong the QT; ***DOFETILIDE AND SOTALOL MUST BE STARTED IN HOSPITAL WITH ECG MONITORING AND RENAL DOSING***, and sotalol additionally beta-blocks.
- **For most sustained ventricular arrhythmias in a structurally abnormal heart, ***AMIODARONE (or lignocaine in ischaemia) plus correction of electrolytes and ischaemia*** is standard, and an ***IMPLANTABLE DEFIBRILLATOR — not a drug — is what prolongs life.***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Digoxin-induced ventricular tachyarrhythmia → **lignocaine**; dofetilide → **class III**; ciclosporin cholestasis → **MDR1**.

**REMEMBER:** *In digoxin toxicity pick the drug that calms the ventricle without touching the AV node.*

## 57. Inverse Agonists and Receptor Terminology

**PEARL:** ***AN INVERSE AGONIST PRODUCES THE OPPOSITE EFFECT TO AN AGONIST BY SUPPRESSING THE RECEPTOR'S CONSTITUTIVE (basal) ACTIVITY.*** Examples: ***PIMAVANSERIN (5-HT2A inverse agonist, for Parkinson-disease psychosis), BETA-CARBOLINES (benzodiazepine site of GABA-A — anxiogenic and proconvulsant), RIMONABANT (CB1) and many "antihistamines" and beta-blockers.*** ***VARENICLINE IS NOT AN INVERSE AGONIST — IT IS A PARTIAL AGONIST at the alpha-4-beta-2 nicotinic receptor.***

**UNDERSTAND:** Classical theory assumed receptors were silent until occupied. In fact many are ***CONSTITUTIVELY ACTIVE***, so three distinct things can be done to them: **an AGONIST increases activity above baseline, a NEUTRAL ANTAGONIST holds it AT baseline (and blocks both agonists and inverse agonists), and an INVERSE AGONIST pushes it BELOW baseline.** The distinction only becomes visible in systems with high constitutive activity — which is why it was discovered late and why it is examined as a concept rather than a practicality.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Varenicline's partial agonism is exactly why it works for ***SMOKING CESSATION***: it provides enough nicotinic stimulation to blunt craving and withdrawal while ***BLOCKING the reward of a smoked cigarette.*** Watch for vivid dreams, nausea and neuropsychiatric effects; alternatives are ***BUPROPION and nicotine replacement therapy.***
- **Other partial agonists in clinical use: ***BUPRENORPHINE (mu), ARIPIPRAZOLE (D2 — a "dopamine system stabiliser"), PINDOLOL and acebutolol (beta), BUSPIRONE (5-HT1A).***
- **Terminology to keep straight: ***AFFINITY (how tightly a drug binds) versus INTRINSIC ACTIVITY/EFFICACY (what it does once bound); an ANTAGONIST has affinity but ZERO intrinsic activity; SPARE RECEPTORS explain how a maximal response can occur with less than full occupancy; TACHYPHYLAXIS is rapid tolerance from receptor desensitisation or transmitter depletion; DOWN-REGULATION and UP-REGULATION explain rebound phenomena after stopping a chronic antagonist (e.g. beta-blocker withdrawal).***
- ***RECEPTOR SUPERSENSITIVITY after chronic dopamine blockade is the leading explanation for TARDIVE DYSKINESIA*** — a direct clinical consequence of up-regulation.
- **Receptor families: ***LIGAND-GATED ION CHANNELS (nicotinic, GABA-A — milliseconds), GPCRs (muscarinic, adrenergic — seconds), ENZYME-LINKED (insulin, growth factor receptors — minutes) and NUCLEAR receptors (steroids, thyroid hormone, PPARs — hours to days).***

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Drug that does NOT act as an inverse agonist → **varenicline (a partial agonist)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Agonist up, antagonist level, inverse agonist below baseline.*

## 58. Local Anaesthetics — Ester vs Amide

**PEARL:** **AMIDES have two "i"s in the name** — l**i**docaine, bup**i**vacaine, pr**i**locaine, rop**i**vacaine, mep**i**vacaine — and are metabolised in the **LIVER**. **ESTERS have one "i"** — cocaine, procaine, chloroprocaine, tetracaine, benzocaine — and are hydrolysed by **plasma pseudocholinesterase** to **PABA**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Count the "i"s **before** the "-caine": **l-i-docaine** has two, **procaine** has one. That single trick predicts metabolism, duration, allergy risk and drug interactions.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **ALLERGY is essentially an ESTER problem** — the metabolite **PABA** is the allergen. True amide allergy is very rare (usually the **methylparaben preservative** is responsible).
- **Esters are short-acting** (rapid plasma hydrolysis) and are **prolonged in pseudocholinesterase deficiency**; **amides are longer-acting** and **accumulate in hepatic failure and low cardiac output**.
- **Mechanism:** block **voltage-gated sodium channels from INSIDE the cell** — so the drug must cross the membrane in its **unionised** form and then act in the **ionised** form. **Onset depends on pKa** (closer to pH 7.4 = faster); **potency on lipid solubility**; **duration on protein binding**.
- **This is why LAs FAIL IN INFECTED/ACIDIC TISSUE** — the low pH keeps the drug ionised so it cannot cross the membrane. Never inject into an abscess: use a **field or regional block** instead.
- **Fibre blockade order:** **autonomic → pain and temperature → touch → pressure → motor** (small, myelinated fibres first).
- **Bupivacaine is the most CARDIOTOXIC** (avoid in IVRA); **ropivacaine and levobupivacaine are the safer long-acting alternatives**. **Adrenaline 1:200 000** prolongs the block and reduces systemic absorption — **avoid in end-arteries** (traditional teaching for digits, penis, nose, ear).
- **Maximum doses:** lignocaine **3 mg/kg** (**7 mg/kg with adrenaline**); bupivacaine **2 mg/kg** (3 with adrenaline). **LAST is treated with 20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Which is NOT an amino ester?" → **lidocaine**. Also asked as the allergy question.

## 59. Antispasmodics in Irritable Bowel Syndrome

**PEARL:** **MEBEVERINE is a DIRECT smooth muscle relaxant** — it acts on the muscle itself, **independently of autonomic receptors**, by blocking **sodium channels** and indirectly reducing calcium influx. **Dicyclomine and hyoscine/scopolamine are ANTICHOLINERGICS** (M-receptor blockers); **racecadotril is an ANTISECRETORY agent** (enkephalinase inhibitor).

**UNDERSTAND:** Because mebeverine bypasses the muscarinic receptor entirely, it produces **no atropinic side effects** — no dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention or tachycardia. That is its whole clinical advantage over dicyclomine, and it is why it is preferred for long-term use in a chronic, benign condition.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **IBS management is symptom-directed:** **antispasmodics (mebeverine, dicyclomine, hyoscine, peppermint oil) for PAIN**; **loperamide for DIARRHOEA (IBS-D)**; **soluble fibre (ispaghula), polyethylene glycol, or linaclotide/lubiprostone for CONSTIPATION (IBS-C)**; **low-dose TRICYCLICS (amitriptyline) for IBS-D and SSRIs for IBS-C** as neuromodulators; and the **low-FODMAP diet**.
- **Rome IV criteria: recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week for 3 months, related to defaecation and/or a change in stool frequency or form** — a **positive diagnosis**, not one of exclusion.
- **RED FLAGS mandating investigation: age >50 at onset, weight loss, RECTAL BLEEDING, ANAEMIA, nocturnal symptoms waking the patient, fever, a family history of colorectal cancer/IBD/coeliac disease, and a palpable mass.** IBS never causes these.
- **Exclude coeliac disease (tTG antibodies) and, in IBS-D, do a faecal calprotectin** to separate it from inflammatory bowel disease.
- **Rifaximin** (a non-absorbed antibiotic) is used for IBS-D with bloating; **alosetron** (5-HT3 antagonist) is restricted because of **ischaemic colitis**; **tegaserod** was withdrawn for cardiovascular events.
- **Racecadotril** is particularly useful in **acute diarrhoea in children** — it reduces secretion **without affecting motility**, so it does not cause the constipation or ileus risk of loperamide.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Direct spasmolytic action on GI smooth muscle" → **mebeverine** (dicyclomine and scopolamine are receptor-mediated, so they are indirect).

**REMEMBER:** *Mebeverine relaxes the muscle itself — no receptor, no atropine side effects.*

## 60. Organophosphate Poisoning — Atropine and Pralidoxime

**PEARL:** **ATROPINE reverses the MUSCARINIC effects of organophosphate poisoning** (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defaecation, bronchorrhoea, bronchospasm, bradycardia, miosis) — and is **titrated to DRYING OF SECRETIONS AND CLEAR LUNGS, not to pupil size or heart rate.** **PRALIDOXIME (2-PAM) is the specific antidote that REACTIVATES the phosphorylated ACETYLCHOLINESTERASE**, and is the only agent that helps the ***NICOTINIC*** features — fasciculations, muscle weakness and **RESPIRATORY MUSCLE PARALYSIS**, which is what actually kills.

**UNDERSTAND:** Organophosphates **phosphorylate acetylcholinesterase**, so acetylcholine accumulates at every cholinergic synapse. Atropine is a **competitive antagonist at MUSCARINIC receptors only — it cannot touch the NICOTINIC neuromuscular junction**, which is precisely why atropine alone lets a patient die of respiratory muscle paralysis with beautifully dry lungs. Pralidoxime works by **prising the phosphate group off the enzyme — but only BEFORE "AGEING"** (irreversible dealkylation of the enzyme–phosphate bond, occurring within hours to days depending on the compound). ***THIS IS WHY 2-PAM MUST BE GIVEN EARLY.***

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The toxidrome: muscarinic — "DUMBELS" (Diarrhoea, Urination, Miosis, Bronchorrhoea/Bronchospasm/Bradycardia, Emesis, Lacrimation, Salivation) or "SLUDGE"; nicotinic — "MTWtHF" (Mydriasis, Tachycardia, Weakness, Hypertension, Fasciculations); central — confusion, seizures, coma, respiratory depression. A GARLIC-LIKE ODOUR is characteristic.**
- **Diagnosis is clinical, supported by reduced RED CELL (true) acetylcholinesterase — the better marker — or plasma pseudocholinesterase (falls earlier, less specific).**
- ***AIRWAY AND OXYGENATION COME FIRST***, with decontamination (remove clothing, wash the skin — protect staff), atropine boluses DOUBLED every 5 minutes until atropinised, then an infusion; and PRALIDOXIME as a bolus and infusion. **Benzodiazepines for seizures; ***AVOID SUCCINYLCHOLINE*** (prolonged paralysis) and morphine, aminophylline and phenothiazines.
- **Three phases of illness: the ACUTE cholinergic crisis; the INTERMEDIATE SYNDROME at 24–96 hours (proximal, neck flexor and respiratory muscle weakness with cranial nerve palsies — ***a common cause of unexpected respiratory arrest in an apparently improving patient***); and ORGANOPHOSPHATE-INDUCED DELAYED POLYNEUROPATHY at 2–3 weeks (distal sensorimotor, from neuropathy target esterase inhibition).**
- **CARBAMATE poisoning produces the same picture but the enzyme binding is reversible and spontaneously hydrolyses — atropine is given, and pralidoxime is generally unnecessary.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Antidote for the MUSCARINIC effects" → **atropine** (the distractor is pralidoxime, which handles the nicotinic ones).

**REMEMBER:** *Atropine dries the patient; pralidoxime is what lets him breathe.*

## 61. Antihypertensives and the Lipid Profile

**PEARL:** **Alpha-1 blockers (prazosin, doxazosin, terazosin) IMPROVE the lipid profile**
— they lower LDL and triglycerides and raise HDL. **Thiazides and non-selective
beta-blockers worsen it.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Alpha-1 blockade increases **lipoprotein lipase** activity and insulin
sensitivity, so triglyceride-rich particles are cleared faster. Conversely,
**beta-blockade removes the β-mediated stimulation of lipoprotein lipase** (triglycerides
rise, HDL falls) and **thiazides induce insulin resistance**, raising LDL and
triglycerides. So the lipid effect follows directly from what each drug does to lipase
and insulin sensitivity.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Metabolically favourable/neutral:** **alpha-blockers (best)**, **ACE inhibitors and ARBs** (improve insulin sensitivity, reduce new-onset diabetes), **calcium channel blockers** (neutral), **carvedilol and nebivolol** (vasodilating beta-blockers — metabolically much kinder than propranolol/atenolol).
- **Metabolically adverse:** **thiazides** (↑LDL, ↑TG, ↑glucose, ↑uric acid, ↓K⁺, ↓Na⁺), **loop diuretics**, **non-selective beta-blockers** (↑TG, ↓HDL, mask hypoglycaemia).
- **Prazosin — first-dose phenomenon:** severe orthostatic hypotension or syncope 30–90 minutes after the first dose or a dose increase → **start low, give at bedtime**; tolerance develops.
- Uses of alpha-blockers: hypertension, **BPH** (tamsulosin/silodosin are uroselective α1A), Raynaud, and **phenoxybenzamine/phentolamine for phaeochromocytoma** (alpha before beta, always — unopposed alpha causes hypertensive crisis).
- Alpha-blockers are **not first-line for hypertension** (ALLHAT showed more heart failure) — they are used when BPH coexists.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A dyslipidaemic hypertensive is given; pick the drug that helps the
lipids. Prazosin over thiazide/propranolol every time.

**REMEMBER:** *Alpha-blockers are the only antihypertensives that fix the lipids too.*

## 62. Linezolid

**PEARL:** **Linezolid binds the 50S subunit (at the P site of the 23S rRNA) and prevents
formation of the initiation complex** — a uniquely early step, which is why there is **no
cross-resistance** with any other protein-synthesis inhibitor.

**UNDERSTAND:** Most 50S drugs (macrolides, chloramphenicol, clindamycin) act during
**elongation**, after translation has begun. Linezolid blocks the **very first step —
assembly of the 70S initiation complex** — so ribosomal modifications that confer
macrolide resistance do not protect the organism. That single mechanistic difference
explains its role as a reserve drug for **MRSA and VRE**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Spectrum: **Gram-positives only** — **MRSA, VRE, penicillin-resistant pneumococcus**, plus **MDR tuberculosis** (a Group A drug in the current WHO regimens) and **Nocardia**. **Excellent oral bioavailability (~100%)** — oral and IV doses are identical, allowing early switch.
- Adverse effects: **thrombocytopenia is the commonest** (dose- and duration-related, >2 weeks), myelosuppression, **peripheral and optic neuropathy** with prolonged use, **lactic acidosis**, and **serotonin syndrome** — linezolid is a **weak MAO inhibitor**, so beware SSRIs, tramadol, pethidine and tyramine-rich foods.
- Resistance in *S. aureus* to methicillin is via **mecA → PBP2a** (an altered penicillin-binding protein), not beta-lactamase — which is why all beta-lactams fail, but **ceftaroline** (a 5th-generation cephalosporin with PBP2a affinity) works.
- Other MRSA options: vancomycin (check trough/AUC), daptomycin (**inactivated by pulmonary surfactant — never for pneumonia**), teicoplanin, tigecycline, co-trimoxazole and clindamycin for skin infections.
- **Tedizolid** is the newer oxazolidinone — once daily, less myelosuppression.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mechanism matching (50S, initiation complex) and the adverse-effect
question (**thrombocytopenia**, serotonin syndrome).

**REMEMBER:** *Linezolid stops translation before it starts — and drops the platelets.*

## 63. Paroxetine and Antidepressants in Pregnancy

**PEARL:** **Paroxetine is the SSRI associated with fetal cardiac defects** — particularly
**VSD and ASD** — and is the one to avoid in a woman planning pregnancy. **Sertraline** is
the preferred SSRI in pregnancy and lactation.

**UNDERSTAND:** Paroxetine's risk is attributed to its exposure profile: it is the most
**potent, shortest-half-life, most anticholinergic** SSRI, crosses the placenta readily,
and its abrupt fluctuations are thought to disturb the first-trimester period of cardiac
septation. Sertraline is favoured because it has the **lowest milk transfer** and the
largest safety database.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Paroxetine — FDA category **D**; risk of cardiac malformations roughly 1.5–2×; also the **worst SSRI for discontinuation syndrome** (short half-life, no active metabolite) and for weight gain and sexual dysfunction.
- **Fluoxetine** has the longest half-life (norfluoxetine) — least discontinuation syndrome, but greater milk transfer.
- **Third-trimester SSRI exposure:** neonatal adaptation syndrome (jitteriness, irritability, feeding difficulty) and a small risk of **persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn**.
- Untreated maternal depression itself carries real risk (poor attendance, substance use, preterm birth, postpartum depression) — **stopping treatment is not automatically the safe choice**.
- Other classic teratogens for quick recall: **lithium → Ebstein anomaly**; **valproate → neural tube defects, lowest IQ**; **carbamazepine → NTD**; **phenytoin → fetal hydantoin syndrome**; **warfarin → chondrodysplasia punctata, nasal hypoplasia**; **ACE inhibitors/ARBs → renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios**; **isotretinoin → CNS/craniofacial/cardiac**; **thalidomide → phocomelia**; **tetracycline → teeth**; **methotrexate → craniofacial and limb defects**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A pre-conception counselling stem with a prior child with a cardiac
defect and an unnamed antidepressant — the answer is **paroxetine** by elimination.

**REMEMBER:** *Paroxetine perforates the septum; sertraline is the safe one.*

## 64. Antacids and Milk–Alkali Syndrome

**PEARL:** **Calcium carbonate** causes **milk–alkali syndrome** — the triad of
**hypercalcaemia, metabolic alkalosis and renal impairment**.

**UNDERSTAND:** It is a self-reinforcing loop. Absorbed calcium causes **natriuresis and
volume depletion**, which drives **bicarbonate reabsorption** in the proximal tubule →
alkalosis. Alkalosis in turn **increases renal calcium reabsorption** in the distal
tubule, worsening the hypercalcaemia, while hypercalcaemia causes renal vasoconstriction
and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, worsening the volume depletion. Each element feeds the
next — which is why it persists after the calcium is stopped unless the patient is
rehydrated.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Now most often seen in women taking **calcium supplements for osteoporosis**, and in pregnancy — it is the **third commonest cause of hypercalcaemia in hospital**, after hyperparathyroidism and malignancy.
- Treatment: **stop the calcium, rehydrate with IV saline**; loop diuretics only after volume repletion; bisphosphonates risk overshoot hypocalcaemia.
- Antacid side-effect map: **aluminium hydroxide → CONSTIPATION** and hypophosphataemia (binds phosphate); **magnesium hydroxide/trisilicate → DIARRHOEA** and hypermagnesaemia in renal failure; **sodium bicarbonate → systemic alkalosis, gas/belching, sodium load**; **calcium carbonate → constipation, acid rebound, milk–alkali**.
- Combined Al + Mg preparations are used precisely to cancel out the bowel effects.
- Antacids **reduce the absorption of tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, iron, ketoconazole and atazanavir** (raise gastric pH / chelate) — separate doses by 2 hours.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** One-line matching of antacid to its complication. **Milk–alkali =
calcium**; constipation = aluminium; diarrhoea = magnesium.

**REMEMBER:** *"CAlcium: Constipation and Alkalosis. MAgnesium: Must go Away (diarrhoea)."*

## 65. Antidepressant Side Effects Mapped to Receptors

**PEARL:** **H1 blockade → sedation and weight gain. Muscarinic blockade → dry mouth,
constipation, blurred vision, urinary retention. 5-HT reuptake inhibition → sexual
dysfunction, nausea, anorgasmia. ALPHA-1 BLOCKADE → POSTURAL HYPOTENSION — NOT
hypertension.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Every "side effect" of an antidepressant is simply a receptor being blocked
somewhere it was doing useful work. Alpha-1 receptors maintain **arteriolar tone**, so
blocking them **drops** the blood pressure — the same mechanism that makes prazosin an
antihypertensive. The drug that raises blood pressure is **venlafaxine at high dose**, and
that is by **noradrenaline reuptake INHIBITION** (more noradrenaline at the receptor), not
by blocking anything.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **TCAs block everything** — hence sedation (H1), anticholinergic effects (M), postural hypotension (α1) and **cardiotoxicity via sodium-channel blockade** (wide QRS in overdose → **sodium bicarbonate** is the antidote). **Amitriptyline** is the most sedating/anticholinergic; **nortriptyline and desipramine** the least.
- **SSRIs:** nausea, **sexual dysfunction (the commonest cause of non-adherence)**, insomnia or somnolence, **hyponatraemia/SIADH in the elderly**, **GI bleeding** (platelets lose their serotonin), and **discontinuation syndrome** (worst with paroxetine, least with fluoxetine).
- **Mirtazapine** — α2 antagonist plus strong H1 blockade and 5-HT2/5-HT3 antagonism → **sedation, weight gain, but NO sexual dysfunction** and antiemetic; useful in the depressed patient with insomnia and weight loss.
- **Bupropion** — NDRI: **no sexual dysfunction, causes weight loss, helps smoking cessation**, but **lowers the seizure threshold** (avoid in eating disorders and epilepsy).
- **Trazodone** — sedating, **priapism**. **Venlafaxine** — dose-dependent **hypertension**. **MAOIs** — **tyramine "cheese reaction" hypertensive crisis** and serotonin syndrome with SSRIs (**2-week washout; 5 weeks after fluoxetine**).
- **Serotonin syndrome** (hyperthermia, **hyperreflexia and clonus**, agitation, diarrhoea; onset within hours) vs **neuroleptic malignant syndrome** (**lead-pipe rigidity, bradyreflexia**, raised CK, days) — treat with **cyproheptadine** and **dantrolene/bromocriptine** respectively.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A four-way match with one deliberately inverted pair. **Alpha-1
blockade lowers pressure — the "hypertension" option is always the false one.**

**REMEMBER:** *Block alpha-1 and the patient falls over, not up.*

## 66. Akathisia: Propranolol, Not Anticholinergics

**PEARL:** **Akathisia** — an inner restlessness with an irresistible urge to move, pacing
and inability to sit still after starting an antipsychotic — is treated with
**PROPRANOLOL**. **Anticholinergics (benztropine, trihexyphenidyl) do NOT work for
akathisia**, though they are the treatment for dystonia and drug-induced parkinsonism.

**UNDERSTAND:** The extrapyramidal syndromes are not one entity. Acute dystonia and
parkinsonism arise from **nigrostriatal dopamine blockade with relative cholinergic
excess** — which is exactly why restoring the dopamine–acetylcholine balance with an
anticholinergic works. Akathisia is thought to involve **mesocortical dopamine and
noradrenergic pathways** instead, so the cholinergic manipulation is irrelevant and a
**lipophilic, CNS-penetrating beta-blocker** is the effective drug.

**MUST KNOW — extrapyramidal syndromes by time of onset:**

| Syndrome | Onset | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute dystonia** | **Hours–days** | Torticollis, **oculogyric crisis**, trismus, laryngospasm; young men | **IM/IV anticholinergic (benztropine, promethazine) or diphenhydramine** |
| **Akathisia** | **Days–weeks** | Subjective restlessness, pacing; **increased suicide risk** | **PROPRANOLOL**; benzodiazepines/mirtazapine second-line; reduce dose |
| **Parkinsonism** | **Weeks–months** | Bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor | Anticholinergic, amantadine, dose reduction |
| **Tardive dyskinesia** | **Months–years** | Orofacial choreoathetosis, lip-smacking; often irreversible | **Stop/switch to clozapine; VMAT2 inhibitors — valbenazine, tetrabenazine.** ANTICHOLINERGICS WORSEN IT |

- **Akathisia is frequently misdiagnosed as psychotic agitation** — and increasing the antipsychotic then makes it worse. It is a recognised risk factor for **suicide**.
- **High-potency typicals (haloperidol, fluphenazine) cause the most EPS**; **clozapine and quetiapine the least**. SSRIs can also cause akathisia.
- **Clozapine** — reserved for treatment-resistant schizophrenia; **agranulocytosis** (mandatory blood counts), myocarditis, seizures, hypersalivation, weight gain — but the only drug with proven benefit for suicidality.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** All four options treat *some* movement disorder — match the syndrome
to the drug. **Restlessness → propranolol; dystonia → anticholinergic; tardive → VMAT2
inhibitor.**

**REMEMBER:** *Anticholinergics for the stiff and the twisted; propranolol for the restless.*

## 67. Cocaine as a Local Anaesthetic

**PEARL:** Cocaine is unique among local anaesthetics in being an **ESTER** that also
**blocks catecholamine reuptake**, giving it **intrinsic vasoconstriction** — so it
provides anaesthesia **and** a bloodless field, which is why it is still used topically in
**nasal and ENT surgery**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Every other local anaesthetic is a **vasodilator**, which is why adrenaline
is added to them — to slow systemic absorption, prolong the block and reduce bleeding.
Cocaine achieves this itself by preventing reuptake of noradrenaline at the sympathetic
nerve terminal, so its own vasoconstriction is built in. The same mechanism is what makes
it cardiotoxic and addictive.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Esters (one "i" before -caine): coCaIne, procaine, chloroprocaine, tetracaine, benzocaine** — metabolised by **plasma pseudocholinesterase**, produce **PABA** → higher allergy rate. **Amides (two "i"s): lidocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine, prilocaine, mepivacaine** — **hepatic** metabolism, rarely allergenic.
- Cocaine is a **Schedule/controlled drug** requiring prescription and strict custody; it is **never injected** clinically.
- Toxicity: **hypertension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, coronary vasospasm and myocardial infarction, stroke, hyperthermia, seizures, nasal septal perforation**. **Treat cocaine chest pain with benzodiazepines, nitrates and PHENTOLAMINE — beta-blockers are contraindicated (unopposed alpha stimulation).**
- **Maximum safe doses:** lidocaine **3 mg/kg (7 mg/kg with adrenaline)**; bupivacaine **2 mg/kg**; **bupivacaine is the most cardiotoxic** (avoid in Bier's block) — treat **LAST (local anaesthetic systemic toxicity)** with **20% Intralipid**.
- **Prilocaine → methaemoglobinaemia** (treat with **methylene blue**); **benzocaine** likewise.
- Local anaesthetics are **weak bases** — they work poorly in **infected, acidic tissue** because the drug is trapped in the ionised form and cannot cross the membrane. **Small, myelinated, rapidly firing fibres are blocked first**: pain and temperature before touch, then motor.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Which statement is true about cocaine?" — the answer is the
**vasoconstriction**; the false ones call it an amide or say it is weaker than lidocaine.

**REMEMBER:** *Every other local anaesthetic needs adrenaline added — cocaine brings its
own.*

## 68. Anticancer Monoclonals and Checkpoint Inhibitors

**PEARL:** **Nivolumab and pembrolizumab block PD-1**; **atezolizumab/durvalumab block
PD-L1**; **ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4**. They **release the brakes on cytotoxic T cells** —
so their toxicity is **autoimmune (immune-related adverse events)**, not myelosuppressive.

**UNDERSTAND:** Tumours survive by exploiting the **physiological brakes** that stop T cells
attacking self. PD-1 on the T cell binds PD-L1 expressed by the tumour, switching the T
cell off inside the tumour; CTLA-4 acts earlier, in the lymph node, outcompeting CD28 for
B7. Blocking either **restores a pre-existing anti-tumour response** rather than killing
cells directly — which explains both the delayed, sometimes durable responses and the
fact that the side effects look like **new-onset autoimmune disease in any organ**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Immune-related adverse events:** **colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, dermatitis, thyroiditis, hypophysitis, type 1 diabetes, nephritis, myocarditis**. **Treat with high-dose corticosteroids** (and infliximab for steroid-refractory colitis) — the reflex of "steroids for toxicity" is the exam point. CTLA-4 blockade causes more toxicity than PD-1 blockade.
- The other listed monoclonals are all correct statements in their own right — the question is which one matches the **checkpoint** class:
  - **Bevacizumab — anti-VEGF (anti-angiogenic)**: side effects **hypertension, proteinuria, bleeding, impaired wound healing, GI perforation**.
  - **Rituximab — anti-CD20**: B-cell depletion for lymphoma, RA, ITP, pemphigus; **reactivates hepatitis B — screen first**, and causes **PML** rarely.
  - **Trastuzumab — anti-HER2** in breast and gastric cancer; **cardiotoxicity (reversible, unlike anthracyclines)** — monitor LVEF.
- Naming convention: **-mab** monoclonal antibody (**-zumab** humanised, **-ximab** chimeric, **-umab** fully human), **-nib** small-molecule kinase inhibitor, **-cept** receptor fusion protein.
- **Anthracycline (doxorubicin) cardiotoxicity is DOSE-DEPENDENT and IRREVERSIBLE** — a dilated cardiomyopathy from free-radical injury; limit cumulative dose (~450–550 mg/m²), monitor echo, and use **dexrazoxane** as a cardioprotectant. Compare: **bleomycin → pulmonary fibrosis**, **cisplatin → nephro/oto/neurotoxicity**, **cyclophosphamide → haemorrhagic cystitis (mesna)**, **vincristine → peripheral neuropathy**, **methotrexate → mucositis and hepatotoxicity (folinic acid rescue)**, **5-FU → mucositis, hand-foot syndrome**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** All four statements may be individually true; pick the one that
answers the **class named in the stem** (checkpoint inhibitor → **nivolumab, PD-1**).

**REMEMBER:** *Checkpoint inhibitors take the brakes off — expect autoimmunity, treat with
steroids.*

## 69. Paracetamol Poisoning and N-Acetylcysteine

**PEARL:** **NAC crosses the placenta and is SAFE — indeed essential — in pregnancy.** It
works by **replenishing glutathione and directly conjugating NAPQI**, preventing
**HEPATIC** (not renal) necrosis, and is **most effective within 8 hours** of ingestion.

**UNDERSTAND:** Paracetamol is normally cleared by glucuronidation and sulfation; in
overdose these saturate and the excess is shunted through **CYP2E1 to NAPQI**, a highly
reactive electrophile that is detoxified by **glutathione**. Once glutathione stores fall
below ~30%, NAPQI binds hepatocyte proteins and causes **centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis**
— zone 3 because that is where CYP2E1 is concentrated and oxygen tension is lowest. NAC is
simply a **cysteine donor** that regenerates glutathione, so it must arrive **before** the
stores are exhausted.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Treatment threshold: plot the 4-hour (or later) paracetamol level on the Rumack–Matthew nomogram.** Treat empirically without waiting if presentation is late, staggered, or the timing is unknown. **NAC has essentially 100% efficacy within 8 hours** and still benefits patients presenting at 24 hours or in established liver failure.
- **IV NAC causes anaphylactoid (non-IgE, histamine-release) reactions in ~10–15%** — not 30% — managed by slowing the infusion and giving antihistamines; **rarely a reason to stop treatment**. **Oral and IV NAC are equally effective**; IV is preferred with vomiting or hepatic failure.
- **Risk factors for toxicity at lower doses: chronic alcohol use, malnutrition/anorexia, fasting, isoniazid, phenytoin/carbamazepine (CYP inducers)** — all deplete glutathione or induce CYP2E1.
- **King's College criteria for transplant referral:** **arterial pH <7.3 after resuscitation**, OR the triad of **INR >6.5 (PT >100 s) + creatinine >300 µmol/L + grade III–IV encephalopathy**. **Lactate and phosphate** are also prognostic.
- Clinical course: asymptomatic/nausea (0–24 h) → RUQ pain with rising transaminases (24–72 h) → peak hepatic failure, encephalopathy, coagulopathy, AKI (72–96 h) → recovery or death.
- **Activated charcoal** is useful within 1–2 hours of ingestion.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A "which statement is TRUE" set — the true one is **NAC's safety in
pregnancy**; the false ones invert the toxicity (renal for hepatic), inflate the
anaphylactoid rate, or claim oral superiority.

**REMEMBER:** *NAC restores glutathione, protects the liver, and is given in pregnancy
without hesitation.*

## 70. Ethionamide and Antitubercular Hypothyroidism

**PEARL:** **ETHIONAMIDE (and PAS) cause HYPOTHYROIDISM with goitre** in patients on MDR-TB
regimens. Treat with **thyroxine replacement — do NOT change the TB regimen**; thyroid
function recovers within ~2 months of completing therapy.

**UNDERSTAND:** Ethionamide is **structurally a thionamide**, like propylthiouracil and
methimazole. It therefore does exactly what those antithyroid drugs do — **inhibits
thyroid peroxidase and the organification of iodine**. The resulting fall in T4 releases
TSH from feedback, and the raised TSH produces the **goitre**. The effect is a predictable,
reversible pharmacological action rather than toxicity, which is why replacement (not
withdrawal) is the answer.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Ethionamide:** a prodrug activated by **EthA**, inhibiting **InhA** (mycolic acid synthesis) — the same target as isoniazid, hence **cross-resistance with INH in *inhA* mutants**. Other adverse effects: **severe gastrointestinal intolerance (the reason it is second-line), hepatotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy and CNS effects (give pyridoxine), gynaecomastia, alopecia**.
- **Antitubercular adverse-effect map:**
  - **Isoniazid** — **peripheral neuropathy (pyridoxine deficiency), hepatitis, lupus-like syndrome, seizures in overdose (antidote pyridoxine)**.
  - **Rifampicin** — **orange discoloration of urine/tears/lenses**, hepatitis, flu-like syndrome, **potent CYP inducer (contraceptive failure, warfarin, ART interactions)**.
  - **Pyrazinamide** — **hyperuricaemia and arthralgia**, hepatotoxicity (the most hepatotoxic first-line drug).
  - **Ethambutol** — **retrobulbar optic neuritis with loss of red–green colour vision**; check visual acuity and colour vision at baseline.
  - **Streptomycin/amikacin** — **vestibulotoxicity and ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity**; avoid in pregnancy (fetal VIII nerve damage).
  - **Cycloserine** — **psychosis, depression, suicidality, seizures**.
  - **Linezolid** — myelosuppression, optic and peripheral neuropathy. **Bedaquiline** — **QT prolongation**.
  - **PAS** — GI intolerance and **hypothyroidism**.
- **All drug-induced hepatitis in a TB patient**: stop all hepatotoxic drugs, wait for LFTs to normalise, then reintroduce sequentially.

profiles.

## 71. Carbamazepine vs Oxcarbazepine

**PEARL:** **HYPONATRAEMIA is MORE common with OXCARBAZEPINE** than with carbamazepine.
Everything else — **rashes/hypersensitivity, blood dyscrasias, hepatotoxicity, enzyme
induction** — is **more common with CARBAMAZEPINE**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Carbamazepine's characteristic toxicities come from its **10,11-EPOXIDE
metabolite** — the reactive intermediate responsible for the rash, the marrow suppression
and the hepatic injury. **Oxcarbazepine is the 10-keto analogue: it cannot form an
epoxide**, so those toxicities largely disappear. Its **antidiuretic effect on the
collecting duct is a property of the parent molecule**, however, and is if anything
stronger — hence the one adverse effect that goes the other way.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Monitor sodium** in elderly patients and those on diuretics starting either drug; hyponatraemia is usually asymptomatic but can cause confusion, falls and seizures.
- **Carbamazepine:** **agranulocytosis and aplastic anaemia**, **SJS/TEN — screen for HLA-B*1502 in patients of Han Chinese/South-East Asian descent (and HLA-A*3101 for hypersensitivity)**, hepatotoxicity, **raised GGT from enzyme induction**, diplopia and ataxia at high levels, teratogenic (neural tube defects). It **autoinduces its own metabolism**, so levels fall after 2–4 weeks and the dose must be raised.
- **Oxcarbazepine** is a **weaker enzyme inducer** — but still enough to reduce the efficacy of **combined oral contraceptives**.
- Both are **sodium-channel blockers** and are **first-line for FOCAL seizures**; carbamazepine is also the **drug of choice for TRIGEMINAL NEURALGIA**.
- **Both can WORSEN absence and myoclonic seizures** — as can phenytoin, gabapentin and vigabatrin. For generalised epilepsy use **valproate (or lamotrigine/levetiracetam)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are more common with carbamazepine EXCEPT" — the single exception
is **hyponatraemia**. The epoxide logic explains every other item on the list.

**REMEMBER:** *No epoxide, no rash and no marrow trouble — but more sodium loss.*

## 72. Tipranavir and Intracranial Haemorrhage

**PEARL:** **TIPRANAVIR** — a protease inhibitor reserved for **multi-PI-resistant HIV** —
carries a black-box warning for **INTRACRANIAL HAEMORRHAGE and hepatotoxicity**. Avoid it in
**bleeding diatheses such as haemophilia** and in head trauma.

**UNDERSTAND:** Tipranavir inhibits **platelet aggregation (thromboxane A2 pathway)** and
inhibits vitamin K-dependent clotting, so it superimposes an acquired platelet defect on a
pre-existing coagulation factor deficiency. In a haemophiliac — already unable to form a
stable fibrin clot — the additional platelet dysfunction converts a trivial bleed into an
intracranial catastrophe. Hence **new headache, vomiting and altered consciousness in a
haemophiliac on ART is a bleed until proven otherwise.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Antiretroviral classes and mechanisms:** **NRTIs** (chain terminators after phosphorylation — zidovudine, tenofovir, lamivudine, abacavir); **NNRTIs** (allosteric inhibition of reverse transcriptase — efavirenz, nevirapine, rilpivirine, doravirine); **protease inhibitors** (block Gag-Pol cleavage → immature non-infectious virions — atazanavir, darunavir, lopinavir, **ritonavir as a pharmacokinetic booster via CYP3A4 inhibition**); **integrase inhibitors** (dolutegravir, raltegravir, bictegravir); **entry/fusion inhibitors** (maraviroc — CCR5, enfuvirtide — gp41); **capsid inhibitor** (lenacapavir).
- **Class toxicities:** **PIs — dyslipidaemia, insulin resistance, LIPODYSTROPHY, GI upset; atazanavir and indinavir cause INDIRECT hyperbilirubinaemia (UGT1A1 inhibition), indinavir causes renal stones.** **NRTIs — lactic acidosis and mitochondrial toxicity; zidovudine → anaemia/myopathy; tenofovir (TDF) → renal tubular injury and bone loss; ABACAVIR → hypersensitivity, test HLA-B*5701 first.** **NNRTIs — rash/SJS; efavirenz → vivid dreams, neuropsychiatric effects; NEVIRAPINE → hepatitis and rash (risk highest in women with higher CD4 counts).** **Dolutegravir — weight gain, insomnia; small early neural-tube-defect signal now largely refuted.**
- **First-line ART in India/WHO: TLD — TENOFOVIR + LAMIVUDINE + DOLUTEGRAVIR.**
- **Haemophilia A: factor VIII deficiency, X-linked, prolonged aPTT with normal PT and platelet count**; treat with recombinant factor VIII, desmopressin for mild disease, and **emicizumab** prophylaxis.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem pairs a **bleeding disorder** with an antiretroviral — the
drug is the one with the bleeding warning: **tipranavir**.

**REMEMBER:** *Tipranavir thins the platelets — never in a haemophiliac.*

## 73. Tricyclic Antidepressant Overdose

**PEARL:** TCA toxicity produces **arrhythmias (wide QRS, prolonged QT), seizures, coma, and
an ANTICHOLINERGIC syndrome — mydriasis, dry hot skin, urinary retention, confusion and
HYPERTHERMIA. HYPOTHERMIA is NOT a feature.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Everything is explained by two mechanisms. **Quinidine-like sodium-channel
blockade** slows cardiac conduction → **QRS widening, right-axis deviation (terminal R in
aVR), ventricular arrhythmia and hypotension**. **Muscarinic blockade** gives the classic
anticholinergic picture — and because sweating is cholinergic, the patient **cannot
thermoregulate and becomes HYPERthermic**, never hypothermic.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Toxidrome mnemonic: "Hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter" — plus "full as a flask" (urinary retention).**
- **ECG is the key prognostic tool: QRS >100 ms predicts seizures; QRS >160 ms predicts ventricular arrhythmia; a terminal R wave >3 mm in aVR is characteristic.**
- **Treatment: IV SODIUM BICARBONATE boluses** — it works two ways, **raising extracellular sodium to overcome channel blockade** and **alkalinising the plasma to reduce free drug** (target pH 7.45–7.55). Also: airway protection, activated charcoal within 1–2 hours, **benzodiazepines for seizures**, IV fluids and noradrenaline for hypotension, and **lipid emulsion in refractory cardiotoxicity**.
- **AVOID: class IA, IC and III antiarrhythmics (they worsen sodium-channel blockade), flumazenil (seizures), and physostigmine (asystole).**
- TCAs have a **narrow therapeutic index** — a few days' supply can be lethal, which is why they are avoided in patients at suicide risk; **dialysis is ineffective** (high protein binding and volume of distribution).
- Anticholinergic burden is why TCAs are also contraindicated in **glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia and the elderly**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Which does NOT suggest toxicity?" — the answer is the item that
**contradicts the anticholinergic syndrome** (hypothermia, miosis, sweating, bradycardia).

**REMEMBER:** *No sweat means no cooling — TCAs make you hot, not cold.*

## 74. Iron Chelation Thresholds

**PEARL:** **IV desferrioxamine (deferoxamine)** is indicated in acute iron poisoning when
the **serum iron exceeds ~500 µg/dL**, or when there are severe clinical features
(shock, altered consciousness, persistent vomiting, metabolic acidosis) regardless of the
level.

**UNDERSTAND:** Toxicity begins when **iron exceeds the binding capacity of transferrin**;
the resulting **non-transferrin-bound "free" iron** catalyses **Fenton free-radical
reactions**, causing lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial injury, increased capillary
permeability and vasodilatation. Chelation is therefore aimed at the **free** fraction —
which is why the threshold is a serum iron above the usual TIBC, and why chelation is
useless once iron is safely bound intracellularly.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Five stages of acute iron poisoning:** (1) 0–6 h — **GI: vomiting, haematemesis, diarrhoea**; (2) 6–24 h — **deceptive latent phase** (the trap); (3) 6–72 h — **shock, metabolic acidosis, hepatic necrosis, coagulopathy**; (4) 12–96 h — hepatic failure; (5) 2–8 weeks — **gastric outlet obstruction from pyloric scarring**.
- **Desferrioxamine turns the urine "vin rosé"/orange-red** (ferrioxamine) — a marker of active chelation. Given **IM, IV or SC — never orally**. Adverse effects: **hypotension with rapid infusion, ARDS with prolonged use, Yersinia infection, retinal and ototoxicity**.
- **Oral chelators for chronic transfusional overload (thalassaemia): DEFERASIROX (once daily; renal and hepatic toxicity, GI bleeding) and DEFERIPRONE (agranulocytosis — monitor the neutrophil count; best for CARDIAC iron)**. Desferrioxamine remains the most effective but requires prolonged subcutaneous infusions, which is why the oral agents are preferred for adherence.
- **Whole-bowel irrigation with polyethylene glycol** is used when tablets are visible on abdominal X-ray; **activated charcoal does NOT bind iron**.
- In thalassaemia major, start chelation after about **10–20 transfusions or when ferritin exceeds 1000 ng/mL**; monitor iron loading with **T2* cardiac and hepatic MRI**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A single number. **>500 µg/dL → chelate.**

**REMEMBER:** *Once iron outruns transferrin, chelate it — 500 is the line.*

## 75. Finerenone vs Spironolactone

**PEARL:** **Finerenone is a SELECTIVE NON-STEROIDAL mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist**
with a **balanced heart–kidney tissue distribution and NO long-lived active metabolites** —
which is why it causes **less severe hyperkalaemia** than spironolactone despite blocking the
same receptor.

**UNDERSTAND:** Spironolactone is a **steroidal** MRA that **concentrates in the kidney** and
generates **canrenone and other active metabolites with long half-lives**, so distal nephron
sodium–potassium exchange is inhibited **continuously and disproportionately in the kidney**
— maximising the potassium effect. Finerenone distributes **evenly between heart and kidney**
and clears without persistent metabolites, so it delivers cardiorenal protection with less
sustained renal potassium retention. Its **non-steroidal structure** also explains the
absence of the endocrine side effects.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Spironolactone's steroidal off-target effects: GYNAECOMASTIA, impotence, menstrual irregularity and hirsutism** (it also blocks androgen and binds progesterone receptors) — hence its use in **PCOS-related hirsutism and acne**. **EPLERENONE and FINERENONE are selective and do not cause gynaecomastia.**
- **Indications: finerenone is approved for CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE ASSOCIATED WITH TYPE 2 DIABETES** (FIDELIO/FIGARO trials — reduces CKD progression and cardiovascular events). **Spironolactone/eplerenone are mortality-reducing drugs in HFrEF**, and spironolactone is used in **ascites from cirrhosis (with furosemide, 100:40), resistant hypertension and primary hyperaldosteronism**.
- **All MRAs cause hyperkalaemia — monitor potassium and creatinine at baseline, 1 week and 1 month**, and avoid combining with **ACE inhibitors/ARBs, potassium supplements, NSAIDs and trimethoprim** without close monitoring. **Contraindicated if K⁺ >5.0 mmol/L.**
- **Amiloride and triamterene block ENaC directly** (not the receptor) — also potassium-sparing but without endocrine effects.
- **Aldosterone escape** explains why an ACE inhibitor alone does not fully block the pathway, and why adding an MRA gives extra benefit.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The answer must explain a **pharmacokinetic/distribution** difference,
not a different mechanism — both drugs block the same receptor.

**REMEMBER:** *Same receptor, gentler pharmacokinetics — finerenone spreads out and leaves
quickly.*

## 76. Bile Acid Sequestrants

**PEARL:** **COLESTIPOL (with cholestyramine and colesevelam) is a BILE ACID SEQUESTRANT** —
it binds bile acids in the gut so they are **excreted in the stool**, forcing the liver to
divert cholesterol into new bile acid synthesis and to **upregulate LDL receptors**, thereby
lowering LDL.

**UNDERSTAND:** Normally **95% of bile acids are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum** and
recycled. Sequestering them interrupts that enterohepatic circulation, so hepatic
cholesterol is consumed making replacements; the falling intrahepatic cholesterol
**upregulates LDL receptors** exactly as a statin does. But the same signal also
**upregulates HMG-CoA reductase**, partly offsetting the effect — which is precisely why
**adding a statin is synergistic**, and why the two are classically combined.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Adverse effects: constipation, bloating and bad taste (poor compliance), and a RISE IN TRIGLYCERIDES — so they are CONTRAINDICATED in significant hypertriglyceridaemia (>400 mg/dL).** They **impair absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)** and of other drugs (**digoxin, warfarin, thiazides, thyroxine, statins**) — give other drugs **1 hour before or 4 hours after**.
- **Unique advantages: they are NOT absorbed systemically**, so they are the **lipid-lowering drugs of choice in PREGNANCY and in children**; **colesevelam also lowers HbA1c** and is licensed for type 2 diabetes; they relieve the **PRURITUS of cholestasis** and treat **bile-acid diarrhoea** (post-cholecystectomy, terminal ileal disease) and **Clostridioides difficile** toxin binding (historic).
- **The other non-statin agents: EZETIMIBE blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption at NPC1L1; PCSK9 INHIBITORS (evolocumab, alirocumab, inclisiran) increase LDL-receptor recycling; BEMPEDOIC ACID inhibits ATP-citrate lyase (a liver-specific step upstream of HMG-CoA reductase — hence no myopathy); FIBRATES (gemfibrozil, fenofibrate) are PPAR-α agonists and are the drugs for high TRIGLYCERIDES; NIACIN raises HDL most; OMEGA-3 fatty acids lower triglycerides.**
- **Statins are always first-line**; the **combination of a fibrate (especially gemfibrozil) with a statin markedly increases the risk of MYOPATHY and rhabdomyolysis** — use fenofibrate if a combination is needed.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem describes the **mechanism** ("increases bile acid excretion in
the stools") — only the sequestrant fits.

**REMEMBER:** *Trap the bile acids in the gut and the liver burns cholesterol to replace
them.*

## 77. SSRI-Induced Hyponatraemia in the Elderly

**PEARL:** **Severe hyponatraemia with confusion, falls and gait instability days to weeks
after starting an SSRI (sertraline, fluoxetine, paroxetine, citalopram) in a frail elderly
woman = SSRI-INDUCED SIADH.** SSRIs are flagged in the **BEERS criteria** as drugs to use
with caution in the elderly for exactly this reason.

**UNDERSTAND:** Serotonin **stimulates hypothalamic ADH release and enhances its renal
effect**, so an SSRI produces a drug-induced SIADH. The elderly are uniquely vulnerable
because ageing brings **reduced renal free-water clearance, a lower total body water,
increased ADH sensitivity and a blunted thirst/osmostat response** — so the same drug effect
produces a far larger fall in sodium. The **timing (within the first 2–4 weeks)** and the
**neurological presentation (falls, confusion, unsteadiness rather than "psychiatric"
symptoms)** are the recognisable pattern.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Check sodium at baseline and 2–4 weeks after starting an SSRI in an elderly patient**, especially if also on **thiazides, or with low body weight, female sex or a history of hyponatraemia**.
- **Management: stop the SSRI, fluid restriction, and correct the sodium SLOWLY (no more than 8–10 mmol/L in 24 hours)** to avoid osmotic demyelination. Consider switching to an antidepressant with a lower risk — **mirtazapine, bupropion or agomelatine**.
- **Other drug causes of SIADH: carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine, TCAs, antipsychotics, cyclophosphamide, vincristine, NSAIDs, PPIs, opioids, desmopressin and "ecstasy" (MDMA).**
- **Antidepressant choices in the elderly: SSRIs remain first-line for efficacy, but start LOW and go SLOW; AVOID TRICYCLICS (anticholinergic effects, falls, cardiotoxicity, cognitive impairment) and PAROXETINE (most anticholinergic SSRI).** **MIRTAZAPINE is useful when insomnia and weight loss coexist.**
- **SIADH diagnosis: euvolaemic hyponatraemia with LOW serum osmolality, inappropriately concentrated urine (>100 mOsm/kg), urine sodium >40 mmol/L, and normal thyroid, adrenal and renal function.**
- **Hyponatraemia is itself an independent risk factor for FALLS, fractures and osteoporosis in the elderly**, even when mild and "asymptomatic".

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives an **elderly patient + a newly started antidepressant +
hyponatraemia**. Among the options, the **SSRI** is the culprit; bupropion, mirtazapine and
selegiline are the low-risk distractors.

**REMEMBER:** *Serotonin tells the kidney to hold water — and an old kidney cannot argue.*

## 78. Clonidine — Rebound Hypertension

**PEARL:** **Abrupt withdrawal of clonidine causes REBOUND HYPERTENSION** — a surge often **above the pre-treatment blood pressure**, with restlessness and sympathetic overactivity. Treat with **phentolamine**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Clonidine is a **central α2 agonist** that chronically suppresses noradrenaline release from the brainstem vasomotor centre. In response, **peripheral α-receptors up-regulate and noradrenaline stores build up**. Remove the central brake suddenly and that stored catecholamine floods onto supersensitive receptors — a pharmacological phaeochromocytoma. **Phentolamine (an α-blocker) treats it; a beta-blocker alone would worsen it by leaving α-vasoconstriction unopposed.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- Adverse effects: **dry mouth and sedation (most common)**, confusion, constipation, impotence, bradycardia.
- Rebound appears if doses are missed for **1–2 days** — hence the classic exam stem of a traveller who forgets the tablets.
- Other clinical uses: **opioid and nicotine withdrawal, ADHD, menopausal flushing, Tourette syndrome, and as an epidural adjuvant**.
- **Moxonidine and rilmenidine are SELECTIVE imidazoline (I1) receptor agonists** — clonidine and brimonidine act at I1 receptors too, but their **main action is α2 agonism**; **methyldopa has no imidazoline action** (it is a false transmitter forming α-methylnoradrenaline).
- Other drugs with dangerous withdrawal: **beta-blockers (rebound angina/MI), opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, corticosteroids (adrenal crisis)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives **sedation and dry mouth on treatment** plus a **hypertensive crisis on stopping** — those two together identify clonidine uniquely.

**REMEMBER:** *Release the central brake and stored catecholamines flood out.*

## 79. Renoprotection in Diabetic Kidney Disease

**PEARL:** **ACE inhibitors/ARBs, SGLT2 inhibitors and finerenone slow the progression of diabetic kidney disease.** **Thiazides do NOT** — they lower blood pressure but have no specific renoprotective effect (and are ineffective as diuretics once eGFR falls below ~30).

**UNDERSTAND:** Diabetic nephropathy is driven by **glomerular HYPERFILTRATION** — the afferent arteriole dilates while the efferent constricts, raising intraglomerular pressure. Renoprotective drugs work by **lowering that pressure**: ACE inhibitors/ARBs **dilate the efferent** arteriole; SGLT2 inhibitors restore **tubuloglomerular feedback** by delivering sodium to the macula densa, **constricting the afferent**. A thiazide lowers systemic pressure without correcting the intraglomerular gradient.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Microalbuminuria (ACR 30–300 mg/g) on two occasions 3–6 months apart** defines incipient nephropathy and mandates ACE inhibitor/ARB therapy **even if blood pressure is normal**.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** cause an **initial reversible dip in eGFR** — this is expected and reflects reduced hyperfiltration, not injury.
- **Finerenone** is a **non-steroidal selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** — anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory, with **less hyperkalaemia and no gynaecomastia** compared with spironolactone.
- **Never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB** — hyperkalaemia and acute kidney injury without benefit.
- **GLP-1 receptor agonists** also show renal benefit.
- **Nesiritide** is a **recombinant BNP given INTRAVENOUSLY only** (it is a peptide, digested if swallowed); causes **natriuresis and vasodilation** in decompensated heart failure but **does not improve mortality or readmission**. Natriuretic peptides are degraded by **neprilysin** — the target of **sacubitril**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not useful in preventing progression" → **hydrochlorothiazide**. Every other option has trial evidence in diabetic kidney disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Lower the pressure inside the glomerulus, not just in the arm.*

## 80. Chlorpropamide and Drug-Induced Hyponatraemia

**PEARL:** **Chlorpropamide, a first-generation sulfonylurea, causes DILUTIONAL HYPONATRAEMIA** by **potentiating ADH action on the collecting duct** — an SIADH-like picture. It also causes a **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Chlorpropamide **sensitises the renal tubule to circulating vasopressin**, increasing water reabsorption without affecting sodium — hence a **dilutional** hyponatraemia with concentrated urine. Its extremely **long half-life (~35 hours)** compounds both this effect and its risk of prolonged hypoglycaemia, which is why it is now obsolete.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Other drug causes of hyponatraemia/SIADH: **SSRIs (especially in the elderly), carbamazepine and OXCARBAZEPINE, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, thiazides, NSAIDs, desmopressin, MDMA**.
- **Oxcarbazepine causes MORE hyponatraemia than carbamazepine**, not less — a favourite reversal. Oxcarbazepine is a **keto analogue and prodrug of carbamazepine**, blocks **voltage-gated sodium channels**, is metabolised by **glucuronidation** (so **no auto-induction**), and is a **weaker CYP inducer**.
- Sulfonylureas act by **closing the ATP-sensitive K⁺ channel (SUR1) on the β cell**; risks are **hypoglycaemia and weight gain**, greatest with long-acting agents in the elderly and in renal impairment.
- **Glipizide and gliclazide** are preferred in the elderly (shorter action, less hypoglycaemia).
- **Tolvaptan** — a **V2 receptor antagonist** — does the opposite: it **blocks vasopressin, lowers renal cAMP, prevents aquaporin-2 insertion, and increases FREE WATER CLEARANCE**. Hence **dehydration, thirst, dry mouth, hypernatraemia and hepatotoxicity**; used in **SIADH, hypervolaemic hyponatraemia and to slow ADPKD**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Oral hypoglycaemic causing dilutional hyponatraemia" → **chlorpropamide**. The tolvaptan question tests the same axis in reverse.

**REMEMBER:** *Chlorpropamide amplifies ADH; tolvaptan blocks it.*

## 81. Antitubercular Drug Toxicities

**PEARL:** **ETHAMBUTOL causes dose-dependent optic neuritis** — reduced acuity and **loss of red–green discrimination**, with a **relative afferent pupillary defect (Marcus Gunn pupil)**. Check **visual acuity and colour vision before and during therapy**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Ethambutol **chelates metals**, and the optic nerve — with its high mitochondrial density — is uniquely vulnerable to the resulting **copper/zinc-dependent mitochondrial dysfunction**. The toxicity is therefore **dose- and duration-dependent and usually reversible** on withdrawal, which is why early detection matters so much.

**MUST KNOW:**
- First-line ATT toxicities: **isoniazid — peripheral neuropathy (give pyridoxine), hepatitis, drug-induced lupus**; **rifampicin — orange secretions, hepatitis, flu-like syndrome, thrombocytopenia, and potent CYP INDUCTION**; **pyrazinamide — hyperuricaemia/gout, hepatitis, arthralgia**; **ethambutol — optic neuritis**; **streptomycin — vestibulotoxicity and nephrotoxicity**.
- Mechanisms: **INH — mycolic acid synthesis (via KatG activation, InhA)**; **rifampicin — DNA-dependent RNA polymerase**; **pyrazinamide — fatty acid synthase I, active in acidic pH inside macrophages**; **ethambutol — arabinosyl transferase** (the only **bacteriostatic** first-line drug).
- **Second-line drugs: fluoroquinolones, ethionamide, cycloserine, capreomycin, kanamycin/amikacin, PAS, linezolid, bedaquiline, clofazimine.** **Ethionamide** is structurally related to INH, blocks mycolic acid synthesis, and causes **hypothyroidism and severe GI upset**.
- **Rifampicin is avoided with HIV protease inhibitors** — it induces CYP3A4 and causes ART failure. **Use RIFABUTIN**, which is the weakest inducer: **rifampicin > rifapentine > rifabutin**.
- Enzyme inducers (**CRAP GPS**): Carbamazepine, Rifampicin, Alcohol (chronic), Phenytoin, Griseofulvin, Phenobarbitone, Sulfonylureas. Inhibitors (**RED VICK**): Ritonavir, Erythromycin, Diltiazem, Verapamil/Valproate, Itraconazole, Clarithromycin/Cimetidine, Ketoconazole.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two questions from one drug list: **optic neuritis → ethambutol**, and **avoid with protease inhibitors → rifampicin**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ethambutol takes the eyes; rifampicin takes the other drugs.*

## 82. Tolerance to Opioids — The Three Exceptions

**PEARL:** Tolerance develops to **almost every opioid effect EXCEPT the "3 Cs": Constricted pupil (miosis), Constipation and Convulsions.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Tolerance arises where **receptor desensitisation and downstream adaptation** can occur — analgesia, euphoria, sedation, nausea and respiratory depression all fade. **Miosis and constipation persist** because they are mediated at sites with **little adaptive capacity**: the Edinger-Westphal nucleus and the enteric nervous system. That persistence is why **pinpoint pupils remain a reliable diagnostic sign of opioid use even in chronic users**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The three exceptions mean: **a chronic user still has pinpoint pupils and still needs laxatives** (prescribe them prophylactically — tolerance never develops).
- Tolerance to **respiratory depression develops** — which is why a relapsing user who returns to their previous dose after abstinence **dies of respiratory arrest**.
- **METHADONE prolongs the QT interval** — unique among opioids — and causes **torsades**, especially with other QT-prolonging drugs (**fluoxetine, antipsychotics, macrolides**). Normal QTc is ~440 ms; **> 440 in men and > 460 in women is prolonged**.
- Methadone is a **long-acting µ agonist** used for **opioid maintenance and chronic pain**; it also has **NMDA antagonist** activity.
- **Pethidine (meperidine)** — active metabolite **normeperidine** causes seizures; **avoid with MAO inhibitors** (serotonin syndrome/hyperpyrexia). **Tramadol** lowers the seizure threshold and causes serotonin syndrome with SSRIs.
- **Naloxone** (short-acting) for acute overdose; **naltrexone** for relapse prevention; **buprenorphine** is a partial agonist with a ceiling on respiratory depression.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Tolerance does NOT develop to" → **miosis**. The methadone/QT question is a separate high-yield item.

**REMEMBER:** *Pinpoint pupils and constipation never wear off.*

## 83. Barbiturates and Acute Intermittent Porphyria

**PEARL:** **Barbiturates are absolutely contraindicated in acute intermittent porphyria** — they induce **ALA synthase** and precipitate an attack.

**UNDERSTAND:** Barbiturates induce **cytochrome P450**, and every new P450 molecule consumes **haem**. The falling free haem pool **releases feedback inhibition of ALA synthase**, the rate-limiting enzyme, which then pours out **ALA and porphobilinogen** — the neurotoxic precursors that cause the attack.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Porphyrinogenic drugs (**"BOOST CHAP"**): **B**arbiturates, **O**ral contraceptives, **O**rphenadrine, **S**ulphonamides, **T**etracyclines, **C**hloramphenicol/**C**arbamazepine, **H**alothane/**H**ydralazine, **A**lcohol, **P**henytoin. Also **griseofulvin, rifampicin, oestrogens** and **fasting/low-calorie diets**.
- **Safe** in porphyria: **propofol, ketamine, nitrous oxide, suxamethonium, gabapentin, opioids, paracetamol, aspirin**.

- Barbiturates remain useful for **refractory status epilepticus** (thiopentone/pentobarbitone coma) and **phenobarbitone treats neonatal jaundice/kernicterus** by inducing **UDP-glucuronosyltransferase** — the same enzyme induction being helpful there and harmful in porphyria.

## 84. Antifungal Mechanisms

**PEARL:** **Azoles (imidazoles and triazoles) inhibit ERGOSTEROL SYNTHESIS** by blocking the fungal **CYP450 enzyme 14-α-sterol demethylase**, preventing conversion of lanosterol to ergosterol.

**UNDERSTAND:** Ergosterol is the fungal equivalent of cholesterol — essential for **membrane fluidity and integrity**. Because azoles inhibit a **cytochrome P450 enzyme**, they inevitably also inhibit **human CYP enzymes**, which is the source of their major clinical problem: **drug interactions** (statins, warfarin, ergot, calcineurin inhibitors) and **endocrine effects** with ketoconazole.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Antifungal mechanisms: **azoles — 14-α-demethylase (ergosterol synthesis)**; **terbinafine — SQUALENE EPOXIDASE**; **amphotericin B and nystatin — bind ergosterol and FORM PORES**; **flucytosine — interferes with DNA/RNA synthesis (converted to 5-FU)**; **echinocandins (caspofungin) — inhibit β-1,3-glucan synthesis (cell WALL)**; **griseofulvin — disrupts microtubules**.
- **Imidazoles: clotrimazole, ketoconazole, miconazole, luliconazole. Triazoles: fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole.** Triazoles have **greater specificity for fungal CYP** and fewer endocrine effects.
- Spectrum notes: **fluconazole has no activity against Aspergillus or Mucor**; **voriconazole is first line for Aspergillus** (causes visual disturbances); **isavuconazole and posaconazole cover Mucor**; **amphotericin B (liposomal)** for mucormycosis and severe systemic disease.
- **Ketoconazole inhibits human steroidogenesis** — used therapeutically in **Cushing syndrome**, but causes gynaecomastia and hepatotoxicity.
- Amphotericin toxicity: **infusion reactions (rigors), nephrotoxicity with hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia, renal tubular acidosis** — reduced by liposomal formulations.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mechanism matching. The **squalene epoxidase** option is included to catch those who confuse terbinafine with the azoles.

**REMEMBER:** *Azoles block the demethylase; terbinafine blocks the epoxidase; amphotericin punches holes.*

## 85. Lipid-Lowering Drugs by Mechanism

**PEARL:** **Ezetimibe blocks intestinal cholesterol ABSORPTION** by inhibiting the **NPC1L1 transporter**. **Fibrates activate PPAR-α** and cause **GALLSTONES** by increasing biliary cholesterol saturation.

**UNDERSTAND:** Ezetimibe and statins are **synergistic** for a mechanistic reason: blocking absorption causes a **compensatory rise in hepatic cholesterol synthesis**, which the statin then blocks. Attacking both the input and the synthesis pathway produces a greater LDL fall than either alone.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Statins — HMG-CoA reductase**; lower LDL most; adverse effects **myopathy/rhabdomyolysis (worse with fibrates, especially gemfibrozil, and with CYP3A4 inhibitors), raised transaminases, new-onset diabetes**.
- **Fibrates — PPAR-α agonists** → **increase lipoprotein lipase, reduce apoC-III, stimulate fatty acid oxidation**; best drugs for **hypertriglyceridaemia**; adverse effects **myopathy, gallstones, raised creatinine**.
- **Bile acid sequestrants (cholestyramine, colesevelam)** — bind bile acids in gut, increase hepatic bile acid synthesis, up-regulate LDL receptors; cause **constipation, raised triglycerides, and malabsorption of fat-soluble vitamins and other drugs**.
- **Niacin** — reduces VLDL secretion; causes **flushing (prostaglandin-mediated, blocked by aspirin), hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia**.
- **PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab, alirocumab) and inclisiran (siRNA)** — increase LDL receptor recycling; most powerful LDL lowering.
- **PPAR subtypes: α — triglyceride lowering (fibrates); γ — insulin sensitisation (thiazolidinediones); δ — fatty acid oxidation.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Directly inhibits absorption of cholesterol" → **ezetimibe**; "antilipidaemic causing gallstones, by what mechanism" → **PPAR-α activation**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ezetimibe blocks the doorway; statins block the factory; fibrates switch on the burner.*

## 86. Antidepressants — Choosing by Side Effect

**PEARL:** **MIRTAZAPINE is the antidepressant of choice when sexual dysfunction must be avoided.** It is also sedating and increases appetite — useful in **depression with insomnia and weight loss**.

**UNDERSTAND:** SSRI-induced sexual dysfunction results from **excess serotonin at 5-HT2 receptors**. Mirtazapine raises noradrenaline and serotonin by blocking **α2 auto- and heteroreceptors**, but simultaneously **blocks 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C** — so the serotonin it releases is steered away from the receptors that cause sexual dysfunction and toward 5-HT1A. Its **H1 blockade** explains the sedation and weight gain.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Mirtazapine adverse effects: **sedation, increased appetite and weight gain, rarely AGRANULOCYTOSIS**.
- Other options with low sexual dysfunction: **bupropion, vilazodone, vortioxetine, agomelatine, trazodone** (trazodone can cause **priapism**).
- **SSRIs: fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline, paroxetine, dapoxetine.** **Duloxetine, venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine and milnacipran are SNRIs** — a classic "odd one out" question.
- SSRI issues: **GI upset, sexual dysfunction, hyponatraemia in the elderly, increased bleeding risk, discontinuation syndrome (worst with paroxetine, least with fluoxetine)**, and **paroxetine is teratogenic (cardiac defects) — avoid in pregnancy**.
- **Fluoxetine has the longest half-life** (active metabolite norfluoxetine) — hence the least withdrawal but the longest washout before an MAOI.
- **Duloxetine and venlafaxine** are also used for **neuropathic pain**; duloxetine for **stress urinary incontinence**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two forms — "which is NOT an SSRI" (**duloxetine**) and "which avoids sexual side effects" (**mirtazapine**).

**REMEMBER:** *Mirtazapine releases serotonin but blocks the receptors that cause the problem.*

## 87. Drug-Induced Ocular Toxicity

**PEARL:** **Chloroquine deposits in the cornea (cornea verticillata / vortex keratopathy) and causes pigmentary "bull's-eye" retinopathy.** **Hydroxychloroquine is far less ocularly toxic.**

**UNDERSTAND:** These are **cationic amphiphilic drugs** — they bind **melanin in the RPE and phospholipids in the corneal epithelium**, accumulating over years. The corneal deposits form a **whorl pattern following the migration lines of the corneal epithelium**, which is why the appearance is identical whatever the causative drug.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Cornea verticillata is caused by: amiodarone, chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine, phenothiazines (chlorpromazine), indomethacin, tamoxifen** — and occurs naturally in **FABRY DISEASE**.
- **Corneal deposits are reversible and harmless**; the **retinopathy is irreversible and sight-threatening** — hence **annual screening (OCT, visual fields, fundus autofluorescence)** after 5 years of hydroxychloroquine, sooner with risk factors (dose > 5 mg/kg, renal impairment, tamoxifen use).
- Other drug–eye associations: **ethambutol — optic neuritis**; **steroids — posterior subcapsular cataract and glaucoma**; **vigabatrin — irreversible visual field constriction**; **tamsulosin — intraoperative floppy iris syndrome**; **sildenafil — blue-tinged vision (PDE6)**; **topiramate — acute angle closure and myopia**; **isotretinoin — night blindness and dry eye**.
- **Chloroquine short-term** causes reversible **blurring and diplopia** (accommodation defect).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Drug deposited in the cornea" → **chloroquine**. The DMARD distractors (methotrexate, leflunomide, sulfasalazine) have their own distinct toxicities.

**REMEMBER:** *Amphiphilic drugs stick to melanin and to corneal phospholipid.*

## 88. Triptans and Coronary Vasoconstriction

**PEARL:** **Triptans are 5-HT1B/1D AGONISTS** — they relieve migraine by constricting dilated cranial vessels, but the same action **constricts coronary arteries**, so they are **contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, and after stroke**.

**UNDERSTAND:** **5-HT1B receptors sit on vascular smooth muscle** — cranial *and* coronary. There is no way to constrict one without the other, which is precisely why triptans cannot be made cardiac-safe and why the **gepants (rimegepant, ubrogepant — CGRP antagonists) and ditans (lasmiditan — selective 5-HT1F)** were developed: they abort migraine **without vasoconstriction**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Triptan actions: **cranial vasoconstriction (5-HT1B)**, **inhibition of trigeminal CGRP release (5-HT1D)**, and central inhibition of trigeminal nucleus transmission.
- Contraindications: **coronary artery disease, Prinzmetal angina, uncontrolled hypertension, previous stroke/TIA, hemiplegic and basilar migraine, peripheral vascular disease, pregnancy (relative)**.
- **Do not combine with ergot alkaloids** (within 24 h) — additive vasoconstriction; caution with **SSRIs/SNRIs** (serotonin syndrome).
- **Ergotamine** is worse: a **non-selective partial agonist at α-adrenergic, 5-HT1 and 5-HT2 receptors** causing **sustained vasoconstriction**; combined with a **CYP3A4 inhibitor (ritonavir, clarithromycin, azoles)** it causes **ERGOTISM — symmetrical limb ischaemia and gangrene**.
- Migraine prophylaxis: **propranolol, topiramate, valproate, amitriptyline, flunarizine, candesartan, and CGRP monoclonals (erenumab, fremanezumab)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Mechanism causing the chest pain" → **5-HT agonism**. Note the option "5-HT antagonist" is the trap — methysergide and pizotifen are the antagonists used in prophylaxis.

**REMEMBER:** *The same receptor narrows the head and the heart.*

## 89. Enalaprilat and Hypertensive Emergencies

**PEARL:** **Enalaprilat is the only intravenously available ACE inhibitor** — the active metabolite of enalapril. **All ACE inhibitors are prodrugs EXCEPT captopril and lisinopril.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Most ACE inhibitors are **esterified to improve oral absorption** and must be **hydrolysed by hepatic esterases** to their active diacid form. Enalaprilat is that active diacid — poorly absorbed orally but perfectly usable **intravenously**, making it the only member of the class available for parenteral use.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Parenteral drugs for hypertensive emergency and their key contraindications: **labetalol/esmolol (avoid in asthma, heart block, decompensated failure)**; **nicardipine/clevidipine (avoid in severe aortic stenosis)**; **nitroglycerin (avoid in tamponade; drug of choice with pulmonary oedema or ischaemia)**; **sodium nitroprusside (cyanide toxicity, avoid in renal/hepatic failure and raised ICP)**; **hydralazine (avoid in coronary disease and dissection)**; **fenoldopam — D1 agonist, caution in glaucoma**; **phentolamine — for catecholamine excess**; **enalaprilat — avoid with a history of angioedema**.

## 90. Prostaglandins and the Ductus Arteriosus

**PEARL:** **PGE1 (alprostadil) KEEPS the ductus arteriosus OPEN**; **indomethacin/ibuprofen (COX inhibitors) CLOSE it.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Ductal patency in fetal life is maintained by **placental PGE2 and low oxygen tension**. After birth the placenta is gone and oxygen rises, so the ductus constricts. Giving **exogenous PGE1** restores the fetal signal and holds it open — buying time in **duct-dependent circulations**; blocking prostaglandin synthesis with an NSAID does the opposite, closing a **persistent** ductus in the preterm infant.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Duct-dependent lesions needing PGE1: transposition of the great arteries, pulmonary atresia, tricuspid atresia, severe tetralogy, critical pulmonary or aortic stenosis, coarctation, hypoplastic left heart.**
- PGE1 side effects: **apnoea (be ready to ventilate), fever, hypotension, flushing**.
- Prostaglandin actions worth knowing: **PGE2 — vasodilation, cervical ripening, uterine contraction, gastric mucosal protection**; **PGF2α — vasoconstriction of large veins, bronchoconstriction, uterine contraction**; **PGI2 (prostacyclin) — vasodilation and ANTI-aggregation**; **TXA2 — vasoconstriction and platelet AGGREGATION**.
- Obstetric uses: **dinoprostone (PGE2) for cervical ripening; misoprostol (PGE1 analogue) for induction, PPH and medical abortion; carboprost (PGF2α) for PPH — contraindicated in ASTHMA**.
- **Misoprostol also protects against NSAID-induced peptic ulcer** (replacing the PGE2 the NSAID removed) — but causes abortion, so it is contraindicated in pregnancy.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Maintains ductal patency" → **PGE1**. The prostaglandin action table is asked in its own right.

**REMEMBER:** *Give the prostaglandin to keep it open; block the prostaglandin to shut it.*

## 91. Hormonal Therapy in Breast Cancer

**PEARL:** **Aromatase inhibitors — letrozole, anastrozole (reversible) and EXEMESTANE (irreversible, steroidal) — are used in POSTMENOPAUSAL women.** **Tamoxifen (a SERM)** is used in premenopausal women.

**UNDERSTAND:** After menopause the ovaries stop producing oestrogen, and the remaining supply comes from **peripheral aromatisation of adrenal androgens in fat, muscle and the tumour itself**. Blocking aromatase therefore removes essentially all oestrogen. In a **premenopausal** woman the ovary simply **increases output through the intact hypothalamic-pituitary feedback loop**, so aromatase inhibitors fail unless ovarian function is suppressed.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Tamoxifen** — antagonist in breast, **AGONIST in endometrium (risk of endometrial carcinoma) and bone (protects against osteoporosis)**; also causes **hot flushes and venous thromboembolism**. **Raloxifene** is an agonist in bone but **not** in the endometrium.
- **Aromatase inhibitors** cause **osteoporosis, arthralgia and hypercholesterolaemia** (no endometrial cancer or thrombosis risk).
- **Ospemifene** — SERM with vaginal oestrogenic action for dyspareunia; **danazol** — a synthetic androgen for **endometriosis and hereditary angioedema**.
- **Fulvestrant** is a selective oestrogen receptor **degrader (SERD)**.
- **Pegvisomant** — a **GROWTH HORMONE RECEPTOR ANTAGONIST** for acromegaly (normalises IGF-1 but does not shrink the tumour); contrast **octreotide/lanreotide** (somatostatin analogues) and **cabergoline** (dopamine agonist).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Class identification. **Exemestane** is the irreversible steroidal aromatase inhibitor; tamoxifen and ospemifene are SERMs; danazol is an androgen.

## 92. Enzyme Induction and Contraceptive Failure

**PEARL:** **Valproate is an enzyme INHIBITOR — it does NOT cause oral contraceptive failure.** **Rifampicin, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, carbamazepine and griseofulvin are inducers and do.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Combined oral contraceptives depend on maintaining **adequate plasma oestrogen and progestogen**. Enzyme inducers accelerate their **hepatic metabolism**, dropping levels below the threshold needed to suppress ovulation. An **inhibitor** does the opposite — it raises levels, so contraception is preserved (though side effects may increase).

**MUST KNOW:**
- Antiepileptics that **do NOT induce** and are therefore contraceptive-safe: **valproate, levetiracetam, lamotrigine (though the COC lowers lamotrigine levels), gabapentin, pregabalin, vigabatrin, benzodiazepines**.
- Inducing antiepileptics: **carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, primidone, topiramate (at high dose), oxcarbazepine**.
- Women on inducers need **a copper IUCD, depot medroxyprogesterone, or a higher-dose oestrogen preparation** — the progestogen-only pill and implant are unreliable.
- **Cyclosporine causes cholestasis by inhibiting the canalicular ABC transporter MDR1** (and BSEP), blocking bile salt and phospholipid efflux. Cyclosporine works by binding **cyclophilin → inhibiting CALCINEURIN → blocking NFAT and IL-2 transcription**; toxicities are **nephrotoxicity, hypertension, hyperkalaemia, gum hypertrophy, hirsutism, tremor** (contrast **tacrolimus** — FKBP-12, no gum hypertrophy or hirsutism, more diabetogenic and neurotoxic).
- **Interferon-beta** is the licensed disease-modifying therapy for **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis** (Lhermitte's sign, **Dawson's fingers** on MRI, **oligoclonal bands** in CSF). **IFN-α** is for infections and some malignancies; **IFN-γ** for chronic granulomatous disease.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Contraceptive failure unlikely with" → **valproate**. Every other option is a classic inducer.

**REMEMBER:** *Inducers burn off the pill; inhibitors preserve it.*

## 93. Antimicrobial Choices — Cotrimoxazole and Tafenoquine

**PEARL:** **Cotrimoxazole is NOT used for chancroid** (treat with **ceftriaxone, azithromycin, erythromycin or ciprofloxacin**). It IS used for **urinary tract infection, prostatitis, nocardiosis and Pneumocystis**.

## 94. Dosing Rate and Steady State

**PEARL:** **Dosing rate = CLEARANCE × target steady-state plasma concentration.** **Loading dose = volume of distribution × target concentration.**

**UNDERSTAND:** At steady state, **input equals elimination**. Elimination rate is by definition **clearance × plasma concentration**, so to hold a chosen concentration you must supply drug at exactly that rate. The two formulas answer different questions: **clearance determines the MAINTENANCE dose (how fast you must keep topping up); volume of distribution determines the LOADING dose (how much it takes to fill the space).**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Steady state is reached in 4–5 half-lives** regardless of dose; a **loading dose shortens the time to effect but not the time to steady state**.
- **First-order kinetics** — a constant **FRACTION** eliminated per unit time; rate proportional to concentration; **clearance constant**. **Zero-order** — a constant **AMOUNT** per unit time; rate independent of concentration; **clearance falls as concentration rises**. Zero-order drugs: **phenytoin (at therapeutic doses), ethanol, aspirin (high dose), theophylline, warfarin** — small dose increases cause disproportionate rises.
- **Half-life = 0.693 × Vd / clearance** — so half-life depends on **both** clearance and volume of distribution.
- **Bioavailability (F)** must be included for oral dosing: **dose rate = CL × Cpss / F**.
- **Therapeutic drug monitoring** is needed for narrow-index drugs: **digoxin, lithium, phenytoin, theophylline, aminoglycosides, vancomycin, ciclosporin, warfarin (INR)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Formula recall. The distractors invert the relationship (clearance ÷ concentration) or substitute loading dose for concentration.

**REMEMBER:** *Clearance sets the maintenance dose; volume sets the loading dose.*

## 95. Potency vs Efficacy

**PEARL:** **ED50 measures POTENCY, not efficacy.** On a log dose–response curve, the **position on the X (dose) axis = potency**; the **height of the plateau = efficacy**. **Efficacy matters more clinically.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Potency simply asks **how much drug you need** — a purely practical question answered by adjusting the dose. Efficacy asks **how much effect the drug can ever produce** — a ceiling you cannot overcome by any dose. That is why a low-potency, high-efficacy drug (furosemide) beats a high-potency, low-efficacy one (a thiazide) in severe fluid overload.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **A leftward shift of the curve = greater potency**; a **higher plateau = greater efficacy**.
- **Therapeutic index = TD50/ED50** (or LD50/ED50); the **certain safety factor = TD1/ED99** is a stricter measure.
- **Full agonist** — maximal efficacy; **partial agonist** — submaximal efficacy however high the dose, and acts as an **antagonist in the presence of a full agonist**; **inverse agonist** — produces the opposite effect to the agonist at a constitutively active receptor; **antagonist** — zero intrinsic activity.
- **Competitive antagonism** shifts the curve **right, parallel, with the same maximum (surmountable)**; **non-competitive/irreversible antagonism lowers the maximum (insurmountable)**.
- **Second messengers: cAMP, cGMP, IP3, DAG, Ca²⁺ and nitric oxide.** **Guanylyl cyclase is an ENZYME that makes cGMP**, not a second messenger itself — the classic distractor.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not true about potency and efficacy" → the statement equating **ED50 with efficacy**. Everything else on the list is correct.

**REMEMBER:** *Potency is the x-axis, efficacy is the y-axis.*

## 96. Drug Metabolism — Phase I vs Phase II

**PEARL:** **Phase I = oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis, cyclisation, deamination** (add or unmask a functional group). **Phase II = CONJUGATION — glucuronidation, sulfation, ACETYLATION, methylation, glycine and glutathione conjugation.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Phase I **exposes or introduces a chemically reactive handle**; Phase II **attaches a large polar molecule to that handle**, making the drug water-soluble and excretable. That sequence explains why Phase I products are sometimes **more toxic than the parent** (paracetamol → NAPQI) — the danger arises when Phase I outruns Phase II capacity.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Microsomal enzymes** live in the **smooth endoplasmic reticulum** (CYP450s and **glucuronyl transferase — the only Phase II enzyme that is microsomal**); non-microsomal enzymes sit in cytoplasm and mitochondria.
- **Acetylation is genetically polymorphic (NAT2)**: **slow acetylators** develop **INH neuropathy and hepatitis, hydralazine and procainamide lupus, sulfonamide toxicity**; fast acetylators may under-respond.
- **DRUG-INDUCED LUPUS** — arthralgia, fever, rash with **ANTI-HISTONE antibodies** (renal and CNS disease are rare). Culprits (**"SHIPP MCQ"**): **Sulfonamides/sulfasalazine, Hydralazine, Isoniazid, Procainamide, Penicillamine, Methyldopa, Chlorpromazine, Quinidine**, plus **minocycline and anti-TNF agents**. **Penicillin does NOT cause it.**
- **Prodrugs**: **enalapril (and all ACE inhibitors except captopril and lisinopril), levodopa, prednisone, sulfasalazine, cyclophosphamide, clopidogrel, oseltamivir, valaciclovir, omeprazole, codeine**. **Imipramine is NOT a prodrug** — it is active itself and merely has an active metabolite (desipramine).
- **First-order kinetics: a constant FRACTION is eliminated per unit time; rate is PROPORTIONAL to plasma concentration; clearance and half-life are constant.** **Zero-order (WATT-P): Warfarin, Alcohol/Aspirin, Tolbutamide, Theophylline, Phenytoin.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are Phase I EXCEPT" → **acetylation**. The prodrug and drug-induced lupus lists are asked in the same style.

**REMEMBER:** *Phase I uncovers the handle; Phase II attaches the tag.*

## 97. Food, Enzymes and Contraceptive Failure

**PEARL:** **Griseofulvin absorption is INCREASED by a fatty meal** (it is highly lipophilic). Most other drugs — ampicillin, aspirin, tetracycline, isoniazid, rifampicin, captopril, atenolol — are **reduced or delayed by food**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Fat stimulates **bile secretion and slows gastric emptying**, so lipophilic drugs are solubilised in micelles and absorbed far more completely. The opposite applies to drugs that **chelate with food ions (tetracyclines with calcium) or are degraded by gastric acid** during a prolonged gastric residence.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Drugs whose absorption is **increased by food**: **griseofulvin, atovaquone, lumefantrine, carbamazepine, diazepam, metoprolol, ritonavir, ganciclovir, cefuroxime, lithium, hydralazine**.
- **Efavirenz is taken on an EMPTY stomach** — a fatty meal raises absorption enough to cause CNS toxicity.
- **Contraceptive failure** occurs with **enzyme inducers (rifampicin, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, carbamazepine, griseofulvin, St John's wort)** and with **broad-spectrum antibiotics such as tetracycline**, which suppress gut flora and **interrupt the enterohepatic recirculation of oestrogen**. **Aspirin and valproate are safe.**
- **Rifampicin induces warfarin metabolism → the warfarin dose must be INCREASED.** Conversely **erythromycin, cimetidine, metronidazole, azoles and amiodarone inhibit it → dose must be reduced**.
- **Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19 and blocks the activation of CLOPIDOGREL** (a prodrug) — switch to **pantoprazole**, which has minimal CYP2C19 interaction.
- **Isoniazid is an inducer of CYP2E1 but an INHIBITOR of CYP3A4** — a genuine exception worth remembering.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** These appear as isolated recall items. The clopidogrel–omeprazole pairing via **CYP2C19** is the most clinically tested.

**REMEMBER:** *Fat carries the lipophilic drug in; antibiotics break the oestrogen recycling loop.*

## 98. Anticancer Drug Classes

**PEARL:** **5-Fluorouracil is an ANTIMETABOLITE (pyrimidine analogue), not an alkylating agent.** Alkylating agents include **nitrogen mustards (cyclophosphamide, melphalan, chlorambucil), nitrosoureas (carmustine, lomustine), busulfan and the triazenes**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The two classes damage DNA at different points in the cell cycle. **Alkylating agents cross-link DNA directly and are CELL-CYCLE NON-SPECIFIC** — they work on resting cells. **Antimetabolites masquerade as nucleotide precursors and can only act during S phase**, so they are **cell-cycle specific** and require dividing cells. This determines scheduling: continuous infusion favours antimetabolites, bolus dosing suits alkylators.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Cell-cycle non-specific**: alkylating agents, **platinum compounds (cisplatin — nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, peripheral neuropathy)**, **antitumour antibiotics (doxorubicin — cardiotoxicity; bleomycin — pulmonary fibrosis; actinomycin-D)**.
- **Cell-cycle specific**: **S phase — antimetabolites (methotrexate, 5-FU, cytarabine, 6-MP, gemcitabine)**; **M phase — vinca alkaloids (block polymerisation; vincristine → neuropathy) and taxanes (block depolymerisation)**; **G2 — bleomycin, etoposide (topoisomerase II)**.
- Signature toxicities: **cyclophosphamide — haemorrhagic cystitis (prevent with MESNA) and SIADH**; **carmustine/busulfan — pulmonary fibrosis**; **methotrexate — mucositis and myelosuppression (rescue with folinic acid)**; **5-FU — mucositis, diarrhoea, hand-foot syndrome, worse in DPD deficiency**.
- Targeted agents: **trastuzumab (anti-HER2 antibody, cardiotoxic)**; **LAPATINIB — an oral small-molecule EGFR/HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibitor used with capecitabine in TRASTUZUMAB-REFRACTORY HER2-positive breast cancer** (adverse effects: acneiform rash, diarrhoea, QT prolongation, hepatotoxicity). **Vemurafenib — BRAF V600E; vismodegib — hedgehog/PTCH (basal cell carcinoma); erlotinib — EGFR (lung).**
- **Aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid are LYSINE ANALOGUES** — they block plasminogen binding to fibrin and are the **antidote to fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) therapy**; use only for life-threatening bleeding, as they can precipitate thrombosis/DIC.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Class identification, plus the targeted-agent matching (**lapatinib for trastuzumab resistance**).

**REMEMBER:** *Alkylators hit any cell; antimetabolites need a dividing one.*

## 99. Botulinum Toxin

**PEARL:** **Botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE proteins and blocks the RELEASE of acetylcholine** from the presynaptic terminal — it does not block the receptor.

**UNDERSTAND:** Neurotransmitter release requires **SNARE-mediated fusion of the vesicle with the presynaptic membrane**. Botulinum toxin is a **zinc protease** that cleaves SNAP-25, syntaxin or synaptobrevin, so the vesicle can never dock — producing a **long-lasting flaccid paralysis** that only resolves when new nerve terminals sprout, which takes about **3 months**. That regeneration timeline is exactly why cosmetic and therapeutic injections must be repeated quarterly.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Therapeutic uses: **glabellar wrinkles, blepharospasm, hemifacial spasm, cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis), spasticity in cerebral palsy and post-stroke, achalasia, anal fissure, axillary hyperhidrosis, chronic migraine, overactive bladder**.
- Adverse effects: **ptosis, diplopia, dysphagia, dry mouth, local weakness**; systemic spread can cause **respiratory paralysis**.
- **Botulism** — the disease — is a **descending, symmetrical, flaccid paralysis with prominent bulbar involvement, dilated fixed pupils and NO sensory loss**; from **home-canned food (preformed toxin)**, **infant botulism (honey, spores)** or wound botulism.
- Contrast **tetanospasmin**, which also cleaves SNARE proteins but blocks the release of the **inhibitory transmitters glycine and GABA** in Renshaw cells — hence **spastic** rather than flaccid paralysis.
- Contrast **Lambert–Eaton syndrome** (antibodies to presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels; improves with repeated activity) and **myasthenia gravis** (postsynaptic receptor antibodies; worsens with activity).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mechanism recall. The distractors offer receptor blockade — the toxin acts **presynaptically on release**, not on the receptor.

**REMEMBER:** *No docking, no release, no acetylcholine.*

## 100. Diuretic Choice in Cirrhotic Ascites

**PEARL:** **Aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone or eplerenone) are the diuretics of choice in cirrhotic ascites** — not loop diuretics alone.

**UNDERSTAND:** Cirrhosis causes **splanchnic vasodilation → reduced effective arterial blood volume → activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system → SECONDARY HYPERALDOSTERONISM**. Since the sodium retention is aldosterone-driven, blocking the mineralocorticoid receptor addresses the actual mechanism. Loop diuretics alone work poorly because the distal tubule simply reabsorbs the extra sodium delivered to it under aldosterone drive.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Standard regimen: **spironolactone 100 mg + furosemide 40 mg, maintaining the 100:40 ratio** to keep potassium stable; titrate together.
- **Eplerenone** is more selective, avoiding **gynaecomastia** — the main reason to switch from spironolactone in men.
- Aim for weight loss of **≤ 0.5 kg/day without oedema and ≤ 1 kg/day with oedema** to avoid precipitating hepatorenal syndrome and encephalopathy.
- Also: **salt restriction (< 2 g/day)**; **large-volume paracentesis with albumin cover (6–8 g per litre removed)** for tense ascites; **TIPS** for refractory ascites.
- **Diuretic sites of action:** **carbonic anhydrase inhibitors — proximal tubule; loop diuretics — thick ascending limb (NKCC2); thiazides — distal convoluted tubule (NCC); potassium-sparing — collecting duct (mineralocorticoid receptor or ENaC)**; **mannitol — osmotic, throughout**.
- Digoxin, in contrast to ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers and aldosterone antagonists, **does NOT prevent ventricular remodelling** in heart failure — it improves symptoms and reduces hospitalisation but **not mortality**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Preferred diuretic in cirrhosis" → an **aldosterone antagonist**. The heart failure question tests the same class distinction: which drugs are **disease-modifying** versus **symptomatic**.

**REMEMBER:** *Cirrhotic ascites is an aldosterone problem, so block aldosterone.*

## 101. Cancer-Associated Thrombosis and Anticoagulant Choice

**PEARL:** **Low-molecular-weight heparin is the anticoagulant of choice for cancer-associated venous thromboembolism** (CLOT trial); **warfarin is inferior**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Cancer patients on warfarin have **unpredictable INRs** — from vomiting, poor nutrition, drug interactions with chemotherapy, and hepatic involvement — and a **high recurrence rate despite therapeutic INRs**, because tumour-derived tissue factor drives thrombosis through pathways warfarin controls poorly. LMWH gives **predictable anticoagulation without monitoring**, which is why it became standard.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Venous thromboembolism is the second leading cause of death in cancer patients receiving chemotherapy.**
- **Direct oral anticoagulants (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban)** are now accepted alternatives, **except with gastrointestinal or genitourinary tumours**, where bleeding risk is higher.
- **Unfractionated heparin** is preferred in **severe renal failure** (LMWH is renally cleared) and when rapid reversal may be needed (**protamine** fully reverses UFH but only partially reverses LMWH).
- **Fondaparinux** for **heparin-induced thrombocytopenia**.
- LMWH advantages: **higher bioavailability, longer half-life, predictable dose response, no routine monitoring** (measure **anti-Xa** if needed — in renal failure, obesity, pregnancy).
- **Warfarin is contraindicated in pregnancy** (except mechanical valves in some protocols) — LMWH is used instead.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Straight drug-of-choice recall, but know **when UFH or fondaparinux replaces LMWH**.

**REMEMBER:** *Cancer makes warfarin unpredictable, so use heparin.*

## 102. Alzheimer Drugs and the Transdermal Route

**PEARL:** **Rivastigmine is available as a TRANSDERMAL PATCH** — the choice when an elderly patient with dementia cannot take or will not comply with oral medication.

**UNDERSTAND:** Cholinesterase inhibitors cause **dose-limiting cholinergic GI effects** because oral dosing produces **sharp peak plasma concentrations**. A patch delivers the drug at a **steady, low rate over 24 hours**, smoothing those peaks and bypassing the gastric mucosa entirely — so the same daily dose is far better tolerated, and a single application replaces multiple tablets in a forgetful patient.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Rivastigmine inhibits BOTH acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase**, and is licensed for **Alzheimer disease AND Parkinson disease dementia**.
- **Donepezil** — long half-life, once daily, purely central AChE inhibition; **galantamine** — AChE inhibition plus **allosteric nicotinic modulation**.
- **Memantine** — an **NMDA receptor antagonist** for moderate-to-severe disease; can be combined with a cholinesterase inhibitor.
- Cholinesterase inhibitor adverse effects: **nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, bradycardia and syncope** — caution with beta-blockers and in sick sinus syndrome.
- Newer disease-modifying agents: **anti-amyloid monoclonals (lecanemab, donanemab)** — risk of **ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities)**.
- Other useful transdermal drugs: **fentanyl, buprenorphine, nitroglycerin, hyoscine, oestradiol, nicotine, clonidine, selegiline, rotigotine**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The clue is **non-compliance with oral drugs** — which selects the drug with a patch formulation, not the "best" cholinesterase inhibitor.

**REMEMBER:** *A patch smooths the peaks and needs no swallowing.*

## 103. Metered-Dose Inhalers and Spacers

**PEARL:** The commonest drawback of a metered-dose inhaler is **OROPHARYNGEAL drug deposition** — leading to local side effects and systemic absorption of the swallowed fraction. A **spacer** is the solution.

**UNDERSTAND:** A pMDI fires an aerosol at **high velocity** in large droplets, and most of it **impacts the back of the throat** rather than reaching the lower airway — especially if inhalation is not perfectly coordinated with actuation. A spacer **holds the aerosol in a chamber, slows the particles and lets the propellant evaporate**, reducing particle size so that a much greater proportion reaches the bronchi.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Benefits of a spacer: **less oropharyngeal deposition, less swallowed drug and fewer systemic effects, better lung deposition, and no need for hand–breath coordination** — hence essential in **children (usable from ~3 years, with a mask in infants) and in the elderly or acutely breathless**.
- Local effects of inhaled corticosteroids from oropharyngeal deposition: **oral candidiasis and dysphonia** — reduced by a spacer and by **rinsing the mouth after use**.
- **Dry powder inhalers** need adequate **inspiratory flow** and are not usable in severe acute attacks or by small children; **nebulisers** need no coordination at all.
- Only about **10–20% of an MDI dose reaches the lung** without a spacer; most of the rest is swallowed and undergoes **first-pass metabolism** (which is why fluticasone, with high first-pass clearance, has fewer systemic effects than older steroids).
- **Acitretin** requires **contraception for 3 YEARS after stopping** (contrast **isotretinoin — 1 month**), is contraindicated in pregnancy, and relatively contraindicated in **hyperlipidaemia, alcoholism, hepatic and renal disease**; half-life ~50 h; causes photosensitivity.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Device image → the answer is the deposition problem, and the follow-up is always the **spacer**.

**REMEMBER:** *Slow the spray down and it reaches the lung instead of the throat.*

## 104. Lipid Drugs — Specific Niches

**PEARL:** **NIACIN is the only lipid-lowering drug that significantly lowers LIPOPROTEIN(a).** **COLESEVELAM is the only bile acid sequestrant licensed to improve glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Lipoprotein(a) is an **LDL particle with apolipoprotein(a) attached** — it is not cleared by LDL receptors, so statins (which up-regulate those receptors) barely touch it. Niacin lowers it by **reducing hepatic apo(a) production**, a different mechanism entirely. Colesevelam's glucose effect is thought to be mediated through **bile-acid signalling at FXR and TGR5 receptors** affecting incretin release.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Niacin** — inhibits **hormone-sensitive lipase** in adipose tissue, reducing free fatty acid delivery to the liver; lowers **LDL, VLDL, triglycerides and Lp(a)**, and **raises HDL the most** of any drug. Adverse effects: **cutaneous flushing and pruritus (prostaglandin-mediated — pretreat with aspirin), hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia/gout, hyperglycaemia**, and **myopathy with statins**.
- **Colesevelam** has fewer GI effects and drug interactions than **cholestyramine and colestipol**; all raise triglycerides and can cause constipation and fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption.
- **New Lp(a)-lowering agents** (pelacarsen, olpasiran) are in trials — currently no routine therapy exists.
- **Vancomycin dosing uses the MATZKE nomogram** (based on creatinine clearance); the **RUMACK–MATTHEW nomogram** is for **paracetamol** hepatotoxicity risk after a single acute ingestion.
- **Scrub typhus: doxycycline is the drug of choice; AZITHROMYCIN in pregnancy** (chloramphenicol is the alternative).
- **Carboprost (PGF2α) is absolutely contraindicated in ASTHMA** — bronchoconstriction; use **oxytocin** first line for PPH, and avoid **methylergometrine** in hypertension and heart disease.
- **ACE inhibitors cause HYPERkalaemia**, not hypokalaemia; other effects include **cough, angioedema, dysgeusia, rash, teratogenicity** and rarely **neutropenia**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Single-fact recall clustered around lipid drugs, nomograms and obstetric prostaglandins.

**REMEMBER:** *Niacin is the Lp(a) drug; colesevelam is the diabetic's resin.*

## 105. Pharmacogenetics

## 106. New Drug Application Timing

**PEARL:** The NDA is filed **after phase 3**. Phase 3 is also called the **multisite/multicentric trial**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Phase 1: healthy volunteers (20–100), safety/PK. Phase 2: patients (100–300), efficacy + dose. Phase 3: large multicentre RCT (500–3000). Phase 4: post-marketing surveillance (pharmacovigilance).
- Phase 0 = microdosing, subtherapeutic, human PK only.

**REMEMBER:** *NDA after Phase 3; Phase 4 needs no NDA (it's post-marketing).*

## 107. Pharmacokinetic Formulas

**PEARL:**

- **Loading dose = Vd × desired steady-state plasma concentration**
- **Maintenance dose = Clearance × desired steady-state plasma concentration**

**MUST KNOW:**

- Loading dose depends only on **Vd** — unchanged in renal/hepatic failure; maintenance dose depends on **clearance** — must be reduced in organ failure.
- Divide by bioavailability (F) for oral dosing.
- Steady state is reached in **4–5 half-lives** regardless of dose.

**REMEMBER:** *Load by volume, maintain by clearance.*

## 108. Drug–Protein Binding

**PEARL:** **Albumin** binds **acidic** drugs (phenytoin, NSAIDs, warfarin, sulfonamides). **α1-acid glycoprotein** binds **basic** drugs (lignocaine, disopyramide, quinidine, propranolol, imipramine).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Only the **free (unbound)** fraction is pharmacologically active and available for metabolism/excretion.
- Hypoalbuminemia (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis) → raised free phenytoin with a normal total level → toxicity at "therapeutic" levels.
- α1-acid glycoprotein is an **acute phase reactant** — rises in MI/inflammation, lowering free lignocaine.

**REMEMBER:** *Albumin = Acidic; α1-Acid Glycoprotein = Basic (name is the opposite of its cargo).*

## 109. P-glycoprotein and Digoxin Toxicity

**PEARL:** P-gp (MDR1, **CD243**) is the efflux pump chiefly responsible for digoxin elimination. Inhibiting P-gp → digoxin toxicity. **Clarithromycin/erythromycin** are classic culprits.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Other P-gp inhibitors: **amiodarone, quinidine, verapamil, diltiazem, cyclosporine, itraconazole, propafenone, flecainide**.
- P-gp inducer: **rifampicin** (lowers digoxin levels).
- P-gp is MDR**1**, not MDR2; it also limits drug entry at the blood–brain barrier and gut.
- Digoxin toxicity: nausea, **yellow-green vision (xanthopsia)**, arrhythmia with hypokalemia; antidote = digoxin-specific Fab.

**REMEMBER:** *P-gp inhibitor + digoxin = toxicity; rifampicin does the opposite.*

## 110. CYP Interactions Worth Memorizing

**PEARL:**

- **Verapamil (CYP3A4 inhibitor) + statins → myopathy/rhabdomyolysis** (atorvastatin, simvastatin, lovastatin are 3A4-metabolized).
- **Erythromycin (CYP1A2 inhibitor) + theophylline → theophylline toxicity.**
- **Ritonavir/lopinavir (potent CYP3A4 inhibitors) + ergotamine → ergotism** (limb ischemia, gangrene).
- **CYP2C9 inhibitors:** amiodarone, azoles, cimetidine, clopidogrel, cotrimoxazole, disulfiram, isoniazid, metronidazole (→ raise warfarin/phenytoin levels).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Statin myopathy is the key toxicity — check CPK; rosuvastatin/pravastatin are less 3A4-dependent.
- **Clarithromycin also inhibits OATP1B1/1B3**, raising **rosuvastatin and pravastatin** — so no statin is entirely safe with it.

**REMEMBER:** *3A4 inhibitors: statins → muscle, ergot → gangrene.*

## 113. Ranolazine and Trimetazidine — Metabolic Antianginals

## 116. Amiodarone — Two Key Toxicities

**PEARL:**

## 119. Antiplatelet Mechanisms — Quick Grid

**PEARL:**

- **Aspirin** irreversibly inhibits **COX-1** → no **TXA2** (a platelet aggregator and vasoconstrictor) → bleeding tendency for the platelet's 7–10 day life.
- **Clopidogrel/prasugrel/ticagrelor** = **P2Y12 (ADP)** blockers.
- **Prasugrel** is a **prodrug**, irreversible, activated by **CYP3A4/2B6** — hence **omeprazole (a CYP2C19 inhibitor) does not blunt it**, unlike clopidogrel. It is ~10× more potent than clopidogrel with faster, more predictable onset.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Clopidogrel needs **CYP2C19** → poor metabolizers and omeprazole reduce its effect. Ticagrelor is reversible and not a prodrug.

**REMEMBER:** *Clopidogrel–2C19–omeprazole problem; prasugrel escapes it.*

## 120. Anticoagulant Reversal Agents

- **Dabigatran is the only oral direct thrombin inhibitor** in clinical use; parenteral DTIs = bivalirudin, argatroban, **lepirudin**.

## 122. Tranexamic Acid & ε-Aminocaproic Acid

**PEARL:** Both are **lysine analogues** that competitively occupy the lysine-binding sites on plasminogen/plasmin, preventing binding to fibrin → **inhibit fibrinolysis**. They are the **antidote to fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) therapy**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- TXA is given IV in **trauma resuscitation (CRASH-2: within 3 h)**, massive hemorrhage, and **postpartum hemorrhage (WOMAN trial)**.
- TXA is ~10× more potent than EACA.
- Also used in menorrhagia, hereditary angioedema prophylaxis, and dental extraction in hemophilia.

**REMEMBER:** *Lysine analogue = anti-fibrinolytic = thrombolytic antidote.*

## 123. Timolol in Glaucoma

**PEARL:** Timolol is a **non-selective β-blocker** that lowers IOP by **reducing aqueous humour production** at the ciliary epithelium.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Ocular ADRs: allergy, **decreased corneal sensation**, punctate keratitis, dry eye.
- Systemic absorption via nasolacrimal duct → **bronchospasm and bradycardia** — avoid in asthma/COPD and heart block (press on the punctum after instilling).
- It does **not** cause miosis or accommodative spasm (unlike pilocarpine) — no dim vision/brow ache.

**REMEMBER:** *Timolol reduces production; asthma is the trap.*

---

## 124. Prostaglandin Analogues in Glaucoma

**PEARL:** PG analogues (latanoprost etc.) are best avoided/last-line when **macular edema** is a concern — cystoid macular oedema is a known side effect.

**MUST KNOW:** They lower IOP most effectively, by increasing **uveoscleral outflow**; ADRs: iris/periocular hyperpigmentation, eyelash growth (used as bimatoprost for hypotrichosis), uveitis reactivation, and CME in aphakic/pseudophakic eyes.

**REMEMBER:** *Latanoprost = best pressure drop, but macular edema + brown iris.*

---

## 125. Brimonidine in Infants

**PEARL:** Brimonidine (α2 agonist) **crosses the blood–brain barrier** → CNS depression and **apnoea** in neonates. **Contraindicated below 2 years of age.**

**REMEMBER:** *Brimonidine = baby apnoea; banned under 2.*

---

## 126. Phenylephrine vs Naloxone in Opioid Miosis

**PEARL:** Phenylephrine (α1 agonist) causes mydriasis by contracting the dilator pupillae, but it will **not reverse opioid-induced miosis**, which is centrally driven (Edinger–Westphal nucleus disinhibition).

**MUST KNOW:** Reverse it with an **opioid antagonist** — naloxone (or levallorphan).

**REMEMBER:** *Opioid miosis is central — you need naloxone, not a mydriatic.*

---

## 127. Levallorphan

**PEARL:** Levallorphan is an opioid antagonist similar to naloxone — reverses severe opioid-induced **respiratory depression**.

**MUST KNOW:** Naloxone is the drug of choice (pure antagonist, IV, t½ ~1 h — repeat doses/infusion needed as it is shorter-acting than most opioids). Naltrexone = oral, long-acting, for maintenance/de-addiction.

**REMEMBER:** *Naloxone = acute overdose; naltrexone = long-term abstinence.*

---

## 128. Levopropoxyphene

**PEARL:** Levopropoxyphene is a **centrally acting opioid antitussive** with **no analgesic activity**; dose 50–100 mg every 4 h; may cause sedation.

**MUST KNOW:** Opioid antitussives: codeine (standard), pholcodine, dextromethorphan (non-opioid sigma/NMDA action, no addiction), noscapine.

**REMEMBER:** *Levopropoxyphene = cough only, no pain relief.*

---

## 129. Tramadol

**PEARL:** Tramadol acts at the **µ-opioid receptor AND inhibits serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Hence risks: **serotonin syndrome** (with SSRIs/MAOIs) and **seizures** (lowers seizure threshold).
- Naloxone reverses only the opioid component.
- It is a **preferred analgesic in CKD** along with fentanyl (see next pearl).

**REMEMBER:** *Tramadol = opioid + SNRI → serotonin syndrome and fits.*

---

## 130. Pentazocine

**PEARL:** Pentazocine is a **partial agonist at µ** and a **full agonist at κ** receptors.

**MUST KNOW:**

- κ agonism → analgesia + **dysphoria/hallucinations**; µ partial agonism → can **precipitate withdrawal** in a morphine-dependent patient and has a **ceiling effect**.
- Raises catecholamines → ↑BP and cardiac work — avoid in MI (unlike morphine).

**REMEMBER:** *Pentazocine = kappa full, mu partial → dysphoria + withdrawal in addicts.*

---

## 131. Analgesia in CKD

**PEARL:** **Paracetamol (acetaminophen)** is the safest non-narcotic analgesic in CKD; for severe pain use **tramadol or fentanyl**.

**MUST KNOW:** Avoid NSAIDs (reduce afferent arteriolar flow, AKI, interstitial nephritis) and **morphine/pethidine** (active metabolites morphine-6-glucuronide and norpethidine accumulate → sedation/seizures). Fentanyl has no active metabolites.

**REMEMBER:** *CKD pain ladder: paracetamol → tramadol/fentanyl. Never NSAIDs or morphine.*

---

## 132. Arachidonic Acid Pathway Inhibitors

**PEARL:**

- **Corticosteroids** → inhibit **phospholipase A2** (via lipocortin/annexin) → block **both** prostaglandins and leukotrienes.
- **NSAIDs** → inhibit **cyclooxygenase** → block prostaglandins/thromboxanes only.
- **Zileuton** → inhibits **5-lipoxygenase** → blocks leukotrienes. (Montelukast/zafirlukast block the CysLT1 **receptor**.)

**REMEMBER:** *Steroid blocks the tap (PLA2); NSAID blocks one pipe (COX); zileuton blocks the other (LOX).*

---

## 133. Eicosanoids

**PEARL:** Eicosanoids (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes) are the most **universally distributed autacoids** — nearly every cell makes one or more. **Platelets mainly make thromboxane (TXA2)**; **endothelium mainly makes PGI2 (prostacyclin)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Platelet = TXA2 (aggregate, vasoconstrict); Endothelium = PGI2 (opposite).*

---

## 134. Disulfiram

**PEARL:** Disulfiram is an **irreversible inhibitor of aldehyde dehydrogenase** (both cytosolic and mitochondrial) → acetaldehyde accumulates. It works by **aversive conditioning**, **not** anti-craving.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Reaction: flushing, throbbing headache, vomiting, hypotension, palpitations, dread.
- **Anti-craving** drugs: **naltrexone, acamprosate** (NMDA modulator), topiramate, baclofen.
- Disulfiram-like reaction also from **metronidazole, tinidazole, cefoperazone, chlorpropamide, griseofulvin**.
- It also inhibits dopamine β-hydroxylase → can precipitate psychosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Disulfiram = aversion (ALDH); naltrexone/acamprosate = craving.*

---

## 135. Oxazepam in Liver Disease

**PEARL:** **Oxazepam** (with lorazepam and temazepam) is safe in hepatic impairment — short-acting and cleared by direct **glucuronidation only**, with no active metabolites and no CYP oxidation.

**REMEMBER:** *"LOT" (Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam) = liver-friendly benzodiazepines.*

---

## 136. Oxcarbazepine and Hyponatremia

**PEARL:** **Hyponatremia occurs more often with oxcarbazepine than carbamazepine.**

**MUST KNOW:** Mechanism = SIADH-like effect; risk rises with age, diuretics, high dose. Oxcarbazepine otherwise has fewer interactions (no epoxide metabolite, weaker enzyme induction) and no need for blood-count monitoring.

**REMEMBER:** *Oxcarbazepine drops sodium more than carbamazepine.*

---

## 137. Carbamazepine — Idiosyncratic Reactions

**PEARL:** Idiosyncratic blood dyscrasias include fatal **aplastic anaemia and agranulocytosis**; the **commonest** idiosyncratic reaction is an **erythematous rash**, and rarely **SJS/TEN**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **HLA-B*1502** (Han Chinese/South-East Asian) predicts SJS/TEN → test before starting.
- Also causes hyponatremia (SIADH), diplopia/ataxia (dose-related), and is a potent **CYP3A4 inducer** with **auto-induction**.
- DOC for trigeminal neuralgia; can worsen absence and myoclonic seizures.

**REMEMBER:** *Carbamazepine: rash common, aplastic anaemia rare but fatal, HLA-B*1502 screen.*

---

## 138. Retigabine (Ezogabine)

**PEARL:** A **K⁺ channel opener** (enhances **KCNQ** transmembrane potassium currents) approved as add-on for focal-onset seizures in adults >18 y; **withdrawn** because of **retinal abnormalities and blue-grey pigmentation** of skin/nails/retina.

**MUST KNOW:** Metabolized by **glucuronidation and acetylation**; t½ 7–11 h → thrice-daily dosing. **Phenytoin/carbamazepine lower its plasma level** → increase the retigabine dose when co-administered.

**REMEMBER:** *Only antiepileptic that opens K⁺ channels — killed by blue retina.*

---

## 139. Sodium Valproate for Refractory Rheumatic Chorea

**PEARL:** **Sodium valproate** is the drug used for refractory/intractable rheumatic (Sydenham's) chorea — preferred because it lacks **extrapyramidal** side effects.

**MUST KNOW:** Alternatives: carbamazepine; haloperidol/antipsychotics work but cause EPS. Most Sydenham chorea is self-limiting — treat only if disabling.

**REMEMBER:** *Sydenham chorea → valproate (no EPS).*

---

## 140. Memantine

**PEARL:** Memantine is a **non-competitive (open-channel, low-affinity) NMDA antagonist** that limits glutamate excitotoxicity and slows progression in **moderate-to-severe** Alzheimer's disease.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Can be given alone or **with cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine — used from mild disease onward).
- Titrate 5 mg OD up to 10 mg BD; give for ≥6 months.
- **Renally excreted** → dose-adjust in renal failure; ADRs are dizziness, headache, confusion.

**REMEMBER:** *Mild AD = cholinesterase inhibitor; moderate-severe = add memantine (renal drug).*

---

## 141. Piracetam

**PEARL:** Piracetam is a **nootropic** (cognition enhancer) used off-label for memory and, importantly, for **myoclonus**.

**REMEMBER:** *Piracetam = nootropic + myoclonus.*

---

## 142. Edaravone

**PEARL:** Edaravone (a free-radical scavenger) is FDA-approved for **amyotrophic lateral sclerosis**.

**MUST KNOW:** The other ALS drug is **riluzole** (inhibits glutamate release) — the only one shown to prolong survival. Both slow, neither cures.

**REMEMBER:** *ALS = Riluzole + Edaravone.*

---

## 143. Trihexyphenidyl

**PEARL:** **Trihexyphenidyl**, a central anticholinergic, is the **drug of choice for drug-induced parkinsonism**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Antipsychotics block D2 → relative cholinergic excess in the striatum; restoring the dopamine–acetylcholine balance requires an anticholinergic, not levodopa (which would worsen psychosis and is ineffective while receptors are blocked).

**MUST KNOW:** Anticholinergics help tremor and rigidity, **not bradykinesia**; avoid in the elderly (confusion) and in **tardive dyskinesia** (they worsen it).

**REMEMBER:** *Drug-induced parkinsonism → anticholinergic, never levodopa.*

---

## 144. Muscarinic Receptor Subtypes

**PEARL:** M1–M5. Broad effects:

- **M1** (CNS, gastric parietal): cognition, gastric acid, **increased seizure activity**
- **M2** (heart, presynaptic): **bradycardia**, tremors, bronchoconstriction, abdominal cramps
- **M3** (smooth muscle/glands): **bladder contraction, miosis, salivary and bronchial secretion**
- **M4/M5**: CNS — M4 cataplexy-like muscle weakness; M5 dopamine release

**MUST KNOW:**

- Odd receptors (M1, M3, M5) → **Gq → IP3/DAG**; even (M2, M4) → **Gi → ↓cAMP + GIRK opening**.
- M3 blockade explains antimuscarinic side effects: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision.

**REMEMBER:** *Odd = Gq (excitatory), Even = Gi (inhibitory).*

---

## 145. GIRK-Coupled Receptors

**PEARL:** These Gi/Go receptors open **G-protein–coupled inwardly rectifying K⁺ channels (GIRK)** → K⁺ efflux → **hyperpolarization/inhibition**: **M2 muscarinic, A1 adenosine, α2 adrenergic, D2 dopamine, 5-HT1, somatostatin, GABA-B, opioid**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The **AT1 (angiotensin 1)** receptor is **Gq** — it acts through IP3/DAG and Ca²⁺, **not** through K⁺ channels. Classic "which does NOT" answer.

**REMEMBER:** *Inhibitory GPCRs open K⁺ (GIRK); AT1 is Gq/Ca²⁺.*

---

## 146. Presynaptic K⁺ Channel Blockade

**PEARL:** Blocking presynaptic **potassium** channels prolongs depolarization → more **voltage-gated Ca²⁺** channel opening → **increased acetylcholine release** at the NMJ.

**MUST KNOW:** This is the basis of **3,4-diaminopyridine/amifampridine** in **Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome**, and of 4-aminopyridine (dalfampridine) in multiple sclerosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Block K⁺ → Ca²⁺ enters → ACh released (LEMS drug).*

---

## 147. Drugs to Avoid in Myasthenia Gravis

**PEARL:** **Aminoglycosides** block Ca²⁺-mediated presynaptic ACh exocytosis and also directly inhibit post-synaptic ACh receptors → worsen MG.

**MUST KNOW:** Other aggravators: **quinolones, macrolides, β-blockers, botulinum toxin, local anaesthetics, magnesium, non-depolarizing muscle relaxants, penicillamine, quinine, phenytoin, chloroquine**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** **Penicillamine** can actually *induce* MG (antibody-mediated); **magnesium** (as in eclampsia therapy) can precipitate a crisis.

**REMEMBER:** *MG mnemonic: "A-B-C" — Aminoglycosides/Antiarrhythmics, Beta-blockers/Botulinum, Ca-blockers/Chloroquine + Magnesium & Penicillamine.*

---

## 148. Adrenaline — Doses

**PEARL:** For anaphylactic shock give **adrenaline 1:1000, 0.5 mL IM** (= 0.5 mg) into the **anterolateral thigh**, repeatable every 5–15 minutes.

**MUST KNOW:**

- 1:1000 = 1 mg/mL (IM); **1:10,000 = 0.1 mg/mL is the IV/cardiac arrest strength**.
- Paediatric dose: 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.5 mg).
- IM thigh gives faster peak levels than subcutaneous or deltoid.

**REMEMBER:** *Anaphylaxis = 1:1000, 0.5 mL, IM thigh. Arrest = 1:10,000 IV.*

---

## 149. Ipratropium — Paradoxical Bronchoconstriction

**PEARL:** Paradoxical bronchoconstriction, especially by **nebulizer**, due to: **hypotonic nebulizer solution**, **preservatives (benzalkonium chloride, EDTA)**, and blockade of **prejunctional M2 autoreceptors** (which normally brake ACh release) → more ACh released.

**REMEMBER:** *M2 autoreceptor block = more ACh = wheeze; blame benzalkonium too.*

---

## 150. Salbutamol Causes Hyperglycemia

**PEARL:** Salbutamol causes **hyperglycemia**, not hypoglycemia (β2 → glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis).

**MUST KNOW:** Other β2 effects: **hypokalemia** (K⁺ driven intracellularly — used therapeutically in hyperkalemia), tremor, tachycardia, lactic acidosis.

**REMEMBER:** *β2 agonist: sugar up, potassium down.*

---

## 151. Theophylline

**PEARL:** Metabolized mainly by **CYP1A2**; **erythromycin (and ciprofloxacin, cimetidine)** inhibit CYP1A2 → theophylline toxicity (arrhythmias, seizures).

**MUST KNOW:** **Smoking induces CYP1A2** → smokers need higher doses; toxicity may appear on quitting. Narrow therapeutic index (10–20 µg/mL).

**REMEMBER:** *Erythromycin raises, smoking lowers, theophylline.*

---

## 152. SGLT2 Inhibitors — Adverse Effects

**PEARL:** Main ADRs are **genital mycotic infections and lower urinary tract infections** from glycosuria. FDA has also warned of an **increased fracture risk**, with effects on **PTH and 1,25-(OH)2 vitamin D** disturbing bone mineral balance.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis** — the high-yield emergency; stop before surgery/fasting.
- Rare but classic: **Fournier's gangrene**, volume depletion/hypotension.
- Benefits: proven cardiovascular and **renal** protection, weight and BP reduction.

**REMEMBER:** *Sugar in urine = yeast + UTI; watch euglycemic DKA and bones.*

---

## 153. Linagliptin in Renal Failure

**PEARL:** **Linagliptin needs no dose adjustment in renal impairment** — it is the DPP-4 inhibitor with predominant **biliary/faecal excretion**.

**MUST KNOW:** Sitagliptin, saxagliptin and vildagliptin are renally excreted and require dose reduction. Class ADRs: pancreatitis, arthralgia, urticaria/angioedema (with ACE inhibitors); weight-neutral, low hypoglycemia risk.

**REMEMBER:** *Linagliptin = "liver-gliptin" = safe kidney.*

---

## 154. 5α-Reductase Inhibitors

**PEARL:** Finasteride/dutasteride competitively inhibit **5α-reductase**, which converts testosterone to the more potent **DHT** → ↓DHT, **↓PSA (roughly halved)**, ↓prostate volume — while **plasma testosterone and LH remain unchanged**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Because PSA halves, **double the measured PSA** when screening a patient on finasteride.
- Takes **6 months** for maximal benefit; ADRs: decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, gynecomastia.
- Also reduces prostate bleeding/hematuria; finasteride 1 mg for androgenetic alopecia. **Category X — pregnant women must not handle crushed tablets.**

**REMEMBER:** *DHT down, PSA halves, testosterone unchanged.*

---

## 155. Tamsulosin for Postoperative Urinary Retention

**PEARL:** **Tamsulosin** is used for postoperative urinary retention, and is the α-blocker of choice in a **COPD** patient (α1A-selective — no bronchial or significant cardiovascular effect).

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **retrograde ejaculation**, dizziness/postural hypotension, and **intraoperative floppy iris syndrome** — always ask before cataract surgery.

**REMEMBER:** *Tamsulosin = uroselective; think floppy iris.*

---

## 156. Erection is Muscarinic (Cholinergic)

**PEARL:** Erection is mediated by **muscarinic (parasympathetic)** transmission → NO release; ejaculation is sympathetic.

**REMEMBER:** *"Point (Parasympathetic) and Shoot (Sympathetic)."*

## 157. Sucralfate

**PEARL:** In an acidic environment (**pH < 4**) sucralfate polymerizes into a viscous sticky gel that adheres to the ulcer crater for ~6 hours.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Therefore give **on an empty stomach** and **do not combine with antacids/PPIs** which raise pH and abolish its action.
- It **blocks absorption of other drugs** — phenytoin, digoxin, cimetidine, ketoconazole, fluoroquinolones, levothyroxine → separate doses by **≥2 hours**.
- **Constipation** is the commonest ADR; contains **aluminium** → avoid in renal failure (aluminium toxicity/encephalopathy); can cause **bezoars**.

**REMEMBER:** *Sucralfate needs acid, sticks to ulcers, steals other drugs — space by 2 hours.*

---

## 158. Prokinetics

**PEARL:** Prokinetics increase GI motility:

- **D2 antagonists** — metoclopramide, domperidone
- **5-HT4 agonists** — cisapride, prucalopride, velusetrag, naronapride
- **Motilin/motilin-receptor agonists** — erythromycin, camicinal
- **Others** — sincalide (CCK octapeptide), relamorelin (ghrelin agonist)

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Prucalopride** is a selective 5-HT4 agonist for chronic constipation — safe because it lacks cisapride's hERG/QT toxicity (cisapride was withdrawn).
- **Domperidone** does not cross the BBB → no extrapyramidal effects, unlike metoclopramide (dystonia, tardive dyskinesia).
- **Diphenylmethane is NOT a prokinetic** — its derivative **bisacodyl** is a stimulant **laxative**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prokinetic ≠ laxative; bisacodyl (diphenylmethane) is a laxative.*

---

## 159. Acute Diarrhea Management

**PEARL:** Acute diarrhea in an **afebrile, stable** patient is managed with **ORS alone** — no antibiotics.

**MUST KNOW:** Add **zinc for 14 days in children** (10 mg <6 months, 20 mg >6 months). Antibiotics only for cholera, dysentery (bloody), severe/systemic illness, or immunocompromised.

**REMEMBER:** *No fever, no blood → ORS (+ zinc in kids).*

## 160. Antibiotic-Associated Colitis

**PEARL:** *Clostridioides difficile* colitis follows destruction of normal gut flora. **Clindamycin (lincosamides)** carries the highest risk per exposure; **fluoroquinolones, broad-spectrum penicillins and cephalosporins** cause the largest number of cases.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Pseudomembranous colitis; toxins A and B; diagnosis by **GDH + toxin EIA/NAAT**.
- Treatment: **oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin** (metronidazole now second-line); recurrent → faecal microbiota transplant, bezlotoxumab.
- Do **not** give antimotility drugs (risk of toxic megacolon).

**REMEMBER:** *Clindamycin = highest risk; treat with oral vancomycin, not IV.*

## 161. Ceftriaxone for Gonorrhea

**PEARL:** **Single dose ceftriaxone 500 mg IM** is the current treatment for gonorrhea. Dual therapy with azithromycin is **no longer routinely recommended**.

**MUST KNOW:** Add doxycycline 100 mg BD × 7 days only if **chlamydial co-infection** is not excluded. Rising azithromycin resistance drove the change; test of cure for pharyngeal infection.

**REMEMBER:** *Gonorrhea = ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once; azithromycin dropped.*

## 162. Doxycycline for Scrub Typhus

**PEARL:** **Doxycycline** is the drug of choice for scrub typhus (*Orientia tsutsugamushi*).

**MUST KNOW:** Alternatives: azithromycin (**preferred in pregnancy and children**), chloramphenicol; rifampicin for doxycycline-resistant areas. Clue: **eschar** + fever + lymphadenopathy; dramatic defervescence within 48 h supports diagnosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Scrub typhus = doxycycline; pregnancy = azithromycin.*

## 163. Chloramphenicol — Gray Baby Syndrome

**PEARL:** Occurs in neonates (especially **premature**) given high doses (~100 mg/kg), often prophylactically.

**UNDERSTAND:** Immature **UDP-glucuronyl transferase** cannot conjugate the drug, and immature renal excretion of the conjugate → accumulation → mitochondrial toxicity.

**MUST KNOW:** Features: vomiting, refusal to feed, abdominal distension, **ashen-grey cyanosis**, hypothermia, flaccidity, circulatory collapse. Chloramphenicol also causes **dose-related reversible marrow suppression** and **idiosyncratic irreversible aplastic anaemia**.

**REMEMBER:** *No glucuronidation → grey baby.*

## 164. Linezolid and Tedizolid

**PEARL:**

- **Linezolid** causes **myelosuppression** — most commonly **thrombocytopenia**, appearing **7–10 days** after starting, and reversible on stopping.
- **Tedizolid** is a newer oxazolidinone, active against **gram-positives** only (poor against gram-negatives and anaerobes), binding the **P site of the 50S** subunit to block protein synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Other linezolid ADRs: peripheral and **optic neuropathy** (prolonged use), lactic acidosis, and **serotonin syndrome** (it is a weak MAO inhibitor — caution with SSRIs).
- Excellent oral bioavailability (~100%) — IV to oral switch without dose change; covers MRSA and VRE.

**REMEMBER:** *Linezolid: platelets down at day 7–10, MAOI effect, 100% oral.*

## 165. Rifampicin

**PEARL:** Rifampicin binds the **β subunit of DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (rpoB)** → suppresses RNA chain formation.

**MUST KNOW:** Resistance = **rpoB mutation** (the target of **GeneXpert/CBNAAT**, used as a surrogate marker for MDR-TB). Potent **CYP3A4 inducer** → OCP failure, reduced warfarin/protease inhibitor levels. Orange-red urine/secretions; flu-like syndrome with intermittent dosing.

**REMEMBER:** *rpoB = rifampicin resistance = GeneXpert.*

## 166. Isoniazid

**PEARL:** INH is a prodrug activated by mycobacterial **KatG**; its reactive metabolite forms an adduct with **NAD** that inhibits **InhA and KasA**, blocking **mycolic acid** synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:** Resistance: **katG** (high-level) and **inhA** (low-level, cross-resistance with ethionamide). ADRs: **peripheral neuropathy** (pyridoxine-responsive, worse in slow acetylators), hepatitis (age-related), lupus-like syndrome, and it is a **CYP inhibitor**.

**REMEMBER:** *KatG activates it, InhA/KasA are the targets, mycolic acid the casualty.*

## 167. Ethambutol

**PEARL:** Inhibits **arabinosyl transferase III** → ↓arabinogalactan → defective mycobacterial cell wall. Key toxicity: **optic (retrobulbar) neuritis** with reduced acuity and **loss of red–green discrimination**.

**MUST KNOW:** Dose-related and largely reversible on stopping; test colour vision before and during treatment; **avoid in young children** who cannot report visual change. It is **bacteriostatic**, added to prevent resistance.

**REMEMBER:** *Ethambutol = Eye (red-green).*

## 168. Pyrazinamide

**PEARL:** A **prodrug** converted by pyrazinamidase (**pncA**) to **pyrazinoic acid**, which acidifies the intracellular environment and **collapses the proton motive force** — killing dormant intracellular bacilli in acidic caseous foci.

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **hyperuricemia/gout** (inhibits urate excretion) and **hepatotoxicity** (the most hepatotoxic first-line drug). Its sterilizing action is why short-course therapy is 6 months.

**REMEMBER:** *PZA works only in acid, gives hyperuricemia and hepatitis.*

---

## 169. Dapsone

**PEARL:** Dapsone inhibits **folic acid synthesis** (dihydropteroate synthase — a sulfone acting like a sulfonamide).

**MUST KNOW:** ADRs: **hemolysis (dose-related, severe in G6PD deficiency)**, **methemoglobinemia**, agranulocytosis, and the **dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome**. Uses beyond leprosy: dermatitis herpetiformis, PCP prophylaxis, linear IgA disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Dapsone = folate blocker → hemolysis + methemoglobinemia; check G6PD.*

---

## 170. Flucytosine

**PEARL:** Flucytosine inhibits **fungal DNA synthesis** (converted by cytosine deaminase into 5-FU → thymidylate synthase inhibition). Clinically useful in the **"3 C's": Cryptococcus, Candida, Chromoblastomycosis**.

**MUST KNOW:** Never used alone (rapid resistance) — combined with **amphotericin B for cryptococcal meningitis** induction. Main toxicity: **bone marrow suppression** (worse in renal impairment, as it is renally excreted).

**REMEMBER:** *Flucytosine = 3 C's, always with amphotericin, marrow toxic.*

## 171. Quinolone Metabolism

**PEARL:**

- **Hepatic:** moxifloxacin, pefloxacin, trovafloxacin (withdrawn for hepatotoxicity) → avoid in liver disease; **not** useful for UTI.
- **Renal:** ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, norfloxacin, lomefloxacin, ofloxacin → dose-adjust in renal failure.

**MUST KNOW:** Class ADRs: **tendinitis/tendon rupture** (Achilles, elderly + steroids), **QT prolongation** (moxifloxacin most), aortic aneurysm/dissection, peripheral neuropathy, dysglycemia, cartilage concerns in children.

**REMEMBER:** *Moxifloxacin = liver = no UTI; the rest = kidney.*

## 172. Indinavir

**PEARL:** Indinavir, a protease inhibitor, classically causes **nephrolithiasis (crystalluria)**, plus the class effects — **central obesity, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance** (lipodystrophy syndrome).

**MUST KNOW:** Prevent stones with high fluid intake. Also causes indirect **hyperbilirubinemia**. All PIs are **CYP3A4 inhibitors** (ritonavir used deliberately as a "booster") → the ergotamine interaction above.

**REMEMBER:** *Indinavir = Indian stones; PIs = fat redistribution + CYP3A4 block.*

## 173. Favipiravir

**PEARL:** Favipiravir (**viral RNA polymerase inhibitor**) was the first **oral** drug approved in India under restricted emergency use for **mild-to-moderate COVID-19** — **not** for severe disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Favipiravir = oral, RNA polymerase, mild-moderate only.*

## 175. Cyclophosphamide

**PEARL:** An **alkylating agent** with antitumour and immunosuppressant use; key toxicities are **hemorrhagic cystitis due to the metabolite acrolein** and later **bladder carcinoma**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Prevent with hydration and **MESNA** (binds acrolein in urine).
- Activated in the **liver by CYP** (a prodrug); also causes **SIADH/hyponatremia**, and is the alkylating agent **least likely to cause thrombocytopenia**.
- Immunosuppressive uses: lupus nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, nephrotic syndrome.

**REMEMBER:** *Acrolein burns the bladder — give MESNA.*

## 176. Methotrexate

**PEARL:** As a DMARD, methotrexate works mainly by **increasing extracellular adenosine** (anti-inflammatory); its antiproliferative action is via inhibition of **AICAR transformylase** and **thymidylate synthetase** (and dihydrofolate reductase).

**MUST KNOW:**

- Anchor DMARD in RA; weekly dosing + **folic acid** to reduce toxicity.
- ADRs: mucositis, marrow suppression, hepatic fibrosis, pneumonitis; **teratogenic** (contraindicated in pregnancy).
- Rescue for high-dose therapy = **leucovorin (folinic acid)**; avoid with NSAIDs/sulfonamides (displacement + reduced renal clearance).

**REMEMBER:** *Adenosine = anti-inflammatory action; folate block = toxicity.*

## 177. Colchicine

**PEARL:** Colchicine binds **tubulin** and prevents **microtubule polymerization** → impaired neutrophil chemotaxis, recruitment and release of chemotactic factors.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Used in acute gout (and prophylaxis when starting allopurinol), familial Mediterranean fever, pericarditis.
- Dose-limiting ADR: **diarrhea/GI upset**; overdose → multiorgan failure, myelosuppression, myopathy.
- It is **not** uricosuric and does not lower urate.

**REMEMBER:** *Colchicine paralyses the neutrophil, not the urate.*

## 178. Drugs Causing Hyperkalemia

**PEARL:** **Triamterene, atenolol (β-blockers), and potassium chloride** cause hyperkalemia.

**MUST KNOW:** Full list: **K⁺-sparing diuretics** (amiloride, triamterene, spironolactone, eplerenone), **ACE inhibitors/ARBs**, NSAIDs, heparin, digoxin toxicity, **succinylcholine**, trimethoprim, cyclosporine/tacrolimus, non-selective β-blockers (block β2-driven cellular K⁺ uptake).

**REMEMBER:** *Anything that blocks aldosterone or β2 raises potassium.*

## 179. ACE Inhibitors/ARBs in Proteinuria

**PEARL:** ACE inhibitors or ARBs are **first-line** in proteinuric renal disease — they lower BP, reduce proteinuria, and slow GFR decline; **CCBs may be added** if BP is still uncontrolled.

**UNDERSTAND:** They dilate the **efferent** arteriole → lower intraglomerular pressure → less protein filtration. Expect a **rise in creatinine up to ~30%** — this is acceptable, not a reason to stop.

**MUST KNOW:** Avoid combining ACEi **with** ARB (hyperkalemia and AKI without added benefit); contraindicated in **bilateral renal artery stenosis** and **pregnancy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Efferent dilation = less proteinuria; 30% creatinine rise is allowed.*

## 180. Tolvaptan

**PEARL:** Tolvaptan is a **V2 receptor antagonist**: blocking vasopressin at the collecting duct lowers adenyl cyclase → ↓cAMP → **aquaporin-2 vesicles are not inserted** into the membrane → increased **free water clearance** and reduced urine osmolality.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Because it is an **aquaretic**, patients get **thirst, dry mouth, polyuria and dehydration** — sodium rises without sodium loss.
- Uses: **euvolemic/hypervolemic hyponatremia (SIADH)**, and slowing cyst growth in **ADPKD**.
- Correct sodium **slowly** (risk of osmotic demyelination); hepatotoxicity limits ADPKD dosing.

**REMEMBER:** *Tolvaptan = water out, sodium up, patient thirsty.*

## 181. Teriparatide

**PEARL:** Recombinant **PTH (1-34) analogue** — the **anabolic** osteoporosis drug; **intermittent** dosing stimulates **osteoblasts**, raising BMD and cutting fracture risk in postmenopausal women.

**UNDERSTAND:** Continuous PTH elevation resorbs bone (as in hyperparathyroidism); once-daily pulses favour bone formation.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Approved for a maximum of **2 years**; indicated for prior osteoporotic fracture, multiple risk factors, or failure/intolerance of other therapy.
- **Give teriparatide before (not after) bisphosphonates** — prior bisphosphonate blunts the anabolic response.
- Must be **followed by an antiresorptive** or gains are lost; contraindicated with Paget's disease, prior skeletal radiation, hypercalcemia.

**REMEMBER:** *Pulse PTH builds bone — 2 years only, then a bisphosphonate.*

## 182. Bisphosphonates

**PEARL:** **Pyrophosphate analogues** that bind hydroxyapatite and directly inhibit **osteoclasts** — causing osteoclast apoptosis and preventing hydroxyapatite dissolution.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Duration **3–5 years**, then consider a **drug holiday** because of **osteonecrosis of the jaw** and **atypical subtrochanteric femur fractures**.
- Nitrogen-containing ones (alendronate, zoledronate) inhibit **farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase**; take oral doses fasting with water, **stay upright 30 min** (esophagitis).
- Correct **vitamin D and calcium first** — otherwise hypocalcemia. Avoid if CrCl <30–35.

**REMEMBER:** *Osteoclast killers — 3–5 years, then holiday (jaw + femur).*

## 183. Hydrocortisone Replacement in Chronic Adrenal Insufficiency

**PEARL:** Chronic (non-crisis) adrenal insufficiency needs **oral hydrocortisone 15–25 mg/day**, typically **20 mg in 2–3 divided doses (10 + 5 + 5)**, with **half the daily dose in the morning** to mimic the circadian rhythm.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Add **fludrocortisone** (0.05–0.2 mg) in **primary** (Addison's) disease only — secondary insufficiency preserves aldosterone.
- **Double or triple the dose during illness/stress ("sick day rules")**; parenteral hydrocortisone 100 mg for crisis.
- Crisis management: IV fluids + hydrocortisone; do not delay for test results.

**REMEMBER:** *15–25 mg/day, most of it on waking; fludrocortisone only for Addison's.*

## 184. Metyrapone

**PEARL:** Metyrapone **reversibly inhibits steroid 11β-hydroxylase**, blocking cortisol synthesis.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Causes accumulation of **11-deoxycortisol** — the basis of the **metyrapone test** for HPA axis integrity (ACTH and 11-deoxycortisol should rise).
- Other steroidogenesis inhibitors: **ketoconazole** (multiple enzymes), **osilodrostat**, **mitotane** (adrenolytic), etomidate (IV, rapid).
- Mineralocorticoid precursors accumulate → hypertension and hypokalemia.

**REMEMBER:** *Metyrapone blocks the last step (11β) → 11-deoxycortisol piles up.*

## 185. Letrozole

**PEARL:** A **non-steroidal, reversible aromatase inhibitor** — blocks conversion of androgens to estrogen. Oral bioavailability ~100%, **t½ ≈ 41 hours**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- ADRs are all estrogen-deprivation effects: **hot flushes, vaginal dryness, accelerated bone loss/osteoporosis, arthralgia, hair thinning, dyslipidemia, GI upset**.
- Used for hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in **postmenopausal** women (aromatase is the only estrogen source), and off-label as a **first-line ovulation inducer in PCOS** (now preferred over clomiphene for live-birth rates).
- Exemestane is the **steroidal, irreversible** aromatase inhibitor.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Tamoxifen (SERM) causes **endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma and thrombosis but protects bone**; aromatase inhibitors do the opposite — **bone loss but no endometrial cancer**.

**REMEMBER:** *Letrozole = no estrogen anywhere → hot flushes + thin bones.*

## 186. Clomiphene

**PEARL:** Clomiphene is a mixture of **enclomiphene and zuclomiphene** — it blocks the **negative feedback of estrogen on the hypothalamus**, causing a surge of **FSH and LH** and hence ovulation; continued FSH drive commonly causes **multiple follicular development**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Consequence: **multiple pregnancy (~8%)** and risk of ovarian hyperstimulation.
- Its **anti-estrogenic effect on cervical mucus and endometrium** is why pregnancy rates lag ovulation rates.
- Requires an intact HPO axis — useless in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (give gonadotropins) or premature ovarian failure.

**REMEMBER:** *Clomiphene fools the hypothalamus into thinking estrogen is low.*

## 187. Tocolytics — Best Outcomes

**PEARL:** Meta-analysis favours **calcium channel blockers (nifedipine)** and **atosiban** for the best maternal and fetal outcomes among tocolytics.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Atosiban** = oxytocin receptor antagonist — fewest maternal side effects. **Nifedipine** = cheap, oral, first-line in India.
- Tocolysis buys **48 hours** for antenatal **corticosteroids** and transfer — it does not improve perinatal mortality by itself.
- **Ritodrine/isoxsuprine (β2 agonists)** are largely abandoned — pulmonary edema, tachycardia, hyperglycemia. **MgSO4** is used for **fetal neuroprotection**, not as a primary tocolytic.

**REMEMBER:** *Nifedipine or atosiban; tocolysis just buys steroid time.*

## 188. Sickle Cell Drugs

**PEARL:** Four drugs, four mechanisms:

- **Hydroxyurea** — raises **HbF** (primary drug); also inhibits **ribonucleotide reductase** (hence its use in CML, polycythemia vera, thrombocythemia).
- **L-glutamine** — antioxidant; increases reduced **glutathione/NAD** in sickle RBCs, lessening oxidative damage.
- **Voxelotor** — **HbS polymerization inhibitor**, stabilizes the oxygenated (R) state of hemoglobin.
- **Crizanlizumab** — anti-P-selectin antibody, reduces vaso-occlusive crises.

**MUST KNOW:** Hydroxyurea reduces crises, acute chest syndrome and transfusion need; monitor for myelosuppression.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** **Pegfilgrastim/filgrastim must be avoided in sickle cell disease — G-CSF can trigger a severe sickle crisis.**

**REMEMBER:** *Hydroxyurea = HbF; voxelotor = stops polymerization; glutamine = antioxidant.*

## 189. Filgrastim

**PEARL:** Filgrastim is recombinant human **G-CSF**; **pegfilgrastim** and **lipegfilgrastim** are the long-acting pegylated forms. Given **subcutaneously or by slow IV infusion only**.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Used for chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and stem-cell mobilization; classic ADR is **bone pain**.
- **Contraindicated in sickle cell disease** (precipitates crisis).
- GM-CSF equivalent = sargramostim.

**REMEMBER:** *G-CSF = filgrastim; pegylated = weekly; never in sickle cell.*

## 190. Checkpoint Inhibitors — PD-1

**PEARL:** **Nivolumab** (fully human **IgG4** monoclonal antibody) and **pembrolizumab** both block **PD-1** binding to its ligands, restoring T-cell anti-tumour activity.

**MUST KNOW:**

- **Pembrolizumab** is approved **tissue-agnostically** for any solid tumour with **MMR deficiency / MSI-high** — the first site-independent cancer approval.
- **Nivolumab** is approved as adjuvant therapy in completely resected **esophageal/gastroesophageal junction cancer** with residual disease after neoadjuvant chemoradiation.
- Class toxicity = **immune-related adverse events**: colitis, hypophysitis/thyroiditis, pneumonitis, hepatitis — treated with **corticosteroids**.
- **Ipilimumab** targets **CTLA-4**; atezolizumab/durvalumab target **PD-L1**.

**REMEMBER:** *PD-1 = nivo/pembro; CTLA-4 = ipilimumab; irAEs = give steroids.*

## 191. Dabrafenib + Trametinib

**PEARL:** **Dabrafenib (BRAF inhibitor)** and **trametinib (MEK inhibitor)** are each active as monotherapy in **BRAF V600E/K melanoma**, and the **combination** is approved for unresectable/metastatic **anaplastic thyroid carcinoma**.

**MUST KNOW:** Combining BRAF + MEK inhibition increases efficacy and **reduces the paradoxical MAPK activation** that causes squamous cell carcinomas with BRAF inhibitor monotherapy. **BRAF inhibitors must not be used in BRAF wild-type tumours** (paradoxical tumour growth).

**REMEMBER:** *BRAF + MEK together: melanoma and anaplastic thyroid cancer.*

## 192. Basiliximab

**PEARL:** Basiliximab is an **anti-CD25 (IL-2 receptor α chain)** monoclonal antibody with high affinity for the IL-2 receptor — used for **induction immunosuppression** in renal transplantation.

**MUST KNOW:** It is non-depleting and non-lymphotoxic → **no cytokine release syndrome** (unlike muromonab/ATG); given as two doses around transplant. Daclizumab was the related agent (withdrawn).

**REMEMBER:** *Basiliximab = blocks IL-2 receptor (CD25) at induction.*

## 193. Vilazodone

**PEARL:** Vilazodone is a **SPARI** — **S**erotonin **PA**rtial agonist (**5-HT1A**) and **R**euptake **I**nhibitor. It selectively blocks the serotonin transporter (no NE/DA effect) **and** is a 5-HT1A partial agonist.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Never combine with an **MAO inhibitor**, or use within **14 days** of starting/stopping one — serotonin syndrome.
- Compare: **vortioxetine** (multimodal), **buspirone** (pure 5-HT1A partial agonist, anxiolytic), **trazodone** (SARI).

**REMEMBER:** *Vilazodone = SSRI + buspirone in one molecule.*

## 194. Mirtazapine

**PEARL:** Mirtazapine and mianserin block **H1** receptors and are potent antagonists at **5-HT2A, 5-HT2C and 5-HT3**. **Sexual dysfunction is NOT a side effect** of mirtazapine.

**UNDERSTAND:** SSRIs cause sexual dysfunction and nausea by stimulating 5-HT2 and 5-HT3; mirtazapine **blocks** those receptors — so it avoids both, and its 5-HT3 blockade is antiemetic.

**MUST KNOW:**

- It is a **NaSSA** — blocks presynaptic **α2** autoreceptors, increasing NE and 5-HT release.
- Its H1 blockade gives **sedation, increased appetite and weight gain** — ideal for depressed patients with insomnia and poor appetite/cachexia.

**REMEMBER:** *Mirtazapine: sleep + appetite up, nausea + sexual dysfunction absent.*

## 195. Lithium

**PEARL:**

- Serum lithium must be sampled **10–12 hours after the previous dose** (standard **12-hour trough**).
- Lithium is a mood stabilizer with a **proven antisuicidal effect** — unique among psychotropics.

**MUST KNOW:**

- Therapeutic range 0.6–1.2 mEq/L; narrow index — toxicity with dehydration, **NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides**; excretion is **purely renal** and it is reabsorbed with sodium (low Na = high lithium).
- Chronic: nephrogenic **diabetes insipidus**, hypothyroidism, tremor, weight gain; **Ebstein anomaly** in first-trimester exposure.
- Severe toxicity → **hemodialysis**.

**REMEMBER:** *12-hour trough; lithium is the antisuicide drug; thiazides and NSAIDs push it up.*

## 196. Propranolol for Infantile Hemangioma

**PEARL:** **Propranolol** is the drug of choice for infantile hemangioma that is **ulcerated, symptomatic, or function-threatening** — acting by **vasoconstriction, induction of apoptosis, and inhibition of angiogenesis** (downregulating VEGF/bFGF).

**MUST KNOW:** Watch for **hypoglycemia, bradycardia, hypotension and bronchospasm** in infants — give with feeds. Topical **timolol** for small superficial lesions. Most untreated hemangiomas involute spontaneously — treat only when complicated.

**REMEMBER:** *Ulcerated hemangioma → propranolol (watch the sugar).*

## 197. Acitretin and Pregnancy

**PEARL:** Women must avoid conception for **3 years** after stopping **acitretin** (retinoid embryopathy).

**UNDERSTAND:** Acitretin is re-esterified to **etretinate**, which is highly lipophilic and stored in fat with a very long half-life — especially if alcohol is consumed. Hence the extraordinarily long wait.

**MUST KNOW:** Contrast: **isotretinoin — 1 month** before pregnancy is safe. Also avoid **blood donation** during and for a period after therapy.

**REMEMBER:** *Isotretinoin = 1 month; acitretin = 3 years (alcohol makes etretinate).*

## 198. Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis — Skeletal Signs

**PEARL:** LCH bone lesions:

- **Skull:** punched-out lytic lesions **without a sclerotic rim**, **beveled edge / "hole within a hole"** (double contour), **button sequestrum**, and **geographic skull**.
- **Mandible:** **"floating teeth"** — loss of the lamina dura.
- **Spine:** **vertebra plana** — LCH is the **commonest cause of vertebra plana in children**, mostly thoracic.

**MUST KNOW:** Birbeck granules on EM; **CD1a and langerin (CD207)** positive. Vertebra plana in LCH typically shows **preserved disc spaces** and can reconstitute with growth.

**REMEMBER:** *Beveled skull hole + floating teeth + vertebra plana = LCH.*
