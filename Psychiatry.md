# Psychiatry — Revision Pearls

*(55 raw pearls consolidated into 45)*

---

# A. Psychosis & Schizophrenia

## 1. Who Coined What

**PEARL:** **Eugen Bleuler** coined **"schizophrenia"**; **Emil Kraepelin** coined **"dementia praecox"**.

**MUST KNOW:** Bleuler's **4 A's** (fundamental symptoms): **Ambivalence, Autism, Affect blunting, Association loosening**. Kraepelin separated dementia praecox from manic-depressive psychosis. **Schneider** gave the first-rank symptoms (thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcast, delusional perception, passivity, third-person hallucinations).

**REMEMBER:** *Kraepelin named the course (praecox); Bleuler named the splitting (schizo).*

---

## 2. Pfropf Schizophrenia

**PEARL:** **Schizophrenia occurring in a person with intellectual disability (mental retardation)** — "pfropf" = grafted-on.

**REMEMBER:** *Pfropf = schizophrenia grafted onto low IQ.*

---

## 3. Marital Skew and Marital Schism

**PEARL:** (Lidz's family theories of schizophrenia)
- **Skew** — one **dominant** parent (usually the mother) and one **submissive** parent; a poor role model, linked to schizophrenia in **male** children.
- **Schism** — chronic **marital conflict** with neither parent yielding; children are used as pawns, linked to schizophrenia in **females**.

**REMEMBER:** *Skew is lopsided (male offspring); Schism is split (female offspring).*

---

## 4. Derailment

**PEARL:** **Derailment (loosening of associations)** is a **formal thought disorder** — a disorder of the **form/flow** of thought, not its content.

**MUST KNOW:** Other formal thought disorders: tangentiality, circumstantiality, thought block, neologisms, **word salad**, clang associations. **Delusions are disorders of thought CONTENT**, not form.

**REMEMBER:** *Form = how thoughts connect; content = what they contain.*

---

## 5. Amphetamine Psychosis

**PEARL:** Amphetamine abuse produces a picture that **resembles paranoid schizophrenia** — persecutory delusions with a clear sensorium, plus tactile hallucinations (formication).

**UNDERSTAND:** Amphetamine floods the synapse with **dopamine** — direct clinical support for the **dopamine hypothesis** of schizophrenia.

**REMEMBER:** *Amphetamine = chemical paranoid schizophrenia.*

---

## 6. Flupenthixol

**PEARL:** A **thioxanthene antipsychotic** blocking both **D1 and D2** receptors; the **depot** form is used in schizophrenia, and in **low dose it is used for depression**.

**MUST KNOW:** Depot (long-acting injectable) antipsychotics are the answer for **non-adherence**. Flupenthixol is unusual in having an antidepressant indication at low dose.

**REMEMBER:** *Flupenthixol = D1 + D2, depot, and antidepressant in low dose.*

---

# B. Mood Disorders

## 7. Hypomania

**PEARL:** Persistently elevated/irritable mood with increased activity/energy lasting **at least 4 days** — same symptoms as mania but **without marked impairment in social/occupational functioning, no hospitalization, and NO psychotic features**. **Any psychotic feature makes it mania by definition.**

**MUST KNOW:** Mania requires **≥7 days** (or any duration if hospitalized). Hypomania + a depressive episode = **BPAD II**; mania (with or without depression) = **BPAD I**.

**REMEMBER:** *4 days, no impairment, no psychosis = hypomania. Psychosis upgrades it to mania.*

---

## 8. Rapid Cycling

**PEARL:** **≥4 mood episodes in 12 months**. It is more strongly associated with **BPAD II than BPAD I**.

**MUST KNOW:** Also associated with **female sex, hypothyroidism, and antidepressant use** (which can induce cycling). **Lithium works poorly** in rapid cycling — prefer **valproate/lamotrigine**, and stop the antidepressant.

**REMEMBER:** *4 episodes a year, commoner in BPAD II, lithium disappoints.*

---

## 9. Biogenic Amines in Mania

**PEARL:** In mania there is an **increase in norepinephrine and dopamine**.

**MUST KNOW:** Depression is the mirror image — **low NE, 5-HT and dopamine** (the monoamine hypothesis). Mania's dopamine excess explains psychotic features and the response to antipsychotics.

**REMEMBER:** *Mania = amines up; depression = amines down.*

---

## 10. Mood-Congruent vs Mood-Incongruent Delusions

**PEARL:**
- **Mood-congruent** (fit the depressive mood): **delusion of poverty, nihilistic delusion (Cotard), delusion of guilt**, delusion of deserved punishment.
- **Mood-incongruent** (cannot be explained by the mood): **delusion of control/passivity**, thought insertion, persecutory delusions without a guilt theme.

**MUST KNOW:** Mood-incongruent psychotic features carry a **poorer prognosis** and suggest schizoaffective disorder.

**REMEMBER:** *Guilt, poverty, nihilism = congruent; being controlled = incongruent.*

---

## 11. Psychotic Depression — Treatment

**PEARL:** Depression with psychotic features (e.g. delusion of guilt) is treated with an **antidepressant PLUS an antipsychotic**.

**MUST KNOW:** **ECT** is highly effective and is the treatment of choice when there is **suicidal risk, stupor, or refusal of food** — an antidepressant alone is inadequate.

**REMEMBER:** *Psychotic depression needs two drugs — or ECT.*

---

## 12. Atypical Depression

**PEARL:** **Mood reactivity** (mood lifts with positive events) + **hypersomnia + weight gain/hyperphagia + leaden paralysis** (heavy limbs) + rejection sensitivity.

**MUST KNOW:** It is the **reverse vegetative pattern** of melancholia (which has early-morning waking, weight loss, anhedonia, diurnal variation worse in the morning, and **no** mood reactivity). Classically responds well to **MAO inhibitors**; SSRIs are used first-line in practice.

**REMEMBER:** *Atypical = eats more, sleeps more, still reacts to good news.*

---

## 13. Sleep-Onset Insomnia

**PEARL:** **Difficulty falling asleep (sleep-onset insomnia) is characteristic of ANXIETY**, not melancholic depression.

**MUST KNOW:** Melancholia gives **terminal insomnia (early-morning awakening)** with reduced REM latency and reduced slow-wave sleep.

**REMEMBER:** *Anxiety can't fall asleep; depression can't stay asleep.*

---

## 14. Aaron Beck's Cognitive Triad

**PEARL:** Negative views of **(1) oneself, (2) the world/surroundings, and (3) the future**.

**MUST KNOW:** This is the theoretical basis of **cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)**; Beck also gave the Beck Depression Inventory and the concept of automatic negative thoughts.

**REMEMBER:** *Me, the world, the future — all bleak.*

---

## 15. Suicide in Bipolar Disorder

**PEARL:** Suicide attempts occur in about **15%** of people with bipolar disorder.

**MUST KNOW:** **Lithium is the only psychotropic with a proven anti-suicidal effect.** Risk is highest in **depressive and mixed** episodes, not manic ones.

**REMEMBER:** *15% attempt; lithium is the protective drug.*

---

## 16. Five Stages of Grief

**PEARL:** **Denial → Anger → Bargaining → Depression → Acceptance** (Kübler-Ross).

**MUST KNOW:** Normal grief may include hearing/seeing the deceased (a **pseudo-hallucination**, with insight preserved) and is **not** an illness. Pathological grief: >6–12 months, marked functional impairment, **worthlessness, guilt unrelated to the deceased, or suicidal ideation** → treat as depression.

**REMEMBER:** *DABDA.*

---

# C. Anxiety, Somatic & Related Disorders

## 17. Commonest Psychiatric Illness

**PEARL:** Per WHO global health statistics (2020), **anxiety disorder is the commonest psychiatric illness worldwide**.

**MUST KNOW:** Depression carries the greater **disability burden (DALYs)**; in India, the commonest psychiatric disorder in community surveys is also anxiety/depressive spectrum. Lifetime prevalence of specific phobia is the highest among individual anxiety disorders.

**REMEMBER:** *Anxiety is commonest; depression is most disabling.*

---

## 18. Body Dysmorphic Disorder

**PEARL:** **SSRIs are the drug of choice**, usually with **CBT** — especially when insight is poor or absent. **Surgery is contraindicated** because it does not touch the underlying psychopathology (and patients are typically dissatisfied afterwards).

**MUST KNOW:** BDD is classified under **obsessive-compulsive and related disorders** in DSM-5 (with hoarding, trichotillomania, excoriation disorder). Requires **higher SSRI doses** and longer trials than depression. High suicide risk.

**REMEMBER:** *BDD = OCD-family → SSRI + CBT, never the scalpel.*

---

## 19. Hypochondriasis (Illness Anxiety Disorder)

**PEARL:** **Persistent preoccupation with the fear or belief of having a serious disease, based on the person's own misinterpretation of bodily functions.**

**MUST KNOW:** Persists **despite normal investigations and reassurance**. In DSM-5 it splits into **somatic symptom disorder** (with prominent symptoms) and **illness anxiety disorder** (little or no symptom). Key contrasts: **BDD** = preoccupation with appearance; **delusional disorder, somatic type** = fixed, no insight; **malingering** = conscious, external incentive.

**REMEMBER:** *Fear of the disease, not the symptom, surviving normal tests.*

---

## 20. Conversion Disorder

**PEARL:** **Conversion disorder = functional neurological symptom disorder** — symptoms affecting **voluntary motor or sensory function** that suggest a neurological condition but are incompatible with it.

**MUST KNOW:** Classic features: **la belle indifférence**, symptoms not intentionally produced (**unconscious**, unlike malingering/factitious), preceded by a psychological stressor, with a **primary gain** (anxiety kept out of awareness). Signs: **Hoover's sign** in functional leg weakness. In **ICD**, it is grouped with the **dissociative** disorders.

**REMEMBER:** *Neurology that doesn't obey anatomy, produced unconsciously.*

---

## 21. Olfactory Reference Disorder

**PEARL:** A **new ICD-11 diagnosis** — persistent preoccupation that one **emits a foul body odour or bad breath**, which others cannot perceive.

**MUST KNOW:** Classified with **OCD-related disorders**; features excessive checking/washing/deodorant use and marked avoidance. Treatment: **SSRI + CBT**.

**REMEMBER:** *Convinced they stink, though nobody smells it — ICD-11, OCD family.*

---

## 22. PTSD Classification

**PEARL:** In **DSM-5**, PTSD is classified under **Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders** — it is **no longer an anxiety disorder**.

**MUST KNOW:** That group also contains **acute stress disorder** (3 days–1 month; PTSD is **>1 month**), adjustment disorder, and reactive attachment disorder. Treatment: **trauma-focused CBT/EMDR** first-line; **SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine)** are the approved drugs; prazosin for nightmares.

**REMEMBER:** *PTSD left the anxiety chapter for the trauma chapter.*

---

## 23. Repression

**PEARL:** **Repression** = unconsciously excluding anxiety-provoking ideas from awareness — the mechanism in **psychogenic (dissociative) amnesia**. It is **not** the defence used in **OCD**.

**MUST KNOW:** OCD's characteristic defences are **isolation of affect, undoing, and reaction formation**. Repression is *un*conscious; **suppression** is the conscious, mature equivalent.

**REMEMBER:** *Repression = amnesia; OCD = undoing, isolation, reaction formation.*

---

## 24. Anterior Cingulotomy

**PEARL:** A **neurosurgical treatment for refractory OCD** — bilateral stereotactic lesions in the **anterior cingulate cortex**.

**MUST KNOW:** Reserved for **severe, treatment-resistant** OCD after failed SSRIs (high dose, adequate trial), clomipramine, augmentation and CBT/ERP. Other options: capsulotomy, **deep brain stimulation**.

**REMEMBER:** *Last-resort OCD surgery = anterior cingulotomy.*

---

## 25. SCOFF Questionnaire

**PEARL:** A **5-item screening tool for eating disorders** (anorexia and bulimia nervosa): **S**ick (make yourself vomit), **C**ontrol (lost control over eating), **O**ne (lost >One stone/~6 kg in 3 months), **F**at (believe yourself fat when others say thin), **F**ood (does food dominate your life).

**MUST KNOW:** **≥2 positive answers** = likely eating disorder → further assessment.

**REMEMBER:** *SCOFF — 2 or more "yes" is a red flag.*

---

# D. Substance Use

## 26. Alcohol Withdrawal and Delirium Tremens

**PEARL:**
- **Benzodiazepines (lorazepam/chlordiazepoxide) are the drugs of choice** to prevent and treat alcohol withdrawal.
- **IV lorazepam** is used for established **delirium tremens**.
- **Gradual withdrawal of alcohol does NOT cause delirium tremens** — DT follows **abrupt** cessation.
- **Acute infection (or any intercurrent illness/surgery) can precipitate DT** in an alcoholic.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Timeline: tremors/anxiety 6–12 h → **seizures 12–48 h** → alcoholic hallucinosis 12–24 h → **DT at 48–72 h** (clouded consciousness, disorientation, visual/tactile hallucinations, autonomic storm) — DT has **mortality up to 5–15%** untreated.
- **Lorazepam and oxazepam are preferred in liver disease** (glucuronidation only).
- Always give **thiamine before glucose**.

**REMEMBER:** *Abrupt stop + infection = DT at day 2–3 → IV lorazepam + thiamine.*

---

## 27. Venlafaxine Discontinuation

**PEARL:** Abrupt stoppage of **venlafaxine** causes **agitation, anxiety and insomnia** (discontinuation syndrome).

**UNDERSTAND:** Venlafaxine has a **short half-life and no long-acting metabolite**, so levels fall fast — hence it and **paroxetine** have the worst discontinuation syndromes; **fluoxetine** (long half-life, active norfluoxetine) has the least.

**MUST KNOW:** Symptoms — **FINISH**: Flu-like, Insomnia, Nausea, Imbalance, Sensory disturbance (**electric-shock "brain zaps"**), Hyperarousal. Management: taper slowly; it is **not** a sign of addiction.

**REMEMBER:** *Short half-life = worst withdrawal; taper venlafaxine and paroxetine.*

# E. Child & Developmental Psychiatry

## 28. ADHD

**PEARL:** Formerly called **minimal brain dysfunction** — inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity, requiring symptoms **before age 12** in **≥2 settings** (home + school). Treatment: **stimulants + behavioural therapy**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- First-line drug: **methylphenidate** (also amphetamines); ADRs — appetite suppression, growth slowing, insomnia, tics.
- **Non-stimulants: atomoxetine** (NE reuptake inhibitor) and the **α2A agonists guanfacine and clonidine**.
- Symptoms must persist **≥6 months** and be developmentally inappropriate.

**REMEMBER:** *Before 12, in 2 settings, for 6 months.*

---

## 29. Guanfacine

**PEARL:** An **α2A adrenergic receptor agonist** — useful in ADHD when **stimulants are not tolerated** or when extra help with **emotional regulation/impulsivity** is needed.

**MUST KNOW:** Acts on prefrontal cortical α2A receptors to strengthen working memory and impulse control; **non-addictive, no abuse potential**, and helps comorbid **tics** and insomnia. Side effects: sedation, hypotension, bradycardia.

**REMEMBER:** *Guanfacine = the calming α2A option when stimulants fail.*

---

## 30. Autism Spectrum Disorder

**PEARL:** ASD frequently shows **inattentiveness, impulsivity and hyperactivity**, overlapping with ADHD — and DSM-5 now permits **both diagnoses together**.

**MUST KNOW:** Core ASD features are **persistent deficits in social communication/interaction** plus **restricted, repetitive behaviours** with onset in early development. No drug treats the core deficit — **behavioural intervention (ABA) is primary**; **risperidone/aripiprazole** are approved only for **irritability/aggression**.

**REMEMBER:** *ASD ≠ ADHD, but they travel together — treat behaviour first.*

---

## 31. Asperger Syndrome

**PEARL:** **Autism WITHOUT delay in language or cognitive development.**

**MUST KNOW:** Normal/high IQ, fluent speech, but impaired social reciprocity, poor non-verbal communication, and circumscribed intense interests; motor clumsiness is common. **Merged into ASD in DSM-5**, but still examined by name.

**REMEMBER:** *Asperger = autism with intact language and intellect.*

---

## 32. Rett Syndrome

**PEARL:** **Normal head circumference at birth** with **deceleration of head growth between 5 and 30 months** — the characteristic feature.

**MUST KNOW:** **MECP2** mutation, **X-linked dominant, almost exclusively girls** (lethal in males). Course: normal early development → regression with **loss of purposeful hand skills** and speech → **stereotypic hand-wringing/washing**, gait apraxia, seizures, breathing irregularity (hyperventilation/apnoea).

**REMEMBER:** *Girl + normal start + shrinking head + hand-wringing = Rett (MECP2).*

---

## 33. Child Physical Abuse — First Step

**PEARL:** A child who has been physically abused should first undergo a **thorough physical examination**.

**MUST KNOW:** Look for injuries at **different stages of healing**, patterned/immersion burns, **bruises in non-ambulant infants**, and a history inconsistent with the injury. Add a **skeletal survey** (<2 years) and fundoscopy (retinal haemorrhage → shaken baby). **Reporting is mandatory** — under **POCSO** for sexual abuse in India.

**REMEMBER:** *Examine the whole child first — document everything.*

# F. Neuropsychiatry, Sleep & Assessment

## 34. Catatonia

**PEARL:** A neuropsychiatric syndrome with **waxy flexibility, negativism, mutism, posturing and purposeless movements**. **Lorazepam is first-line** for rapid relief; **ECT is the treatment of choice** (especially if lorazepam fails or the patient is malignant/stuporous).

**MUST KNOW:**
- Catatonia is commoner in **mood disorders** than in schizophrenia.
- **Malignant catatonia** (fever, autonomic instability, rigidity) resembles **neuroleptic malignant syndrome** — **antipsychotics can worsen it**; ECT is life-saving.
- Other signs: echolalia, echopraxia, **mitgehen**, ambitendency; the **lorazepam challenge test** is both diagnostic and therapeutic.

**REMEMBER:** *Lorazepam first, ECT best; avoid antipsychotics if malignant.*

---

## 35. REM Sleep

**PEARL:** REM sleep **follows NREM**; the EEG shows **sawtooth waves**, low-voltage **beta** activity resembling wakefulness, and a **return of alpha** activity.

**MUST KNOW:**
- REM = **paradoxical sleep**: active brain, **atonic body**, with dreaming, penile tumescence, and irregular pulse/BP.
- Cycles are ~90 minutes; **REM increases towards morning**, deep NREM (stage 3) dominates the first third.
- Stage markers: **alpha = relaxed wakefulness**; theta + **vertex sharp waves** = N1; **K complexes and sleep spindles** = N2; **delta** = N3.
- **Depression: reduced REM latency and increased REM density**; most antidepressants suppress REM.

**REMEMBER:** *REM = sawtooth + beta + paralysis + dreams.*

---

## 36. Somniloquy and Catathrenia

**PEARL:**
- **Somniloquy** = unconscious **speech during sleep**; benign, and may accompany **night terrors and sleepwalking**.
- **Catathrenia (sleep-related groaning)** = chronic, prolonged **loud groaning** at any sleep stage, often beginning in childhood, with **no psychiatric or physiological abnormality and no known treatment**.

**MUST KNOW:** Contrast the NREM parasomnias — **sleepwalking and night terrors occur in slow-wave (N3) sleep, in the first third of the night, with amnesia for the event**; **nightmares occur in REM, in the later night, and are recalled**.

**REMEMBER:** *Talking and groaning are harmless; terrors are N3-amnesic, nightmares are REM-remembered.*

---

## 37. Temporal Lobe Epilepsy — Personality

**PEARL:** **Left** temporal lobe epilepsy may generate preoccupation with **personal destiny and philosophical/religious themes**, with a **humourless** approach to life.

**MUST KNOW:** This is the **interictal (Geschwind) personality syndrome**: **hyperreligiosity, hypergraphia, viscosity (stickiness) of thought, and hyposexuality**. TLE also gives **déjà vu, jamais vu, olfactory/gustatory auras, and automatisms**.

**REMEMBER:** *Left TLE = philosophical, humourless, hypergraphic.*

---

## 38. Forced Thinking

**PEARL:** **Forced thinking** — intrusive, distressing thoughts outside the patient's control — is typically seen in **frontal lobe epilepsy**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Do not confuse with **thought insertion** (schizophrenia — the thoughts are believed to be *someone else's*) or **obsessions** (recognized as one's own, resisted, with insight).

**REMEMBER:** *Forced thinking = frontal lobe seizure, not schizophrenia.*

---

## 39. Delirium — EEG

**PEARL:** Delirium shows **generalized slowing of EEG waves** (theta/delta).

**MUST KNOW:** Delirium = **acute onset, fluctuating course, clouded consciousness, impaired attention** with visual hallucinations — versus dementia (insidious, clear consciousness, normal or mildly slowed EEG). **Exception: delirium tremens shows FAST activity**, and hepatic encephalopathy shows **triphasic waves**. Treatment of choice for agitation in delirium is **haloperidol** (benzodiazepines only for alcohol/sedative withdrawal).

**REMEMBER:** *Delirium slows the EEG — except DT, which speeds it up.*

---

## 40. Emotion — Neuroanatomy

**PEARL:** **Generation of emotion and the rating of emotional importance = the limbic system, specifically the AMYGDALA**. **Regulation (control) of emotion = the FRONTAL lobe.**

**MUST KNOW:** Amygdala damage → **Klüver–Bucy syndrome** (placidity, hyperorality, hypersexuality, visual agnosia); prefrontal damage → disinhibition, poor judgement, emotional lability (the Phineas Gage picture).

**REMEMBER:** *Amygdala feels it; the frontal lobe holds it back.*

---

## 41. Affect

**PEARL:** **Affect** = the **short-lived** emotional response to an idea or event (observed, cross-sectional).

**MUST KNOW:** **Mood** is the sustained, pervasive emotional state (subjective, longitudinal) — *"affect is the weather, mood is the climate."* Affect is described as blunted, flat, restricted, labile, or **inappropriate/incongruent**.

**REMEMBER:** *Affect = weather; mood = climate.*

---

## 42. MMSE (Folstein Test)

**PEARL:** The **Folstein test = Mini-Mental State Examination**, used to assess **cognitive function**.

**MUST KNOW:** Scored out of **30**; **<24 suggests cognitive impairment**. It is influenced by **education and literacy** and is weak on **executive function** — MoCA is more sensitive for mild impairment. It is a screening, not diagnostic, tool.

**REMEMBER:** *MMSE = 30 points, cut-off 24, screening only.*

---

## 43. Stanford–Binet Test

**PEARL:** The **Stanford–Binet** test measures **intelligence (IQ)**.

**MUST KNOW:** IQ = (mental age/chronological age) × 100. Other intelligence tests: **Wechsler (WAIS/WISC)** — the most widely used; **Raven's Progressive Matrices** and **Bhatia's battery** — culture-fair/non-verbal, useful in India. Contrast **projective personality tests**: Rorschach, TAT; and **objective**: MMPI.

**REMEMBER:** *Stanford–Binet and Wechsler = intelligence; Rorschach/TAT = personality.*

---

## 44. Schizoid Personality Disorder

**PEARL:** A **loner by choice**, emotionally cold and detached, indifferent to praise or criticism — with **low novelty seeking** (Cloninger's temperament model).

**MUST KNOW:** Cluster **A** ("odd/eccentric") with paranoid and **schizotypal** (odd beliefs, magical thinking, eccentric — **wants relationships but is anxious in them**). The key contrast: **schizoid does not desire relationships; avoidant personality desires them but fears rejection.**

**REMEMBER:** *Schizoid doesn't want company; avoidant wants it but is afraid.*

---

## 45. Affective Learning

**PEARL:** **Affective learning** involves changes in **feelings, attitudes and values** — e.g. students developing a positive attitude towards health behaviours and willingly promoting them.

**MUST KNOW:** Bloom's three domains: **cognitive (knowledge), affective (attitude), psychomotor (skills)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Knows = cognitive; feels = affective; does = psychomotor.*
