# A. Disinfection & Sterilization

## 1. Spaulding Classification

**PEARL:** **Non-critical items require intermediate- or low-level disinfection.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Critical** — enter sterile tissue/vasculature (surgical instruments, implants, catheters) → **STERILIZATION**.
- **Semi-critical** — contact intact mucous membranes (endoscopes, laryngoscope blades, respiratory equipment) → **high-level disinfection**.
- **Non-critical** — contact only intact skin (BP cuffs, stethoscopes, bedpans, floors) → **intermediate/low-level disinfection**.

**REMEMBER:** *Sterile tissue = sterilize; mucosa = high-level; skin = low-level.*

---

## 2. Best Disinfectant for Blood Spills

**PEARL:** **Sodium hypochlorite** (a chlorine compound) is the best disinfectant for **surface blood spills**, used at **0.5–5%**.

**MUST KNOW:** **1% for general surfaces, 5% (or 10% household bleach) for blood/body-fluid spills.** It is virucidal (including **HIV and hepatitis B**), cheap, and rapid-acting — but **inactivated by organic matter** (so wipe up the bulk first), corrosive to metals, and must be **freshly prepared**. **Never mix with acids or ammonia** (chlorine gas).

**REMEMBER:** *Blood spill = hypochlorite; wipe first, because organic matter kills it.*

---

## 3. Bleaching Powder for Excreta

**PEARL:** **50 g per litre** of bleaching powder solution is used for disinfecting **faeces and urine**.

**MUST KNOW:** Bleaching powder (calcium hypochlorite) should contain **≥33% available chlorine** when fresh; it loses potency on storage and needs a **cool, dry, dark, airtight** container. Contact time for excreta ≈ 1–2 hours before disposal.

**REMEMBER:** *50 g/L for excreta; 33% available chlorine when fresh.*

---

## 4. Rideal–Walker Coefficient

**PEARL:** The **Rideal–Walker coefficient** measures the **power of a disinfectant relative to PHENOL** (the phenol coefficient).

**UNDERSTAND:** It is the ratio of the dilution of the test disinfectant to the dilution of phenol that kills *Salmonella typhi* in the same time — so **coefficient >1 means more potent than phenol**.

**MUST KNOW:** Its weakness: it is tested **without organic matter**, so it overstates real-life performance. The **Chick–Martin test** corrects this by adding organic matter (yeast/faeces) — hence Chick–Martin values are more realistic and **lower**.

**REMEMBER:** *Rideal–Walker = clean conditions; Chick–Martin = dirty conditions (more real).*

---

## 5. Antiviral Disinfectants

**PEARL:** Disinfectants effective against viruses: **chlorine compounds, phenol (at high concentration), aldehydes (formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde), and hydrogen peroxide**.

**MUST KNOW:** **Non-enveloped viruses (polio, hepatitis A, norovirus) are far more resistant** than enveloped ones (HIV, hepatitis B, influenza), which are readily killed by alcohol and detergents. **Quaternary ammonium compounds are poorly virucidal** against non-enveloped viruses.

**REMEMBER:** *Chlorine, phenol, aldehydes, peroxide kill viruses — QACs don't.*

---

## 6. Quaternary Ammonium Compounds

**PEARL:** QACs (e.g. **benzalkonium chloride, cetrimide/Cetavlon**) are **LOW-level disinfectants** — **sporostatic but NOT sporicidal**, and bactericidal mainly against **vegetative gram-positive** organisms. **Cetrimide contains no chlorine** despite the name.

**MUST KNOW:** They are **cationic detergents** — **inactivated by soap/anionic detergents, hard water and organic matter**. Poor against *Pseudomonas*, mycobacteria, spores and non-enveloped viruses; QAC solutions have famously been **contaminated by *Pseudomonas***. Cetrimide + chlorhexidine = **Savlon**.

**REMEMBER:** *QACs: low level, stop spores but don't kill them, and soap cancels them.*

---

## 7. Iodophors

**PEARL:** **Iodine complexed with a solubilizing carrier** (e.g. povidone in povidone-iodine), forming a **sustained-release reservoir** that liberates small amounts of **free iodine** in aqueous solution.

**UNDERSTAND:** Free iodine is the active agent, but is irritant and staining — the carrier keeps its concentration low and steady, giving **less irritation, less staining, better solubility and prolonged action**. Paradoxically, **dilute povidone-iodine can act faster** than the concentrate (more free iodine is released).

**REMEMBER:** *Iodophor = iodine with a slow-release chaperone.*

---

## 8. Glutaraldehyde and the Hot Air Oven

**PEARL:**
- **2% glutaraldehyde** is used to sterilize **endoscopes and fibreoptic instruments** (heat-sensitive, semi-critical items).
- The **hot air oven** sterilizes at **160°C for 2 hours**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Glutaraldehyde needs **activation (alkaline pH)**; **20 minutes for high-level disinfection, 10 hours for sterilization**; it is irritant to eyes and airways.
- **Hot air oven (dry heat)** is for **glassware, powders, oils, sharp instruments, glass syringes** — items ruined by moisture. Alternatives: 170°C/1 h, 180°C/30 min.
- **Autoclave (moist heat)** — **121°C at 15 psi for 15–20 minutes** — is the most reliable and is the standard for surgical instruments and dressings; sterility is monitored with ***Geobacillus stearothermophilus*** spores (dry heat uses *Bacillus atrophaeus*).

**REMEMBER:** *Dry heat 160°C × 2 h; autoclave 121°C × 15 min at 15 psi; scopes get 2% glutaraldehyde.*

# B. Immunization & Cold Chain

## 9. Hold-over Time

**PEARL:** **Hold-over time** = the time cold-chain equipment takes, **during a power failure**, for the internal temperature to rise to the **maximum limit of its recommended range**.

**MUST KNOW:** ILR (ice-lined refrigerator) holds **+2°C to +8°C** and has a hold-over time of ~18–20 hours or more with a full ice lining — which is why ILRs are preferred in areas with unreliable power. **Do not open the lid unnecessarily**, and keep it full.

**REMEMBER:** *Hold-over time = how long the cold survives the blackout.*

---

## 10. Solar Refrigerator Freezer

**PEARL:** The freezer compartment of a **solar refrigerator (battery-driven)** is maintained at **−7°C**, and is used mainly to store **ice packs**.

**MUST KNOW:** Vaccine sensitivity — **freeze-sensitive (must NEVER be frozen): DPT, TT, Hepatitis B, IPV, PCV, Rotavirus, Typhoid** (all adsorbed/liquid vaccines); **heat-sensitive (most damaged by heat): OPV (most sensitive), measles/MR, BCG**. The **shake test** detects a previously frozen adsorbed vaccine; the **VVM (vaccine vial monitor)** detects heat exposure.

**REMEMBER:** *Freezer for ice packs only; never freeze DPT/TT/HepB.*

---

## 11. IPV — Trace Antibiotics

**PEARL:** IPV may contain trace amounts of **streptomycin, neomycin or polymyxin B**.

**MUST KNOW:** So a history of **anaphylaxis to these antibiotics is a contraindication** to IPV. IPV is a **killed (Salk)** vaccine given **intramuscularly** — it produces **humoral (IgG) immunity but poor intestinal (IgA) immunity**, so it protects the individual from paralysis but does not stop transmission as well as **OPV (live, Sabin)**. OPV's risk is **VAPP and VDPV**.

**REMEMBER:** *IPV carries streptomycin/neomycin/polymyxin traces — ask about antibiotic anaphylaxis.*

---

## 12. Hepatitis B Vaccine

**PEARL:** A **subunit (recombinant) vaccine** made from **HBsAg**.

**MUST KNOW:** Produced in yeast by recombinant DNA — hence **contraindicated in yeast anaphylaxis**. Schedule: **birth dose within 24 hours** + 6, 10, 14 weeks. Protective **anti-HBs ≥10 mIU/mL**. Other subunit vaccines: acellular pertussis, HPV, influenza (split). Contrast **toxoid** (tetanus, diphtheria), **live** (BCG, OPV, measles), **killed** (IPV, rabies, hepatitis A).

**REMEMBER:** *HBsAg made in yeast = subunit vaccine; birth dose within 24 hours.*

---

## 13. Tetanus Vaccine — Brachial Neuritis

**PEARL:** **Brachial neuritis is a known complication of tetanus vaccination.** Also — **tetanus does NOT spread vertically/transplacentally**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Neonatal tetanus comes from **contamination of the umbilical stump**, not from the mother's blood — which is why it is prevented by **maternal TT/Td immunization (passive IgG transfer) plus the 3 cleans**: clean hands, clean surface, clean cord cutting/care.

**MUST KNOW:** Brachial neuritis (Parsonage–Turner-like) is a recognized compensable event under vaccine injury schedules; the commoner reactions are local pain/swelling and, with too-frequent boosters, **Arthus reaction**.

**REMEMBER:** *No vertical spread — neonatal tetanus enters through the cord stump.*

# C. Epidemiology

## 14. Carrier States

**PEARL:**
- **Convalescent carriers** shed the organism during the **recovery phase** — **diphtheria, typhoid, cholera, pertussis, hepatitis B**.
- **Healthy carriers** had a **subclinical infection** and became carriers without overt disease — **Cholera, Meningitis (meningococcus), Diphtheria, Polio, Typhoid** (**"CM-DPT"**).

**MUST KNOW:** Also classified by duration: **temporary/transient** vs **chronic** (e.g. **typhoid gallbladder carrier — "Typhoid Mary"**, hepatitis B, hepatitis C). **Incubatory carriers** shed *before* symptoms — measles, mumps, chickenpox, hepatitis B, and (importantly) polio and COVID-19.

**REMEMBER:** *Healthy carriers = CM-DPT; convalescent = still shedding while recovering.*

---

## 15. Matching

**PEARL:**
- **Group (frequency) matching** — used when a **large stratum** is studied and a **1:1 case:control ratio** can be achieved.
- **Individual matching (matching by pairs)** — used when the **study is small**, and more than one control can be selected per case.

**MUST KNOW:** Matching controls **confounding at the design stage**, but a **matched variable can no longer be studied as a risk factor**, and matched data require **matched analysis (McNemar's test, conditional logistic regression)**. **Over-matching** reduces efficiency.

**REMEMBER:** *Match to remove a confounder — and lose the ability to study it.*

---

## 16. Control of Confounding

**PEARL:**
- **At the design stage:** **randomization, matching, restriction**.
- **At the analysis stage:** **stratification, and statistical modelling** — regression/multivariate analysis and the **Mantel–Haenszel** method.

**MUST KNOW:** **Randomization is the only method that controls UNKNOWN/unmeasured confounders** — which is why the RCT is the gold standard. All the analysis-stage methods can handle only the confounders you actually measured.

**REMEMBER:** *Only randomization controls the confounders you never thought of.*

---

## 17. Systematic Error vs Random Error

**PEARL:** **Systematic errors are biases** — distinct from random errors.

**UNDERSTAND:** **Bias shifts results consistently in one direction** and **is NOT reduced by increasing sample size** — it affects **validity (accuracy)**. **Random error** is scattered in both directions, **is reduced by a larger sample**, and affects **precision (reliability)**.

**MUST KNOW:** Main biases: **selection bias** (Berkson's, healthy worker, non-response), **information/measurement bias** (recall, interviewer, misclassification), and **confounding**.

**REMEMBER:** *Bias = wrong target (bigger sample won't help); random error = scattered shots (bigger sample helps).*

---

## 18. Case Fatality Rate vs Survival Rate

**PEARL:**
- **Case fatality rate** reflects the **killing power (virulence/severity) of a disease** — with **no specific time interval** attached.
- **Survival rate** is the yardstick for **assessing standards of therapy**.

**MUST KNOW:** **CFR = (deaths from the disease / total cases) × 100** — most useful in **acute, short-duration** conditions (outbreaks, food poisoning). **Five-year survival rate** is the standard measure in cancer, and can be inflated by **lead-time and length-time bias** from screening.

**REMEMBER:** *CFR judges the disease; survival rate judges the treatment.*

---

## 19. Public Health Surveillance

**PEARL:** Surveillance is aimed **preferentially at health PLANNING** — the continuous scrutiny of data for action.

**MUST KNOW:** Its uses: detect outbreaks early, monitor trends, evaluate control programmes, set priorities and plan services. **"Information for action"** — surveillance is not merely data collection; the loop is **collection → analysis → interpretation → dissemination → action**. **Sentinel surveillance** covers selected sites; **active** surveillance seeks cases out, **passive** waits for reports.

**REMEMBER:** *Surveillance = information for action, mainly for planning.*

---

## 20. Silent Epidemic of the Century

**PEARL:** **Alzheimer's disease** is called the **"silent epidemic of the century"**.

**MUST KNOW:** Driven by **population ageing**; it is the commonest cause of dementia (~60–70%) and a leading cause of **disability and dependence** in the elderly, with a huge caregiver burden. Note other nicknames often confused with it: **obesity/diabetes — "the modern epidemic"**, **hypertension — "the silent killer"**.

**REMEMBER:** *Alzheimer's = silent epidemic; hypertension = silent killer.*
