## 1. Predictive Values and Prevalence

**PEARL:** **POSITIVE PREDICTIVE VALUE = TP / (TP + FP)** — the denominator is **ALL THOSE WHO TESTED POSITIVE.** **NEGATIVE PREDICTIVE VALUE = TN / (TN + FN).** **SENSITIVITY = TP/(TP+FN) and SPECIFICITY = TN/(TN+FP) are INTRINSIC to the test and do NOT change with prevalence — but PREDICTIVE VALUES DO, and PREVALENCE AFFECTS THEM MORE THAN SENSITIVITY OR SPECIFICITY DOES.** PPV rises and NPV falls as prevalence rises.

**UNDERSTAND:** Sensitivity and specificity read the table **VERTICALLY (starting from disease status)**, while predictive values read it **HORIZONTALLY (starting from the test result)** — which is the question the clinician actually asks: *"my patient tested positive, what is the chance he has the disease?"* Because the number of false positives depends on **how many disease-free people are being tested**, a highly specific test still generates mostly false positives when the disease is rare — the reason **screening a low-prevalence population wastes effort and alarms the well.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Work the standard calculation by assuming a population of 100 (or 1000): prevalence gives the disease column totals, sensitivity gives TP, specificity gives TN, and the rest follows by subtraction. Example: prevalence 30%, sensitivity 90%, specificity 80% in 100 people → TP 27, FN 3, TN 56, FP 14 → PPV = 27/41 = 66%, NPV = 56/59 = 95%.**
- **A HIGHLY SENSITIVE test is used to RULE OUT disease when NEGATIVE ("SnNOut") and is chosen for SCREENING, for serious but treatable disease, and when the cost of missing a case is high. A HIGHLY SPECIFIC test RULES IN disease when POSITIVE ("SpPin") and is used for CONFIRMATION.**
- **Moving the CUT-OFF trades one for the other: lowering the cut-off raises sensitivity and lowers specificity. The ROC CURVE plots sensitivity against (1 − specificity), and the AREA UNDER THE CURVE measures overall accuracy; the best cut-off lies nearest the top-left corner.**
- **When two tests are used in SERIES (both must be positive), specificity and PPV rise; in PARALLEL (either positive counts), sensitivity and NPV rise.**
- **Screening criteria (WILSON AND JUNGNER): the disease must be an important health problem with a recognisable LATENT/early stage and a known natural history; there must be an ACCEPTABLE, safe, valid and inexpensive test; there must be an AGREED TREATMENT and facilities available; and screening must be a CONTINUING process, not a one-off, with the cost balanced against the benefit.**
- **Beware LEAD-TIME BIAS (survival appears longer merely because diagnosis is earlier) and LENGTH BIAS (screening preferentially detects slow-growing, better-prognosis disease) when evaluating any screening programme.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Denominator for PPV" → **true positives + false positives**; NPV calculations are pure 2×2 arithmetic.

**REMEMBER:** *Sensitivity and specificity belong to the test; predictive values belong to the population.*

## 2. Incidence, Prevalence and the Rates That Matter

**PEARL:** **INCIDENCE = NEW cases in a defined population over a defined period — it is a RATE, is NOT affected by disease duration, and is the best indicator of the EFFICACY of health services and of ACUTE disease.** **PREVALENCE = ALL existing cases at a point/period — a PROPORTION, given by PREVALENCE ≈ INCIDENCE × DURATION**, and useful for planning services and for chronic disease. **CASE FATALITY RATE = deaths from a disease / total cases × 100 — the "KILLING POWER" of a disease, with NO time interval specified. SURVIVAL RATE (usually 5-year) is the yardstick for assessing STANDARDS OF THERAPY.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Because **prevalence is the product of incidence and duration**, anything that prolongs life without curing (better treatment of HIV, diabetes, heart failure) **RAISES prevalence while incidence falls** — a paradox that makes prevalence a poor measure of programme success. Conversely, **a rapidly fatal or rapidly cured disease has a high incidence but a low prevalence.** This is why an effective treatment programme can make a disease look commoner.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Incidence FALLS with effective PRIMARY prevention; prevalence falls with cure or (grimly) with death. Incidence rises are what outbreak detection is built on.**
- **ATTACK RATE is an incidence rate used in epidemics over a short period; the SECONDARY ATTACK RATE (new cases among contacts of a primary case, within the incubation period, divided by susceptible contacts) measures INFECTIVITY/spreading power.**
- **Other essential rates: CRUDE DEATH RATE (deaths/mid-year population × 1000 — crude, affected by age structure); SPECIFIC death rates (age, sex, cause); STANDARDISED (adjusted) rates allow comparison between populations of different age structures — DIRECT standardisation applies the study rates to a standard population, INDIRECT gives the STANDARDISED MORTALITY RATIO. PROPORTIONAL MORTALITY RATE is the proportion of all deaths due to a particular cause.**
- **INFANT MORTALITY RATE (deaths under 1 year per 1000 LIVE BIRTHS) is the single most sensitive indicator of the AVAILABILITY, UTILISATION AND EFFECTIVENESS of health care, and of overall socio-economic conditions; the UNDER-5 MORTALITY RATE is the best indicator of SOCIO-ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT.**
- **Neonatal (<28 days) versus post-neonatal (28 days–1 year) mortality: neonatal deaths reflect ENDOGENOUS factors (prematurity, birth asphyxia, congenital anomalies) and post-neonatal deaths EXOGENOUS ones (infection, diarrhoea, malnutrition) — so the ratio tells you which interventions a district needs.**
- **PERINATAL MORTALITY (stillbirths + first-week deaths per 1000 total births) is the most sensitive index of obstetric and neonatal care.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "True about incidence" → **new cases in a defined period, and it is a rate**; "killing power with no time interval" → **case fatality rate**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prevalence is incidence multiplied by how long people stay ill — so curing without preventing raises it.*

## 3. Levels of Prevention

**PEARL:** **PRIMORDIAL prevention PREVENTS THE EMERGENCE OF RISK FACTORS — e.g. instilling healthy lifestyle habits IN CHILDHOOD.** **PRIMARY prevention acts on risk factors already present in healthy people (health promotion, specific protection — immunisation, lifestyle change in ADULTS); SECONDARY prevention detects and treats EARLY DISEASE (screening, early diagnosis and treatment); TERTIARY prevention limits DISABILITY and REHABILITATES.** **PROSPECTIVE screening (of healthy people to prevent disease, e.g. screening donated blood) is PRIMARY prevention; PRESCRIPTIVE screening (case detection for the patient's own benefit, e.g. neonatal hypothyroidism screening, Pap smear) is SECONDARY prevention.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The classification is defined by **WHERE IN THE NATURAL HISTORY OF DISEASE the intervention acts** — before the risk factor exists (primordial), before the disease exists (primary), in the pre-symptomatic phase (secondary), or after disability has developed (tertiary). The **same action can occupy different levels depending on the target**: teaching a child not to smoke is PRIMORDIAL; helping an adult smoker quit is PRIMARY; treating his early lung cancer is SECONDARY; and pulmonary rehabilitation is TERTIARY.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The "modes of intervention" (Park): HEALTH PROMOTION and SPECIFIC PROTECTION are primary; EARLY DIAGNOSIS AND TREATMENT is secondary; DISABILITY LIMITATION and REHABILITATION are tertiary.**
- **Rehabilitation has four components — MEDICAL, VOCATIONAL, SOCIAL and PSYCHOLOGICAL.**
- **Disease definitions: CONTROL — reducing incidence, prevalence, morbidity and mortality to an acceptable level, with measures continuing. ELIMINATION — INTERRUPTION OF TRANSMISSION / reduction to zero (or a very low target) in a DEFINED GEOGRAPHICAL AREA, with continued measures required (leprosy, neonatal tetanus, measles). ERADICATION — permanent WORLDWIDE reduction to zero with NO further measures needed (SMALLPOX only; rinderpest in animals). EXTINCTION — the organism no longer exists even in the laboratory.**
- **MONITORING is the routine day-to-day measurement of ongoing activities and the analysis of routine measurements to detect changes; SURVEILLANCE is the CONTINUOUS SCRUTINY of the factors determining disease occurrence and distribution, with feedback and action; SENTINEL SURVEILLANCE identifies MISSED cases through selected reporting sites, supplementing notification.**
- **EVALUATION differs again — it judges whether objectives were achieved, using indicators of relevance, adequacy, effectiveness, efficiency and impact.**
- **HEALTH PROMOTION (Ottawa) and DISEASE PREVENTION are complementary but distinct: promotion is about enabling people to increase control over their health, whatever their disease status.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Lifestyle change in CHILDHOOD → **primordial**; prospective screening → **primary prevention**.

**REMEMBER:** *Primordial stops the risk factor, primary stops the disease, secondary catches it early, tertiary limits the damage.*

## 4. The Iceberg Phenomenon

**PEARL:** The **ICEBERG PHENOMENON** describes disease in which the **clinically apparent cases (the tip) are far outnumbered by SUBCLINICAL, latent and undiagnosed cases (the submerged bulk).** It is shown by **POLIO, INFLUENZA, RUBELLA, MUMPS, JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS, DIPHTHERIA, HEPATITIS A and B, TUBERCULOSIS, DIABETES, HYPERTENSION and MALNUTRITION.** It is **NOT shown by RABIES, TETANUS or MEASLES — diseases in which almost every infection is clinically apparent.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The iceberg exists whenever the **INFECTION-TO-DISEASE RATIO is high** — most infections are subclinical because of partial immunity, low inoculum or the organism's own biology. Its practical consequence is central to public health: **the visible cases are a poor guide to the true burden and to transmission**, because **subclinical cases still transmit** (the great difficulty in polio and hepatitis control). Conversely, **measles, rabies and tetanus have almost no submerged portion**, so notified cases approximate the true burden and eradication by case-finding is feasible.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The iceberg's layers, from the surface downwards: clinical disease → subclinical/inapparent disease → latent infection/carrier state → the "healthy" but susceptible population.**
- **The practical implications: notification rates UNDER-ESTIMATE disease; SEROLOGICAL SURVEYS (serological epidemiology) are needed to reveal the submerged portion; and CARRIERS and subclinical cases sustain transmission unseen.**
- **Related infectivity concepts: INFECTIVITY (ability to invade and multiply — measured by the secondary attack rate), PATHOGENICITY (ability to cause clinically apparent disease — the ratio of clinical cases to total infections), and VIRULENCE (ability to cause severe disease and death — measured by the case fatality rate). MEASLES has high infectivity and high pathogenicity but low virulence; RABIES has low infectivity but 100% virulence; POLIO has high infectivity but LOW pathogenicity — hence its iceberg.**
- **Carrier states: INCUBATORY, CONVALESCENT, HEALTHY, and CHRONIC (typhoid "Typhoid Mary", hepatitis B) — chronic carriers are the most dangerous because they are unrecognised and long-lasting.**
- **DIABETES and HYPERTENSION are the classic NON-COMMUNICABLE icebergs — roughly half of cases in India are undiagnosed, which is the rationale for opportunistic screening in the population-based NCD programme.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Shows the iceberg phenomenon" → **rubella** (over tetanus, rabies and measles).

**REMEMBER:** *If most infections are silent, the notified cases are only the tip.*

## 5. Concepts and Determinants of Health

**PEARL:** The **WHO definition: "health is a state of complete PHYSICAL, MENTAL and SOCIAL WELL-BEING and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity"** — later extended to include **"the ability to lead a socially and economically productive life". EMOTIONAL well-being is NOT part of the definition.** **SOCIO-ECONOMIC determinants are ECONOMIC STATUS (per capita GNP), EDUCATION, OCCUPATION and the POLITICAL SYSTEM. LIFESTYLE is a BEHAVIOURAL/socio-cultural determinant, NOT a socio-economic one.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The WHO definition matters because it shifted health from a **medical to a social construct** — if health includes social well-being and productivity, then **doctors and hospitals cannot deliver it alone**, and the determinants that matter most (income, education, housing, sanitation, employment) lie outside the health sector. This is precisely why the **"POSITIVE HEALTH" ideal is called a MIRAGE**: perfect adaptation to a constantly changing environment is unattainable, which is why the goal has shifted to **improving QUALITY OF LIFE.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **STANDARD OF LIVING (WHO) = INCOME AND OCCUPATION, standards of HOUSING, SANITATION and NUTRITION, and the level of provision of HEALTH, EDUCATIONAL and RECREATIONAL services — it depends on per capita GNP. LEVEL OF LIVING (UN) has NINE components, adding HEALTH, employment, education, consumption, transport, housing, clothing, recreation, human freedom and — the discriminator — SOCIAL SECURITY. QUALITY OF LIFE is the subjective, value-based assessment of well-being.**
- **Socio-economic INDICATORS of health ("H-FLAGGED"): Housing (persons per room), Family size, Literacy rate (especially FEMALE literacy), per capita calorie Availability, GNP per capita, population Growth rate, unEmployment, Dependency ratio. **ANNUAL FAMILY INCOME is NOT one of them** (per capita GNP is used instead).
- **Other determinants of health: BIOLOGICAL (genetics, age, sex), BEHAVIOURAL/lifestyle, ENVIRONMENTAL (the "internal" and "external" environment), socio-economic, HEALTH SERVICES, and other sectors (agriculture, education, industry, rural development).**
- **WHO recommends spending at least 5% of GDP on health; INDIA SPENDS ABOUT 2% — a key indicator of political commitment, and the National Health Policy 2017 targets 2.5%.**
- **The DIMENSIONS of health extend beyond the definition: physical, mental, social, spiritual, emotional, vocational and others — which is why "emotional" appears in the dimensions but NOT in the WHO definition.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not in the WHO definition" → **emotional well-being**; "not a socio-economic determinant" → **lifestyle**.

**REMEMBER:** *Physical, mental, social — and productive. Emotional is a dimension, not part of the definition.*

## 6. Health Indicators

**PEARL:** **MORTALITY indicators: crude death rate, expectation of LIFE, infant and under-5 mortality, maternal mortality, proportional and disease-specific mortality — LIFE EXPECTANCY is a POSITIVE mortality indicator.** **MORBIDITY indicators: incidence and prevalence, NOTIFICATION rates, ATTENDANCE RATES at OPD, admission/discharge rates, DURATION OF STAY in hospital, spells of sickness.** **DISABILITY indicators: EVENT-type — DAYS OF RESTRICTED ACTIVITY, bed disability days, work/school loss days; PERSON-type — limitation of mobility and of activity. Composite: DALY, HALE, Sullivan's index.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The categories exist because **each answers a different question**: mortality indicators measure how many die, morbidity how many are ill, disability how much function is lost, and composite indicators combine mortality and morbidity into a single number. The shift towards **DALYs and HALE reflects the epidemiological transition** — as deaths fall and chronic disease rises, counting deaths alone no longer captures the burden of illness.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **DALY = YEARS OF LIFE LOST (YLL, from premature death) + YEARS LIVED WITH DISABILITY (YLD). ONE DALY = ONE LOST YEAR OF HEALTHY LIFE, so the aim of health programmes is to REDUCE DALYs. HALE (health-adjusted life expectancy) = the years a newborn can expect to live IN FULL HEALTH. SULLIVAN'S INDEX (disability-free life expectancy) = expectation of life free of disability, but it does NOT account for premature death.**
- **Other categories of indicators: HEALTH CARE DELIVERY indicators (doctor–population ratio, nurse–doctor ratio, population–bed ratio, population per health centre), UTILISATION rates (antenatal care coverage, immunisation coverage, contraceptive prevalence rate), SOCIAL AND MENTAL health indicators (suicide, homicide, road accidents, drug abuse), ENVIRONMENTAL indicators, socio-economic indicators, HEALTH POLICY indicators (proportion of GNP spent on health) and QUALITY OF LIFE indices.**
- **The characteristics of a GOOD indicator: VALID, RELIABLE and objective, SENSITIVE, SPECIFIC, FEASIBLE and RELEVANT.**
- **HDI (Human Development Index) combines LONGEVITY (life expectancy at birth), KNOWLEDGE (MEAN years of schooling and EXPECTED years of schooling — the adult literacy rate and gross enrolment ratio were dropped after 2009) and STANDARD OF LIVING (GNI per capita in PPP). Its "GOALPOSTS" for life expectancy are a MINIMUM OF 20 AND A MAXIMUM OF 85 YEARS; each dimension index = (actual − minimum)/(maximum − minimum), and the HDI is their geometric mean, ranging 0–1.**
- **PQLI (Physical Quality of Life Index) uses INFANT MORTALITY, LIFE EXPECTANCY AT AGE ONE and LITERACY — it deliberately EXCLUDES income. HPI (Human Poverty Index, now replaced by the MULTIDIMENSIONAL POVERTY INDEX) measures DEPRIVATION in the same basic dimensions as the HDI.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Mortality indicator" → **life expectancy**; "not a morbidity indicator" → **days of restricted activity** (disability); years lived in full health → **HALE**; deprivation index → **HPI**.

**REMEMBER:** *Mortality counts deaths, morbidity counts illness, disability counts lost function — DALY adds the first two together.*

## 7. The Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion

**PEARL:** The **OTTAWA CHARTER (First International Conference on Health Promotion, 21 November 1986) identified FIVE ACTION AREAS: (1) BUILD HEALTHY PUBLIC POLICY, (2) CREATE SUPPORTIVE ENVIRONMENTS, (3) STRENGTHEN COMMUNITY ACTION, (4) DEVELOP PERSONAL SKILLS, and (5) REORIENT HEALTH SERVICES.** **"Building a social security system" is NOT one of them.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The charter's radical claim was that **health is created where people live, love, work and play — not in hospitals** — so health promotion means **changing policy, environments and communities**, not merely educating individuals. That is why **"REORIENT HEALTH SERVICES"** is one of the five: services must shift from a purely curative role towards prevention and health promotion, and health must become **everybody's business rather than the health sector's alone ("health in all policies").**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The charter also names three basic STRATEGIES — ADVOCATE (for health), ENABLE (equity, so all can achieve their potential) and MEDIATE (between differing interests in society) — and lists the PREREQUISITES for health: peace, shelter, education, food, income, a stable ecosystem, sustainable resources, social justice and equity.**
- **Landmark declarations to keep in order: ALMA-ATA 1978 — "HEALTH FOR ALL BY 2000" through PRIMARY HEALTH CARE, with the four principles of EQUITABLE DISTRIBUTION, COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION, INTERSECTORAL COORDINATION and APPROPRIATE TECHNOLOGY, and the eight essential elements ("ELEMENTS"). OTTAWA 1986 — health promotion. BANGKOK CHARTER 2005 — health promotion in a globalised world. ASTANA 2018 — renewed commitment to primary health care and UNIVERSAL HEALTH COVERAGE.**
- **UNIVERSAL HEALTH COVERAGE means all people receive the quality health services they need WITHOUT FINANCIAL HARDSHIP — its three dimensions are the POPULATION covered, the SERVICES covered, and the PROPORTION OF COSTS covered. Its central challenge is providing care to everyone REGARDLESS OF A COUNTRY'S INCOME — i.e. mobilising sufficient resources in low-income settings — alongside health inequities and the need to reduce CATASTROPHIC OUT-OF-POCKET EXPENDITURE (very high in India, where it drives millions below the poverty line each year).**
- **India's UHC vehicles: AYUSHMAN BHARAT — Health and Wellness Centres for comprehensive primary care, and PM-JAY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana) providing ₹5 lakh per family per year of secondary and tertiary hospitalisation cover.**
- **SDG 3 ("ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages") includes UHC as target 3.8.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not in the Ottawa charter" → **build a social security system**.

**REMEMBER:** *Policy, environments, communities, skills, services — five actions, and none of them is social security.*

## 8. The Death Certificate

**PEARL:** On the international death certificate, **PART I(a) is the IMMEDIATE (direct) cause of death — the DISEASE that directly led to death; I(b) and I(c) are ANTECEDENT causes leading to it; and the LAST entry in Part I is the UNDERLYING CAUSE OF DEATH — which is what is CODED and used in national statistics. PART II lists other significant conditions contributing to death but not related to the causal sequence.** ***MODES OF DYING — "respiratory failure", "cardiac arrest", "circulatory failure" — MUST NEVER BE WRITTEN as the cause of death.***

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction is between **HOW someone died and WHY.** Every death ends in cardiorespiratory arrest, so recording it conveys nothing; the certificate exists to capture the **UNDERLYING CAUSE — the disease or injury that initiated the train of events**, because that is the only entry on which prevention can act. In the example of an HIV-positive patient with MDR tuberculosis dying of lobar pneumonia: **I(a) lobar pneumonia, I(b) MDR tuberculosis, Part II HIV.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The UNDERLYING CAUSE is defined as "the disease or injury which initiated the train of morbid events leading directly to death, or the circumstances of the accident or violence which produced the fatal injury" — and it is the one used in the INTERNATIONAL CLASSIFICATION OF DISEASES (ICD-11) for mortality statistics.**
- **The approximate INTERVAL between onset and death is entered against each line.**
- **In India, registration of births and deaths is compulsory under the REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS ACT 1969; the MEDICAL CERTIFICATION OF CAUSE OF DEATH (MCCD) scheme provides the cause-of-death data, though coverage remains incomplete — which is why the SAMPLE REGISTRATION SYSTEM (SRS) is the main source of fertility and mortality rates, and VERBAL AUTOPSY is used where medical certification is unavailable.**
- **Sources of health information: census (every 10 years), civil registration, SRS, notification of diseases, hospital records, disease registries (cancer), record linkage, epidemiological surveillance (IDSP), NFHS and other national surveys, and environmental and health-manpower statistics.**
- **Errors in certification (writing a mode of dying, omitting the underlying cause, or wrong sequence) are the commonest source of unusable mortality data — the reason this is examined so persistently.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** HIV + MDR-TB dying of lobar pneumonia → **I(a) = lobar pneumonia** (respiratory failure is a mode of dying).

**REMEMBER:** *Respiratory failure is how he died; lobar pneumonia is why — and the last line of Part I is what gets counted.*

## 9. Assessing Body Fat and Nutritional Status

**PEARL:** Methods of assessing BODY FAT include **QUETELET'S INDEX (BODY MASS INDEX = weight in kg / height in m²), skinfold thickness, waist–hip ratio, TOTAL BODY WATER, TOTAL BODY POTASSIUM, bioelectrical impedance, densitometry and DEXA.** **BRESLOW'S INDEX is NOT one — it measures the DEPTH OF INVASION IN MALIGNANT MELANOMA.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The indirect methods all exploit the fact that **FAT IS ANHYDROUS AND POTASSIUM-FREE**, whereas lean body mass has a predictable water and potassium content. So measuring **total body water (by isotope dilution) or total body potassium (by whole-body ⁴⁰K counting) gives the LEAN mass, and fat is obtained by SUBTRACTION.** Understanding this makes the list memorable rather than arbitrary.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **BMI classification (WHO): <18.5 underweight; 18.5–24.9 normal; 25–29.9 overweight; ≥30 obese. ***ASIAN/INDIAN CUT-OFFS ARE LOWER*** — overweight ≥23 and obese ≥25 — because Asians develop central adiposity, insulin resistance and cardiovascular risk at a lower BMI.**
- **WAIST CIRCUMFERENCE and WAIST–HIP RATIO measure CENTRAL (visceral) obesity, which predicts cardiometabolic risk better than BMI. Indian cut-offs: waist ≥90 cm in men and ≥80 cm in women; WHR >0.90 in men and >0.85 in women.**
- **BMI's limitation is that it does not distinguish fat from muscle — a muscular athlete and a sarcopenic elderly person can share a BMI.**
- **SKINFOLD THICKNESS is measured with HARPENDEN CALIPERS at four sites (biceps, triceps, subscapular, suprailiac); TRICEPS skinfold and MID-UPPER ARM CIRCUMFERENCE (MUAC) are the field measures. **MUAC is age-independent between 1 and 5 years: <11.5 cm indicates SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION and 11.5–12.5 cm moderate.**
- **Child nutritional assessment: WEIGHT-FOR-AGE (UNDERWEIGHT), HEIGHT-FOR-AGE (STUNTING — chronic malnutrition), WEIGHT-FOR-HEIGHT (WASTING — acute malnutrition), plotted as Z-SCORES on WHO standards; the GOMEZ, WATERLOW and IAP classifications are the older systems. SHAKIR'S TAPE and the BANGLE TEST are field screening tools.**
- **Nutritional assessment overall combines CLINICAL examination, ANTHROPOMETRY, BIOCHEMICAL tests, DIETARY survey (24-hour recall, food frequency) and VITAL/health statistics.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not used to assess body fat" → **Breslow index**.

**REMEMBER:** *Fat holds no water and no potassium — so measure the lean mass and subtract.*

## 10. Measures of Dispersion

**PEARL:** The **COEFFICIENT OF VARIATION (CV = standard deviation / mean × 100) is used to COMPARE the variability of TWO DIFFERENT DATA SETS**, especially when their units or means differ. **STANDARD DEVIATION measures variability within one set; VARIANCE is its square; and the STANDARD ERROR OF THE MEAN (SD/√n) measures the precision of the SAMPLE MEAN as an estimate of the population mean.**

**UNDERSTAND:** A standard deviation cannot be compared across data sets because it **carries the units and scale of its own data** — an SD of 5 kg means something quite different for birth weights than for adult weights. The CV solves this by expressing variability as a **percentage of the mean, making it dimensionless** — which is exactly why it is used to compare, for example, the variability of blood pressure with that of cholesterol.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **In a NORMAL distribution: MEAN = MEDIAN = MODE; ±1 SD covers 68.3%, ±2 SD covers 95.4% (and ±1.96 SD exactly 95%), and ±3 SD covers 99.7% of observations. The curve is bell-shaped and symmetrical, defined entirely by its mean and SD.**
- **Choosing the measure of central tendency: use the MEAN for symmetrical data; use the MEDIAN for SKEWED data or when there are extreme outliers (income, hospital stay); the MODE for the commonest value and for nominal data.**
- **SD versus SEM: SD describes the SPREAD OF THE DATA and does not shrink with a larger sample; SEM describes the PRECISION OF THE ESTIMATE and FALLS as the sample size rises. CONFIDENCE INTERVALS are built from the SEM (95% CI = mean ± 1.96 SEM).**
- **Choosing a significance test: NORMALLY DISTRIBUTED quantitative data → t-TEST (paired or unpaired) and ANOVA for more than two groups; QUALITATIVE/categorical data → CHI-SQUARE test (FISHER'S EXACT test when expected cell counts are small); non-parametric data → Mann–Whitney U, Wilcoxon, Kruskal–Wallis. CORRELATION (Pearson's r, −1 to +1) measures association; REGRESSION predicts one variable from another.**
- **A p value <0.05 means the result would occur by chance less than 5% of the time — it says nothing about the SIZE or CLINICAL IMPORTANCE of the effect, which is why CONFIDENCE INTERVALS are preferred. TYPE I error (α) = rejecting a true null hypothesis (false positive); TYPE II error (β) = missing a true difference; POWER = 1 − β, conventionally 80%.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Comparing variability between two data sets → **coefficient of variation**.

**REMEMBER:** *Standard deviation carries units, the coefficient of variation does not — so only the CV can compare apples with oranges.*

## 11. Nipah Virus

**PEARL:** **NIPAH VIRUS is an RNA PARAMYXOVIRUS (genus HENIPAVIRUS) whose NATURAL RESERVOIR is the FRUIT BAT (*Pteropus*, family Pteropodidae).** It shows **ANIMAL-TO-HUMAN (bats, PIGS, date palm sap) AND HUMAN-TO-HUMAN transmission**, causing **acute respiratory illness and FATAL ENCEPHALITIS. There is NO vaccine and NO specific treatment — care is SUPPORTIVE**, and it is on the **WHO Blueprint list of priority diseases.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Nipah's danger lies in the combination of a **very high case fatality rate (40–75%), person-to-person spread through respiratory droplets and body fluids, and a bat reservoir that cannot be eliminated.** Because transmission is amplified in **hospitals and households caring for the sick**, control depends entirely on **early detection, strict barrier nursing and contact tracing** — the same logic as Ebola, which is why it appears repeatedly in Indian outbreaks (Siliguri 2001, Kerala 2018 onwards).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Transmission routes to remember: consumption of RAW DATE PALM SAP or fruit contaminated by bat saliva/urine (the Bangladesh/Kerala pattern), contact with infected PIGS (the Malaysia 1998 pattern), and direct contact with an infected person's secretions — including NOSOCOMIAL spread to health workers and family carers.**
- **Clinical course: 4–14 days of incubation, then fever and headache progressing to drowsiness, disorientation, SEGMENTAL MYOCLONUS, seizures and coma within 24–48 hours; some develop ARDS. RELAPSING or LATE-ONSET ENCEPHALITIS may occur MONTHS TO YEARS later — a feature almost unique to this virus.**
- **Diagnosis: RT-PCR of throat/nasal swabs, CSF, urine and blood in the acute phase, and ELISA for IgM/IgG later; handle specimens at BSL-4 for isolation (BSL-2/3 for PCR with appropriate precautions).**
- **Control: isolate cases with STANDARD, CONTACT AND DROPLET precautions (with airborne precautions for aerosol-generating procedures), trace and quarantine contacts for 21 days, avoid raw date palm sap and fallen fruit, and use PPE for animal handling. RIBAVIRIN and monoclonal antibodies (m102.4) have been used with uncertain benefit.**
- **The other bat-borne henipavirus is HENDRA (Australia, horses).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Life-cycle diagram with fruit bats, pigs and humans → **Nipah virus**.

## 12. Oral Cholera Vaccine

**PEARL:** **SHANCHOL is a BIVALENT (O1 AND O139), KILLED WHOLE-CELL ORAL cholera vaccine given as TWO LIQUID DOSES 14 DAYS APART to anyone aged 1 YEAR AND ABOVE, and it does NOT require a bicarbonate buffer.** (DUKORAL, by contrast, is a monovalent O1 whole-cell vaccine WITH recombinant B subunit that DOES require a buffer.) Protection lasts about **3 years**, and a booster is given after 2 years.

**UNDERSTAND:** An **ORAL, killed whole-cell vaccine is used because cholera is a purely LUMINAL, non-invasive infection** — protection depends on **SECRETORY IgA in the gut, which only mucosal (oral) immunisation reliably induces.** Shanchol needs no buffer because it does not contain the acid-labile cholera toxin B subunit that Dukoral must protect from gastric acid — which is precisely what makes it cheaper, simpler and suitable for mass campaigns in outbreaks.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Oral cholera vaccine is an ADJUNCT, never a substitute for WATER, SANITATION AND HYGIENE — the definitive control measures. WHO maintains a global OCV stockpile for outbreak and humanitarian use.**
- **Cholera control in an outbreak: early case detection and treatment (ORS for all, IV RINGER'S LACTATE for severe dehydration, and a single dose of DOXYCYCLINE or azithromycin as an adjunct), safe water and sanitation, food hygiene, health education, and OCV in high-risk populations. ***MASS CHEMOPROPHYLAXIS AND TRAVEL RESTRICTIONS/quarantine ARE NOT RECOMMENDED*** — only selective chemoprophylaxis of close household contacts.**
- **Cholera is one of the three INTERNATIONALLY NOTIFIABLE diseases under the International Health Regulations, along with PLAGUE and YELLOW FEVER.**
- **A "cholera case" for surveillance: acute watery diarrhoea with or without vomiting in a person aged 5 or more in an area where cholera is not known to be present, or any case of acute watery diarrhoea in an area where it is.**
- **Vaccines that require special handling are examinable: OPV and measles are the most heat-sensitive; the OPEN VIAL POLICY, the VACCINE VIAL MONITOR and the COLD CHAIN (+2 to +8 °C at the health facility) govern their use. Freeze-sensitive vaccines — HEPATITIS B, DPT, TT and the diluent — must NEVER be frozen.**

## 13. Environmental Health Standards

**PEARL:** **CONTROLLED TIPPING (SANITARY LANDFILL) is the most satisfactory method of refuse disposal where land is available — the TRENCH METHOD requires about 1 ACRE OF LAND PER 10,000 POPULATION PER YEAR.** Its variants: **TRENCH (level ground), RAMP (sloping terrain) and AREA (filling depressions, quarries and clay pits).** **The DAYLIGHT FACTOR should be 10% IN KITCHENS and 8% in living rooms.**

**UNDERSTAND:** In controlled tipping, refuse is **compacted and covered with earth at the END OF EACH DAY** — the covering is what distinguishes it from crude dumping, because it excludes flies and rodents, prevents wind-blown litter and smell, and forces **anaerobic decomposition, which completes in 4–6 MONTHS** with a temperature rise to about 60 °C that kills pathogens and larvae. Understanding the daily cover explains every advantage claimed for the method.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other refuse disposal methods: COMPOSTING (aerobic — BANGALORE method is ANAEROBIC/hot fermentation in pits, taking 4–6 months; BANGALORE is the one recommended for night soil, while the INDORE method is aerobic, taking ~4 months with turning), INCINERATION (for hospital and hazardous waste), MANURE PITS and burial (rural), and modern SANITARY LANDFILL with leachate and gas management.**
- **BIOMEDICAL WASTE colour coding (2016 rules): YELLOW — human/animal anatomical, soiled, chemical, cytotoxic and expired medicines (incineration/deep burial); RED — contaminated recyclable plastics such as tubing, catheters and syringes without needles (autoclave/microwave, then shred and recycle); WHITE (translucent, puncture-proof) — SHARPS (autoclave and shred); BLUE — broken/discarded glass and metallic implants (disinfect and recycle).**
- **Housing standards worth carrying: floor space at least 100 sq ft for one person and 50 sq ft per additional person; a room height of 10 feet; window area at least ONE-FIFTH of the floor area; the sill not more than 3 feet above the floor; and a daylight factor of 8–10%. Optimum comfort: 21–23 °C, humidity 40–60%, air movement 6–9 m/min; the COMFORT ZONE by effective temperature is 20–25 °C.**
- **Water standards: WHO guideline of ZERO *E. COLI* per 100 mL; residual chlorine of 0.5 mg/L after 1 hour of contact; the minimum daily requirement is ~150–200 litres per capita per day for urban supply; and the HORROCK'S APPARATUS determines the bleaching powder dose for well disinfection.**
- **Air pollution indicators (SO2, NO2, PM10 and PM2.5), noise limits (residential day 55 dB, night 45 dB) and the ANTI-FLY measures follow the same "standard plus rationale" pattern in exams.**

## 14. Management Methods in Health Programmes

**PEARL:** Management techniques are either **QUANTITATIVE or BEHAVIOURAL. BEHAVIOURAL-SCIENCE methods: MANAGEMENT BY OBJECTIVES (MBO), organisational design, personnel management, communication and information systems. QUANTITATIVE methods: COST–BENEFIT and COST-EFFECTIVENESS ANALYSIS, cost accounting, input–output analysis, MODELS, SYSTEMS ANALYSIS, NETWORK ANALYSIS (PERT and CPM), PPBS (planning–programming–budgeting system), WORK SAMPLING and DECISION MAKING.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The division reflects what each technique manipulates. **Quantitative methods handle RESOURCES, TIME AND MONEY** — they answer "what does it cost, how long will it take, which option gives most health per rupee?" **Behavioural methods handle PEOPLE** — they answer "how do we get staff to own the objectives and communicate?" **MBO is behavioural precisely because its mechanism is participative goal-setting between manager and subordinate**, not calculation.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **COST-EFFECTIVENESS ANALYSIS compares the cost of achieving the same health outcome (cost per life saved, per DALY averted) — outcomes are in NATURAL units. COST–BENEFIT ANALYSIS expresses BOTH costs and outcomes in MONEY. COST–UTILITY uses QALYs/DALYs. Cost-effectiveness is the one used most in public health because it avoids putting a price on a life.**
- **NETWORK ANALYSIS: PERT (Programme Evaluation and Review Technique) and CPM (Critical Path Method) schedule interdependent activities and identify the CRITICAL PATH — the longest sequence, which determines the project's minimum duration and where delay cannot be tolerated.**
- **The MANAGEMENT PROCESS/cycle: planning → organising → staffing → directing → coordinating → reporting → budgeting ("POSDCORB"), with EVALUATION feeding back into planning.**
- **PLANNING CYCLE in health: analysis of the situation → establishment of objectives and priorities → assessment of resources → fixing priorities → writing up the formulated plan → programming and implementation → monitoring → evaluation.**
- **Evaluation criteria (WHO): RELEVANCE, ADEQUACY, ACCESSIBILITY, ACCEPTABILITY, EFFECTIVENESS (does it work in practice?), EFFICIENCY (output per unit input) and IMPACT.**
- **HEALTH SYSTEM building blocks (WHO): service delivery, health workforce, information, medical products and technologies, financing, and leadership/governance.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Technique based on behavioural sciences" → **management by objectives**.

**REMEMBER:** *Numbers for resources, behaviour for people — MBO is about agreeing goals, not calculating them.*

## 16. Choosing the Study Design

**PEARL:** **DESCRIPTIVE studies are used for a NEW disease or one of UNKNOWN AETIOLOGY** — they define the problem in terms of TIME, PLACE and PERSON and generate hypotheses. **LONGITUDINAL (cohort) studies establish the NATURAL HISTORY of disease, incidence and risk factors.** **RANDOMISED CONTROLLED TRIALS are the STRONGEST design for PROVING CAUSATION** and for testing interventions. The **HIERARCHY OF EVIDENCE: META-ANALYSIS/SYSTEMATIC REVIEW > RCT > COHORT > CASE-CONTROL > CASE SERIES/REPORTS > expert opinion.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The hierarchy is ordered by **how effectively each design eliminates BIAS AND CONFOUNDING.** Only RANDOMISATION distributes both known AND UNKNOWN confounders equally between groups — which is why an RCT alone can support a causal claim. Observational designs can only adjust for confounders that the investigator thought to measure. **Systematic reviews rank higher still because they combine several RCTs, increasing precision and reducing the play of chance.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The workflow of epidemiology: DESCRIPTIVE (what, who, where, when → hypothesis) → ANALYTICAL (case-control or cohort → tests the hypothesis) → EXPERIMENTAL (RCT → proves it) → and then application to prevention.**
- **NON-RANDOMISED (quasi-experimental) trials include BEFORE-AND-AFTER COMPARISON STUDIES (with or without control), UNCONTROLLED trials and NATURAL EXPERIMENTS (Doll and Hill's smoking studies; John Snow's cholera). Randomised trial types: CLINICAL trials, PREVENTIVE trials (vaccines), RISK-FACTOR trials, CESSATION experiments, TRIALS OF AETIOLOGICAL AGENTS, and EVALUATION OF HEALTH SERVICES.**
- **EVIDENCE-BASED MEDICINE = integrating the BEST AVAILABLE RESEARCH EVIDENCE with CLINICAL EXPERTISE and PATIENT VALUES to make decisions about individual patients — explicitly demoting authority and conventional wisdom.**
- **A SPOT MAP (shaded map) shows the LOCAL distribution, frequency, boundaries and pattern of disease — famously used by JOHN SNOW in the 1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak — but it CANNOT depict rural–urban differences.**
- **Cyclical (periodic) trends: MEASLES recurred every 2–3 YEARS in the pre-vaccine era and RUBELLA every 6–9 YEARS — the interval reflects how long it takes for a new susceptible birth cohort to accumulate. SECULAR trends occur over decades, and SEASONAL trends within a year.**
- **ECOLOGICAL (correlational) studies compare POPULATIONS rather than individuals and are the WEAKEST design — vulnerable to the ECOLOGICAL FALLACY (inferring individual risk from group data).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Unknown aetiology → **descriptive study**; natural history → **longitudinal**; causation → **RCT**.

**REMEMBER:** *Describe, then compare, then randomise — only randomisation balances the confounders you never thought of.*

## 17. Reading the Epidemic Curve

**PEARL:** **COMMON-SOURCE, SINGLE-EXPOSURE (POINT-SOURCE) epidemic — all cases occur WITHIN ONE INCUBATION PERIOD, giving a sharp rise and rapid fall (food poisoning). COMMON-SOURCE, CONTINUOUS/MULTIPLE EXPOSURE — cases span MORE THAN ONE INCUBATION PERIOD from the same source (an outbreak of hepatitis A from June to September, about three incubation periods, peaking in July). PROPAGATED (progressive) epidemic — PERSON-TO-PERSON spread, producing a series of progressively taller peaks ONE INCUBATION PERIOD APART, with a slow rise and slow fall.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The shape of the curve is a **direct readout of the mode of transmission**, which is why drawing it is the first analytical step in any outbreak. **The distance between successive peaks equals ONE INCUBATION PERIOD** (so the curve itself estimates the incubation period), and the **duration of the outbreak relative to that period distinguishes a single exposure from a continuing one.** Counting back one incubation period from the peak identifies the probable time of exposure — the key to finding the source.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **AN AREA IS DECLARED FREE OF AN EPIDEMIC WHEN NO NEW CASES HAVE OCCURRED FOR TWICE THE INCUBATION PERIOD after the last case** — e.g. typhoid (incubation ~14 days) requires **28 DAYS**.
- **Steps in investigating an epidemic, in order: VERIFY THE DIAGNOSIS → CONFIRM THE EXISTENCE OF AN EPIDEMIC (compare with the expected/endemic level) → define the population at risk (map and count) → rapid SEARCH FOR ALL CASES (medical survey, epidemiological case sheets) → ANALYSE DATA BY TIME, PLACE AND PERSON → FORMULATE and then TEST the hypothesis → evaluate ecological factors → further investigation of the population at risk → WRITE THE REPORT — with control measures instituted in parallel from the outset.**
- **The FINAL REPORT includes background (geography, demography, surveillance data and normal prevalence), HISTORICAL data (previous epidemics, the primary case), METHODOLOGY (case definition, questionnaires, laboratory techniques), ANALYSIS (clinical and epidemiological data, modes of transmission) and CONTROL MEASURES with evaluation. **The disease's incubation period is textbook knowledge, NOT a finding to be reported.**
- **ENDEMIC — the constant, usual presence of a disease in an area (40–50 dengue cases a week where 48 occur this week is ENDEMIC, not an epidemic). EPIDEMIC — cases clearly in EXCESS of the expected number. OUTBREAK — an epidemic in a limited geographical area. PANDEMIC — an epidemic crossing international boundaries. SPORADIC — scattered, irregular cases. EXOTIC — imported from elsewhere. ZOONOSIS — transmissible from animals to humans. EPIZOOTIC/ENZOOTIC — the animal equivalents.**
- **The "EPIDEMIC" definition is relative to the EXPECTED level — one case of smallpox or polio constitutes an epidemic.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Outbreak spanning three incubation periods → **common source, multiple exposure**.

**REMEMBER:** *One incubation period means one exposure; peaks one incubation period apart mean person-to-person.*

## 18. Measures of Association and Impact

**PEARL:** **RELATIVE RISK (incidence in exposed / incidence in unexposed) measures the STRENGTH of association and is obtainable only from a COHORT study. ODDS RATIO (ad/bc, the "cross-product ratio") is the measure used in a CASE-CONTROL study**, which cannot yield incidence or relative risk. **ATTRIBUTABLE RISK (%) = (incidence in exposed − incidence in unexposed) / incidence in exposed × 100** — the proportion of disease in the exposed that is due to the exposure. Note the shortcut: **if RR = 5, then AR% = (5−1)/5 = 80%; in general AR% = (RR − 1)/RR × 100.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The two measures answer different questions. **RELATIVE RISK answers "how much does this exposure multiply an individual's risk?" — the question of CAUSATION. ATTRIBUTABLE RISK answers "how much disease would disappear if we removed the exposure?" — the question of PUBLIC HEALTH ACTION.** A rare exposure with a huge RR may matter less to a population than a common exposure with a modest one — which is why POPULATION ATTRIBUTABLE RISK, not RR, drives policy.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **From a treatment trial: ABSOLUTE RISK REDUCTION (ARR) = risk in controls − risk in treated. RELATIVE RISK REDUCTION (RRR) = ARR / control risk = 1 − RR. NUMBER NEEDED TO TREAT (NNT) = 1/ARR. Worked example: risk 11.85% on placebo and 10.53% on drug → ARR ≈ 1.3%, RR = 0.89, RRR ≈ 0.1 (10%), NNT ≈ 76. RRR always sounds more impressive than ARR — the classic way trial results are oversold.**
- **Interpreting the ratios: RR or OR = 1 → no association; >1 → positive association (risk factor); <1 → protective. The 95% confidence interval must NOT INCLUDE 1 for statistical significance.**
- **The ODDS RATIO approximates the relative risk well only when the disease is RARE — which is exactly the situation in which case-control studies are used.**
- **POPULATION ATTRIBUTABLE RISK (Levin) accounts for the prevalence of exposure in the whole population and estimates the benefit of eliminating it from the community.**
- **BRADFORD HILL'S CRITERIA for causation: STRENGTH of association (the most important), CONSISTENCY, SPECIFICITY, TEMPORALITY (the only ABSOLUTELY ESSENTIAL criterion — the cause must precede the effect), BIOLOGICAL GRADIENT (dose–response), PLAUSIBILITY, COHERENCE, EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE and ANALOGY.**
- **Case-control studies give the ODDS RATIO, are cheap, quick and suit RARE DISEASES, but are prone to RECALL and SELECTION BIAS; cohort studies give INCIDENCE, RELATIVE RISK and attributable risk and suit RARE EXPOSURES, but are expensive, slow and suffer LOSS TO FOLLOW-UP.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Strength of association in a case-control study → **odds ratio**; RR of 5 → **attributable risk 80%**.

**REMEMBER:** *Relative risk is for the individual, attributable risk is for the population — and the odds ratio is all a case-control study can give you.*

## 19. Bias, Confounding and Random Error

**PEARL:** **CONFOUNDING is controlled at the DESIGN stage by RANDOMISATION, MATCHING and RESTRICTION, and at the ANALYSIS stage by STRATIFICATION and STATISTICAL MODELLING (regression, multivariate analysis, Mantel–Haenszel). "DELETION" is NOT a method.** **RANDOM ERROR (chance) arises from biological variation, SAMPLING error and measurement error — it can be REDUCED by a larger sample but NEVER completely eliminated, and it DOES NOT CAUSE BIAS. SYSTEMATIC error IS bias.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction is directional. **Random error scatters results symmetrically around the truth — so it reduces PRECISION but not VALIDITY, and averaging more observations converges on the right answer.** **Bias pushes results consistently in one direction, so collecting more data only makes a wrong answer more precise.** That is why sample size fixes random error and only better DESIGN fixes bias.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **A CONFOUNDER must be (1) associated with the exposure, (2) an INDEPENDENT risk factor for the outcome, and (3) NOT an intermediate step in the causal pathway. Age and smoking are the archetypes.**
- **Major biases: SELECTION bias (including BERKSONIAN bias from hospital controls, healthy worker effect, non-response), INFORMATION/MEASUREMENT bias (RECALL bias — the classic weakness of case-control studies; interviewer bias; misclassification), and BIAS DUE TO CONFOUNDING. BLINDING (single, double, triple) prevents observer and subject bias; an INTENTION-TO-TREAT analysis preserves the benefit of randomisation.**
- **CONTROLS in a case-control study should be free of the disease and comparable to cases in all respects except the exposure; they may come from the general population, hospitals, neighbours, colleagues or relatives — up to 4 controls per case improves power in small studies. ***SIBLINGS MUST NOT BE USED AS CONTROLS FOR A GENETIC DISEASE***, because they share the very exposure (genotype) under study — "over-matching", which biases the result towards the null.**
- **MATCHING controls confounding but prevents you from studying the matched variable itself, and over-matching on a factor closely related to exposure destroys the study.**
- **RANDOMISATION is the only technique that controls UNKNOWN confounders; it is not the same as random SAMPLING (which addresses generalisability, not confounding).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Will not reduce confounding" → **deletion**; "not true about random error" → **it causes bias**.

**REMEMBER:** *More data cures chance and entrenches bias — only design cures bias.*

## 20. Carriers, Latency and Subclinical Infection

**PEARL:** In a **LATENT INFECTION the host has NO symptoms AND DOES NOT SHED the organism** (herpes simplex between attacks, Brill–Zinsser disease, slow viruses). In a **SUBCLINICAL infection the host has no symptoms BUT DOES SHED** the organism (hepatitis A, rubella, mumps). **CARRIERS: INCUBATORY (shed during the LATTER PART of the incubation period — measles, polio, mumps, whooping cough, diphtheria, influenza, hepatitis A); CONVALESCENT (shed during recovery — diphtheria, typhoid, cholera, pertussis); HEALTHY (never had overt disease — "CM-DPT": Cholera, Meningitis, Diphtheria, Polio, Typhoid).**

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction turns entirely on **whether the organism is being replicated and released**. In latency the genome persists in a quiescent state (herpesvirus in a ganglion) with no virion production — so the person is **not infectious**, which is precisely why **RABIES is NOT communicable during its incubation period** (the virus is travelling up the nerve, not being shed in saliva). Once shedding begins, the person becomes a source whether or not they feel ill — the entire basis for the difficulty of controlling polio and hepatitis.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Carriers are classified three ways: by TYPE (incubatory, convalescent, healthy), by DURATION (temporary <6 months, chronic — typhoid, hepatitis B), and by PORTAL OF EXIT (urinary, intestinal, respiratory, others).**
- **Chronic carriers are epidemiologically the most dangerous — unrecognised, long-lasting, and often occupationally hazardous (the food handler with typhoid, "Typhoid Mary").**
- **The COMMUNICABLE (infectious) PERIOD is when the agent can be transferred; the INCUBATION PERIOD is from infection to the first sign; the LATENT PERIOD is from infection to the start of infectiousness; and the GENERATION TIME is the interval between the receipt of infection and the maximal infectivity of the host — the generation time, not the incubation period, governs the spacing of peaks in a propagated epidemic.**
- **ISOLATION periods to know: MEASLES — 4 days before to 5 days after the onset of rash; RUBELLA — 7 DAYS after the onset of rash; CHICKENPOX and HERPES ZOSTER — 6 days after the onset of rash (or until all lesions crust); mumps — until swelling subsides; pertussis — 5 days after starting antibiotics; diphtheria — until 2 negative cultures.**
- **QUARANTINE (limiting the movement of WELL contacts for the maximum incubation period) is distinct from ISOLATION (separating the SICK for the period of communicability).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Host does NOT shed the organism" → **latent infection**; "not communicable in the incubation period" → **rabies**.

**REMEMBER:** *Latent means silent and non-infectious; subclinical means silent but shedding.*

## 21. Modes of Transmission

**PEARL:** **DIRECT transmission includes direct CONTACT, DROPLET spread, CONTACT WITH SOIL, INOCULATION into the skin, and VERTICAL (transplacental) transmission. INDIRECT transmission includes VEHICLE-borne, VECTOR-borne, AIRBORNE (DROPLET NUCLEI and dust), FOMITE-borne and unclean HANDS.** **DROPLETS are >5 µm, travel about 30–60 cm (up to ~1 metre) and require closeness — diphtheria, meningococcus, common cold. DROPLET NUCLEI are 1–5 µm residues of evaporated droplets, remain airborne for long periods and travel far — tuberculosis, measles, chickenpox, influenza, Q fever.** **TETANUS does NOT show vertical transmission** (herpes, HIV, rubella and varicella do).

**UNDERSTAND:** The classification hinges on whether the agent **survives independently in the environment between hosts.** Droplet spread is classed as DIRECT despite the air gap, because the particles **fall within a metre and must impinge directly on the mucosa of a close contact** — there is no intermediate vehicle or survival phase. **Droplet nuclei have evaporated, become light enough to stay suspended, and can infect someone who enters the room later** — hence indirect, airborne, and hence the need for negative-pressure rooms and N95 respirators rather than surgical masks.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **NEONATAL TETANUS is acquired at the UMBILICAL STUMP from unclean instruments and dressings — NOT transplacentally. Maternal immunisation protects the newborn by transplacental passage of IgG ANTIBODIES, not by preventing vertical spread of the organism.**
- **BIOLOGICAL VECTOR TRANSMISSION has three patterns: PROPAGATIVE — the agent MULTIPLIES only (plague in the rat flea, arboviruses). CYCLO-DEVELOPMENTAL — the agent DEVELOPS/changes form but does NOT multiply (WUCHERERIA BANCROFTI in *Culex* — microfilaria to infective larva, with no increase in number). CYCLO-PROPAGATIVE — the agent BOTH develops AND multiplies (MALARIA in *Anopheles*, trypanosomes). MECHANICAL transmission involves no development at all (the housefly carrying cholera or typhoid).**
- **TRANSOVARIAL transmission (agent passed to the vector's offspring) occurs in scrub typhus, KFD and dengue — making the mite or tick a RESERVOIR as well as a vector.**
- **AIRBORNE precautions apply to the "MY CHICKEN HAS TB" group — Measles, Chickenpox, disseminated Herpes zoster, Tuberculosis — while droplet precautions cover diphtheria, mumps, pertussis, influenza and meningococcus.**
- **Basic VENTILATION and avoidance of OVERCROWDING reduce both droplet and droplet-nuclei transmission — the reason housing standards are a public-health measure.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not a direct transmission" → **droplet nuclei**; "no vertical transmission" → **tetanus**; filaria in the mosquito → **cyclo-developmental**.

**REMEMBER:** *Droplets fall within a metre and are direct; nuclei float and are airborne. Filaria develops without multiplying; malaria does both.*

## 22. Secondary Attack Rate

**PEARL:** **SECONDARY ATTACK RATE = (number of EXPOSED SUSCEPTIBLE persons developing the disease within one incubation period ÷ total number of SUSCEPTIBLE close/exposed contacts) × 100** — with the **PRIMARY CASE EXCLUDED from both numerator and denominator, and the VACCINATED/immune excluded from the denominator.** It measures **COMMUNICABILITY (infectivity).** Worked example: 100 children, 60% vaccinated, one primary case, 26 develop measles → susceptible contacts = 100 − 60 − 1 = **39**, so SAR = 26/39 = **66%.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The denominator is the whole point of the calculation: **only SUSCEPTIBLE contacts are truly "at risk", so including the immune would dilute the rate and understate the organism's infectivity.** The same logic excludes the primary case, who was infected from elsewhere. This is why SAR is the standard measure of infectivity and why it is used to assess **vaccine efficacy in household studies.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **VACCINE EFFICACY = (attack rate in unvaccinated − attack rate in vaccinated) / attack rate in unvaccinated × 100 — structurally identical to the attributable-risk formula.**
- **ATTACK RATE is an incidence rate used for a narrow time period during an epidemic (e.g. a food-poisoning outbreak), usually expressed as a percentage; the FOOD-SPECIFIC ATTACK RATE identifies the offending item by comparing attack rates among those who did and did not eat each dish.**
- **The BASIC REPRODUCTION NUMBER (R0) is the average number of secondary cases generated by one case in a fully susceptible population; the epidemic grows if R > 1 and dies out if R < 1.**
- **HERD IMMUNITY THRESHOLD = 1 − 1/R0 — so measles (R0 12–18) requires ~92–95% coverage while polio (R0 5–7) requires ~80–85%.**
- **In a household or institutional outbreak, a HIGH SAR indicates high communicability and points to person-to-person spread; a LOW SAR with many simultaneous cases points to a common source.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Denominator for SAR → **all susceptible close/exposed contacts**; the arithmetic requires subtracting the vaccinated AND the primary case.

**REMEMBER:** *Only the susceptible are at risk — subtract the immune and the index case before you divide.*

## 23. Herd Immunity

**PEARL:** **HERD IMMUNITY is the resistance of a GROUP to the spread of an infectious agent, based on the immunity of a high proportion of its members — it protects even the UNIMMUNISED. IT CAN NEVER REACH 100%, and it does not need to: interrupting transmission requires only that coverage exceed the HERD IMMUNITY THRESHOLD (1 − 1/R0).** The threshold varies with the **VIRULENCE/transmissibility of the agent, the EFFICACY of the vaccine, and the CONTACT PARAMETER of the population — but NOT with the NUMBER OF VACCINES available for the disease.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Herd immunity works by **reducing the number of susceptible contacts each infectious person meets**, so that the effective reproduction number falls below 1 and chains of transmission break. That mechanism explains its two great limitations: **it does NOT operate for diseases that are not transmitted person-to-person — TETANUS is the classic example, where an unvaccinated individual gains no protection from everyone else's immunity — and it is UNSTABLE, because births, deaths and migration constantly replenish the susceptible pool.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Herd immunity is also influenced by the presence and distribution of ALTERNATIVE (animal) HOSTS and by ENVIRONMENTAL reservoirs — which is why diseases with animal reservoirs (rabies, Japanese encephalitis) cannot be eradicated by human immunisation alone.**
- **It is diminished by "EPIDEMIOLOGICAL SHIFT" — the accumulation of susceptibles (new births, waning immunity, vaccine refusal) — which is why measles outbreaks recur in pockets of low coverage even in highly vaccinated countries.**
- **Herd immunity may be acquired by NATURAL INFECTION or by IMMUNISATION; only immunisation achieves it without the disease's morbidity.**
- **Vaccine classification worth carrying: LIVE ATTENUATED (BCG, OPV, measles/MMR, varicella, yellow fever, rotavirus, typhoid Ty21a) — strong, long-lasting immunity, usually a single dose, but contraindicated in pregnancy and immunosuppression. KILLED/INACTIVATED (IPV, rabies, hepatitis A, whole-cell pertussis, cholera) — safer but needing boosters. TOXOIDS (diphtheria, tetanus). SUBUNIT (HEPATITIS B, using HBsAg; HPV, using the L1 capsid protein) — produced by recombinant DNA technology. POLYSACCHARIDE (pneumococcal PPSV23, meningococcal, typhoid Vi) and CONJUGATE vaccines.**
- ***THE MAIN DRAWBACK OF PLAIN POLYSACCHARIDE VACCINES IS THAT THE RESPONSE IS SEROTYPE-SPECIFIC, T-CELL INDEPENDENT, POOR UNDER 2 YEARS OF AGE, AND GIVES NO MEMORY OR HERD PROTECTION*** — conjugating the polysaccharide to a protein carrier converts it into a T-cell-dependent response that works in infants, produces memory and reduces nasopharyngeal carriage (hence herd immunity), which is exactly why Hib, pneumococcal and meningococcal CONJUGATE vaccines transformed those diseases.
- **DPT contains two toxoids plus a killed component; BCG is live; IPV is killed — a common matching question.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False about herd immunity" → **100% herd immunity is possible**; drawback of polysaccharide vaccines → **serotype-specific immunity**; subunit vaccine → **hepatitis B**.

**REMEMBER:** *Herd immunity breaks chains of transmission — so it does nothing for tetanus, which has no chain.*

## 24. BCG Vaccine and Its Adverse Effects

**PEARL:** After BCG, a **papule appears at 2–3 WEEKS, ULCERATES (the COMMONEST adverse effect), and heals with a SCAR BY ABOUT 6–12 WEEKS (within 3 months).** Other reactions: **REGIONAL (axillary) LYMPHADENITIS, SUPPURATIVE lymphadenitis with sinus formation, cold abscess (from a too-deep injection), and rarely BCG OSTEOMYELITIS.** In the **IMMUNOCOMPROMISED it can cause DISSEMINATED BCG-osis.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The ulcer is not a complication but the **expected local delayed-hypersensitivity response to a live attenuated organism** — its appearance is evidence that the vaccine "took". This is why parents must be counselled in advance and why **the ulcer must NOT be treated with antibiotics, dressings or incision** — interfering with it delays healing and adds infection. Conversely, a **cold abscess indicates faulty (subcutaneous rather than INTRADERMAL) technique.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **BCG is a LIVE ATTENUATED *M. BOVIS* vaccine given INTRADERMALLY over the LEFT DELTOID (the site is standardised for verification of scarring), 0.1 mL (0.05 mL under 1 month), AT BIRTH in India — and can be given up to 1 year of age.**
- **It protects mainly against DISSEMINATED TB AND TUBERCULOUS MENINGITIS IN CHILDREN, with variable (0–80%) protection against adult pulmonary tuberculosis. ABSENCE OF A SCAR does not reliably indicate failure and is NOT an automatic indication to revaccinate.**
- **CONTRAINDICATIONS: symptomatic HIV and other immunodeficiency (risk of disseminated disease), generalised skin disease, and pregnancy. It is safe in asymptomatic HIV-exposed infants per current national policy.**
- **BCG is also used INTRAVESICALLY for superficial bladder carcinoma and (as *M. indicus pranii*) as an immunomodulator.**
- **The immunisation schedule in India (UIP): AT BIRTH — BCG, OPV-0, hepatitis B birth dose. 6, 10, 14 WEEKS — OPV, PENTAVALENT (DPT + hepatitis B + Hib), ROTAVIRUS, fIPV (at 6 and 14 weeks), PCV (6, 14 weeks and 9 months). 9–12 MONTHS — MR-1, JE-1, vitamin A. 16–24 MONTHS — MR-2, DPT booster, OPV booster, JE-2. 5–6 YEARS — DPT booster. 10 and 16 YEARS — Td. PREGNANCY — Td 2 doses/booster.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Most likely side effect after BCG" → **ulceration**.

**REMEMBER:** *The ulcer is the vaccine working — leave it alone and let it scar.*

## 25. Adverse Events Following Immunisation

**PEARL:** **MMR can cause THROMBOCYTOPENIA** (usually 2–3 weeks after vaccination, transient and self-limiting), along with fever, rash and transient arthralgia. **TETANUS TOXOID is the classic cause of BRACHIAL NEURITIS** — severe deep aching pain in the shoulder and arm followed by weakness and wasting, treated symptomatically. **IPV may contain trace STREPTOMYCIN, NEOMYCIN and POLYMYXIN B; MMR contains NEOMYCIN** — antibiotics added during manufacture to prevent bacterial contamination of the tissue culture.

**UNDERSTAND:** The trace antibiotics matter only because they are the **basis of a genuine contraindication**: a person with documented ANAPHYLAXIS to neomycin should not receive MMR or IPV. Similarly, **egg-based vaccines (influenza, yellow fever) matter for severe egg allergy**, and **yeast-derived hepatitis B for yeast allergy.** Knowing WHY the excipient is there converts a list into a decision rule — and prevents the far commoner error of withholding vaccines for trivial reasons.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **AEFI classification: VACCINE PRODUCT-RELATED, vaccine QUALITY DEFECT-related, IMMUNISATION ERROR-related (the commonest and the only fully PREVENTABLE category — wrong dose, wrong route, contaminated multidose vial), IMMUNISATION ANXIETY-related (syncope, hyperventilation), and COINCIDENTAL. All serious AEFIs must be REPORTED AND INVESTIGATED.**
- **Vaccine-specific associations worth carrying: OPV — VACCINE-ASSOCIATED PARALYTIC POLIOMYELITIS (VAPP) and vaccine-derived poliovirus (the reason for the switch to IPV/fIPV). WHOLE-CELL PERTUSSIS — persistent screaming, high fever, hypotonic–hyporesponsive episodes and febrile seizures (fewer with acellular vaccine). MEASLES — fever and rash at 5–12 days. ROTAVIRUS — a small risk of INTUSSUSCEPTION. INFLUENZA — Guillain–Barré syndrome (very rare). Any vaccine — ANAPHYLAXIS, for which ADRENALINE and 30 minutes of observation must be available at every session.**
- **TRUE CONTRAINDICATIONS are few: anaphylaxis to a previous dose or a component; LIVE vaccines in PREGNANCY and severe immunosuppression; and postponement in moderate-to-severe acute illness. ***MINOR ILLNESS, LOW-GRADE FEVER, DIARRHOEA, MALNUTRITION, ANTIBIOTIC USE, PREMATURITY AND BREASTFEEDING ARE NOT CONTRAINDICATIONS*** — treating them as such is the commonest cause of missed opportunities for immunisation.**
- **Simultaneous administration of multiple vaccines at different sites is safe and recommended; if two LIVE injectable vaccines are not given on the same day, they must be separated by at least 4 WEEKS.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Thrombocytopenia after a vaccine → **MMR**; brachial neuritis → **tetanus toxoid**; streptomycin/neomycin traces → **IPV**.

**REMEMBER:** *Look up why an excipient is there before you withhold a vaccine — most "contraindications" are missed opportunities.*

## 26. Passive Immunisation with Immunoglobulin

**PEARL:** **NORMAL HUMAN IMMUNOGLOBULIN (NHIG) is pooled from AT LEAST 1000 DONORS, must contain ≥90% intact IgG with MINIMAL AGGREGATES and a LOW IgA concentration, and is used chiefly against MEASLES and HEPATITIS A.** ***LIVE VACCINES MUST NOT BE GIVEN WITH IT*** — wait **AT LEAST 12 WEEKS (3 months) AFTER immunoglobulin before giving a live vaccine, and 2 WEEKS after a live vaccine before giving immunoglobulin.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The interval exists because **passively transferred antibody NEUTRALISES the live vaccine virus before it can replicate**, and replication is exactly what a live vaccine needs in order to immunise — so giving them together simply wastes the vaccine. **The low IgA requirement is equally mechanistic**: patients with SELECTIVE IgA DEFICIENCY may have anti-IgA antibodies and suffer ANAPHYLAXIS if given IgA-containing preparations.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **PASSIVE immunity is IMMEDIATE but SHORT-LIVED (weeks to a few months) and confers NO memory; ACTIVE immunity is slower to develop but long-lasting with memory. COMBINED (passive–active) immunisation is used when protection is needed at once AND for the future — TETANUS (TIG + toxoid), RABIES (RIG + vaccine), HEPATITIS B (HBIG + vaccine at birth) — and the two must be given at SEPARATE SITES WITH SEPARATE SYRINGES so the immunoglobulin does not neutralise the vaccine.**
- **SPECIFIC (hyperimmune) immunoglobulins come from recently recovered or hyperimmunised donors and give higher titres against one organism: TIG, HBIG, RIG, VZIG, and anti-D.**
- **NATURAL PASSIVE immunity is the transplacental transfer of maternal IgG (protecting the infant for ~6 months and the reason measles vaccine is deferred to 9 months) and secretory IgA in breast milk.**
- **The exception to the interval rule: OPV, yellow fever vaccine and live typhoid Ty21a are NOT significantly inhibited by immunoglobulin and may be given at any time; ANTI-D does not interfere with MMR given postpartum in practice, though a check of rubella seroconversion is advised.**
- **Immunoglobulin also has therapeutic uses (Kawasaki disease, ITP, Guillain–Barré syndrome, primary immunodeficiency) at much higher doses.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False about NHIG" → **all live vaccines can be given simultaneously with it**.

**REMEMBER:** *Passive antibody kills a live vaccine before it can work — twelve weeks after, two weeks before.*

## 27. The Cold Chain

**PEARL:** **OPV (SABIN) IS THE MOST HEAT-SENSITIVE VACCINE; HEPATITIS B is among the LEAST heat-sensitive** (with meningococcal A, freeze-dried Hib and pneumococcal vaccines). Vaccines are stored at **+2 to +8 °C at the health facility**; the **FREEZER compartment of a solar refrigerator (battery drive) is kept at −7 °C, mainly for ICE PACKS**, while the **vaccine compartment stays at +2 to +8 °C.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Heat sensitivity follows the vaccine's nature: **LIVE viral vaccines (OPV, measles) contain fragile virus that loses potency with warmth, while TOXOIDS AND ADSORBED, ALUMINIUM-CONTAINING vaccines (DPT, TT, hepatitis B) tolerate heat but are DESTROYED BY FREEZING**, because freezing disrupts the antigen–adjuvant bond and causes irreversible loss of potency. Hence the crucial asymmetry: **the fridge protects some vaccines from heat and threatens others with cold.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Order of heat sensitivity (most to least): OPV > measles/MR > BCG (reconstituted) > DPT/hepatitis B/TT. FREEZE-SENSITIVE VACCINES THAT MUST NEVER BE FROZEN: HEPATITIS B, DPT, TT, Td, IPV, PCV, rotavirus and — importantly — ALL DILUENTS. The SHAKE TEST detects a previously frozen adsorbed vaccine (sedimentation within 30 minutes indicates damage — discard).**
- **In an ILR (ice-lined refrigerator), place OPV and measles in the COLDEST part (basket at the bottom near the freezing coils) and DPT/TT/hepatitis B/diluents AWAY from the freezing compartment. Never store vaccines in the door.**
- **Cold chain levels in India: the WALK-IN COOLER/FREEZER at the regional store, DEEP FREEZER (−15 to −25 °C, for OPV and measles bulk storage) and ILR (+2 to +8 °C) at district and PHC level, VACCINE CARRIERS with 4 ice packs for the sub-centre session (holding cold for ~12 hours), and DAY CARRIERS for short trips.**
- **The VACCINE VIAL MONITOR (VVM) is a heat-sensitive square on the label: USE the vial while the inner square is LIGHTER than the outer ring, and DISCARD once it is the same colour or darker. The OPEN VIAL POLICY allows opened multi-dose vials of OPV, hepatitis B, Td, IPV and pentavalent to be used for up to 4 WEEKS if unexpired, VVM-valid, aseptically withdrawn and kept at 2–8 °C — but NOT for reconstituted BCG and measles/MR, which must be DISCARDED WITHIN 4 HOURS.**
- **SOLAR DIRECT-DRIVE refrigerators use the sun's energy to freeze phase-change material and require no batteries — the modern solution for off-grid facilities.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Most/least heat-sensitive → **OPV / hepatitis B**; solar refrigerator freezer temperature → **−7 °C**.

**REMEMBER:** *Live vaccines die of heat and adsorbed vaccines die of cold — and the diluent must never be frozen.*

## 28. RDA vs EAR

**PEARL:** **RDA = EAR + 2 SD.** The **Estimated Average Requirement (EAR)** meets the needs of **50%** of a healthy population; the **Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)** is set two standard deviations above it, so it meets the needs of **97–98%**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Nutrient requirements are **normally distributed** across a population. Setting the recommendation at the *average* would leave half the population deficient — so a **safety margin of 2 SD** is added, exactly as with any reference range.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **RDA is for INDIVIDUALS; EAR is for assessing and planning POPULATION intakes.** Using the RDA to judge a population's adequacy over-estimates deficiency — a classic nutrition-epidemiology error.
- The other reference values: **AI (Adequate Intake)** — used when there is insufficient evidence to set an EAR/RDA; **UL (Tolerable Upper Intake Level)** — above which toxicity risk rises; **EER (Estimated Energy Requirement)**.
- **Energy is the exception — energy requirement is expressed as the EAR (average), NOT as RDA + 2 SD**, because over-recommending energy causes obesity, whereas over-recommending a vitamin is harmless.
- In India these are issued by the **ICMR–NIN** as **RDA/Nutrient Requirements for Indians**, using the **reference Indian man and woman** and the **consumption unit (CU)** system for family requirements.
- **Reference Indian adult man:** 60 kg, 18–29 years, sedentary/moderate work — the standard against which requirements are scaled.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "RDA is represented by" → **EAR + 2 SD**.

**REMEMBER:** *EAR covers half the people; add 2 SD and you cover nearly everyone — that is the RDA.*

## 29. Constitutional Articles Protecting Children

**PEARL:** **Article 21A — RIGHT TO EDUCATION** (free and compulsory, 6–14 years). **Article 24 — PROHIBITION OF CHILD LABOUR** (no child under 14 in a factory, mine or hazardous employment). **Article 39(e) and 39(f) — the State shall ensure children are not abused and are given opportunities to develop healthily with dignity.** **Article 42 is NOT a child article — it concerns just and humane conditions of WORK and MATERNITY RELIEF.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction the question tests is **whose right is being protected**. Articles 21A, 24 and 39(e)/(f) are all framed around **the child**; **Article 42 is framed around the WORKER (and specifically the working mother)** — maternity benefit is a labour right, not a paediatric one.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Article 21A was inserted by the 86th Constitutional Amendment (2002)** and is implemented through the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009**.
- **Article 47 — the State's duty to raise the level of NUTRITION and the standard of living and to improve PUBLIC HEALTH** — is the constitutional basis most often quoted for health programmes, and includes **prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs**.
- **Article 21 — Right to Life** — has been judicially expanded to include the **right to health and emergency medical care** (Parmanand Katara case: a doctor must treat a medico-legal emergency first and complete formalities later).
- **Key child-protection legislation to pair with these:** **POCSO Act 2012** (sexual offences), **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015**, **Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016** (bans employment under 14 entirely, and adolescents 14–18 in hazardous work), **PCPNDT Act 1994** (sex determination), **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006**, and the **RTE Act 2009**.

## 30. Transmission Assessment Survey — Lymphatic Filariasis

**PEARL:** The **TRANSMISSION ASSESSMENT SURVEY (TAS) belongs to the LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS elimination programme.** It is the survey used to decide **WHEN MASS DRUG ADMINISTRATION (MDA) CAN BE STOPPED** — i.e. whether infection has fallen below the threshold at which mosquitoes can sustain transmission — and afterwards it serves as the **post-MDA SURVEILLANCE tool**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Filariasis elimination does not require eradicating every worm — it requires pushing prevalence **below the threshold at which a mosquito is unlikely to pick up and pass on the parasite**, which then lets transmission collapse on its own. TAS exists because that threshold must be **measured, not assumed**: it is surveyed in **6–7 year-old CHILDREN**, since they were born after MDA began, so any infection in them proves **ongoing transmission** rather than old infection.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **TAS is done after AT LEAST 5 EFFECTIVE ANNUAL ROUNDS of MDA with ≥65% coverage, and requires two further passes at 2–3 year intervals before the district can be declared free.**
- **MDA regimens: DEC + ALBENDAZOLE in India; IVERMECTIN + albendazole where ONCHOCERCIASIS coexists (DEC is CONTRAINDICATED there — it causes the severe MAZZOTTI reaction and can cause blindness); the newer IDA triple therapy is IVERMECTIN + DEC + ALBENDAZOLE.**
- **India's programme is the NATIONAL VECTOR BORNE DISEASE CONTROL PROGRAMME — annual single-dose MDA to the entire at-risk population EXCEPT children under 2, pregnant women and the seriously ill, plus MORBIDITY MANAGEMENT AND DISABILITY PREVENTION (limb hygiene, hydrocelectomy).**
- **Diagnosis: NIGHT BLOOD SMEAR between 10 pm and 2 am (nocturnal periodicity, matching the biting habit of the *CULEX quinquefasciatus* vector), or the daytime FILARIAL ANTIGEN CARD TEST (ICT) — which is what TAS actually uses.**
- **Elimination terminology is examinable: CONTROL (reducing incidence to an acceptable level) · ELIMINATION (reducing incidence to zero in a defined geographical area, with continued measures needed) · ERADICATION (permanent worldwide reduction to zero, with intervention no longer required — achieved only for SMALLPOX, and RINDERPEST in animals) · EXTINCTION (the agent no longer exists even in the laboratory).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Transmission assessment survey is carried out for" → **filariasis**.

**REMEMBER:** *TAS asks one question — can we stop the mass drug administration yet?*

## 31. Oral Cholera Vaccines

**PEARL:** **DUKORAL is a KILLED WHOLE-CELL *VIBRIO CHOLERAE* O1 vaccine combined with the recombinant B SUBUNIT of cholera toxin (WC-rBS), given ORALLY to induce LOCAL INTESTINAL (mucosal, secretory IgA) IMMUNITY** — blocking both bacterial colonisation and toxin binding. It **does NOT cover O139.** **SHANCHOL / EUVICHOL are bivalent killed whole-cell O1 + O139 vaccines WITHOUT the B subunit** — cheaper, needing no buffer, and the vaccines used in WHO's stockpile for outbreaks and endemic areas.

**UNDERSTAND:** Cholera is a **purely luminal, non-invasive infection** — the organism never enters the bloodstream, so **circulating IgG is largely irrelevant and protection must be MUCOSAL.** That is why the vaccine is given by mouth rather than by injection, and why the **B subunit is included in Dukoral**: it is the toxin's binding moiety (harmless alone), so antibody against it prevents the toxin docking on the GM1 ganglioside. The B subunit also **cross-protects briefly against enterotoxigenic *E. coli* LT** — the reason Dukoral is marketed to travellers for traveller's diarrhoea.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Dukoral must be taken with a BUFFER (bicarbonate) so gastric acid does not destroy the B subunit — 2 doses 1–6 weeks apart (3 doses in children 2–5 years), giving ~2 years of protection (6 months in young children). Shanchol needs no buffer: 2 doses 14 days apart from age 1, protection ~3 years.**
- ***VACCINATION NEVER REPLACES THE CORE CONTROL MEASURES*** — safe water, sanitation, hygiene and prompt ORAL REHYDRATION. Case-fatality with adequate rehydration is under 1%, and untreated over 50%.
- **Treatment of cholera: rapid rehydration (Ringer lactate for severe cases; ORS otherwise) plus a single dose of DOXYCYCLINE (or azithromycin in children and pregnancy), which shortens the illness and the duration of shedding.**
- **The injectable killed whole-cell parenteral cholera vaccine of the past is OBSOLETE — poor efficacy, short protection, painful, and no herd effect. ***NO COUNTRY MAY REQUIRE CHOLERA VACCINATION AS A CONDITION OF ENTRY UNDER THE INTERNATIONAL HEALTH REGULATIONS.***
- **Cholera is a NOTIFIABLE disease internationally; *V. cholerae* O1 (biotypes classical and EL TOR; serotypes Ogawa, Inaba, Hikojima) and O139 are the epidemic strains, and El Tor is the biotype of the ongoing seventh pandemic.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Dukoral → **killed *V. cholerae* O1 with the B subunit, inducing intestinal immunity**.

**REMEMBER:** *A gut infection needs a gut vaccine — killed bugs plus the harmless half of the toxin.*

## 32. BCG Vaccine

**PEARL:** BCG is a **live attenuated bacterial vaccine** given **intradermally over the
left deltoid insertion**: **0.05 mL for neonates (<4 weeks), 0.1 mL thereafter**. The
**freeze-dried (lyophilised) preparation is MORE stable** than the liquid one — the
statement that it is less stable is the classic false option.

**UNDERSTAND:** Freeze-drying removes the water that bacteria and enzymes need for
degradation, so the vaccine survives transport and storage far better — which is why
essentially all BCG supplied today is lyophilised, reconstituted at the point of use.
The same logic explains the 3-hour discard rule: **once reconstituted, the live bacilli
begin to die and contamination risk begins**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **WHO-recommended strain: Danish 1331**; reconstitute with **normal saline** (distilled water is irritant); **discard the leftover within 3–4 hours**; store at **2–8 °C, protected from light**.
- Timeline after vaccination: **papule at 2–3 weeks → shallow ulcer at 5–6 weeks → scar by 6–12 weeks**; a **normal individual becomes Mantoux-positive at ~8 weeks**. **Do not treat the ulcer** or apply dressings.
- Complications: **BCG adenitis** (commonest, usually self-limiting; needle aspiration if suppurative — antitubercular drugs are not routinely indicated), **BCG-itis/disseminated BCG in immunodeficiency (SCID, HIV with immunosuppression)** — the only true contraindications, plus **keloid** at the site.
- **BCG is the only widely used live BACTERIAL vaccine**; it is given at **birth** in India (with **OPV-0 and hepatitis B birth dose**), and can be given **up to 1 year of age**.
- Protection is best against **severe childhood forms — miliary TB and TB meningitis**; protection against adult pulmonary TB is highly variable (0–80%).
- BCG is also used **intravesically for superficial bladder carcinoma (non-muscle-invasive, high-grade/CIS)**.
- **Live vaccines: BCG, OPV, measles/MMR, varicella, yellow fever, rotavirus, typhoid oral, intranasal influenza, JE live.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Asked as "identify the incorrect statement" — the false one is
usually about the **freeze-dried preparation's stability** or a **swapped dose (0.05 vs
0.1 mL)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Freeze-dried is tougher; 0.05 for the newborn, 0.1 for everyone else,
Mantoux-positive at 8 weeks.*

## 33. HPV Vaccination Schedules (WHO-SAGE)

**PEARL:** **Immunocompromised individuals (including HIV-positive) need 2–3 doses** of
HPV vaccine, regardless of age — the single-dose and two-dose simplifications apply only
to **immunocompetent** people.

**UNDERSTAND:** The reduced schedules rest on the observation that adolescent immune
systems generate exceptionally robust, durable antibody responses to virus-like
particles. That premise fails in immunodeficiency, where seroconversion is lower and
antibody wanes faster — so the full multi-dose priming is retained precisely in the group
that also has the highest risk of persistent HPV infection and cervical cancer.

**MUST KNOW (WHO 2022 position):**
- **Girls 9–14 years (primary target): 1 or 2 doses**; **15–20 years: 1 or 2 doses**; **>21 years: 2 doses, 6 months apart**.
- **Immunocompromised, including HIV: at least 2 doses, and where possible 3.**
- Vaccines: **bivalent (16, 18)**, **quadrivalent (6, 11, 16, 18 — also prevents genital warts)**, **nonavalent (adds 31, 33, 45, 52, 58)**; India has the indigenous quadrivalent **CERVAVAC**.
- **16 and 18 cause ~70% of cervical cancers**; **6 and 11 cause ~90% of genital warts** and juvenile recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.
- Best given **before sexual debut**; vaccination does **not** replace screening — the WHO **90–70–90 by 2030** targets are 90% of girls vaccinated by 15, 70% of women screened twice with a high-performance test (**HPV DNA**) at 35 and 45, and 90% of those with disease treated.
- Screening in India (national programme): **VIA (visual inspection with acetic acid) every 5 years for women 30–65** where HPV testing is unavailable.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The question turns on the immune status stated in the stem. Normal
adolescent → 1–2 doses; **immunocompromised → 2–3 doses**.

**REMEMBER:** *Fewer doses only for the young and immunocompetent.*

## 34. Quantitative Techniques in Health Programme Management

**PEARL:** The **quantitative** management techniques are **cost–benefit analysis,
cost-effectiveness analysis, network analysis (PERT/CPM), systems analysis, model/input–
output analysis and planning-programming-budgeting systems**. **Management Information
Systems are a BEHAVIOURAL-science-based tool, not a quantitative technique.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The division mirrors the two things a manager must do: **decide** (which
requires numbers — comparing costs, outcomes and schedules) and **organise people**
(which requires behavioural tools — communication, information flow, motivation, decision
making). An information system moves information between people; it does not itself
compute the optimal choice, so it sits on the behavioural side of the classification.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Cost–benefit analysis:** both costs and outcomes expressed in **money** — tells you whether a programme is worth doing at all.
- **Cost-effectiveness analysis:** cost per **natural unit of outcome** (per death averted, per case detected) — compares programmes with the **same** objective. **Cost–utility** uses **QALYs/DALYs**.
- **Network analysis — PERT (Programme Evaluation and Review Technique)** and **CPM (Critical Path Method)**: sequence activities, identify the **critical path**, the longest chain that determines total project duration.
- **Work sampling, input–output analysis, systems analysis, planning-programming-budgeting system (PPBS)** are the other quantitative tools.
- **Behavioural-science-based:** management/health information systems, decision making, communication, organisational behaviour, leadership.
- Related evaluation vocabulary: **structure–process–outcome (Donabedian)** for quality of care; **efficiency vs effectiveness vs efficacy** — efficacy is under ideal conditions (trial), effectiveness in the field, efficiency is output per unit resource.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Which is NOT a quantitative technique" — the answer is the one about
**information/communication/behaviour**.

**REMEMBER:** *If it produces a number to compare options, it's quantitative; if it moves
information between people, it's behavioural.*

## 35. Internal vs External Validity

**PEARL:** **External validity = GENERALISABILITY** — can the result be applied to
populations beyond the study sample? **Internal validity** = is the result true **within**
the study (free of bias, confounding and chance)?

**UNDERSTAND:** The two are ordered, not parallel: **internal validity is a prerequisite
for external validity**. A study that is biased internally cannot be generalised, because
there is nothing true to generalise. And the measures that improve internal validity —
strict inclusion criteria, highly selected patients, tightly controlled settings — are
exactly the ones that **reduce** external validity. That trade-off is the reason explanatory
(efficacy) trials and pragmatic (effectiveness) trials both exist.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Threats to internal validity:** selection bias, information/measurement bias, recall bias, confounding, loss to follow-up, chance. Controlled by **randomisation, blinding, matching, restriction, stratification and multivariable adjustment**.
- **Threats to external validity:** unrepresentative sample, volunteer effect, atypical setting or intensity of intervention, healthy-worker effect.
- **Reliability (repeatability/precision) ≠ validity (accuracy)** — a test can be consistently wrong. Reliability is measured by **kappa** (agreement beyond chance) and the correlation coefficient; validity by **sensitivity and specificity against a gold standard**.
- **Replicability** is about reproducing the study; **objectivity** is freedom from observer influence; **causality** is judged by **Bradford Hill criteria** (temporality is the only essential one; also strength, dose–response, consistency, biological plausibility, specificity, reversibility, coherence, analogy).
- A **randomised controlled trial has the highest internal validity**; a well-conducted **community-based cohort** often has better external validity.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Straight definition-matching. **External = generalisation** is the
single fact.

**REMEMBER:** *Internal = true here. External = true elsewhere.*

## 36. Cancer Screening Methods

**PEARL:** **Per-rectal examination is NOT a screening test for colorectal cancer** (it
reaches only the distal rectum), and **oral cancer screening requires INSPECTION AND
PALPATION of the whole oral cavity, not just external visual inspection.** **Pap smear
for cervical cancer** and **mammography for breast cancer** are valid screening tests.

**UNDERSTAND:** A screening test must reach the site where the disease actually arises
and must detect it before symptoms. A finger reaches ~8–10 cm of a 150 cm colon, so most
tumours are anatomically out of range — that is why the world moved to **faecal occult
blood/FIT and colonoscopy**. Similarly, many oral premalignant lesions sit on the
**floor of the mouth, ventral tongue and buccal sulci** and are **palpable before they are
visible** — external inspection misses them.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Colorectal:** **FIT/gFOBT annually** or **colonoscopy every 10 years** (or flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5), from **45–50 years** in average-risk adults.
- **Breast:** **mammography** biennially from 40–50 to 74. Note the classic epidemiological trap in the stem — introducing screening **increases measured PREVALENCE** (it detects existing subclinical disease sooner) without changing true incidence; this is **lead-time bias** and **length bias** territory, not evidence of harm. Clinical breast examination is the pragmatic Indian national programme tool.
- **Cervical:** **HPV DNA testing (preferred), Pap/cytology, or VIA** — the Indian national programme uses **VIA every 5 years for women 30–65**.
- **Oral:** **systematic visual inspection AND palpation of the oral cavity and neck nodes** by a trained provider — the only cancer screening with a demonstrated mortality benefit in a randomised Indian trial (Kerala/TMC), particularly in tobacco and alcohol users.
- Screening criteria (**Wilson–Jungner**): important health problem, recognisable latent stage, suitable and acceptable test, effective treatment available, facilities for diagnosis and treatment, agreed policy on whom to treat, cost-effective, continuous process.
- Biases inflating screening's apparent benefit: **lead-time bias, length bias, overdiagnosis, healthy volunteer (selection) bias** — which is why **mortality**, not survival, is the valid outcome.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Multiple-response "which statements are FALSE" — the false ones are
the anatomically inadequate methods (**PR exam, external-only oral inspection**).

**REMEMBER:** *The finger doesn't reach the colon and the eye doesn't reach under the
tongue.*

## 37. Tetanus Prophylaxis in Wound Management

**PEARL:** For a **clean minor wound** in someone who completed a primary course but whose
**last dose was >10 years ago**, give **a single booster dose of tetanus toxoid — no
immunoglobulin.** TIG is reserved for **tetanus-prone wounds in the incompletely
immunised**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Two independent questions drive every answer: **how tetanus-prone is the
wound?** and **how complete is the immunisation?** Toxoid produces **active** immunity
(takes days, gives durable protection); TIG provides **immediate passive** antitoxin for
the current wound. A fully primed person mounts a rapid **anamnestic response** to a
booster, so passive cover is unnecessary — hence no TIG despite the long interval.

**MUST KNOW:**

| Immunisation status | **Clean minor wound** | **All other (tetanus-prone) wounds** |
|---|---|---|
| **≥3 doses, last <5 yrs** | Nothing | Nothing |
| **≥3 doses, last 5–10 yrs** | Nothing | **Td booster** |
| **≥3 doses, last >10 yrs** | **Td booster** | **Td booster** |
| **<3 doses / unknown** | **Td (complete the course)** | **Td + TIG (250–500 IU IM at a separate site)** |

- **Tetanus-prone wounds:** >6 hours old, deep (>1 cm), contaminated with soil/saliva/faeces, **puncture, crush, burn, frostbite, avulsion, devitalised tissue, missile injury**, sepsis. A **clean-cut wound presenting within 4 hours** is a **clean minor wound**.
- **Wound toilet and debridement are the most important single measure** — *C. tetani* is a strict anaerobe that needs devitalised tissue.
- **Human TIG is preferred to equine ATS** (no serum sickness/anaphylaxis); give at a **different site with a different syringe** from the toxoid.
- Tetanus itself confers **no immunity** — survivors must still be fully immunised.
- **Neonatal tetanus prevention:** maternal **2 doses of TT/Td**, **clean delivery practices (5 cleans)**, clean cord care.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives the wound type, the time since injury and the years
since the last dose — plug all three into the grid. "Clean wound + primed + >10 years" =
**single booster only**.

**REMEMBER:** *Toxoid for the future, immunoglobulin only when the past is incomplete.*

## 38. Tests of Pasteurisation and Milk Quality

**PEARL:** The tests used on pasteurised milk are the **PHOSPHATASE test** (checks that
pasteurisation was adequate), the **standard plate count** (total bacterial load) and the
**methylene blue reduction test** (bacterial activity). The **iodine test is for starch
adulteration**, not pasteurisation.

**UNDERSTAND:** The phosphatase test works because **alkaline phosphatase in raw milk is
destroyed at a temperature just above that required to kill *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and
*Coxiella burnetii***. So if phosphatase is absent, the milk must have reached a
pathogen-killing temperature — a neat biological thermometer that needs no equipment. A
**positive phosphatase test therefore means under-pasteurisation or contamination with raw
milk.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Pasteurisation methods:** **Holder/LTLT — 63–66 °C for 30 minutes**; **HTST (flash) — 72 °C for 15 seconds** (the commonest commercial method); **UHT — 125–150 °C for a few seconds** (sterilises, long shelf life).
- **Methylene blue reduction test:** bacteria consume oxygen and decolourise the dye — the **faster the decolourisation, the worse the milk**. Grade: >4½ hours = excellent, <30 minutes = very poor.
- **Standard plate count** — total viable bacteria; **coliform count** indicates faecal contamination or post-pasteurisation contamination.
- **Adulteration tests:** **iodine → starch**; **Hansa test → adulteration with another species' milk**; **freezing point/lactometer (specific gravity) → added water**; **Rose–Gottlieb / Gerber method → fat content**.
- **Milk-borne diseases:** **from the animal — bovine tuberculosis, brucellosis, Q fever, streptococcal and staphylococcal infection**; **from human handlers/contaminated water — typhoid, paratyphoid, cholera, dysentery, diphtheria, viral hepatitis, campylobacter**.
- Pasteurisation kills the vegetative pathogens but **does not sterilise** — spores survive, hence refrigeration is still required.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are pasteurisation tests EXCEPT" — the exception is the
**adulteration** test (iodine, or lactometer if offered).

**REMEMBER:** *Phosphatase dies at the same heat as TB — that's why it certifies the milk.*

## 39. Box-and-Whisker Plots and Skew

**PEARL:** In a box plot the **box spans Q1 to Q3 (the interquartile range) with the MEDIAN
as the line inside**, and the whiskers reach the extremes. **A median sitting toward the
LOWER end of the box with a LONGER UPPER whisker = POSITIVE (right) skew.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Skew is named for **where the long tail points**, not where the bulk of the
data sits. If a few very high values stretch the distribution upward, the tail (and the
long whisker) points right/positive, and the median gets pushed **toward the low end** of
the box by the mass of ordinary values below the outliers. Everything about skew follows
from that one asymmetry.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Positive (right) skew: MEAN > MEDIAN > MODE**, long right tail. Typical of **income, hospital length of stay, serum triglycerides, incubation periods**.
- **Negative (left) skew: MEAN < MEDIAN < MODE**, long left tail.
- **Symmetrical/normal: mean = median = mode**, median exactly central in the box.
- **The median is resistant to outliers and skew — which is why the median (and IQR) is the appropriate summary for skewed data**, and the mean (with SD) for normal data.
- Normal distribution: **68.3% within ±1 SD, 95.4% within ±2 SD, 99.7% within ±3 SD**; **95% of observations lie within ±1.96 SD**.
- **Standard deviation** measures the scatter of individual observations; **standard error of the mean (SE = SD/√n)** measures the precision of the estimate of the mean and is what generates **confidence intervals**. **Variance = SD²**.
- Skewed data are handled by **log transformation** or **non-parametric tests (Mann–Whitney U, Wilcoxon, Kruskal–Wallis, Spearman)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Read the position of the median line and the length of the whiskers.
**Long whisker on top = positive skew**, regardless of which group has "higher readings".

**REMEMBER:** *The skew follows the tail, and the mean chases the tail.*

## 40. Measures of Central Tendency

**PEARL:** **The MODE is the only measure of central tendency that can be used for
QUALITATIVE (nominal) data.** Mean and median require quantitative data; the **mean is the
only one distorted by extreme values**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Each measure demands a different mathematical property of the data. The
**mode only needs counting** ("which category occurs most often"), so it works for blood
groups or religions where arithmetic is meaningless. The **median needs ordering**, so it
requires at least ordinal data. The **mean needs addition and division**, so it requires
interval/ratio data — and because every value enters the sum, a single outlier drags it,
while the median only cares about **position**, not magnitude.

**MUST KNOW:**

| | Mean | Median | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data type | **Quantitative only** | Quantitative/ordinal | **Any, including QUALITATIVE** |
| Affected by outliers | **Yes** | No | No |
| Uses all observations | **Yes** | No | No |
| Uniqueness | Always one | Always one | **May be none, one or several (bimodal)** |
| Best for | **Symmetrical/normal** data | **SKEWED data** | Nominal data, "commonest" |

- **Mean = ΣX/n; median = the middle value (average of the two middle values if n is even); mode = the most frequent value.**
- In a **normal** distribution **mean = median = mode**; in **positive skew mean > median > mode**; in **negative skew mean < median < mode**.
- Corresponding **measures of dispersion:** **range** (crudest), **interquartile range** (goes with the median), **standard deviation** (goes with the mean), and **coefficient of variation = SD/mean × 100** — the only one that allows comparison between variables with different units.
- **Geometric mean** is used for data spanning orders of magnitude — **antibody titres, parasite counts, incubation periods**; **harmonic mean** for rates.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** True/false statement sets. Anchor on the two facts that resolve every
version: **the mean alone is pulled by outliers**, and **the mode alone handles qualitative
data**.

**REMEMBER:** *Mode counts, median ranks, mean calculates — so only the mean feels the
outlier.*

## 41. IMNCI Classification of Pneumonia

**PEARL:** **Fast breathing cut-offs: ≥60/min under 2 months; ≥50/min from 2–12 months;
≥40/min from 12 months to 5 years.** A **9-month-old with a rate of 48/min and NO chest
indrawing has "cough or cold — NO pneumonia"** → home care, **explain the danger signs**,
no antibiotic.

**UNDERSTAND:** Respiratory rate is used because it is the **single most sensitive
field-usable sign of pneumonia** and needs no equipment. The thresholds fall with age
because **normal respiratory rate falls with age** — an infant's normal 40 would be
alarming in a four-year-old. **Lower chest wall indrawing** marks the transition to
severe disease because it indicates the child has to generate large negative intrathoracic
pressures — a sign of significantly reduced compliance.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Current IMNCI/WHO classification (2 months–5 years):**
  - **No pneumonia (cough or cold):** normal rate, no indrawing → **home care, soothe the throat, explain danger signs, follow up in 3 days if not improving.**
  - **Pneumonia:** **fast breathing OR chest indrawing** → **oral amoxicillin at home** (chest indrawing was moved from "severe" to this group in the revised guidance) with follow-up in 3 days.
  - **Severe pneumonia:** any **general danger sign — stridor in a calm child, inability to drink/breastfeed, persistent vomiting, convulsions, lethargy or unconsciousness, central cyanosis, severe respiratory distress** → **refer urgently after the first dose of injectable antibiotic**.
- **Count the respiratory rate for a full minute, twice, in a calm child** — crying invalidates it.
- **Danger signs to teach every mother:** not feeding, becomes sicker, fast or difficult breathing, blood in stool, drinking poorly, fever.
- **IMNCI in India covers 0–5 years** and integrates assessment of pneumonia, diarrhoea, fever/malaria/measles, ear problems, **malnutrition and anaemia, and immunisation status**; the young-infant module (0–2 months) additionally covers **possible serious bacterial infection, jaundice and feeding problems**.
- **Pneumonia is the leading infectious cause of under-5 deaths**; prevention is by **exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, PCV and Hib vaccination, vitamin A, zinc for diarrhoea, and reducing indoor air pollution**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives the age and the rate — you must apply the **right
threshold for that age**. 48/min at 9 months is below 50 → **no pneumonia**.

**REMEMBER:** *60, 50, 40 — and chest indrawing turns "no pneumonia" into pneumonia.*

## 43. Levels of Community Participation

**PEARL:** The **highest level of community participation is involvement in PLANNING and
decision-making** for health programmes — above merely contributing resources, having
their ideas considered, or having their felt needs identified.

**UNDERSTAND:** The hierarchy measures **how much control the community holds**, not how
much it does. Contributing money or labour is participation in **implementation**, where the
agenda has already been set by someone else. Participating in planning means the community
**defines the problem and chooses the solution** — genuine power-sharing, which is what
Alma-Ata meant when it made community participation a pillar of primary health care.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Ascending ladder: **being informed → being consulted (ideas/felt needs considered) → contributing resources (money, labour, materials) → participating in implementation → PARTICIPATING IN PLANNING AND DECISION-MAKING (highest)**.
- **Alma-Ata Declaration (1978) — "Health for All by 2000":** the **five principles of primary health care** are **equitable distribution, community participation, intersectoral coordination, appropriate technology, and a focus on prevention**.
- **Community participation in Indian practice: ASHA workers, Village Health Sanitation and Nutrition Committees (VHSNC), Rogi Kalyan Samitis, Mahila Arogya Samitis, Panchayati Raj involvement** under the NHM.
- **"Felt needs"** are what the community itself perceives; **"real/normative needs"** are what professionals identify — effective programmes must address both, and starting from felt needs is what secures participation.
- WHO/UNICEF define community participation as **"the process by which individuals and families assume responsibility for their own health and welfare and for those of the community"**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Rank the options by **who decides**. Anything describing planning or
decision-making outranks anything describing contribution or consultation.

**REMEMBER:** *Giving resources is helping; making the plan is participating.*

## 44. The HPV Vaccine Is an L1 Virus-Like Particle

**PEARL:** The recombinant HPV vaccine contains the **L1 MAJOR CAPSID PROTEIN**, which
self-assembles into **virus-like particles (VLPs)** — **no viral DNA, so it cannot cause
infection or cancer**. **E6 and E7 are the ONCOPROTEINS, not vaccine components.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Protection depends on **neutralising antibody against conformational
epitopes on the intact capsid** — antibody must bind the virus **before** it enters the
basal keratinocyte. Only the **assembled L1 capsid** presents those epitopes, which is why
the vaccine uses self-assembling VLPs rather than a linear peptide. **E6 and E7 are
intracellular** proteins expressed only *after* infection (E6 degrades **p53**, E7
inactivates **Rb**), so antibodies against them would be useless prophylactically — they
are instead the target of **therapeutic** vaccines under development.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Vaccines: bivalent (16, 18), quadrivalent (6, 11, 16, 18), NONAVALENT (adds 31, 33, 45, 52, 58)**; India's indigenous quadrivalent is **CERVAVAC**.
- **16 and 18 → ~70% of cervical cancers** (and oropharyngeal, anal, vulval, vaginal, penile cancers); **6 and 11 → 90% of genital warts and juvenile recurrent respiratory papillomatosis**.
- **WHO 2022 schedule:** **1 or 2 doses for girls 9–14 and young women 15–20; 2 doses (6 months apart) for women over 21; at least 2 (preferably 3) doses if immunocompromised or HIV-positive.**
- Best given **before sexual debut**; it is **prophylactic, not therapeutic** — it does not clear an existing infection, and **screening must continue**.
- **HPV integration** disrupts the **E2 gene**, releasing E6/E7 from regulation → uncontrolled proliferation; **persistent high-risk HPV infection is necessary for cervical cancer**.
- **WHO 90–70–90 elimination targets by 2030:** 90% of girls vaccinated by 15, 70% of women screened twice with a high-performance test at 35 and 45, 90% of those with disease treated.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** One-line recall of the antigen. **L1 = vaccine; E6/E7 = oncogenes.**

**REMEMBER:** *L for the Look-alike particle in the vaccine; E for the Evil oncoproteins.*

## 45. Triage in Disaster and Emergency Care

**PEARL:** Triage sorts casualties into **IMMEDIATE (red), DELAYED (yellow), MINIMAL/
minor "walking wounded" (green) and EXPECTANT/dead (black)** — the guiding principle in a
mass-casualty situation is **the greatest good for the greatest number**, not the sickest
patient first.

**UNDERSTAND:** This inverts everyday clinical ethics for a specific reason: when demand
overwhelms resources, spending an hour on an unsalvageable patient costs the lives of
several salvageable ones. Triage therefore ranks by **who will benefit most from immediate
intervention** — which is why the **moribund are categorised EXPECTANT (black)** and the
**walking wounded are deliberately deprioritised (green)**, even though both extremes feel
counter-intuitive at the bedside.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **RED (Priority I — immediate):** life-threatening but salvageable with prompt intervention — airway obstruction, tension pneumothorax, massive haemorrhage, shock.
- **YELLOW (Priority II — delayed):** serious injuries that can safely wait a few hours — long-bone fractures, stable abdominal injuries, burns without airway involvement.
- **GREEN (Priority III — minimal):** minor injuries, the "walking wounded"; they can also be recruited to help.
- **BLACK (Priority IV — expectant/dead):** dead, or injuries so severe that survival is improbable given available resources — **comfort care only**.
- **Triage is DYNAMIC — patients are re-triaged repeatedly** as their condition and the available resources change.
- **START (Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment)** uses **respiration (>30/min), perfusion (capillary refill >2 s or absent radial pulse) and mental status (cannot obey commands)** to sort adults in under 60 seconds; **JumpSTART** is the paediatric version.
- **Disaster management cycle: PREVENTION/mitigation → PREPAREDNESS → RESPONSE (search and rescue, triage, definitive care) → RECOVERY/rehabilitation.** India's framework is the **Disaster Management Act 2005 with the NDMA and NDRF**.
- In-hospital "reverse triage" discharges stable inpatients to create surge capacity.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The answer is the **priority-based** list (immediate/delayed/minimal),
not anatomical or administrative categories.

**REMEMBER:** *Red now, yellow soon, green last, black never — the greatest good for the
greatest number.*

## 47. Leprosy: The Indicator of Early Detection

**PEARL:** The **PROPORTION OF NEWLY DIAGNOSED PATIENTS WITH GRADE-2 DISABILITY** is the
best indicator of how early a programme is detecting leprosy. A **falling grade-2 disability
rate means cases are being found sooner**; treatment completion and case-detection rates say
nothing about the stage at diagnosis.

**UNDERSTAND:** Nerve damage in leprosy is **cumulative and largely irreversible**, so
**visible deformity is a marker of how long the disease went untreated**. A patient
presenting with a claw hand or a lagophthalmos has, by definition, been infected and
undiagnosed for years. That makes grade-2 disability a **built-in clock** measuring delay —
which is why WHO adopted the **grade-2 disability rate per million population** as its
headline indicator, and why the **new case detection rate alone can be misleading** (it may
rise simply because a survey was conducted).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **WHO disability grading: GRADE 0 — no anaesthesia, no visible deformity; GRADE 1 — LOSS OF SENSATION in the hand, foot or eye, without visible damage; GRADE 2 — VISIBLE DAMAGE OR DEFORMITY (ulcer, clawing, lagophthalmos, visual impairment, absorption of digits).**
- **Classification (WHO, operational): PAUCIBACILLARY — 1–5 skin lesions, no bacilli on smear → RIFAMPICIN + DAPSONE for 6 months. MULTIBACILLARY — more than 5 lesions or smear-positive → RIFAMPICIN + DAPSONE + CLOFAZIMINE for 12 months.** Rifampicin is given **monthly and supervised**; a single dose kills ~99.9% of viable bacilli, so **patients become non-infectious within days**.
- **Ridley–Jopling spectrum: TT (tuberculoid — few lesions, strong cell-mediated immunity, LEPROMIN POSITIVE) → BT → BB → BL → LL (lepromatous — numerous lesions, high bacillary load, LEPROMIN NEGATIVE, leonine facies, saddle nose, madarosis).**
- **Reactions: TYPE 1 (reversal) — cell-mediated, in borderline disease, causing acute nerve damage → PREDNISOLONE. TYPE 2 (ERYTHEMA NODOSUM LEPROSUM) — immune-complex mediated, in BL/LL, with crops of tender nodules, fever and iritis → THALIDOMIDE (or steroids; thalidomide is contraindicated in women of childbearing potential).** **Reactions are the leading cause of nerve damage and are treated as emergencies.**
- **NLEP (National Leprosy Eradication Programme)**: elimination as a public health problem = **<1 case per 10,000 population**, achieved nationally in 2005; the current focus is on **zero disability among new child cases** and active case finding (Leprosy Case Detection Campaigns), plus **single-dose rifampicin post-exposure prophylaxis** for contacts.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The question asks which metric reflects **early detection**, not
programme performance. Grade-2 disability is the only one measuring **delay**.

**REMEMBER:** *Deformity at diagnosis measures the years the disease went unseen.*

## 48. Managing a Blood Spill

**PEARL:** The correct sequence for a blood spill is: **(1) wear gloves and PPE; (2) cover
and absorb the spill with absorbent material; (3) then apply 1% sodium HYPOCHLORITE for
20–30 minutes; (4) clean the area and dispose of the waste as infectious.** Absorption comes
**before** disinfection.

**UNDERSTAND:** Hypochlorite is **rapidly inactivated by organic matter**. Pouring it onto a
pool of blood consumes the free chlorine in oxidising the protein, so little is left to kill
pathogens; it also **generates chlorine gas** with large volumes of organic material and can
**aerosolise the spill**. Removing the bulk of the organic load first means the disinfectant
meets a thin residual film it can actually sterilise — hence "absorb, then disinfect".

**MUST KNOW:**
- **1% sodium hypochlorite (10,000 ppm available chlorine) for blood spills and laboratory discard jars; 0.5% for general surface disinfection.** Hypochlorite is **corrosive to metals, inactivated by organic matter and by sunlight, and must be freshly prepared.**

- **Needle-stick injury: DO NOT squeeze or suck the wound; wash with soap and running water; report immediately; assess the source and start HIV POST-EXPOSURE PROPHYLAXIS WITHIN 72 HOURS (ideally within 2 hours) for 28 days; check hepatitis B status and give HBIG plus vaccine if non-immune; follow up hepatitis C by PCR.**
- **Risk of transmission per percutaneous exposure: HEPATITIS B ~30%, HEPATITIS C ~3%, HIV ~0.3%** — the reverse of most people's intuition.
- **Standard precautions apply to EVERY patient**, regardless of known infection status.

## 49. Tracking of Blood Pressure

**PEARL:** **"TRACKING" is the tendency of an individual to maintain their RELATIVE POSITION
(centile) within a population distribution over time** — children with high blood pressure
become adults with high blood pressure. It is the epidemiological justification for
**PRIMORDIAL prevention**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Tracking means that risk is **established early and persists**, so waiting
until adulthood to intervene means acting after the trajectory is already set. This is why
public health targets the **determinants themselves — diet, salt intake, obesity, physical
inactivity — in childhood**, which is exactly what primordial prevention means: preventing
the **emergence of the risk factor**, not just its consequences.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Levels of prevention: PRIMORDIAL — prevent the emergence of risk factors in a population that does not yet have them (discouraging children from taking up smoking or a high-salt diet); PRIMARY — prevent disease in those with risk factors (health promotion and specific protection, e.g. immunisation); SECONDARY — early diagnosis and treatment (SCREENING); TERTIARY — limit disability and rehabilitate.**
- **RULE OF HALVES (hypertension):** about half of hypertensives are **undiagnosed**; of those diagnosed, half are **untreated**; of those treated, half are **inadequately controlled** — so only about **one in eight is adequately controlled**.
- **STEPwise approach (WHO STEPS)** — surveillance of non-communicable disease risk factors in three steps: **Step 1 questionnaire (behavioural), Step 2 physical measurements (BP, height, weight, waist), Step 3 biochemical measurements (glucose, lipids)**.
- **High-risk vs POPULATION (mass) strategy (Geoffrey Rose):** the population strategy shifts the **whole distribution** a little and prevents more disease overall — the **"prevention paradox"**, since it brings large benefit to the community but little apparent benefit to each individual.
- Other applied terms: **herd immunity, epidemiological transition, and the National Programme for Prevention and Control of NCDs (NP-NCD).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem describes people **keeping their relative rank over decades** —
that is tracking, not the rule of halves (which is about detection and control).

**REMEMBER:** *Your blood pressure centile follows you from childhood — so prevention must
start there.*

## 50. The Ishikawa (Fishbone) Diagram

**PEARL:** The **ISHIKAWA / FISHBONE / CAUSE-AND-EFFECT diagram** traces a problem back to
its **root causes**, branching like a fish skeleton. It is a **quality-improvement tool**,
not a statistical graph — bar diagrams and histograms **display data**, whereas the fishbone
**analyses causation**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The diagram exists because complex health-system problems (a rise in
surgical site infections, a maternal death, a missed diagnosis) almost never have a single
cause. Placing the problem at the "head" and forcing contributors into standard branch
categories — traditionally the **6 Ms: Man (people), Machine (equipment), Method (process),
Material, Measurement and Milieu/environment** — makes the team **look systematically in
every domain instead of stopping at the first plausible explanation** (usually blaming an
individual). It is the structured antidote to premature closure.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Related quality tools: **PARETO chart (the "80/20 rule" — ranks causes by frequency to find the vital few), PDSA/PDCA cycle (Plan–Do–Study–Act), FLOW CHART/process map, CONTROL chart (monitors a process over time), SCATTER diagram, CHECK sheet, and HISTOGRAM** — collectively "the seven basic tools of quality".
- **ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS** (of which the fishbone is a component) is the standard response to a **SENTINEL EVENT**; **"the five whys"** is its simplest form.
- **Data-display graphs and their uses: BAR diagram — comparing QUALITATIVE/discrete categories (bars separated); HISTOGRAM — a CONTINUOUS quantitative frequency distribution (bars touching); LINE/trend graph — change over time; PIE chart — proportions of a whole; SCATTER diagram — correlation between two continuous variables; PICTOGRAM — for lay audiences; SPOT/shaded MAP — geographic distribution of disease; FREQUENCY POLYGON — comparing two distributions.**
- **EPIDEMIC CURVE** is the specific histogram plotting cases against time of onset — its shape distinguishes a **point-source (single sharp peak), continuous common-source (plateau) and propagated/person-to-person (successive peaks one incubation period apart)** outbreak.
- **Multiple regression** is an analytical technique to adjust for confounders, not a graph.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The question tests whether you can separate **display** from
**analysis**. Anything about tracing *causes* of a problem = **Ishikawa**.

**REMEMBER:** *The fishbone asks "why", the histogram only shows "how many".*

## 52. Choosing a Test of Significance

**PEARL:** **Qualitative (categorical) data → CHI-SQUARE.** **Quantitative, 2 independent groups → UNPAIRED t-test.** **Quantitative, same group before and after → PAIRED t-test.** **Quantitative, more than 2 groups → ANOVA.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The algorithm has only two decision points. First ask **what kind of variable** you are comparing — counts/proportions or measurements. Then, for measurements, ask **how many groups and whether they are the same individuals**. Every exam question is solved by answering those two questions in order.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Chi-square**: tests **difference between proportions** and tells you whether an association is **present or absent** — it does **NOT measure the strength** of association (that needs odds ratio, relative risk, or a correlation coefficient). It is a **non-parametric** test and needs expected cell counts ≥ 5 (otherwise **Fisher's exact test**).
- **Non-parametric tests** make **no assumption of normality**, so they are used for **skewed data, ordinal data and small samples**. Examples: **chi-square, Mann–Whitney U (≈ unpaired t), Wilcoxon signed-rank (≈ paired t), Kruskal–Wallis (≈ ANOVA), Spearman correlation**.
- **ANOVA is parametric** — a common distractor.
- Worked examples: "malnourished children in rural vs urban" → counts → **chi-square**; "birth weight in supplemented vs unsupplemented mothers" → measurement, two independent groups → **unpaired t**; "heights across several school classes" → measurement, > 2 groups → **ANOVA**.
- **Correlation** measures strength and direction (**r from −1 to +1**; **|r| > 0.5 is strong**); **r² (coefficient of determination)** gives the **percentage of variation explained**. **Pearson** for continuous linear relationships; **Spearman** for ordinal or non-linear.
- **Correlation is not causation** — the standard interpretation trap.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Three or four questions per paper come from this single algorithm. Identify **variable type first**, then **group number**.

**REMEMBER:** *Counts use chi-square; measurements use t or ANOVA depending on how many groups.*

## 53. Distribution Shape — Mean, Median and Mode

**PEARL:** **Positively (right) skewed: mean > median > mode.** **Negatively (left) skewed: mean < median < mode.** **Symmetrical unimodal: mean = median = mode.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The **mean is dragged toward the tail** because it is the only measure that uses every value's magnitude. The **mode sits at the peak** and never moves, and the **median lies between them**. So the direction of the tail — which defines the skew — tells you the order automatically.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The skew is named after the TAIL, not the peak** — a curve with a long left tail is **negatively skewed** even though the bulk of data sits on the right.
- In a **bimodal** distribution the **mean = median** (if symmetrical) but there are **two modes** — a favourite comparison question.
- **Age at death** typically gives a **negatively skewed** curve (most deaths at old age, a long tail of early deaths); **income, hospital stay and incubation periods** are typically **positively skewed**.
- **Coefficient of variation (CV) = SD × 100 / mean** — the measure used to **compare variability between two different data sets** (different units or very different means), since SD alone cannot. **CV < 5% good, > 10% poor** for laboratory method performance.
- **Standard error of the mean = SD/√n** — measures precision of the estimate, not the spread of the data.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two forms — read a curve and state the mean/median/mode order, or choose the measure that compares variability across data sets (**coefficient of variation**).

**REMEMBER:** *The mean chases the tail.*

## 54. Validity vs Reliability

**PEARL:** **Validity = accuracy** (closeness to the true value). **Reliability = repeatability/precision** (consistency on repeated testing). A test whose **readings scatter widely but whose MEAN matches the standard** has **high validity and low reliability**.

**UNDERSTAND:** They are independent properties. Think of a dartboard: **darts scattered evenly around the bullseye average out correctly** (valid) but are **inconsistent** (unreliable); darts tightly grouped in one corner are **reliable but not valid**. In the classic exam stem you must **compute the mean of the readings and compare it with the reference value**, then separately judge the spread.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Worked example: readings 9.4–12.1 (widely scattered) with **mean 10.0** against a standard of **10.2** → **valid but unreliable**.
- **Reliability (repeatability, precision, reproducibility)** depends on **observer variation (intra- and inter-observer), biological variation in the subject, and errors of the instrument/method**.
- Validity is measured by **sensitivity, specificity, predictive values**; reliability by **kappa statistic** (for categorical agreement) and **correlation/coefficient of variation** (for continuous).
- **A test can be reliable without being valid, but a systematically biased test is never valid however precise.**
- **ROC curve** plots **sensitivity (y) against 1 − specificity (x)** across cut-offs; **the better test lies closer to the upper LEFT corner and has the greater area under the curve**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Calculate the mean before answering. Students who judge "inconsistent, therefore bad test" pick the wrong option.

**REMEMBER:** *Scattered but centred is accurate yet imprecise.*

## 55. Sampling Methods

**PEARL:** Sampling **in successive stages using progressively smaller units** — schools → sections → students — is **MULTISTAGE sampling**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The purpose is **logistical**: you cannot list every student in a state, but you can list every school. Sampling in stages means you only need a **sampling frame for the units you actually select at each level**, which is what makes large national surveys (NFHS, coverage evaluation) feasible.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Simple random** — every unit has an equal chance (lottery, random number table). **Randomness refers to the method of selection, not the outcome.**
- **Systematic** — every kth unit after a random start; risk of bias if there is **periodicity** in the list.
- **Stratified** — divide into **homogeneous strata** first (age, sex, income), then sample randomly within each; **increases precision** and guarantees representation of small subgroups.
- **Cluster** — divide into clusters, select whole clusters at random and study **everyone** in them; used in the **WHO 30-cluster immunisation coverage survey**.
- **Multistage** — sequential sampling of progressively smaller units.
- **Non-random methods**: **convenience, purposive, quota**, and **snowball sampling** — the last used for **hidden or stigmatised populations** (IV drug users, sex workers, alcoholics), where existing subjects recruit others.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The word **"then... then..."** in the stem signals stages. Cluster differs because you study everyone in a selected cluster rather than sampling within it.

**REMEMBER:** *Stages of ever-smaller units means multistage.*

## 56. Interpreting an Odds Ratio Table

**PEARL:** A factor is a **significant risk factor** only if **OR > 1 AND p < 0.05**. **OR < 1 with p < 0.05 means PROTECTIVE.** **A non-significant p-value makes the OR uninterpretable, however large.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The odds ratio gives the **direction and size** of the association; the p-value tells you whether the finding is likely to be **more than chance**. Both are required. A rural OR of 2.4 with **p = 0.1** looks alarming but is statistically indistinguishable from 1 — so no conclusion can be drawn.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **OR = 1** → no association; **> 1** → risk factor; **< 1** → protective.
- Worked reading of the classic table: **age (rising OR, all p < 0.001) → risk increases with age ✔**; **illiterate OR 1.2, p < 0.01; literate OR 0.4 → illiteracy is a risk factor ✔**; **female OR 0.3 (protective, not risk) ✘**; **rural OR 2.4 but p = 0.1 (not significant) ✘**.
- Better still, use the **95% confidence interval** — if it **includes 1**, the result is not significant.
- **Odds ratio approximates relative risk when the disease is rare**; it is the measure used in **case-control** studies, while **relative risk** belongs to cohort studies.
- **p < 0.05** conventionally = statistically significant, i.e. < 5% probability the result arose by chance alone. Statistical significance ≠ clinical importance.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Check **both columns** for every row. Most wrong answers come from reading the OR and ignoring the p-value, or vice versa.

**REMEMBER:** *Direction from the odds ratio, credibility from the p-value.*

## 57. Food Adulteration Syndromes

**PEARL:** **Epidemic dropsy = SANGUINARINE from Argemone mexicana oil adulterating mustard oil.** Presents with **bilateral pitting leg oedema, diarrhoea, breathlessness, and characteristically GLAUCOMA (hypersecretory)** — with death from **cardiac failure**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Sanguinarine is a **capillary toxin** that increases vascular permeability throughout the body — hence oedema everywhere, including the eye, where excess aqueous production raises intraocular pressure. That systemic capillary leak also explains the cardiac failure and the pulmonary symptoms.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The four classic adulterants: **Argemone oil in mustard oil → sanguinarine → EPIDEMIC DROPSY**; **Lathyrus sativus (khesari dal) in pulses → BOAA (β-oxalyl-amino-alanine) → LATHYRISM** (spastic paraparesis, scissoring gait); **Crotalaria (jhunjhunia) seeds in millet → pyrrolizidine alkaloids → ENDEMIC ASCITES** (veno-occlusive liver disease); **Claviceps purpurea on bajra/rye → ergot alkaloids → ERGOTISM** (gangrene, convulsions).
- Detection of argemone oil: **nitric acid test** (screening) and **paper chromatography (most sensitive)**.
- The glaucoma is the discriminating clinical clue — no other adulterant causes it.
- Governed in India by the **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSSAI)**, replacing the PFA Act.
- Also worth knowing: **aflatoxin (Aspergillus flavus on groundnut/maize) → hepatocellular carcinoma**; **fluorosis** from water; **methanol** in illicit liquor.

## 58. Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

**PEARL:** A **previously fully immunised** person (PrEP or a complete PEP course) needs only **2 booster doses on days 0 and 3** — **NO immunoglobulin**, regardless of category.

**UNDERSTAND:** A primed immune system mounts an **anamnestic response within days**, so passive antibody would only **interfere with the memory response**. Immunoglobulin exists to bridge the ~7–14 day gap before an unprimed person makes their own antibody — a gap that does not exist in someone already immunised.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Categories: I** — touching/feeding, licks on intact skin → **no PEP**. **II** — **nibbling of uncovered skin, minor scratches or abrasions WITHOUT bleeding** → vaccine only. **III** — **transdermal bites, scratches with bleeding/oozing, licks on broken skin or mucosa, bat exposure** → **vaccine + rabies immunoglobulin**.
- **An abrasion with oozing is category III**, not II — the presence of bleeding is the discriminator.
- **Wound care is the single most important step: wash with soap under running water for 15 minutes**, then apply **povidone-iodine or spirit**. **Do not suture**; if unavoidable, infiltrate immunoglobulin first and **delay suturing by a few hours**, using minimal loose sutures.
- Immunoglobulin doses: **human RIG 20 IU/kg**, **equine RIG 40 IU/kg**, infiltrated **into and around the wound**, given **once only** (never a second dose, and never after day 7 of the vaccine course).
- Vaccine schedules: **intradermal (Updated Thai Red Cross) 2 sites on days 0, 3, 7, 28**; **Essen IM: days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28**.
- In India, **the biting animal's vaccination status does not by itself justify withholding prophylaxis**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two variables decide the answer: **wound category** and **prior immunisation status**. Read both before choosing.

**REMEMBER:** *Already vaccinated means two boosters and no immunoglobulin.*

## 59. Biomedical Waste Segregation

**PEARL:** **RED bag** — contaminated **recyclable** plastics: **IV tubing and sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles, gloves, bottles**. **YELLOW** — anatomical and soiled waste. **WHITE (translucent puncture-proof container)** — sharps. **BLUE** — glassware and metallic implants.

**UNDERSTAND:** The colour code is organised by **final disposal route**, not by degree of contamination. **Yellow goes to incineration** (things that must be destroyed), **red is autoclaved/microwaved then shredded and recycled** (plastics that can be reclaimed), **white is disinfected then shredded** (things that can injure), and **blue is disinfected and recycled** (glass and metal, which cannot be incinerated).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Yellow (non-chlorinated plastic bag)**: **human and animal anatomical waste**, **soiled waste** (dressings, cotton, plaster casts, blood bags), **expired/discarded medicines**, **chemical waste**, **microbiology and biotechnology waste**, **discarded linen** — disposal by **incineration/plasma pyrolysis/deep burial**.
- **Red**: **autoclaving/microwaving/hydroclaving, then shredding and recycling**. **Chlorinated plastic bags are prohibited** (dioxin release on incineration).
- **White**: needles, scalpels, blades — after disinfection, **shredding/encapsulation/concrete pit**.
- **Blue**: broken/discarded glass, metallic implants — **disinfection then recycling**.
- **Healthcare workers handling biomedical waste must be vaccinated against HEPATITIS B and TETANUS** (and have annual health checks).
- **Blood spill:** **first remove the blood with absorbent material** (discard in yellow), **then decontaminate the surface with sodium hypochlorite** — **1:100 for < 10 mL, 1:10 followed by 1:100 for large spills or culture spills**. Pouring hypochlorite onto pooled blood first is wrong — organic matter inactivates it.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Bag colour to waste type, and the **order** of steps in a spill (**absorb first, disinfect second**).

**REMEMBER:** *Yellow burns, red recycles, white contains sharps, blue takes glass.*

## 60. Vaccine Vial Monitor

**PEARL:** The **VVM records CUMULATIVE HEAT exposure** and is read by health workers in the field. **If the inner square is the SAME shade as or DARKER than the outer circle, DISCARD the vial.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The VVM is a **heat-sensitive chemical indicator whose colour change is irreversible and time–temperature integrated** — it therefore reflects the vial's entire journey, not the temperature at one moment. That is exactly why it **cannot indicate freezing damage**, and why it **cannot alter the printed expiry date**: expiry reflects inherent shelf-life, a separate variable.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The VVM assesses **vaccine POTENCY, not vaccine efficacy** — potency is the vial's remaining ability to immunise; **efficacy** is a trial-derived population measure.
- **A usable vial has the inner square LIGHTER than the outer circle.**
- **An expired vaccine must be discarded even if the VVM is acceptable.**
- VVMs do **not** detect freezing. Freezing damages **T-series (adsorbed) vaccines — DPT, TT, DT, hepatitis B, and diluents** — detected instead by the **SHAKE TEST**.
- Cold chain temperatures: **most vaccines +2 to +8 °C**; **OPV and measles may be stored frozen (−15 to −25 °C)** at higher levels. The **most heat-sensitive vaccine is OPV**; **most sensitive to freezing is hepatitis B (and DPT)**.
- In the ILR: **T-series vaccines are kept away from the freezing compartment**, on the upper/warmer shelves.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Multi-statement format. The two true statements are always about **monitoring heat and cumulative exposure**; the false ones claim it measures efficacy or changes the expiry date.

**REMEMBER:** *Inner square darker means throw it away.*

## 61. Leprosy — Cardinal Signs and MDT

**PEARL:** Diagnose leprosy (and start MDT) on **ANY ONE** of three cardinal signs: **(1) definite loss of sensation in a hypopigmented/reddish patch; (2) thickened peripheral nerve WITH sensory loss or muscle weakness; (3) acid-fast bacilli in a slit-skin smear**. **Nerve thickening ALONE is not sufficient.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Leprosy is fundamentally a **disease of nerves**, so the diagnosis requires **evidence of nerve damage (sensory loss or weakness)** or **direct evidence of the organism**. A thickened nerve alone is non-specific — it occurs in **amyloidosis, Charcot–Marie–Tooth, and other neuropathies** — which is why the criterion demands thickening *plus* functional loss.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **WHO MDT — paucibacillary (≤ 5 lesions): rifampicin 600 mg monthly + dapsone 100 mg daily for 6 months.** **Multibacillary (> 5 lesions): rifampicin 600 mg monthly + clofazimine 300 mg monthly and 50 mg daily + dapsone 100 mg daily for 12 months.** (WHO now recommends the **three-drug regimen for all cases**.)
- **Slit-skin smear** sites and **bacteriological index**; **skin biopsy** for classification.
- **Lepra reactions: Type 1 (reversal, delayed hypersensitivity, in borderline cases) → steroids**; **Type 2 (erythema nodosum leprosum, immune complex, in BL/LL) → steroids, thalidomide, clofazimine**. **Do not stop MDT during a reaction.**
- **Clofazimine causes reddish-black skin pigmentation** and ichthyosis — the main adherence problem; **dapsone causes haemolysis (check G6PD) and dapsone syndrome**.
- Programme indicators: **new case detection rate**, **proportion with grade 2 disability at diagnosis (marker of delayed detection)**, **child case proportion (marker of ongoing transmission)**.
- **Post-exposure prophylaxis: single-dose rifampicin** to contacts.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The multi-statement version hinges on whether "peripheral nerve thickening" is qualified by sensory loss. Read the qualifier.

**REMEMBER:** *A thick nerve only counts if it has stopped working.*

## 62. Malaria Treatment Under the National Programme

**PEARL:** For **P. falciparum in the NORTH-EASTERN states, use ACT with ARTEMETHER–LUMEFANTRINE (AL)**. In the **rest of India, use artesunate + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (AS+SP)**. Both are followed by **primaquine**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The regional split exists because of **documented sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine resistance in the North-East** — the partner drug, not the artemisinin, is the problem. Using a failing partner drug exposes the artemisinin to selection pressure and breeds artemisinin resistance, which is why the NVBDCP mandates a different ACT there.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **P. vivax**: **chloroquine 3 days + primaquine 0.25 mg/kg for 14 days** (radical cure of hypnozoites). **Check G6PD** before primaquine; **contraindicated in pregnancy and infants < 6 months**.
- **P. falciparum**: **ACT for 3 days + single-dose primaquine 0.75 mg/kg on day 2** (gametocytocidal, to block transmission).
- **P. malariae → treat as falciparum; P. ovale → treat as vivax.**
- **Severe malaria**: parenteral **artesunate** (drug of choice), artemether or quinine for at least 24 h, then complete with a full oral ACT course.
- **Chemoprophylaxis: short-term (≤ 6 weeks) doxycycline** daily, starting 1 day before travel and continuing 4 weeks after; **long-term (> 6 weeks) mefloquine** weekly, starting 2 weeks before and continuing 4 weeks after.
- Diagnosis before treatment: **rapid diagnostic test or microscopy** — the programme is now "test, treat, track."

## 64. Intrauterine Device — Contraindications and Complications

**PEARL:** **Cardiac disease is NOT a contraindication to an IUCD.** Absolute contraindications are **suspected pregnancy, active PID, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, and genital malignancy**. The **commonest complication is vaginal bleeding**, followed by **abdominal pain**.

**UNDERSTAND:** IUCD contraindications are **local and gynaecological**, because the device acts locally and its risks (infection, bleeding, perforation) are confined to the uterus. Systemic conditions such as heart disease are irrelevant — indeed the IUCD is **preferred** in women with cardiac, hepatic or thrombotic disease precisely because it **avoids systemic hormones**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Absolute:** suspected/confirmed **pregnancy**, **active or recent PID**, **undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding**, **carcinoma of cervix, uterus or adnexa**, and (for copper devices) **Wilson disease/copper allergy**.
- **Relative:** anaemia, **menorrhagia**, previous PID, purulent cervical discharge, **distorted cavity from fibroids**, uterine malformations, previous ectopic pregnancy, an unmotivated user.
- Complications in order: **bleeding (commonest) > pain > pelvic infection (highest in the first 20 days) > expulsion > perforation > pregnancy (including ectopic)**.
- **Copper-T 380A** — effective for **10 years**; **LNG-IUS (Mirena)** — 5 years, and **reduces** menstrual blood loss (so it is the choice when menorrhagia coexists).
- **Ideal timing of insertion**: during or just after menstruation, immediately post-abortion, or **postpartum (PPIUCD — within 48 hours or after 6 weeks)**.
- **Copper IUCD is also the most effective emergency contraceptive** — within 5 days.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The multi-select list mixes absolute and relative contraindications; **cardiac disease is on neither list**.

**REMEMBER:** *The IUCD's risks are all inside the uterus.*

## 65. DPT Vaccine — Contraindications vs Precautions

**PEARL:** **Only anaphylaxis to a previous dose and encephalopathy within 7 days (for the pertussis component) are true CONTRAINDICATIONS.** Fever, inconsolable crying and seizures are **PRECAUTIONS**, not contraindications. A temperature above **37 °C is certainly not** a contraindication.

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction is a **risk–benefit judgement**. A contraindication means the risk of the next dose **exceeds** the benefit; a precaution means the risk is **increased but usually outweighed** by the benefit, so vaccination proceeds with counselling and monitoring. Withholding vaccine for minor febrile events is the commonest cause of **missed opportunities for immunisation**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Precautions after DTwP:** temperature **> 40.5 °C within 48 h**, **persistent inconsolable crying ≥ 3 h**, **hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode**, **seizures within 3 days**.
- **Encephalopathy within 7 days of a pertussis-containing vaccine → give DT (omit pertussis) for subsequent doses.**
- The **whole-cell pertussis component is not given after 7 years of age** (reactogenicity); use **Tdap/Td**, in which the **diphtheria toxoid dose is reduced** (lower-case "d").
- Catch-up: **unimmunised 5–7 years → DPT 1, 2, 3 at 1-month intervals plus a booster**; **above 7 years → Tdap then Td**.
- Given **intramuscularly in the anterolateral thigh** in infants.
- **Minor illness, low-grade fever, breastfeeding, malnutrition, prematurity and antibiotic use are NOT contraindications to any vaccine.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False regarding the vaccine" → the statement calling a **mild temperature a contraindication**. The others (thigh site, no whole-cell pertussis after 7 years, reduced adult diphtheria dose) are all true.

**REMEMBER:** *Only anaphylaxis and encephalopathy stop the vaccine.*

## 66. Arthropod Vectors

**PEARL:** **Relapsing fever is transmitted by SOFT ticks (Ornithodoros) and by the LOUSE — not by hard ticks.** Hard (**Ixodid**) ticks transmit **Kyasanur Forest disease, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, tularaemia, Lyme disease, babesiosis, ehrlichiosis, tick paralysis and Crimean–Congo haemorrhagic fever**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The split follows the **feeding behaviour**. **Hard ticks attach and feed for days**, allowing slow-replicating organisms (Borrelia burgdorferi, rickettsiae, viruses) to migrate from gut to salivary gland. **Soft ticks feed for minutes**, so they transmit only organisms already present in the salivary glands or coxal fluid — like *Borrelia recurrentis/duttoni*.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Anopheles** — malaria, filariasis. **Culex** — **Bancroftian filariasis, Japanese encephalitis, West Nile**. **Aedes** — **dengue, yellow fever, chikungunya, Zika**. **Mansonia** — Brugian filariasis.
- **Sandfly (Phlebotomus)** — **kala-azar, oriental sore, sandfly fever, Oroya fever**. **Tsetse fly** — African trypanosomiasis. **Reduviid (kissing) bug** — Chagas disease. **Black fly (Simulium)** — onchocerciasis.
- **Louse** — **epidemic typhus, relapsing fever, trench fever**. **Rat flea** — **bubonic plague, endemic (murine) typhus**. **Mite (trombiculid)** — **scrub typhus**. **Hard tick** — as above.
- **Mechanical transmission**: **housefly** (typhoid, cholera, dysentery, trachoma, polio).
- **Dengue with warning signs**: the cornerstone of treatment is **IV crystalloid fluids** — the pathology is **plasma leakage**, not bleeding. **Platelets only for severe thrombocytopenia (< 10,000) with active bleeding**; **FFP and cryoprecipitate are not indicated**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not transmitted by hard ticks" → **relapsing fever**. Learn the **soft tick and louse** pair as the exception.

**REMEMBER:** *Long feeders carry slow organisms; quick feeders carry relapsing fever.*

## 67. BCG — What It Actually Prevents

**PEARL:** **BCG protects against DISSEMINATED and CNS (miliary and meningeal) tuberculosis in children.** It does **NOT prevent primary infection**, and does **not prevent reactivation of latent pulmonary disease** — so its effect on community transmission is limited.

**UNDERSTAND:** BCG induces a **cell-mediated response that limits haematogenous DISSEMINATION** of bacilli once infection has occurred, rather than preventing entry into the lung. Since **adult pulmonary TB — the source of community spread — arises mostly from reactivation**, a vaccine that does not prevent reactivation cannot interrupt transmission.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Efficacy against **miliary TB and TB meningitis in children is high (~80%)**; efficacy against adult pulmonary TB is highly variable (0–80%) across trials — attributed to **environmental mycobacteria, BCG strain differences and latitude** (the **Chingleput trial in India showed no protection** against pulmonary TB).
- Dose: **0.1 mL intradermal (0.05 mL under 1 month) over the left deltoid insertion**; a **live attenuated** vaccine.
- Normal course: papule at 2–3 weeks → ulcer at 5–6 weeks → **scar by 6–12 weeks**.
- Adverse effects: **injection abscess, regional lymphadenitis (suppurative), keloid**; **disseminated BCG-osis in SCID or symptomatic HIV** — hence **contraindicated in symptomatic HIV and primary immunodeficiency**.
- **Given at birth** under the National Immunisation Schedule (up to 1 year of age if missed).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The temptation is to answer "pulmonary TB". The evidence-based answer is **CNS and disseminated disease**.

**REMEMBER:** *BCG stops the spread inside the child, not the spread between adults.*

## 68. The Normal Distribution — The Empirical Rule

**PEARL:** **Mean ± 1 SD = 68%; mean ± 2 SD = 95%; mean ± 3 SD = 99.7%.** Because the curve is symmetrical, the remainder is **split equally into the two tails**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Every one of these questions is solved by converting the percentage asked into a **tail fraction**. If 95% lies within ±2 SD, then 5% lies outside, so **2.5% below mean − 2 SD** and **2.5% above mean + 2 SD**. Ask "how much is in the tail?" first, then count the standard deviations.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Worked examples: mean 105, SD 10 → **95% lie between 85 and 125**; 68% between 95 and 115; 99.7% between 75 and 135.
- Mean IOP 30, SD 1 → **97.5% have IOP > 28** (only the lower 2.5% tail is excluded).
- Mean Hb 10.6, SD 2 → the **5% cut-off is at mean − 1.645 SD ≈ 7.3 g/dL** (not mean − 2 SD, which is the 2.5% point). **1.645 SD = 5% one-tailed; 1.96 SD = 5% two-tailed.**
- **If every value in a sample is identical, the standard deviation and the variance are ZERO** — there is no variability to measure.
- **Variance = SD²**; the sum of all deviation scores is always zero, which is why deviations are squared.
- **Degrees of freedom for a chi-square contingency table = (rows − 1) × (columns − 1)**.
- **Probability**: independent events multiply (**0.16 × 0.16 = 0.0256** for both children affected); "either one" uses **p(A) + p(B) − p(A and B)**.
- **A p-value of 0.03 means: if the null hypothesis were true, there is a 3% chance of obtaining this result or a more extreme one.** It is **NOT** the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Read whether the question asks for a **two-tailed range** (95% between) or a **one-tailed cut-off** (5% below / 97.5% above). That distinction decides between the 2-SD and 1.645-SD answers.

**REMEMBER:** *Convert the percentage into a tail before counting SDs.*

## 69. Attributable Risk vs Population Attributable Risk

**PEARL:** **Attributable risk of 90% means 90% of cases AMONG THE EXPOSED would be prevented** by removing the exposure. **Population attributable risk** is the corresponding figure **for the whole population**, and depends on how prevalent the exposure is.

**UNDERSTAND:** AR answers a question about **exposed individuals** — it is essentially "what proportion of a smoker's lung cancer risk is due to smoking?" PAR answers a question about the **community** — and must be smaller than AR unless everyone is exposed, because non-smokers' cancers cannot be prevented by stopping smoking.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Attributable risk (risk difference) = (incidence in exposed − incidence in unexposed)**; **attributable risk PERCENT = that difference ÷ incidence in exposed × 100**.
- **Population attributable risk = (incidence in total population − incidence in unexposed)**; **PAR% = PAR ÷ incidence in total population × 100**. PAR depends on **prevalence of exposure** — the basis of prioritising public health action.
- **Relative risk** = incidence in exposed ÷ incidence in unexposed — measures **strength of association** (aetiological importance). **Attributable risk measures public health impact.**
- Relative risk comes from **cohort** studies; **odds ratio** substitutes for it in **case-control** studies (valid when the disease is rare).
- **Number needed to treat = 1 ÷ absolute risk reduction.**
- Attributable risk is the number that justifies a preventive programme; relative risk is the number that establishes causation.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The four options permute **90/10** and **smokers/population**. Anchor: **AR applies to the exposed group.**

**REMEMBER:** *Attributable risk speaks about the exposed; population attributable risk speaks about everyone.*

## 70. Water — Hardness, Disinfection and Sanitation Numbers

**PEARL:** **Water hardness: soft < 50 mg/L; moderately hard 50–150; HARD 150–300; very hard > 300 mg/L (as CaCO₃).** Drinking water should be **moderately hard**; soften when hardness exceeds **3 mEq/L (150 mg/L)**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Hardness is the **soap-destroying power of water** — dissolved **calcium and magnesium** precipitate soap as scum. **Temporary (carbonate) hardness** comes from **bicarbonates and is removed by boiling** (which drives off CO₂ and precipitates the carbonate); **permanent hardness** comes from **sulphates, chlorides and nitrates** and requires chemical methods.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Removal: **boiling, addition of lime (Clark's process), base exchange/zeolite, and ion-exchange resins**. **1 mEq/L = 50 mg/L CaCO₃.**
- **Horrock's apparatus** estimates the **chlorine demand of water** for well disinfection: 6 white cups + 1 black cup, spoons holding **2 g** of bleaching powder each. **Dose = (n × 2) grams of bleaching powder per 455 litres**, where **n is the number of the FIRST cup showing a distinct blue colour** with starch-iodide.
- **Free residual chlorine of 0.5 mg/L after 1 hour contact** is the standard; minimum contact time **1 hour**; **break-point chlorination** satisfies the chlorine demand first.
- **RCA-type latrine (water-seal, hand-flush)**: sited **more than 15 m from any water source**, with an **effective water seal of 2–2.5 cm**, which blocks flies, odour and gases. This design was central to controlling **hookworm and soil-transmitted helminths** from open defecation.
- **Aedes aegypti (house) index must be kept < 1%** to achieve zero risk of yellow fever transmission under the International Health Regulations.
- **McArdle's maximum allowable sweat rate:** predicted 4-hour sweat rate — **comfort 1–3 L, just tolerable 3–4.5 L, INTOLERABLE > 4.5 L per 4 hours** in acclimatised healthy young men.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** These are pure number-recall items. The Horrock's formula (**first blue cup × 2 g**) is the one that requires an actual calculation.

**REMEMBER:** *Hard water is 150 to 300; the first blue cup times two gives the grams.*

## 72. Iron, Folic Acid and Iodine — Programme Doses

**PEARL:** Under **Anaemia Mukt Bharat**, **women of reproductive age (and adolescents) take 60 mg elemental iron + 500 µg folic acid WEEKLY**; **pregnant and lactating women take the same dose DAILY** for 180 + 180 days.

**UNDERSTAND:** The weekly schedule for non-pregnant women is deliberate: **intestinal mucosal cells become refractory to iron for 3–5 days after a dose (the "mucosal block")**, so weekly dosing achieves comparable haemoglobin response with **fewer side effects and better compliance**. Pregnancy's much higher requirement justifies daily dosing despite the side effects.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Tablet colours and doses: **children 6–59 months — 1 mL IFA syrup (20 mg iron + 100 µg folic acid) BIWEEKLY**; **children 5–9 years — PINK tablet (45 mg + 400 µg) weekly**; **adolescents 10–19 years — BLUE tablet (60 mg + 500 µg) weekly**; **women of reproductive age — RED tablet (60 mg + 500 µg) weekly**; **pregnant/lactating — RED tablet daily**.

- **Weight gain in pregnancy (ICMR-NIN 2024, Indian BMI cut-offs): normal BMI 18.5–23 → 10–12 kg; overweight (23–27.5) or obese (> 27.5) → 5–9 kg.**
- **Mid-Day Meal (School Lunch Programme, since 1961)** must supply at least **one-third of the daily energy** and **half of the daily protein** requirement — a **supplement, not a substitute** for the home diet, using locally available foods with a varied menu.

## 73. Demography — Dependency Ratio and Couple Protection Rate

**PEARL:** **Dependency ratio = (population < 15 y + population > 65 y) ÷ population 15–64 y × 100.** With 30% under 15 and 10% over 65, the working group is 60%, so the ratio is **40/60 = 66.6%**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The denominator is **only the economically productive age band**, not the total population — a distinction that trips up most candidates. The ratio expresses **how many dependants each 100 workers must support**, so a growing figure signals either a youthful, high-fertility population or an ageing one.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Couple protection rate (CPR)** — the percentage of eligible couples **effectively protected** by an approved family planning method. **CPR must exceed 60% to achieve NRR = 1**, i.e. replacement-level fertility (roughly restricting all third and higher-order births).
- **Net reproduction rate (NRR) = 1** means each woman is replaced by exactly one daughter surviving to reproductive age — the demographic goal.
- **Pearl index = number of accidental pregnancies per 100 woman-years of exposure**; the denominator deducts **10 months per full-term delivery and 4 months per abortion**.
- **Expected ANC registrations** at a subcentre: population **5000** (plains) × CBR/1000 = expected births, **add 10% for pregnancy wastage**, then **half of that** is the realistic registration figure (CBR 20 → 100 → 110 → ~55, i.e. about 60).
- **ASHA newborn home visits under the SSBSK schedule: up to day 42** — **6 visits after institutional delivery (days 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42)** and **7 after home delivery (adding day 1)**; **more visits for at-risk newborns**. ASHA: a woman **25–45 years**, resident of the village, educated to about class 10.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The dependency ratio calculation fails if you divide by 100 instead of by the working-age population. Write the three age bands out first.

**REMEMBER:** *Divide the dependants by the workers, not by everyone.*

## 74. Drug Inventory — The Reorder Formula

**PEARL:** **Reorder factor (Rf) = Lead Time + Procurement Period.** With a lead time of 1 month and a monthly procurement cycle, **Rf = 2**, and the quantity ordered must also allow for **buffer/safety stock**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Two separate intervals must be covered by any order: the **time until the next order is placed** (procurement period) and the **time that order takes to arrive** (lead time). Stock must last through both, or a **stock-out** occurs before delivery. Adding them gives the multiplier applied to average monthly consumption.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Quantity to order = average consumption × reorder factor + safety stock − stock in hand.**
- Worked example: 40 patients × 1 amlodipine + 10 × 1 lisinopril = **50 tablets/day = 1500/month**; with no existing safety stock, order slightly more — **1600 tablets, Rf = 2**.
- **Lead time** = interval from placing the order to receiving the goods; **procurement period** = interval until the next order is placed; **buffer stock** covers unexpected demand and delivery delay.
- **ABC analysis** classifies items by **annual expenditure (A = 70% of cost, 10% of items)**; **VED analysis** by **criticality (Vital, Essential, Desirable)**. Combining them prioritises **AV items** for the closest control.
- Related management tools: **FSN (fast/slow/non-moving)**, **HML (high/medium/low unit cost)**, and **two-bin/expiry-date (FEFO) issue systems**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Compute **average monthly consumption first**, then the reorder factor. The safety-stock adjustment explains why the answer is slightly above the arithmetic figure.

**REMEMBER:** *Cover the wait for the order and the wait until the next order.*

## 75. Intelligence Quotient Classifications

**PEARL:** **Levine and Marks (1928): idiot 0–24, imbecile 25–49, MORON 50–74, borderline 75–84, dull 85–94, average 95–104.** **Wechsler: average = 91–110.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The two scales are historically distinct instruments, so the same IQ falls into differently-named bands. The exam exploits this by naming the classification in the stem — **read which scale is being asked about before choosing**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Historical IQ = (mental age ÷ chronological age) × 100**; modern tests use deviation IQ with **mean 100 and SD 15**.
- **Modern (ICD/DSM) classification of intellectual disability: mild 50–69, moderate 35–49, severe 20–34, profound < 20**; **above 70 is normal**.
- The obsolete terms (idiot, imbecile, moron) survive only in exam questions — clinically, **"intellectual disability"** with a severity grade based on **adaptive functioning**, not IQ alone, is used.
- Common tests: **Wechsler (WAIS/WISC), Stanford–Binet, Binet–Simon (the first), Raven's progressive matrices (culture-fair), Seguin form board and Bhatia's battery (used in India)**.
- **Down syndrome** typically gives IQ in the **mild-to-moderate** range (~50 average).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** IQ 56 under **Levine and Marks** = **moron**; the same value under the modern system = **mild intellectual disability**. Both appear as questions.

**REMEMBER:** *The scale named in the question decides the label.*

## 76. Ridley–Jopling Classification of Leprosy

**PEARL:** Ridley–Jopling classifies leprosy on **four parameters: CLINICAL, BACTERIOLOGICAL, IMMUNOLOGICAL and HISTOLOGICAL** — into the spectrum **TT – BT – BB – BL – LL**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The spectrum is fundamentally a **map of cell-mediated immunity**. At the tuberculoid pole, strong CMI produces **few, well-defined, bacillus-poor lesions with well-formed granulomas and a positive lepromin test**; at the lepromatous pole, absent CMI allows **innumerable, symmetrical, bacillus-rich lesions with foamy macrophages and a negative lepromin test**. Every one of the four parameters is simply reading that same immune gradient in a different way.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **TT**: 1–3 asymmetrical, well-defined, anaesthetic hypopigmented patches with **raised sloping edges ("saucer right side up")**; AFB negative; lepromin **strongly positive**.
- **BT**: up to ~10 lesions, satellite lesions and pseudopodia.
- **BB (mid-borderline)**: **"Swiss cheese" / inverted-saucer / punched-out** annular lesions, 10–30 in number; the **most immunologically unstable** — highest risk of **type 1 lepra reaction**.
- **BL and LL**: numerous, symmetrical macules, papules, plaques and nodules; **leonine facies, madarosis, saddle nose, glove-and-stocking anaesthesia**; AFB heavily positive; lepromin **negative**. **Type 2 reaction (ENL)** occurs at this end.
- **WHO operational classification** (for field use) is far simpler: **paucibacillary ≤ 5 lesions, multibacillary > 5** — used to decide MDT regimen without histology.
- **Nerve thickening** is commonest in the **ulnar, common peroneal, great auricular, posterior tibial and supraorbital** nerves.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The four-parameter list is asked verbatim. The distractors substitute **epidemiological, therapeutic or operational** for **histological**.

**REMEMBER:** *Clinical, bacteriological, immunological, histological — four ways of measuring the same immunity.*

## 77. Yellow Fever Vaccine and International Travel

**PEARL:** The **yellow fever vaccine becomes valid 10 DAYS after administration** — travel to an endemic area is permitted from day 10, when protective antibody has developed.

**UNDERSTAND:** The **10-day interval is set by the immune response**, not by the vaccine itself. Under the International Health Regulations the certificate becomes valid on day 10 precisely because that is when the majority of recipients have seroconverted; before then the traveller is unprotected and could carry infection.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The vaccine is the **live attenuated 17D strain**, given **subcutaneously**; a **single dose now confers lifelong protection** (the old 10-year revalidation was dropped in 2016).
- **Unvaccinated travellers arriving from an endemic area may be quarantined for 6 days** — the maximum incubation period of yellow fever.
- Contraindications: **infants < 9 months, pregnancy, egg allergy, thymus disorders, symptomatic HIV and immunosuppression** (it is a live vaccine).
- Yellow fever is transmitted by **Aedes aegypti**; **India has never had a case despite having the vector**, which is why the **Aedes index must be kept < 1%** and quarantine regulations are enforced at ports.
- The vaccination certificate is the **only one still required under the IHR** for international travel (plus meningococcal and polio requirements for the Hajj/specified countries).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Pure number recall — **10 days for validity, 6 days for quarantine**. The two figures are often swapped in the options.

**REMEMBER:** *Ten days to be protected, six days to be quarantined.*
