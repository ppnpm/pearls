## 1. Oculomotor Nerve — Nucleus, Palsy and the Pupil Rule

**PEARL:** The **OCULOMOTOR (III) NUCLEUS lies in the midbrain at the level of the SUPERIOR COLLICULUS; the TROCHLEAR (IV) nucleus at the INFERIOR COLLICULUS; and the ABDUCENS (VI) nucleus in the pons at the FACIAL COLLICULUS.** A complete third nerve palsy gives an eye that is **"DOWN AND OUT" with PTOSIS, a DILATED unreactive PUPIL and loss of accommodation.** **DIABETIC (ischaemic) third nerve palsy is PUPIL-SPARING and presents with PTOSIS and diplopia; a COMPRESSIVE lesion (POSTERIOR COMMUNICATING ARTERY ANEURYSM, uncal herniation) involves the PUPIL EARLY.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The pupil rule is pure anatomy. The **PARASYMPATHETIC pupillomotor fibres run SUPERFICIALLY (peripherally) in the nerve, supplied by the pial vessels**, while the **central somatic fibres depend on the vasa nervorum**. So **microvascular ischaemia (diabetes, hypertension) infarcts the CORE and spares the surface — pupil spared**; whereas **anything pressing from outside crushes the surface first — pupil blown.** A painful third nerve palsy with a dilated pupil is an aneurysm until imaging proves otherwise.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Nuclear anatomy details: a SINGLE CENTRAL CAUDAL NUCLEUS supplies the levator palpebrae of BOTH eyes (so a nuclear lesion gives BILATERAL ptosis); the SUPERIOR RECTUS subnucleus projects to the CONTRALATERAL eye (crossed); all other subnuclei are ipsilateral. The EDINGER–WESTPHAL nucleus supplies parasympathetics to the sphincter pupillae and ciliary muscle.**
- **The eye sits "down and out" because of the UNOPPOSED action of the LATERAL RECTUS (VI) and SUPERIOR OBLIQUE (IV).**
- **WEBER SYNDROME — ipsilateral third nerve palsy with contralateral hemiplegia (midbrain, cerebral peduncle). BENEDIKT — third nerve palsy with contralateral tremor/red nucleus. NOTHNAGEL — third nerve palsy with cerebellar ataxia. PARINAUD — dorsal midbrain, with upgaze palsy and LIGHT–NEAR DISSOCIATION (pinealoma).**
- **The fourth nerve is the ONLY cranial nerve to DECUSSATE and to exit DORSALLY, and it is the LONGEST and thinnest intracranial course — hence its vulnerability in head trauma. IV palsy causes a HEAD TILT AWAY from the affected side.**
- **The sixth nerve has the LONGEST INTRACRANIAL COURSE and is the classic FALSE LOCALISING SIGN in raised intracranial pressure.**
- **Diabetic cranial mononeuropathy recovers spontaneously over 3 months; failure to recover demands imaging.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Nuclear level questions and the pupil rule are the two things asked.

**REMEMBER:** *Parasympathetics ride on the outside — pressure blows the pupil, ischaemia spares it.*

## 2. Diabetic Retinopathy

**PEARL:** **NEOVASCULARISATION — at the disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE) — DEFINES PROLIFERATIVE diabetic retinopathy** and is what separates it from any grade of NPDR. **NPDR shows microaneurysms, dot-and-blot haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, venous beading and IRMA — but NO new vessels.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Chronic hyperglycaemia damages pericytes and the blood–retinal barrier, producing **capillary non-perfusion and retinal ISCHAEMIA**; the ischaemic retina then secretes **VEGF**, which drives new vessels. Those vessels are **fragile and grow on a scaffold of fibrous tissue**, which is precisely why the complications are **VITREOUS HAEMORRHAGE and TRACTIONAL RETINAL DETACHMENT** — and why the treatment (**PAN-RETINAL PHOTOCOAGULATION**) works by **destroying ischaemic peripheral retina to remove the VEGF stimulus**, sacrificing peripheral field to save central vision.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The "4-2-1 RULE" defines SEVERE NPDR (any one of): severe haemorrhages/microaneurysms in ALL 4 quadrants, VENOUS BEADING in 2 or more quadrants, or IRMA in 1 or more quadrant. Severe NPDR carries a high risk of progressing to PDR within a year.**
- **DIABETIC MACULAR OEDEMA is the commonest cause of visual loss in diabetes and can occur at ANY stage — it is treated with INTRAVITREAL ANTI-VEGF (ranibizumab, bevacizumab, aflibercept), not primarily with laser.**
- **PDR treatment: PAN-RETINAL PHOTOCOAGULATION ± anti-VEGF; VITRECTOMY for non-clearing vitreous haemorrhage or tractional detachment threatening the macula.**
- **Screening: TYPE 1 — first eye examination 5 YEARS after diagnosis; TYPE 2 — AT DIAGNOSIS (retinopathy may already be present); then annually. In PREGNANCY, examine each trimester — retinopathy can progress rapidly, especially with abrupt tightening of control.**
- **Risk factors: DURATION of diabetes (the strongest), poor glycaemic control, hypertension, nephropathy, dyslipidaemia, anaemia and pregnancy.** **PDR is commonest in TYPE 1 diabetes after 15–20 years.**
- **Cotton-wool spots are nerve fibre layer INFARCTS (also seen in hypertension, HIV, SLE, anaemia); hard exudates are LIPID deposits at the margin of oedema.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** New vessels on FFA → **proliferative diabetic retinopathy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ischaemia makes VEGF, VEGF makes vessels — burn the ischaemic retina and the vessels regress.*

## 3. Retinal Layers and Phototransduction

**PEARL:** The retina has **10 LAYERS**, from inner (vitreous side) outward: **INTERNAL LIMITING MEMBRANE → nerve fibre layer → ganglion cell layer → INNER PLEXIFORM layer (the 4th) → inner nuclear layer → OUTER PLEXIFORM layer → outer nuclear layer → external limiting membrane → photoreceptors → RETINAL PIGMENT EPITHELIUM.** **LIGHT causes HYPERPOLARISATION of photoreceptors with DECREASED glutamate release.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Photoreceptors are unique in being **DEPOLARISED IN THE DARK**: cGMP holds sodium channels open (the "dark current") and glutamate pours out continuously. **Light converts 11-cis-retinal to all-trans-retinal → activates TRANSDUCIN (a G protein) → phosphodiesterase → cGMP falls → sodium channels CLOSE → hyperpolarisation → less glutamate.** So the visual signal is a **REDUCTION in a tonic signal**, not a burst — an inverted logic that is a favourite exam item.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The INNER plexiform layer is where bipolar cells synapse with ganglion cells (with amacrine cells); the OUTER plexiform layer is where photoreceptors synapse with bipolar and horizontal cells. Fluid collecting in the outer plexiform (HENLE'S) layer produces CYSTOID MACULAR OEDEMA.**
- **The RPE is metabolically critical: it phagocytoses shed photoreceptor discs, recycles retinal (the visual cycle), absorbs stray light, and forms the OUTER BLOOD–RETINAL BARRIER. Its dysfunction underlies ARMD and retinitis pigmentosa.**
- **The potential space between the RPE and the neurosensory retina is the embryological remnant of the optic vesicle cavity — which is why RETINAL DETACHMENT separates exactly there.**
- **The FOVEA contains only CONES with a 1:1 cone-to-ganglion-cell ratio (highest acuity) and NO inner layers (light reaches photoreceptors directly); the periphery is rod-dominated (scotopic vision).**
- **Retinal blood supply is DUAL: the CENTRAL RETINAL ARTERY supplies the inner two-thirds; the CHORIOCAPILLARIS supplies the OUTER third including the photoreceptors — which is why choroidal disease damages photoreceptors and why the fovea survives on choroidal supply in central retinal artery occlusion (the CHERRY-RED SPOT with a preserved cilioretinal artery).**
- **Rhodopsin (rods) needs VITAMIN A — deficiency causes NIGHT BLINDNESS first.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Layer identification on histology; and "on exposure to light" → **hyperpolarisation with decreased neurotransmitter release**.

**REMEMBER:** *Dark means depolarised and glutamate flowing — light switches it off.*

## 4. Presbyopia and Bifocals

**PEARL:** **BIFOCALS are indicated when BOTH distance and near vision need different corrections — PRESBYOPIA (the definitive indication), APHAKIA, PAEDIATRIC PSEUDOPHAKIA, and non-refractive ACCOMMODATIVE ESOTROPIA.** They are **NOT used for simple HYPERMETROPIA or MYOPIA**, which need a single uniform power. Rough presbyopic add by age in an emmetrope: **+1.0 to +1.25 D at 45; +1.5 to +1.75 D at 50; +2.0 to +2.25 D at 55; +2.5 to +3.0 D at 60.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Presbyopia is **loss of ACCOMMODATION from progressive sclerosis of the lens and reduced elasticity of the capsule** — the ciliary muscle still works, but the lens cannot change shape. Since **distance vision is unaffected**, only the near correction changes with age, and the add grows in step with the falling amplitude of accommodation. **Prescribe the WEAKEST plus lens that gives clear near vision** — over-correction shortens the working distance and causes asthenopia.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Always correct the DISTANCE refractive error FIRST, then ADD the near correction to it.**
- **APHAKIA (no lens): hypermetropia of about +10 D, LOSS OF ACCOMMODATION, a DEEP anterior chamber with IRIDODONESIS, a jet-black pupil and absent Purkinje images 3 and 4. Spectacle correction causes ~30% magnification, ring scotoma and "JACK-IN-THE-BOX" phenomenon — which is why an INTRAOCULAR LENS is preferred, and contact lenses next.**
- **An IOL implanted uneventfully is NEVER routinely replaced. IOL EXCHANGE is done only for a wrong-power lens, dislocation/decentration, or persistent complications. AFTER-CATARACT (posterior capsular opacification) is treated by Nd:YAG LASER CAPSULOTOMY, not by lens exchange.**
- **Types of presbyopic correction: unifocal reading glasses, BIFOCAL (executive, D-segment, round-segment; the main problem is IMAGE JUMP at the segment line), TRIFOCAL, PROGRESSIVE (no line, but with peripheral distortion), and multifocal IOLs.**
- **Premature presbyopia suggests uncorrected hypermetropia, general debility, or a systemic cause (diabetes) — and DIABETES can also cause transient index myopia with fluctuating vision.**
- **IOL POWER CALCULATION: SRK formula P = A − 2.5L − 0.9K (A = lens constant, L = axial length by A-scan, K = keratometry). SRK-II and SRK/T refine it; HOFFER Q for short eyes; and after CORNEAL REFRACTIVE SURGERY (LASIK), standard formulas fail — use HAIGIS-L, the double-K or Masket methods, because keratometry no longer reflects true corneal power.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Bifocals not used in" → **hypermetropia**; age-based presbyopic power is straight recall.

**REMEMBER:** *Bifocals are for eyes that need two different powers — hypermetropia only needs one.*

## 5. Sturge–Weber Syndrome and the Phakomatoses

**PEARL:** A **PORT-WINE STAIN (naevus flammeus) in the trigeminal (especially V1) distribution + LEPTOMENINGEAL ANGIOMA + SEIZURES = STURGE–WEBER SYNDROME**, and its **COMMONEST OCULAR MANIFESTATION IS GLAUCOMA** (in ~30–70%, ipsilateral to the naevus). Presenting in infancy it causes **BUPHTHALMOS.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The glaucoma has **two mechanisms depending on age**: in infants, **maldevelopment of the anterior chamber angle**; in older children and adults, **RAISED EPISCLERAL VENOUS PRESSURE from the episcleral haemangioma**, which obstructs aqueous outflow at the level of the collector channels. This second mechanism explains the classic operative hazard — **CHOROIDAL EFFUSION AND EXPULSIVE HAEMORRHAGE during filtration surgery**, because the congested choroid decompresses suddenly.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Other ocular features: DIFFUSE CHOROIDAL HAEMANGIOMA ("TOMATO-KETCHUP FUNDUS"), serous retinal detachment, iris heterochromia, episcleral and conjunctival vascular lesions, and HOMONYMOUS HEMIANOPIA from the occipital leptomeningeal angioma.**
- **Sturge–Weber is caused by a somatic mosaic GNAQ mutation and — unlike most phakomatoses — is NOT INHERITED. Intracranial calcification gives the classic "TRAM-TRACK" appearance on skull X-ray/CT.**
- **The other phakomatoses and their eyes: NEUROFIBROMATOSIS 1 — LISCH NODULES (iris hamartomas), optic nerve glioma, café-au-lait spots, axillary freckling. NF2 — bilateral vestibular schwannomas and JUVENILE POSTERIOR SUBCAPSULAR CATARACT. TUBEROUS SCLEROSIS — retinal ASTROCYTIC HAMARTOMA ("mulberry" lesion), adenoma sebaceum, ash-leaf macules, shagreen patch, seizures. VON HIPPEL–LINDAU — RETINAL CAPILLARY HAEMANGIOBLASTOMA with dilated feeder vessels, plus cerebellar haemangioblastoma, renal cell carcinoma and phaeochromocytoma. ATAXIA-TELANGIECTASIA — conjunctival telangiectasia and oculomotor apraxia.**
- **Any child with a facial port-wine stain involving the EYELIDS needs LIFELONG IOP measurement and fundus examination.**
- **Treatment of the glaucoma is often difficult — medical therapy first, then goniotomy/trabeculotomy in infants or trabeculectomy with precautions in adults; the skin lesion is treated with pulsed-dye laser.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Port-wine stain + seizures → **glaucoma** is the commonest ocular finding.

**REMEMBER:** *The angioma raises episcleral venous pressure — and the eye pays with glaucoma.*

## 6. Neuromyelitis Optica

**PEARL:** **SEVERE optic neuritis (often BILATERAL, with profound visual loss) plus TRANSVERSE MYELITIS, relapsing and steroid-responsive, = NEUROMYELITIS OPTICA (DEVIC'S DISEASE)** — an autoimmune ASTROCYTOPATHY with **antibodies against AQUAPORIN-4 (AQP4-IgG)**, the water channel on astrocyte foot processes.

**UNDERSTAND:** AQP4 is concentrated in the **optic nerves, spinal cord, area postrema and periependymal regions** — which is exactly why the disease targets those sites and why it produces a **LONGITUDINALLY EXTENSIVE TRANSVERSE MYELITIS spanning ≥3 vertebral segments**, unlike the short segmental lesions of multiple sclerosis. It is an attack on **astrocytes, not myelin** — demyelination is secondary — which is why it is more destructive and leaves worse residual deficits.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **NMO vs MS is a guaranteed comparison: NMO — bilateral/severe optic neuritis, LONGITUDINALLY EXTENSIVE cord lesions, AREA POSTREMA syndrome (intractable HICCUPS, nausea and vomiting — highly characteristic), a usually NORMAL BRAIN MRI early, CSF OLIGOCLONAL BANDS USUALLY ABSENT with a neutrophilic pleocytosis, and AQP4-IgG positive. MS — unilateral milder optic neuritis, short cord lesions, PERIVENTRICULAR ovoid lesions (DAWSON'S FINGERS), oligoclonal bands PRESENT.**
- ***MS DISEASE-MODIFYING DRUGS (interferon-beta, natalizumab, fingolimod) WORSEN NMO*** — so the distinction is not academic. **Acute attacks: high-dose IV methylprednisolone, then PLASMA EXCHANGE if refractory. Maintenance: RITUXIMAB, azathioprine, mycophenolate, or the newer targeted agents ECULIZUMAB (anti-C5), SATRALIZUMAB (anti-IL-6R) and INEBILIZUMAB (anti-CD19).**
- **MOG-antibody disease is the third entity — often bilateral optic neuritis with disc swelling, more steroid-responsive, better recovery, commoner in children.**
- **Typical MS optic neuritis: a young woman with SUBACUTE unilateral visual loss, PAIN ON EYE MOVEMENT, dyschromatopsia (red desaturation), a central scotoma and a RELATIVE AFFERENT PUPILLARY DEFECT; "THE PATIENT SEES NOTHING AND THE DOCTOR SEES NOTHING" in retrobulbar neuritis (two-thirds have a normal disc). UHTHOFF'S PHENOMENON is worsening with heat/exercise.**
- **The ONTT showed that IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery but does not change final acuity, and that ORAL PREDNISOLONE ALONE IS CONTRAINDICATED (it increases recurrence).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Optic neuritis + transverse myelitis → **neuromyelitis optica**.

**REMEMBER:** *Aquaporin-4 lives in the optic nerve and cord — attack it and both go together.*

## 7. The Stenopaeic Slit

**PEARL:** A **STENOPAEIC SLIT** (a narrow slit aperture in an opaque disc) is used to **DETERMINE THE AXIS IN ASTIGMATISM, to perform FINCHAM'S TEST (distinguishing the coloured halos of glaucomatous corneal oedema from those of immature cataract), and to select the OPTIMAL SITE FOR OPTICAL IRIDECTOMY** in central corneal opacity. **It has NO role in CORNEAL TATTOOING.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The slit works by **restricting light to a single meridian** — so rotating it isolates each axis in turn, allowing the astigmatic axis to be found subjectively. In **FINCHAM'S TEST**, halos caused by **LENTICULAR (cataract) water clefts are BROKEN UP into segments** by the slit, whereas halos caused by **CORNEAL oedema remain INTACT** — because the diffracting structures are at different depths relative to the aperture.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **HALOES around lights occur in ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA (corneal epithelial oedema), immature cataract, mucopurulent conjunctivitis (mucus on the cornea) and after refractive surgery — Fincham's test separates the first two.**
- **OPTICAL IRIDECTOMY is performed for a central corneal (leucomatous) opacity with clear peripheral cornea, creating a clear "window" through which light can enter — usually inferonasally.**
- **CORNEAL TATTOOING is a cosmetic procedure for a disfiguring corneal scar in an eye with NO USEFUL VISION.**
- **The PINHOLE test is the everyday equivalent: improvement of vision through a 1 mm pinhole indicates a REFRACTIVE cause; no improvement suggests organic disease (macular or optic nerve). It works by eliminating peripheral rays and increasing depth of focus — but it will NOT improve vision beyond about 6/9 because of diffraction.**
- **Other bedside optical tests: RETINOSCOPY (objective refraction), the DUOCHROME (red–green) test using chromatic aberration to refine the spherical correction, and the JACKSON CROSS CYLINDER to refine astigmatism.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Stenopaeic slit used for all EXCEPT" → **corneal tattooing**.

**REMEMBER:** *A slit isolates one meridian — so it finds the astigmatic axis and breaks up lenticular haloes.*

## 8. Drusen and Age-Related Macular Degeneration

**PEARL:** **DRUSEN are extracellular eosinophilic deposits BETWEEN THE RPE AND BRUCH'S MEMBRANE**, composed of lipids, complement factors and cellular debris — the hallmark of **DRY (non-exudative, ~90%) AGE-RELATED MACULAR DEGENERATION.** **WET (exudative) ARMD is defined by CHOROIDAL NEOVASCULARISATION.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Drusen represent **failure of RPE waste disposal**: the RPE phagocytoses shed photoreceptor discs, and with age the undigested residue accumulates beneath it, thickening Bruch's membrane. This impairs **exchange between the choriocapillaris and photoreceptors**, causing progressive atrophy — and the resulting **hypoxia plus complement activation drives VEGF**, which lets new choroidal vessels break through Bruch's membrane into the subretinal space. **Dry ARMD is a disposal problem; wet ARMD is its ischaemic consequence.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Clinically: painless, GRADUAL loss of CENTRAL vision with METAMORPHOPSIA (distortion) and a central scotoma; PERIPHERAL VISION AND NAVIGATION ARE PRESERVED, so patients never go completely blind. SUDDEN distortion or a sudden drop in vision suggests conversion to WET ARMD and is an emergency.**
- **The AMSLER GRID is the self-monitoring tool; OCT is the investigation of choice for fluid; FFA/OCT-angiography demonstrates the neovascular membrane.**
- **TREATMENT: WET ARMD — INTRAVITREAL ANTI-VEGF (ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab) is the mainstay and can improve vision. DRY ARMD — no specific treatment; the AREDS/AREDS2 formulation (vitamins C and E, ZINC, copper, LUTEIN and ZEAXANTHIN — beta-carotene REPLACED because it increases lung cancer risk in smokers) slows progression in INTERMEDIATE disease.**
- **Risk factors: AGE (the strongest), SMOKING (the strongest modifiable factor), family history and CFH complement gene polymorphisms, hypertension, sunlight exposure and female sex.**
- **ARMD is the leading cause of irreversible blindness in the ELDERLY in developed countries; in India, CATARACT remains the leading cause of blindness overall, and GLAUCOMA the leading cause of irreversible blindness.**
- **Distinguish "hard exudates" (intraretinal lipid, sharply defined, in diabetes) from DRUSEN (sub-RPE, yellowish, at the macula in ARMD) — a classic image confusion.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Histology showing deposits between RPE and Bruch's membrane → **drusen**.

**REMEMBER:** *Drusen are the RPE's rubbish — pile it up and the choroid grows through.*

## 9. Cystoid Macular Oedema

**PEARL:** A **"FLOWER-PETAL" (petalloid) pattern of late hyperfluorescence on fundus fluorescein angiography = CYSTOID MACULAR OEDEMA** — fluid accumulating in cystoid spaces of the **OUTER PLEXIFORM (HENLE'S) LAYER and inner nuclear layer** after a breakdown of the blood–retinal barrier. On OCT there is **loss of the foveal depression with intraretinal cystoid spaces.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The petal pattern is anatomy made visible: **Henle's fibres radiate OBLIQUELY AND RADIALLY from the fovea**, so fluid pooling between them forms **radially arranged cystoid spaces** that fill with dye in a flower-petal arrangement. This is unique to the fovea — elsewhere the oedema is diffuse.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **IRVINE–GASS SYNDROME is CME after CATARACT SURGERY (typically 6–10 weeks postoperatively) — the commonest cause; risk is higher with complicated surgery, capsule rupture and vitreous loss. Treat with topical NSAIDs and steroids.**
- **Other causes: DIABETIC retinopathy, retinal VEIN OCCLUSION, UVEITIS, RETINITIS PIGMENTOSA, epiretinal membrane, and DRUGS — TOPICAL PROSTAGLANDIN ANALOGUES (especially in aphakia/pseudophakia and diabetics), NIACIN, epinephrine and fingolimod.**
- **Symptoms: painless blurring, METAMORPHOPSIA and MICROPSIA; fundoscopy shows a HONEYCOMB appearance at the macula with loss of the foveal reflex.**
- **Compare the other classic angiographic patterns: "SMOKE-STACK" or "INKBLOT" leak → CENTRAL SEROUS CHORIORETINOPATHY (a young stressed man, type-A personality, steroid use — a serous neurosensory detachment that usually resolves spontaneously). "DOUBLE CIRCULATION" → CHOROIDAL MELANOMA. "MACULAR STAR" of hard exudates → hypertension, neuroretinitis (CAT-SCRATCH disease).**
- **Treatment: treat the cause; topical NSAIDs and steroids, then intravitreal steroids or anti-VEGF, and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide) which are especially useful in retinitis pigmentosa-related CME.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Petalloid FFA pattern → **cystoid macular oedema**.

**REMEMBER:** *Henle's fibres radiate like petals — so foveal fluid pools into a flower.*

## 10. Chloroquine and Hydroxychloroquine Retinopathy

**PEARL:** A patient on **long-term chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine for rheumatoid arthritis or SLE with declining vision and a "BULL'S-EYE MACULOPATHY"** (a ring of RPE depigmentation sparing the very centre) has **ANTIMALARIAL RETINAL TOXICITY.** Safe daily doses: **CHLOROQUINE ≤2.3 mg/kg (older texts 6 mg/kg) and HYDROXYCHLOROQUINE ≤5 mg/kg of REAL body weight** — hydroxychloroquine is much safer.

**UNDERSTAND:** These drugs **bind MELANIN in the RPE and accumulate over years**, which is why toxicity is a function of **CUMULATIVE DOSE AND DURATION (risk rises steeply after 5 years or >1000 g total)** rather than of a single dose. Because the damage begins in the **PARAFOVEAL photoreceptors and RPE while sparing the very centre**, patients keep good central acuity until late — hence **PARACENTRAL SCOTOMAS long before acuity falls**, and hence the need for objective screening rather than symptoms.

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***THE RETINOPATHY IS IRREVERSIBLE AND MAY PROGRESS EVEN AFTER STOPPING THE DRUG*** — which is the entire justification for screening. Early corneal changes (VERTICILLATA/whorl keratopathy), night-vision difficulty, blurring and diplopia ARE reversible.
- **SCREENING: a BASELINE examination within the first year, then ANNUAL screening after 5 YEARS of use (earlier with risk factors). The sensitive modern tests are SD-OCT (showing parafoveal outer retinal thinning — the "FLYING SAUCER" sign), automated VISUAL FIELDS (10-2), MULTIFOCAL ERG and fundus autofluorescence. Fundus changes (bull's-eye) are a LATE finding.**
- **Risk factors: daily dose above the threshold, duration >5 years, RENAL IMPAIRMENT, concurrent TAMOXIFEN, and pre-existing macular disease.**
- **In ASIAN patients the damage is often PERICENTRAL rather than parafoveal — so wider field testing (24-2) is advised.**
- **The bull's-eye maculopathy differential: chloroquine toxicity, STARGARDT DISEASE, cone dystrophy, ARMD and Batten disease.**
- **Other drug-induced ocular toxicities worth carrying: ETHAMBUTOL — optic neuritis with RED-GREEN dyschromatopsia (dose-related, reversible). AMIODARONE — verticillata and optic neuropathy. STEROIDS — POSTERIOR SUBCAPSULAR CATARACT and steroid-induced glaucoma. TAMOXIFEN — crystalline retinopathy. VIGABATRIN — irreversible peripheral field constriction. SILDENAFIL — blue-tinged vision. TOPIRAMATE — acute angle-closure glaucoma with myopic shift.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Chronic arthritis + bull's-eye macula → **chloroquine toxicity**.

**REMEMBER:** *It binds melanin and accumulates for years — so screen the fields and OCT, not the fundus.*

## 11. Choroidal Melanoma

**PEARL:** A **solitary elevated grey-brown dome-shaped SUBRETINAL mass with orange PIGMENT (lipofuscin) over it**, causing painless visual loss and metamorphopsia, is a **CHOROIDAL MELANOMA — the commonest PRIMARY intraocular malignancy in ADULTS.** On **ANGIOGRAPHY it shows "DOUBLE CIRCULATION"** (the tumour's own vessels plus the overlying retinal vessels); on **B-SCAN ULTRASOUND it shows the pathognomonic "COLLAR-STUD/MUSHROOM" shape** with **LOW INTERNAL REFLECTIVITY and choroidal excavation.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The collar-stud shape is a mechanical consequence: the tumour grows within the choroid until it **RUPTURES BRUCH'S MEMBRANE**, after which it mushrooms into the subretinal space with a constricted "waist" at the break. **Recognising that the two classic signs belong to two different investigations** (double circulation on FFA, collar-stud on ultrasound) is precisely what the exam tests.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **BIOPSY IS AVOIDED — diagnosis is CLINICAL plus ULTRASOUND, because of the risk of tumour seeding.**
- **Choroidal melanoma spreads HAEMATOGENOUSLY, overwhelmingly to the LIVER (~90% of metastases) — because the eye has NO LYMPHATICS. Lifelong liver surveillance is required, and metastatic disease carries a dismal prognosis.**
- **Prognostic factors: LARGE size, EPITHELIOID cell type (worse than spindle), extraocular extension, and — most powerfully — MONOSOMY 3 and BAP1 mutation on genetic testing.**
- **Treatment: PLAQUE BRACHYTHERAPY (ruthenium-106 or iodine-125) or proton beam for small/medium tumours (the COMS trial showed no survival difference versus enucleation); ENUCLEATION for large tumours, painful blind eyes or optic nerve involvement; transpupillary thermotherapy as an adjunct.**
- **The differential of a pigmented fundus lesion: CHOROIDAL NAEVUS (flat, <2 mm thick, with DRUSEN over it and no orange pigment or fluid — the "TFSOM" risk factors predict growth), congenital hypertrophy of the RPE, choroidal haemangioma and metastasis (the commonest INTRAOCULAR malignancy overall is METASTASIS — from breast and lung).**
- **RETINOBLASTOMA is the commonest intraocular malignancy of CHILDHOOD — LEUKOCORIA, strabismus, RB1 gene on chromosome 13q14, calcification on ultrasound/CT, and FLEXNER–WINTERSTEINER ROSETTES histologically.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Expected finding on angiography" → **double circulation** (collar-stud is a B-scan sign).

**REMEMBER:** *Double circulation on the angiogram, collar-stud on the ultrasound, and the metastasis goes to the liver.*

## 12. Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect

**PEARL:** In the **SWINGING FLASHLIGHT TEST**, when the light is swung to the affected eye **BOTH PUPILS PARADOXICALLY DILATE** — a **RELATIVE AFFERENT PUPILLARY DEFECT (MARCUS GUNN PUPIL)**, the earliest and most reliable objective sign of **UNILATERAL/ASYMMETRIC OPTIC NERVE DISEASE.** The RAPD is in the eye with the **worse afferent input**; both pupils are otherwise equal in size.

**UNDERSTAND:** The consensual reflex means **both pupils always match**, and their size at any moment reflects **the TOTAL afferent input reaching the midbrain from whichever eye is being lit.** When the light moves from the good eye (strong input, both constricted) to the diseased eye (weak input), the total drive suddenly falls — so **both pupils dilate**. It is therefore a test of **RELATIVE difference between the two nerves**, which is why **BILATERAL SYMMETRICAL disease produces NO RAPD** and why a **DENSE CATARACT does NOT cause one** (media opacity scatters but does not reduce total light reaching the retina).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Causes: OPTIC NEURITIS, glaucoma (asymmetric), ischaemic optic neuropathy, optic nerve compression/tumour, and large retinal lesions (extensive detachment, central retinal artery or ischaemic vein occlusion) — a RETINAL cause requires very extensive damage to produce a RAPD, whereas a small optic nerve lesion produces a large one.** **A dense amblyopia gives at most a trivial RAPD.**
- **AN RAPD NEVER OCCURS FROM CATARACT OR REFRACTIVE ERROR — a very useful negative when a patient claims poor vision.**
- **Contrast the abnormal pupils: ARGYLL ROBERTSON PUPIL — small, irregular, BILATERAL, does NOT react to light but DOES react to near (LIGHT–NEAR DISSOCIATION), classically NEUROSYPHILIS ("prostitute's pupil — accommodates but does not react"). ADIE'S TONIC PUPIL — a large pupil in a young woman with a slow, segmental ("vermiform") light reaction and a slow tonic near response, with absent tendon reflexes (HOLMES–ADIE syndrome); it CONSTRICTS TO 0.1% PILOCARPINE (denervation supersensitivity). HORNER'S SYNDROME — miosis with ptosis and anhidrosis, worse in the dark (dilatation lag), confirmed with apraclonidine or cocaine drops. THIRD NERVE PALSY — a fixed dilated pupil with ptosis and an eye down and out.**
- **PHARMACOLOGICAL TESTING of a FIXED DILATED PUPIL: 1% PILOCARPINE CONSTRICTS a third nerve palsy pupil but does NOT constrict a pharmacologically dilated (atropinised) pupil; 0.1% PILOCARPINE constricts an ADIE'S pupil but not a normal one.**
- **Testing requires a bright light, a distant fixation target and a dim room — and the swing must be brisk and symmetrical.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Swinging-flashlight image with paradoxical dilatation → **RAPD, seen in optic neuritis**.

**REMEMBER:** *Swing to the bad eye and both pupils escape — the defect is in the nerve, never in the lens.*

## 13. An Enlarged Blind Spot

**PEARL:** The **physiological blind spot is an absolute scotoma corresponding to the optic disc (no photoreceptors).** It is **ENLARGED in PAPILLOEDEMA, PRIMARY OPEN-ANGLE GLAUCOMA, MEDULLATED (myelinated) NERVE FIBRES, optic disc DRUSEN, optic disc COLOBOMA and MYOPIC DISC with a crescent.** **OPTIC DISC HYPOPLASIA does NOT enlarge it** — the disc is SMALL (with the "DOUBLE-RING SIGN").

**UNDERSTAND:** The blind spot enlarges whenever the **functional area of "no photoreceptors" enlarges** — either because the disc physically swells (papilloedema, drusen), because tissue that is not photoreceptive extends beyond the disc (myelinated fibres, myopic crescent), or because peripapillary retina is damaged. **Hypoplasia produces the opposite geometry — a smaller disc — so the scotoma cannot enlarge.** The logic is entirely about area, not about function.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **PAPILLOEDEMA is BILATERAL disc swelling from RAISED INTRACRANIAL PRESSURE, with PRESERVED visual acuity early, TRANSIENT VISUAL OBSCURATIONS, an enlarged blind spot, and NO RAPD (it is symmetrical). PAPILLITIS (optic neuritis with disc swelling) is usually UNILATERAL with EARLY SEVERE VISUAL LOSS, pain on eye movement, a CENTRAL scotoma and an RAPD.**
- **Field defects and their localisation: enlarged blind spot → disc pathology; ARCUATE (BJERRUM) SCOTOMA, NASAL STEP and generalised constriction → GLAUCOMA; CENTRAL scotoma → macular or optic nerve disease; CENTROCAECAL → toxic/nutritional optic neuropathy; ALTITUDINAL → ischaemic optic neuropathy or retinal vascular occlusion; BITEMPORAL HEMIANOPIA → chiasma (pituitary adenoma); HOMONYMOUS HEMIANOPIA → retrochiasmal tract/radiation, with MACULAR SPARING in occipital lesions; superior quadrantanopia ("PIE IN THE SKY") → temporal (Meyer's loop); inferior quadrantanopia → parietal.**
- **Glaucomatous field loss follows the arrangement of the NERVE FIBRE LAYER, which is why it is ARCUATE and respects the HORIZONTAL RAPHE, and why the nasal step appears.**
- **MEDULLATED NERVE FIBRES are a benign congenital anomaly — feathery white patches with frayed edges continuous with the disc, obscuring the vessels.**
- **OPTIC DISC DRUSEN cause PSEUDOPAPILLOEDEMA — an elevated disc with NO hyperaemia, NO obscured vessels and autofluorescence; important because it mimics true swelling.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Causes of an enlarged blind spot EXCEPT" → **optic disc hypoplasia**.

**REMEMBER:** *A bigger disc means a bigger blind spot — hypoplasia makes it smaller.*

## 14. The Parks–Bielschowsky Three-Step Test

**PEARL:** For a **VERTICAL deviation, work through three steps: (1) WHICH EYE is hyper- or hypotropic in primary gaze; (2) does the deviation WORSEN ON RIGHT OR LEFT GAZE; (3) does it WORSEN ON RIGHT OR LEFT HEAD TILT (BIELSCHOWSKY).** Each step halves the four candidate muscles until one remains. **A superior oblique palsy — by far the commonest — worsens on OPPOSITE gaze and SAME-SIDE head tilt.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The test works because the **vertical muscles have different actions in different horizontal positions**: the **RECTI act as pure elevators/depressors in ABDUCTION**, whereas the **OBLIQUES act as pure elevators/depressors in ADDUCTION.** So step 2 separates recti from obliques. Step 3 exploits the **ocular counter-rolling reflex**: on head tilt, the ipsilateral eye INTORTS (superior rectus + superior oblique) and the contralateral eye EXTORTS — and if one intorter is weak, its **unopposed vertical action** exaggerates the deviation.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The mnemonic for superior oblique palsy: the deviation is Worse on the OPPOSITE GAZE and worse on the SAME-SIDE HEAD TILT — hence the compensatory HEAD TILT TO THE OPPOSITE SHOULDER, face turn and chin depression.**
- **Fourth nerve palsy is the commonest cause of an acquired vertical squint and of CONGENITAL vertical deviations; head trauma is the classic acquired cause (bilateral palsies with a V-pattern esotropia and marked excyclotorsion).**
- **The test presumes a single paretic muscle and is invalid in restrictive disease (thyroid eye disease, blowout fracture), myasthenia, previous surgery and skew deviation — always do a FORCED DUCTION TEST to exclude restriction.**
- **PARALYTIC squint: SECONDARY deviation is GREATER than the primary deviation (Hering's law — the increased innervation needed to move the paretic eye over-drives its yoke muscle), with diplopia, limited ductions, false projection, abnormal head posture and a NEGATIVE forced duction test. RESTRICTIVE squint: limited ductions with a POSITIVE forced duction test. COMITANT squint: the angle is EQUAL in all gazes, ductions are full and there is NO diplopia (suppression) — the typical childhood squint.**
- **A long-standing paralytic squint eventually becomes comitant (spread of comitance), obscuring the original cause.**
- **Never forget MYASTHENIA GRAVIS — it can mimic any pattern of ocular motility disturbance and characteristically fatigues and varies.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Work the three steps mechanically; head tilt is the decisive step.

**REMEMBER:** *Recti act in abduction, obliques in adduction — and the head tilt tests the intorters.*

## 15. Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma

**PEARL:** In POAG, **gonioscopy shows a NORMAL, WIDE OPEN angle with all structures visible — the trabecular abnormality is microscopic and CANNOT be seen; pupil DILATATION does NOT raise the IOP (that is ANGLE-CLOSURE); the risk to FIRST-DEGREE RELATIVES is high (~10% in siblings, 4% in children); family members are more likely to be STEROID RESPONDERS; and VISUAL FIELD DEFECTS CAN EXIST WITH A NORMAL CUP-DISC RATIO.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Gonioscopy's role in POAG is **to exclude a closed or secondary angle, not to demonstrate the disease** — the outflow resistance lies in the **juxtacanalicular trabecular meshwork at a microscopic level.** And the cup-disc ratio is a **crude, highly variable measure**: up to **40% of retinal nerve fibres can be lost before a field defect appears**, and a physiologically small disc has a small cup even when damaged — which is why **structure (OCT RNFL) and function (fields) must both be assessed.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **DIAGNOSIS requires any 2 of 3: raised IOP (>21 mmHg on more than one occasion, diurnal variation >8 mmHg, or asymmetry >5 mmHg between eyes), characteristic OPTIC DISC changes, and characteristic VISUAL FIELD loss. IOP IS A RISK FACTOR, NOT THE DEFINITION — hence NORMAL-TENSION glaucoma and OCULAR HYPERTENSION.**
- **Disc signs of glaucoma: increased cup-disc ratio, ASYMMETRY >0.2 between eyes, VERTICAL cup elongation, NOTCHING of the neuroretinal rim, violation of the ISNT RULE (rim thickness Inferior > Superior > Nasal > Temporal), BAYONETTING of vessels, LAMINAR DOT sign, disc HAEMORRHAGE (Drance) and peripapillary atrophy.**
- **Field loss: paracentral scotoma → NASAL STEP → ARCUATE (Bjerrum) scotoma → RING scotoma → tubular field with a preserved TEMPORAL ISLAND (central vision is lost LAST, which is why POAG is silent until advanced).**
- **Medical therapy: PROSTAGLANDIN ANALOGUES first-line (latanoprost — increases UVEOSCLERAL outflow; side effects are conjunctival hyperaemia, iris and periocular HYPERPIGMENTATION, eyelash growth and periorbital fat loss). BETA-BLOCKERS (timolol — reduce aqueous PRODUCTION; avoid in asthma, COPD and heart block). ALPHA-2 AGONISTS (brimonidine — reduce production and increase uveoscleral outflow). CARBONIC ANHYDRASE INHIBITORS (dorzolamide, acetazolamide — reduce production). MIOTICS (pilocarpine — increase TRABECULAR outflow). Then LASER TRABECULOPLASTY and TRABECULECTOMY (with mitomycin C).**
- **RISK FACTORS: raised IOP, age, AFRICAN ancestry, family history, MYOPIA, thin central corneal thickness (which also causes IOP to be UNDER-ESTIMATED by applanation), diabetes and steroid responsiveness.**
- **Screening matters because POAG is IRREVERSIBLE and ASYMPTOMATIC — the fellow eye and relatives must be examined.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Multi-statement question → the true statements are **steroid responsiveness in relatives** and **field defects with a normal C:D ratio**.

**REMEMBER:** *The angle looks normal, the pressure is only a risk factor, and the field can go before the cup does.*

## 16. Antiglaucoma Drugs — Mechanisms and Contraindications

**PEARL:** **LATANOPROST (a prostaglandin analogue) lowers IOP by INCREASING UVEOSCLERAL OUTFLOW** — but it must be **AVOIDED IN ACTIVE UVEITIS, cystoid macular oedema and herpetic keratitis**, because it is pro-inflammatory. **ATROPINE (and any mydriatic) is CONTRAINDICATED IN ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE ("congestive") GLAUCOMA**, where it precipitates or worsens the attack — yet atropine is a *treatment* in corneal ulcer, anterior uveitis and MALIGNANT glaucoma. **AMITRIPTYLINE and other ANTICHOLINERGICS must be avoided in angle-closure glaucoma.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Everything follows from what the pupil does to the angle. **Dilatation heaps the peripheral iris into the angle**, blocking trabecular outflow in an already narrow angle — hence mydriatics are catastrophic in angle-closure. But in **MALIGNANT (aqueous misdirection) glaucoma**, the problem is the opposite — aqueous is diverted posteriorly and the lens–iris diaphragm is pushed forward — so **CYCLOPLEGIA WITH ATROPINE tightens the zonules, pulls the lens back and is the correct treatment.** The same drug is therefore contraindicated in one glaucoma and indicated in another.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Mechanisms: INCREASE UVEOSCLERAL outflow — PROSTAGLANDINS. INCREASE TRABECULAR outflow — PILOCARPINE (miotics). DECREASE AQUEOUS PRODUCTION — BETA-BLOCKERS, CARBONIC ANHYDRASE INHIBITORS, alpha-2 agonists (which also increase uveoscleral outflow). OSMOTIC agents (MANNITOL, glycerol) reduce vitreous volume — for acute IOP lowering.**
- **ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA: severe unilateral pain, headache, vomiting, blurred vision with COLOURED HALOES, a red eye with a HAZY CORNEA, a FIXED MID-DILATED OVAL pupil and a rock-hard eye. Treatment: IV/oral ACETAZOLAMIDE, topical beta-blocker, alpha-agonist and steroid, hyperosmotics, and PILOCARPINE (once the pressure begins to fall — it is ineffective at very high IOP because the sphincter is ischaemic); definitive treatment is LASER PERIPHERAL IRIDOTOMY — TO BOTH EYES (the fellow eye is anatomically at risk).**
- **Drugs that can precipitate angle closure: ANTICHOLINERGICS (atropine, TCAs, antihistamines, antispasmodics), sympathomimetics (including nebulised ipratropium/salbutamol reaching the eye), and TOPIRAMATE (a distinct idiosyncratic mechanism with ciliochoroidal effusion and myopic shift — treated by stopping the drug, NOT by iridotomy).**
- **Beta-blocker contraindications: ASTHMA/COPD, bradycardia, heart block, heart failure — and they MASK hypoglycaemic symptoms in diabetics.**
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: avoid in SULPHA allergy, renal stones, and sickle cell disease; systemic acetazolamide causes paraesthesiae, metabolic acidosis and hypokalaemia.**
- **NEOVASCULAR GLAUCOMA needs anti-VEGF and pan-retinal photocoagulation as well as pressure-lowering; PILOCARPINE AND PROSTAGLANDINS ARE AVOIDED in inflamed/neovascular eyes.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Uveitis with high IOP → **avoid prostaglandin analogues**; angle-closure → **avoid atropine and amitriptyline**.

**REMEMBER:** *Anything that dilates the pupil shuts a narrow angle — except in malignant glaucoma, where atropine is the cure.*

## 17. Roth Spots

**PEARL:** **ROTH SPOTS are retinal HAEMORRHAGES WITH A WHITE/PALE CENTRE** — the white centre is a **fibrin–platelet plug (or leucocytes/septic material)** surrounded by a red rim of haemorrhage. Causes: **LEUKAEMIA (especially acute), SUBACUTE BACTERIAL ENDOCARDITIS, DIABETES, hypertensive retinopathy, SLE, HIV retinopathy, sickle cell disease, severe anaemia and multiple myeloma.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The lesion is a **haemorrhage that has been plugged** — rupture of a capillary followed by aggregation of platelets and fibrin at the rupture site, or by an embolic/leucaemic infiltrate at the centre. So the finding indicates **either an embolic/septic process or a haematological disorder of viscosity, platelets or white cells** — which is exactly the differential list, and why finding one mandates a systemic work-up, not an ocular one.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Leukaemic retinopathy also shows: dilated tortuous veins with segmentation ("boxcarring"), flame-shaped and white-centred haemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, perivascular sheathing, leukaemic infiltrates, and — in acute leukaemia — a HYPOPYON of leukaemic cells or optic nerve infiltration (a medical emergency requiring urgent radiotherapy to save vision).**
- **ENDOCARDITIS: Roth spots, conjunctival petechiae, splinter haemorrhages, Osler nodes and Janeway lesions — the eye can be the clue that prompts blood cultures and echocardiography.**
- **Retinal haemorrhage morphology localises the layer: FLAME-SHAPED (superficial, in the nerve fibre layer, following its arrangement — hypertension); DOT-AND-BLOT (deeper, in the inner nuclear/outer plexiform layers — diabetes); SUBHYALOID/pre-retinal (boat-shaped with a fluid level — Valsalva, subarachnoid haemorrhage/TERSON SYNDROME, severe anaemia); SUB-RPE (dark, from choroidal neovascularisation).**
- **Bilateral retinal haemorrhages in an INFANT with encephalopathy raise NON-ACCIDENTAL INJURY (shaken baby syndrome) — a medico-legally crucial finding.**
- **HYPERTENSIVE RETINOPATHY (Keith–Wagener): I — arteriolar attenuation; II — AV nipping, copper/silver wiring; III — haemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, hard exudates and a MACULAR STAR; IV — plus DISC OEDEMA (malignant hypertension, an emergency).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Roth spots seen in" → **acute leukaemia** (among the options offered).

**REMEMBER:** *A red haemorrhage with a white heart means look at the blood or the heart valves.*

## 18. Ophthalmia Neonatorum

**PEARL:** **Conjunctivitis in an infant within the first 30 DAYS of life = OPHTHALMIA NEONATORUM.** In **DEVELOPED countries the commonest cause is now *CHLAMYDIA TRACHOMATIS* (serotypes D–K)** — gonococcal disease having been largely eliminated by prophylaxis. **Timing is the clue: CHEMICAL (silver nitrate) within 24 HOURS; GONOCOCCAL at 2–5 DAYS; CHLAMYDIAL at 5–14 DAYS; HERPES SIMPLEX at 1–2 WEEKS.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The incubation periods reflect the organisms' growth rates, and they matter because the **treatment differs completely.** **GONOCOCCUS is hyperacute and sight-threatening** — it is one of the few organisms that can **penetrate an INTACT corneal epithelium**, causing rapid ulceration and PERFORATION. **Chlamydia is intracellular and slower**, causing a subacute mucopurulent conjunctivitis that also seeds the nasopharynx and lungs — hence the risk of **CHLAMYDIAL PNEUMONITIS at 4–12 weeks.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **GONOCOCCAL ophthalmia is an OPHTHALMIC EMERGENCY: profuse purulent discharge with marked lid oedema and chemosis. Treat with a SINGLE DOSE OF IM/IV CEFTRIAXONE (avoid in hyperbilirubinaemia — use cefotaxime) plus FREQUENT SALINE IRRIGATION of the eye; topical therapy alone is inadequate.**
- **CHLAMYDIAL ophthalmia requires SYSTEMIC ORAL ERYTHROMYCIN (or azithromycin) for 14 DAYS — topical treatment does NOT eradicate nasopharyngeal carriage and does not prevent pneumonitis. Note that neonates have NO FOLLICLES (the conjunctiva is immature) — they get a papillary reaction instead.**
- **In BOTH, TREAT THE MOTHER AND HER PARTNER, and screen for other sexually transmitted infections.**
- **PROPHYLAXIS at birth: single-application ERYTHROMYCIN 0.5% ointment (or povidone-iodine 2.5%); CREDE'S METHOD with 1% silver nitrate is historical and itself causes CHEMICAL conjunctivitis.**
- **HERPES SIMPLEX ophthalmia neonatorum (usually HSV-2) may be accompanied by disseminated or CNS disease — treat with systemic ACYCLOVIR.**
- **Any neonate with discharge or watering in the first week must have ophthalmia neonatorum excluded; take conjunctival swabs for Gram stain, Giemsa (intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies in chlamydia), culture and NAAT.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Commonest cause in developed countries" → ***Chlamydia***; the day of onset identifies the organism.

**REMEMBER:** *Day one is chemical, days two to five gonococcal, the second week chlamydial.*

## 19. Central Retinal Vein Occlusion and "100-Day Glaucoma"

**PEARL:** **"100-DAY (or THREE-MONTH) GLAUCOMA" is NEOVASCULAR GLAUCOMA following ISCHAEMIC CENTRAL RETINAL VEIN OCCLUSION**, appearing about 3 months after the event. Ischaemic CRVO presents with **sudden severe visual loss, an RAPD, and a fundus of massive venous dilatation and tortuosity with widespread haemorrhages in all four quadrants — the "BLOOD AND THUNDER" or "SPLASHED TOMATO" fundus** — plus cotton-wool spots and disc oedema.

**UNDERSTAND:** Extensive capillary non-perfusion releases **VEGF, which diffuses forward into the anterior segment** and produces **RUBEOSIS IRIDIS — new vessels on the iris and in the angle** — and a fibrovascular membrane that first blocks and then contracts, dragging the angle closed. Hence the glaucoma is **initially open-angle then SECONDARY ANGLE-CLOSURE with peripheral anterior synechiae**, painful, and notoriously difficult to control — which is why **prevention by PRP and anti-VEGF matters more than treatment.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **NON-ISCHAEMIC CRVO (the commoner form, ~75%) has better acuity, NO or minimal RAPD, fewer haemorrhages, and a much lower risk of neovascularisation — but up to a third convert to the ischaemic type, so follow-up with regular gonioscopy and iris examination is essential.**
- **The single most important test is a careful UNDILATED IRIS EXAMINATION AND GONIOSCOPY at each visit — rubeosis in the angle can precede iris rubeosis.**
- **Management: treat MACULAR OEDEMA with intravitreal ANTI-VEGF (first-line) or dexamethasone implant; PAN-RETINAL PHOTOCOAGULATION for neovascularisation (of the iris, angle, disc or retina) — the CVOS showed PRP should be given when neovascularisation APPEARS, not prophylactically.**
- **Investigate the cause: HYPERTENSION (the commonest association), diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, glaucoma (a major ocular risk factor), smoking, hyperviscosity states and — in the young — thrombophilia, oral contraceptives and vasculitis.**
- **BRANCH RVO occurs at ARTERIOVENOUS CROSSINGS (where artery and vein share an adventitial sheath), most often SUPEROTEMPORALLY, with a wedge-shaped sector of haemorrhage.**
- **Contrast CENTRAL RETINAL ARTERY OCCLUSION: SUDDEN PAINLESS TOTAL visual loss, a PALE oedematous retina with a CHERRY-RED SPOT at the fovea, "cattle-trucking" of the arteries, and a dense RAPD — a STROKE EQUIVALENT requiring urgent ocular massage, anterior chamber paracentesis and acetazolamide, plus URGENT investigation for carotid disease, cardiac emboli and — in the elderly — GIANT CELL ARTERITIS (check ESR/CRP and start steroids immediately if suspected).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "100-day glaucoma" → **central retinal vein occlusion**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ischaemic vein occlusion makes VEGF for three months — then the angle grows shut.*

## 20. Fungal Corneal Ulcer

**PEARL:** **SATELLITE LESIONS around the main ulcer are characteristic of a FUNGAL keratitis**, along with **DRY, RAISED, greyish-white slough with FEATHERY/serrated margins, an IMMUNE (WESSELEY) RING, a THICK IMMOBILE HYPOPYON, and endothelial plaques.** History of **TRAUMA WITH VEGETABLE MATTER** is the classic clue.

**UNDERSTAND:** The **hypopyon is thick and IMMOBILE because fungi actually INVADE through Descemet's membrane into the anterior chamber**, so the exudate is fungal-laden and sticky — unlike the **sterile, mobile, fluid hypopyon of bacterial ulcers**, which is a purely reactive collection of sterile pus. This same invasive tendency explains why fungal ulcers are **indolent but deep and prone to perforation**, and why steroids are so dangerous.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **CORNEAL SCRAPING is essential before treatment: GRAM and GIEMSA stains, KOH MOUNT/calcofluor white (fastest for fungi), and culture on SABOURAUD DEXTROSE AGAR and blood agar. Confocal microscopy is a non-invasive alternative.**
- **Treatment: topical NATAMYCIN 5% is first-line for FILAMENTOUS fungi (*FUSARIUM*, *ASPERGILLUS* — the commonest in India); topical/oral VORICONAZOLE or amphotericin for *CANDIDA* and refractory cases; ORAL azoles for deep/severe infection. ***TOPICAL STEROIDS ARE ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED*** — they accelerate fungal proliferation and are the classic iatrogenic disaster. ATROPINE is given as a cycloplegic. Therapeutic keratoplasty for perforation or failure.**
- **Compare the other ulcers: BACTERIAL (*Pseudomonas*, *Staph*, *Strep pneumoniae*) — rapid onset, dense stromal infiltrate, MOBILE hypopyon; *PSEUDOMONAS* is especially aggressive in CONTACT LENS wearers and can melt the cornea within 48 hours. HERPETIC — a DENDRITIC ulcer with TERMINAL BULBS on fluorescein staining, REDUCED CORNEAL SENSATION, recurrent; treat with topical acyclovir; STEROIDS ARE CONTRAINDICATED in epithelial (dendritic) disease but ARE used with cover in stromal disease. *ACANTHAMOEBA* — contact lens wearers using tap water, PAIN OUT OF PROPORTION to signs, a RING infiltrate and radial perineuritis. MOORE'S/MOOREN'S ULCER — a painful peripheral autoimmune ulcer with an overhanging edge.**
- **Risk factors for infective keratitis in India: agricultural trauma, contact lens misuse, dry eye, prior herpes, topical steroid abuse and traditional eye medicines.**
- **A "REVERSE HYPOPYON" (pus rising to the top) occurs with silicone oil in the anterior chamber.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Characteristic feature of a fungal ulcer" → **satellite lesions**.

**REMEMBER:** *Feathery edges, satellites and a hypopyon that will not move — and never a steroid.*

## 21. Eyelid Terminology

**PEARL:** **DISTICHIASIS = an EXTRA ROW OF EYELASHES arising BEHIND THE GREY LINE** (from the meibomian gland orifices). **TRICHIASIS = normally sited lashes that are MISDIRECTED inwards, with a NORMAL lid margin position. ENTROPION = inturning of the whole LID MARGIN. TYLOSIS = thickening of the lid margin/tarsal plate. MADAROSIS = LOSS of eyelashes or eyebrows. POLIOSIS = WHITENING of the lashes.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The **GREY LINE is the surgical landmark of the lid margin** — the mucocutaneous junction marking the plane between the anterior lamella (skin and orbicularis) and the posterior lamella (tarsus and conjunctiva). **Lashes normally arise ANTERIOR to it; distichiatic lashes arise POSTERIOR to it** because meibomian glands (which are modified sebaceous glands of the posterior lamella) have differentiated into hair follicles. That anatomy also dictates the treatment — **LID SPLITTING ALONG THE GREY LINE with cryotherapy to the aberrant follicles.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **All of these damage the cornea the same way — chronic abrasion causing punctate keratopathy, ulceration and scarring — so treatment is directed at protecting the cornea: epilation (temporary), ELECTROLYSIS or CRYOTHERAPY for a few lashes, and surgery (lid split, tarsal rotation) for many.**
- **TRACHOMA is the classic cause of trichiasis and cicatricial entropion worldwide, and the "T" of the WHO **SAFE** strategy (Surgery for trichiasis, Antibiotics — azithromycin, Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement). Its stages (McCallan/WHO FISTO): Follicles, Intense inflammation, Scarring (ARLT'S LINE), Trichiasis, Corneal Opacity — with HERBERT'S PITS (limbal follicle scars) being pathognomonic.**
- **MADAROSIS causes: LEPROSY (classically the lateral third of the eyebrow), chronic blepharitis, alopecia areata, hypothyroidism, syphilis, trichotillomania, burns and chemotherapy.**
- **POLIOSIS: VOGT–KOYANAGI–HARADA syndrome, sympathetic ophthalmitis, vitiligo, Waardenburg syndrome and chronic blepharitis.**
- **Distinguish the lid lumps: HORDEOLUM EXTERNUM (stye — acute staphylococcal infection of a lash follicle/Zeis gland, tender, points anteriorly); HORDEOLUM INTERNUM (acute meibomian infection, points posteriorly); CHALAZION (a chronic, PAINLESS lipogranuloma of a meibomian gland — treat with warm compresses, then incision and curettage from the CONJUNCTIVAL side; a RECURRENT chalazion in an elderly patient must be biopsied to exclude SEBACEOUS GLAND CARCINOMA).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Extra row of lashes behind the grey line" → **distichiasis**.

**REMEMBER:** *Behind the grey line means distichiasis; in front but pointing in means trichiasis.*

## 22. Thyroid Eye Disease

**PEARL:** **THYROID EYE DISEASE (Graves' orbitopathy) is the COMMONEST CAUSE OF BOTH UNILATERAL AND BILATERAL PROPTOSIS IN ADULTS — and it can occur in a EUTHYROID patient.** Features: **LID RETRACTION (the commonest sign), lid lag, proptosis, periorbital oedema and CHEMOSIS, restrictive myopathy with diplopia, and — the sight-threatening complications — EXPOSURE KERATOPATHY and COMPRESSIVE OPTIC NEUROPATHY.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Orbital fibroblasts express the **TSH receptor** and are stimulated by **TSH-receptor autoantibodies** to produce **glycosaminoglycans**, which draw in water and swell the **EXTRAOCULAR MUSCLE BELLIES WHILE SPARING THE TENDONS** — the radiological signature that distinguishes it from orbital myositis (which involves the tendons). Muscle enlargement in the confined orbital apex is what compresses the optic nerve, and the fibrotic muscles then **restrict** movement, giving a **POSITIVE FORCED DUCTION TEST.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The muscles enlarge in a characteristic order — "I'M SLOW": INFERIOR RECTUS > MEDIAL RECTUS > SUPERIOR RECTUS > LATERAL RECTUS. Because the INFERIOR rectus is affected first and tethers the eye, the earliest motility defect is LIMITATION OF ELEVATION — which can be mistaken for a superior rectus palsy.**
- **Eponymous signs: VON GRAEFE'S (lid lag on downgaze), DALRYMPLE'S (lid retraction with sclera visible above the limbus), STELLWAG'S (infrequent blinking), MOBIUS' (poor convergence), JOFFROY'S (absent forehead wrinkling on upgaze).**
- **Assessment uses the CAS (Clinical Activity Score) for inflammation and the NOSPECS/EUGOGO grading for severity. CT/MRI shows fusiform muscle enlargement sparing tendons and apical crowding.**
- **Management: STOP SMOKING (the single strongest modifiable risk factor and the strongest predictor of severity), render EUTHYROID, SELENIUM for mild disease, lubricants and taping at night. ACTIVE MODERATE-SEVERE disease — IV METHYLPREDNISOLONE pulses (superior to oral), with TEPROTUMUMAB (anti-IGF-1R), rituximab or tocilizumab as newer options, and orbital radiotherapy. SIGHT-THREATENING compressive optic neuropathy — high-dose IV steroids and URGENT ORBITAL DECOMPRESSION.**
- ***RADIOIODINE CAN WORSEN THYROID EYE DISEASE*** (especially in smokers) — give steroid cover or prefer another modality in active eye disease.
- **Rehabilitative surgery follows a strict order once the disease is INACTIVE: DECOMPRESSION → SQUINT surgery → LID surgery. Doing them out of order undoes the previous step.**
- **The differential of proptosis: orbital cellulitis (painful, febrile, unilateral — an emergency), ORBITAL PSEUDOTUMOUR (painful, steroid-responsive), orbital lymphoma (painless, elderly, superolateral mass), cavernous haemangioma (the commonest benign adult orbital tumour), and in children RHABDOMYOSARCOMA (rapidly progressive) and capillary haemangioma.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Bilateral proptosis in a EUTHYROID adult → **thyroid ophthalmopathy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Muscle bellies swell and tendons are spared — the inferior rectus goes first and the eye cannot look up.*

## 23. Visual Acuity and the Snellen Chart

**PEARL:** With **6/6 vision, the WHOLE LETTER subtends 5 MINUTES OF ARC at the nodal point, and each of its component parts (limbs and gaps) subtends 1 MINUTE OF ARC** — the **MINIMUM ANGLE OF RESOLUTION of the normal eye.** Snellen acuity is written as **DISTANCE OF TESTING / DISTANCE AT WHICH THAT LETTER SUBTENDS 5 MINUTES** (6/60, 6/36, 6/24, 6/18, 6/12, 6/9, 6/6).

**UNDERSTAND:** Resolution is limited by **cone spacing at the fovea** — two points can be discriminated only if their images fall on two cones separated by an unstimulated cone, which corresponds to about **1 minute of arc.** The letters are built on a 5×5 grid so that each stroke equals that minimum resolvable angle: the letter is legible only if the eye can resolve its parts, not merely detect the whole.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **If the top (6/60) letter cannot be read at 6 m, move the patient closer and record as 5/60, 4/60 etc.; below that, test COUNTING FINGERS → HAND MOVEMENTS → PERCEPTION OF LIGHT (with projection of rays) → NO PERCEPTION OF LIGHT (total blindness).**
- **A PINHOLE improves acuity in REFRACTIVE error but not in organic disease — the single most useful bedside discriminator.**
- **WHO categories: BLINDNESS is presenting acuity <3/60 in the better eye (India previously used <6/60); low vision <6/18 to 3/60. India's National Programme for Control of Blindness targets a prevalence below 0.3%.**
- **Alternatives for special groups: LANDOLT'S BROKEN RING and E-CHART (illiterate patients), ALLEN pictures and TELLER ACUITY CARDS/preferential looking (children), CATFORD DRUM and OPTOKINETIC NYSTAGMUS (infants and malingerers), and LogMAR/ETDRS charts (research standard, with equal letters per line and a geometric progression).**
- **NEAR vision is tested with JAEGER (N notation) charts at 25–35 cm.**
- **CONTRAST SENSITIVITY and COLOUR VISION may be grossly abnormal despite 6/6 acuity — as in early optic neuritis and glaucoma — so normal Snellen acuity never excludes optic nerve disease.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Minimum angle of resolution for 6/6" → **the letter subtends 5 minutes; each part 1 minute.**

**REMEMBER:** *Five minutes for the letter, one minute for the stroke — that is what 6/6 means.*

## 24. Pterygium

**PEARL:** A **PTERYGIUM is a triangular, wing-shaped FIBROVASCULAR ingrowth of degenerative bulbar conjunctiva onto the CORNEA**, almost always on the **NASAL side of the interpalpebral fissure**, caused by chronic **ULTRAVIOLET exposure, dry heat, wind and dust.** Its parts are the **CAP (an avascular halo of infiltrate ahead of the head), HEAD (on cornea), NECK (at the limbus) and BODY.** **Recurrence after simple bare-sclera excision is high (~30–80%), NOT 95%.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Its nasal predilection is explained by **light entering temporally being focused by the cornea onto the NASAL limbus ("the peripheral light focusing effect")**, concentrating UV damage exactly where limbal stem cells sit. Damaged limbal stem cells then fail to hold the barrier between conjunctiva and cornea — which is why the **definitive surgical solution is to REPLACE that barrier with a CONJUNCTIVAL AUTOGRAFT (or limbal stem cell graft)**, reducing recurrence to under 5–10%.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Types: PROGRESSIVE — thick, fleshy, vascular, with a cap. ATROPHIC/REGRESSIVE — thin, pale, membranous, no cap. Symptoms: cosmetic concern, irritation and grittiness from DELLEN (localised corneal drying beside the elevated head), ASTIGMATISM (with-the-rule flattening) as it advances, and visual loss once it crosses the pupillary axis.**
- **STOCKER'S LINE — a brown iron deposit in the corneal epithelium AHEAD of the head — indicates a slowly growing/stationary lesion.**
- **Indications for surgery: encroachment on the visual axis, induced astigmatism, recurrent inflammation, restriction of ocular motility (diplopia), and cosmesis. Adjuncts to reduce recurrence: conjunctival AUTOGRAFT (best), amniotic membrane, and MITOMYCIN C.**
- **PSEUDOPTERYGIUM is the mimic: it follows a CHEMICAL BURN or ulcer, can occur at ANY meridian, is NOT adherent at the neck — so a PROBE CAN BE PASSED UNDER IT — and is stationary.**
- **PINGUECULA is the related lesion: a yellowish-white limbal nodule that does NOT cross onto the cornea and needs no treatment.**
- **Any atypical, leucoplakic or rapidly growing limbal lesion must raise OCULAR SURFACE SQUAMOUS NEOPLASIA — biopsy rather than assume.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not a feature of pterygium" → **95% recurrence rate**.

**REMEMBER:** *Ultraviolet light focuses on the nasal limbus — and only a graft stops it coming back.*

## 25. The Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex and Doll's Eye Testing

**PEARL:** The **OCULOCEPHALIC ("DOLL'S EYE") REFLEX tests cranial nerves VIII (afferent), III and VI (efferent) and their brainstem connections.** In an unconscious patient with an INTACT brainstem, turning the head makes the **eyes move CONJUGATELY IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION.** The **GAIN of the vestibulo-ocular reflex is 1** — eye velocity equals head velocity, in the opposite direction.

**UNDERSTAND:** A gain of exactly 1 is required to hold the **image stationary on the retina during head movement** — any less and the world appears to slip (OSCILLOPSIA). The reflex is a **three-neuron arc** (vestibular nerve → vestibular nucleus → MEDIAL LONGITUDINAL FASCICULUS → III and VI nuclei), which is **fast enough (latency ~10 ms) to work without visual feedback** — far faster than smooth pursuit. When we deliberately track a moving object with head and eyes together, **vision CANCELS the VOR.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **In a COMATOSE patient, a PRESENT doll's-eye response means the BRAINSTEM IS INTACT; its ABSENCE indicates brainstem injury or drug intoxication, and is one of the criteria examined in BRAIN DEATH testing. NEVER perform it if cervical spine injury is possible — use COLD CALORIC testing instead.**
- **CALORIC TESTING — "COWS": COLD water produces nystagmus with the fast phase to the OPPOSITE side; WARM water to the SAME side. In coma with an intact brainstem, cold water produces TONIC DEVIATION TOWARDS the irrigated ear with NO fast phase (the cortex generates the fast phase).**
- **INTERNUCLEAR OPHTHALMOPLEGIA is the MLF lesion: FAILURE OF ADDUCTION of the ipsilateral eye with NYSTAGMUS OF THE ABDUCTING eye, and PRESERVED CONVERGENCE. BILATERAL INO in a young person = MULTIPLE SCLEROSIS; unilateral in an older person = brainstem stroke. ONE-AND-A-HALF SYNDROME adds a PPRF/sixth nucleus lesion, leaving only abduction of one eye.**
- **The HEAD IMPULSE TEST (a rapid small-amplitude head turn producing a corrective saccade) identifies a PERIPHERAL vestibular lesion; a NORMAL head impulse test in acute vertigo is worrying for a CENTRAL cause — part of the HINTS examination (Head Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew), which outperforms early MRI for posterior circulation stroke.**
- **Conjugate gaze pathways: horizontal gaze is generated by the PPRF (pons), vertical gaze by the rostral interstitial nucleus of the MLF (midbrain) — hence vertical gaze palsy in PARINAUD/dorsal midbrain syndrome and in progressive supranuclear palsy.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Oculocephalic reflex tests which nerves" → **III, VI and VIII**; "gain of the VOR" → **1**.

**REMEMBER:** *Equal and opposite — gain of one keeps the world still.*

## 26. Optic Neuritis — Types, Treatment and the ONTT

**PEARL:** Optic neuritis has three anatomical forms: **RETROBULBAR NEURITIS — a completely NORMAL FUNDUS with NO disc oedema (the commonest form in adults: "the patient sees nothing and the doctor sees nothing"); PAPILLITIS — a swollen hyperaemic disc (commonest in children); and NEURORETINITIS — disc swelling PLUS a MACULAR STAR (think CAT-SCRATCH DISEASE, syphilis, Lyme — and NOT multiple sclerosis).** **Optic neuritis is the PRESENTING feature of MS in ~20–30% (not 70%), and occurs at some point in ~50%.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The hallmark of optic nerve disease is that **COLOUR VISION AND CONTRAST SENSITIVITY ARE AFFECTED OUT OF PROPORTION to the Snellen acuity** — because the small-calibre parvocellular fibres carrying colour and fine contrast are damaged preferentially. A patient with 6/9 acuity may be unable to see red at all ("RED DESATURATION"). Expecting the colour loss to be proportional is the classic error.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The ONTT (Optic Neuritis Treatment Trial) established three things: IV METHYLPREDNISOLONE speeds visual recovery but does NOT alter the final visual outcome; ***ORAL PREDNISOLONE ALONE IS CONTRAINDICATED*** because it DOUBLES the recurrence rate; and MRI BRAIN is the strongest predictor of conversion to MS — the 15-year risk is ~25% with a normal MRI and ~72% with ≥1 demyelinating lesion.**
- **Typical features: a young woman, SUBACUTE unilateral visual loss over hours to days, PAIN ON EYE MOVEMENT (~90%), a CENTRAL scotoma, an RAPD, and spontaneous recovery over 4–6 weeks. UHTHOFF'S PHENOMENON (worsening with heat/exercise) and PULFRICH'S phenomenon (altered depth perception) follow.**
- **ATYPICAL features demanding a wider work-up: age <15 or >50, BILATERAL simultaneous involvement, NO pain, severe disc swelling with haemorrhages, NO recovery by 4 weeks, or recurrence — test for AQUAPORIN-4 (NMO) and MOG ANTIBODIES, syphilis, tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, B12 and Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy.**
- **Work-up of a first episode: MRI BRAIN AND ORBITS with contrast, NMO/MOG antibodies, and BLOOD GLUCOSE (both to exclude diabetic optic neuropathy and because steroids will destabilise a diabetic).**
- **Distinguish ANTERIOR ISCHAEMIC OPTIC NEUROPATHY: an older patient with PAINLESS sudden ALTITUDINAL field loss and a pale swollen disc. ARTERITIC AION (giant cell arteritis) — jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, very high ESR/CRP — is an EMERGENCY: give HIGH-DOSE STEROIDS IMMEDIATELY, before the biopsy, to save the fellow eye.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "True about optic neuritis" → **it can have a normal fundus with no disc oedema**.

**REMEMBER:** *Colour and contrast go first and go further than the acuity — and never give oral steroids alone.*

## 27. Glaucomatous Visual Field Progression

**PEARL:** Glaucoma field loss follows a sequence: **EARLY NON-SPECIFIC — isopter contraction and BARING OF THE BLIND SPOT. EARLY SIGNIFICANT — a PARACENTRAL SCOTOMA in BJERRUM'S AREA (10–20° from fixation), SEIDEL'S SCOTOMA (a paracentral scotoma joining the blind spot), and a peripheral NASAL STEP. LATE — ARCUATE (BJERRUM) SCOTOMA, RING/double arcuate scotoma, and RÖNNE'S CENTRAL NASAL STEP. ADVANCED — TUBULAR VISION with a preserved TEMPORAL ISLAND, then no perception of light.** **RÖNNE'S NASAL STEP IS A FIELD DEFECT, NOT A DISC SIGN.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Every one of these shapes is dictated by the **anatomy of the retinal nerve fibre layer.** The arcuate fibres from the temporal retina **sweep in arcs around the macula and respect the HORIZONTAL RAPHE** — so damage produces an **arcuate scotoma**, and because the superior and inferior arcuate bundles are damaged unequally, the two defects meet at the horizontal meridian as a **STEP** in the nasal field. Central vision is served by the papillomacular bundle, which is spared until the very end — hence a patient with a tubular field can still read 6/6.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Disc signs (as opposed to field signs): increased and asymmetric cup-disc ratio, VERTICAL cup elongation, NOTCHING and thinning of the neuroretinal rim (violating the ISNT rule), BAYONETTING of vessels at the disc edge, NASAL SHIFTING of vessels, BARING OF THE CIRCUMLINEAR VESSEL, LAMINAR DOT sign, DRANCE (splinter) HAEMORRHAGE at the disc margin, and peripapillary atrophy.**
- **The temporal island survives longest because the nasal retina (which serves it) is damaged last — which is why patients retain a scrap of peripheral vision when central function has gone.**
- **Modern practice uses OCT RNFL and ganglion cell analysis alongside automated perimetry (Humphrey 24-2/10-2), because up to 40% of fibres are lost before a field defect appears.**
- **Symptoms are ABSENT until advanced — POAG is a silent disease, hence the value of screening relatives (siblings have ~10% risk).**
- **A useful early clinical clue is FREQUENT CHANGE OF PRESBYOPIC GLASSES, from failing accommodation as the ciliary muscle and its nerve supply are compromised by raised pressure.**
- **LASER TRABECULOPLASTY (argon or selective) is indicated in OPEN-angle glaucomas — POAG, PSEUDOEXFOLIATIVE and PIGMENTARY glaucoma — never in angle-closure, neovascular or congenital glaucoma. Pre-treat with pilocarpine/acetazolamide to blunt the transient IOP spike.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not a feature of the optic disc in glaucoma" → **Rönne's nasal step** (a field defect).

**REMEMBER:** *The nerve fibres arc around the macula and stop at the raphe — so the field loss arcs and steps.*

## 28. The Cherry-Red Spot

**PEARL:** A **MACULAR CHERRY-RED SPOT** occurs when the **surrounding retina becomes opaque/white while the FOVEA — which has no inner retinal layers and is nourished by the CHOROID — retains its normal red colour.** Causes: **CENTRAL RETINAL ARTERY OCCLUSION (the commonest), TAY–SACHS DISEASE, NIEMANN–PICK disease, SANDHOFF, GM1 gangliosidosis, sialidosis, METACHROMATIC LEUKODYSTROPHY, Farber disease, quinine toxicity and commotio retinae (Berlin's oedema).**

**UNDERSTAND:** The mechanism is identical in every case — **the ganglion cell layer is thick around the macula and absent at the fovea.** In CRAO the ganglion cells swell with ischaemic oedema; in the sphingolipidoses they swell with stored lipid. Either way the **thickened, opaque perifovea contrasts with the thin transparent fovea, through which the red choroid shows through.** **GAUCHER DISEASE is the sphingolipidosis that does NOT cause it.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **TAY–SACHS: HEXOSAMINIDASE A deficiency, GM2 ganglioside accumulation, autosomal recessive, commonest in ASHKENAZI JEWS — an exaggerated STARTLE RESPONSE to sound, progressive loss of motor milestones from ~6 months, MACROCEPHALY, cherry-red spot, and death by 3–5 years. NO HEPATOSPLENOMEGALY (the distinguishing feature from Niemann–Pick, which has hepatosplenomegaly and foam cells).**
- **In CRAO the cherry-red spot appears within hours and the retina becomes pale; a CILIORETINAL ARTERY (present in ~20%) can preserve central vision. Treat immediately (ocular massage, paracentesis, acetazolamide) and investigate for carotid disease, cardiac emboli and GIANT CELL ARTERITIS.**
- **The other ocular storage-disease signs: CORNEAL CLOUDING in HURLER and Hunter (mucopolysaccharidoses — note that HUNTER's is X-linked and classically SPARES the cornea), and a cherry-red spot without corneal clouding in the sphingolipidoses.**
- **Compare with LEUKOCORIA (a white pupillary reflex) — a completely different sign, whose causes are RETINOBLASTOMA (must be excluded first), CONGENITAL CATARACT, retinopathy of prematurity, persistent fetal vasculature, Coats' disease and toxocariasis.**
- **Any infant with developmental regression plus an abnormal fundus needs metabolic and enzyme studies plus genetic counselling — these disorders are autosomal recessive and carrier screening is available.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Cherry-red spot + regression + no organomegaly → **Tay–Sachs**.

**REMEMBER:** *The fovea has no inner retina to go white — so it stays red while everything around it turns pale.*

## 29. Gonioscopy

**PEARL:** **DIRECT gonioscopes (KOEPPE, Barkan, Swan–Jacob, Richardson) give a DIRECT, upright view of the angle** and are used supine, often under anaesthesia in children. **INDIRECT gonioprisms (GOLDMANN, ZEISS, Posner, Sussman) use mirrors and show a MIRROR IMAGE OF THE OPPOSITE angle** at the slit lamp. The **angle structures, from front (cornea) to back (iris), are: SCHWALBE'S LINE → TRABECULAR MESHWORK → SCLERAL SPUR → CILIARY BODY BAND → IRIS ROOT.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The angle cannot be seen directly because **light from it undergoes TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION at the tear–air interface of the cornea.** A goniolens eliminates that interface by replacing air with a medium of similar refractive index — a **steeply curved direct lens refracts the light out, while a mirrored indirect lens reflects it back into the observer's path.** Everything about gonioscopy follows from that single optical problem.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The SCLERAL SPUR is the key landmark — if it is visible, the angle is open. Grading uses SHAFFER (grade 4 = 35–45°, wide open; grade 0 = closed) or SPAETH.**
- **INDENTATION (dynamic) GONIOSCOPY with a four-mirror lens (Zeiss/Sussman) distinguishes APPOSITIONAL closure (which opens on indentation) from SYNECHIAL closure (permanent peripheral anterior synechiae) — this determines whether laser iridotomy will help.**
- **A GOLDMANN lens requires COUPLING FLUID and cannot indent; a ZEISS four-mirror lens uses the tear film alone and permits indentation.**
- **Uses: diagnosing angle-closure and secondary glaucomas, detecting NEOVASCULARISATION OF THE ANGLE (before iris rubeosis is visible), angle recession after trauma, foreign bodies and tumours, assessing peripheral anterior synechiae, and guiding goniotomy, trabeculoplasty and MIGS procedures.**
- **GONIOSCOPY IS MANDATORY IN EVERY GLAUCOMA PATIENT — you cannot classify glaucoma without it, and treatment differs completely between open and closed angles.**
- **Other landmarks worth knowing: SAMPAOLESI'S LINE (pigment anterior to Schwalbe's line) in PSEUDOEXFOLIATION and pigment dispersion.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Which are direct gonioscopes" → **Koeppe and Richardson**.

**REMEMBER:** *Direct lenses bend the light out, mirrored lenses bounce it back — and the scleral spur tells you the angle is open.*

## 30. Refractive Surgery

**PEARL:** **RADIAL KERATOTOMY (RK) is CONTRAINDICATED in anyone at risk of BLUNT TRAUMA — boxers, martial artists, military personnel** — because deep (90% depth) radial incisions permanently **WEAKEN the cornea and predispose to GLOBE RUPTURE.** It is obsolete anyway, superseded by **PRK, LASIK and SMILE.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Each procedure trades **corneal integrity for refractive change** in a different way. **RK cuts through nearly the full corneal thickness and never truly heals**, so the scars remain permanent lines of weakness; **LASIK creates a lamellar FLAP** which also never regains full tensile strength (hence flap displacement with trauma and the risk of ECTASIA); **PRK removes the epithelium and ablates the surface with no flap** (safest structurally but painful with slower recovery); and **SMILE extracts a lenticule through a small 2–4 mm incision**, preserving the most corneal biomechanical strength — which is why it is preferred for those in contact sports or the armed forces.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **LASIK is the commonest procedure for myopia: an excimer laser reshapes the stromal bed under a microkeratome or femtosecond flap. Advantages — rapid painless recovery. Complications — DRY EYE (the commonest), flap striae and displacement, DIFFUSE LAMELLAR KERATITIS ("sands of the Sahara"), epithelial ingrowth, glare/haloes, and the feared POST-LASIK ECTASIA.**
- **PREOPERATIVE ASSESSMENT is what prevents disaster: stable refraction for ≥1 year, age ≥18–21, adequate CORNEAL THICKNESS with a residual stromal bed ≥250–300 µm, CORNEAL TOPOGRAPHY/tomography to exclude KERATOCONUS or forme fruste ectasia, and normal tear function.**
- **CONTRAINDICATIONS: keratoconus or suspicious topography, thin corneas, uncontrolled autoimmune/connective tissue disease, PREGNANCY and lactation, uncontrolled diabetes, active ocular infection or severe dry eye, and unrealistic expectations.**
- **PHAKIC INTRAOCULAR LENSES (ICL) are used for HIGH myopia beyond the safe ablation range; refractive lens exchange is an option in the presbyopic age group.**
- **After ANY corneal refractive surgery, later CATARACT SURGERY needs SPECIAL IOL FORMULAE (HAIGIS-L, double-K, Masket) because keratometry no longer reflects true corneal power; IOP measured by applanation also reads FALSELY LOW on a thinned cornea.**
- **CROSS-LINKING (riboflavin + UV-A) is the treatment that halts keratoconus progression — a different procedure with the opposite aim of strengthening the cornea.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Boxer wanting refractive surgery → **avoid RK**.

**REMEMBER:** *Deep radial cuts never heal — the eye stays a burst waiting to happen.*

## 31. Lens-Induced Glaucomas

**PEARL:** **PHACOLYTIC GLAUCOMA — an elderly patient with a HYPERMATURE/MORGAGNIAN cataract, acute pain, high IOP, a DEEP anterior chamber with flare and white protein particles (PSEUDOHYPOPYON), and an OPEN angle on gonioscopy.** Contrast **PHACOMORPHIC glaucoma — an INTUMESCENT (swollen) cataract pushing the iris forward, giving a SHALLOW anterior chamber and a CLOSED angle.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The anterior chamber depth is the discriminator, and it follows the mechanism exactly. In **phacolytic glaucoma the lens is SHRUNKEN and leaking high-molecular-weight PROTEIN through an intact capsule; macrophages laden with that protein clog the TRABECULAR MESHWORK** — an outflow blockage with a normal-depth or deep chamber. In **phacomorphic glaucoma the lens is SWOLLEN and physically shallows the chamber**, closing the angle. **Both are cured by REMOVING THE LENS**, after medically lowering the pressure.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The other lens-related glaucomas: LENS-PARTICLE glaucoma (retained cortical material after surgery or trauma obstructing the meshwork), PHACOANAPHYLACTIC/phacoantigenic uveitis (a granulomatous autoimmune reaction to lens protein after capsule rupture), and LENS DISLOCATION into the anterior chamber or pupillary block (MARFAN — superotemporal subluxation; HOMOCYSTINURIA — inferonasal; WEILL–MARCHESANI — microspherophakia).**
- **MALIGNANT (AQUEOUS MISDIRECTION) GLAUCOMA occurs after intraocular surgery in a small eye: aqueous is diverted posteriorly into the vitreous, pushing the lens–iris diaphragm forward, giving a SHALLOW OR FLAT central AND peripheral anterior chamber with a HIGH IOP and a PATENT IRIDOTOMY. Treatment is CYCLOPLEGIA WITH ATROPINE (never miotics, which worsen it), aqueous suppressants, hyperosmotics, then laser hyaloidotomy or vitrectomy.**
- **Cataract maturity: IMMATURE (iris shadow present) → MATURE (complete opacity, no iris shadow) → HYPERMATURE (MORGAGNIAN — liquefied cortex with a sunken brown nucleus; or sclerotic/shrunken). Hypermature cataract also risks lens SUBLUXATION.**
- **Other complications of neglected cataract are precisely these glaucomas — which is the public-health argument for timely surgery rather than "waiting for it to ripen".**
- **Treatment principle: lower the IOP medically (avoiding miotics in phacomorphic disease and prostaglandins in inflamed eyes), control inflammation with steroids, then perform CATARACT EXTRACTION, which is definitive.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** High IOP + DEEP chamber + hypermature cataract → **phacolytic**; SHALLOW chamber + swollen lens → **phacomorphic**.

**REMEMBER:** *Deep chamber means leaking protein; shallow chamber means a swollen lens.*

## 32. Viscoelastics and Intraocular Lenses

**PEARL:** **OPHTHALMIC VISCOSURGICAL DEVICES (viscoelastics) MAINTAIN THE ANTERIOR CHAMBER during capsulorhexis and IOL implantation and PROTECT THE CORNEAL ENDOTHELIUM** — they do not injure it. The commonest are **SODIUM HYALURONATE and HYDROXYPROPYL METHYLCELLULOSE (HPMC).** **They MUST BE REMOVED at the end of surgery** — retained viscoelastic causes a **postoperative IOP SPIKE.** **RIGID IOLs are made of PMMA; FOLDABLE IOLs of silicone, acrylic, hydrogel or Collamer.**

**UNDERSTAND:** These agents work because they are **VISCOELASTIC — behaving as a viscous fluid at low shear (so they stay put and hold space) and as an elastic solid under stress (so they cushion the endothelium).** COHESIVE agents (high molecular weight, e.g. sodium hyaluronate) **maintain space and are easy to remove**; DISPERSIVE agents (lower molecular weight, e.g. HPMC) **coat and protect the endothelium but are harder to remove** — hence the "SOFT-SHELL TECHNIQUE" combining both.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The CORNEAL ENDOTHELIUM DOES NOT REGENERATE (cell density ~2500–3000/mm² in adults, falling with age) — below ~500 cells/mm² corneal decompensation and BULLOUS KERATOPATHY occur. Protecting it is the whole purpose of viscoelastics, and PSEUDOPHAKIC BULLOUS KERATOPATHY is a leading indication for corneal transplantation.**
- **Do NOT confuse viscoelastics with VITREOUS SUBSTITUTES/TAMPONADE AGENTS — SILICONE OIL, PERFLUOROPROPANE (C3F8) and SF6 GAS — which are used in RETINAL DETACHMENT surgery. Patients with intraocular gas must POSTURE and MUST NOT FLY OR HAVE NITROUS OXIDE ANAESTHESIA (expansion of the bubble raises IOP catastrophically).**
- **IOL positions: POSTERIOR CHAMBER, IN-THE-BAG placement is ideal; sulcus, iris-fixated, scleral-fixated and anterior chamber lenses are used when capsular support is absent (anterior chamber IOLs risk endothelial damage and glaucoma).**
- **Special IOLs: MULTIFOCAL and EDOF (spectacle independence, at the cost of glare and reduced contrast), TORIC (for astigmatism), and blue-light-filtering lenses.**
- **PHACOEMULSIFICATION with a foldable IOL through a small self-sealing incision is the modern standard — less astigmatism, faster rehabilitation — while manual small-incision cataract surgery (MSICS) remains the workhorse of high-volume Indian camps.**
- **POSTERIOR CAPSULAR OPACIFICATION (after-cataract) is the commonest late complication, treated with Nd:YAG capsulotomy; SQUARE-EDGED IOL design reduces it.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "True about viscoelastics" → **they prevent anterior chamber collapse during capsulorhexis and IOL placement**.

**REMEMBER:** *Viscous when still, elastic when pushed — they hold the chamber open and cushion the endothelium.*

## 33. Sudden Painless Loss of Vision

**PEARL:** **BILATERAL SUDDEN PAINLESS visual loss occurs in ADVANCED DIABETIC RETINOPATHY** (vitreous haemorrhage, tractional detachment, macular oedema) — a bilateral disease. The other causes of **SUDDEN PAINLESS loss: CENTRAL RETINAL ARTERY or VEIN OCCLUSION, VITREOUS HAEMORRHAGE, RETINAL DETACHMENT, ischaemic optic neuropathy, and — with pain on eye movement — optic neuritis.** **PAINFUL sudden loss: ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE GLAUCOMA, acute IRIDOCYCLITIS, keratitis, endophthalmitis and trauma.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The two questions — **painful or painless, unilateral or bilateral** — narrow the differential faster than any test, because they map onto anatomy: **PAIN implicates the richly innervated ANTERIOR SEGMENT (cornea, iris, ciliary body) or raised pressure; PAINLESS loss implicates the RETINA, OPTIC NERVE OR VITREOUS, which have no pain fibres.** And **BILATERAL simultaneous loss implies a systemic or bilateral disease** (diabetes, malignant hypertension, toxins like methanol, bilateral occipital infarction) rather than a local one.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **GRADUAL painless loss: CATARACT, open-angle GLAUCOMA, ARMD, diabetic maculopathy, refractive error, chronic simple glaucoma and compressive optic neuropathy.**
- **The KEY EMERGENCIES to recognise instantly: ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE (painful red eye, mid-dilated fixed pupil, hazy cornea), CRAO (a stroke equivalent — check for GIANT CELL ARTERITIS in the elderly), GIANT CELL ARTERITIS itself (start steroids before the biopsy), RETINAL DETACHMENT (flashes, floaters, a curtain, and a relative field defect — refer the same day), CHEMICAL INJURY (IRRIGATE FIRST, ask questions later — ALKALI is worse than acid because it saponifies membranes and penetrates deeply) and ENDOPHTHALMITIS.**
- **RETINAL DETACHMENT: RHEGMATOGENOUS (a break, in myopes and after cataract surgery or trauma — the commonest); TRACTIONAL (diabetes, ROP); EXUDATIVE (tumours, Coats' disease, VKH, hypertension). Macula-ON detachment is a surgical emergency; macula-OFF is less urgent because the acuity is already lost.**
- **VITREOUS HAEMORRHAGE presents with sudden floaters and a red haze with loss of the fundal glow — causes are PROLIFERATIVE DIABETIC RETINOPATHY (the commonest), retinal tear, vein occlusion, trauma and Eales' disease (in young Indian males, with periphlebitis).**
- **In any sudden visual loss, check the pupil for an RAPD (indicating an optic nerve or extensive retinal cause) and take the history for pain, floaters, flashes and preceding amaurosis fugax.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Cause of BILATERAL sudden painless loss" → **diabetic retinopathy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Pain means the front of the eye; painless means the back — and bilateral means the whole patient.*

## 34. Retinitis Pigmentosa

**PEARL:** **NIGHT BLINDNESS (nyctalopia) is an EARLY — usually the FIRST — symptom of RETINITIS PIGMENTOSA**, because **RODS degenerate before cones.** The triad on fundoscopy is **BONE-SPICULE PIGMENTATION in the mid-periphery, ATTENUATED ARTERIOLES, and a WAXY PALE OPTIC DISC.** The **ERG shows REDUCED AMPLITUDE OF BOTH a AND b WAVES (subnormal or extinguished) — and is abnormal LONG BEFORE symptoms**, making it the diagnostic test of choice.

**UNDERSTAND:** Rods are concentrated in the **mid-periphery and are responsible for scotopic vision**, so their loss produces **night blindness first, then a RING (annular) SCOTOMA that expands to TUNNEL VISION, with central acuity preserved until late** (cones at the fovea survive longest). The bone-spicule appearance is **RPE pigment migrating into the retina around degenerating perivascular tissue** — the visible footprint of that mid-peripheral loss.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Inheritance: autosomal recessive (commonest, ~ worst-to-intermediate prognosis), autosomal DOMINANT (the BEST prognosis), and X-LINKED RECESSIVE (the WORST, earliest and most severe). Rhodopsin gene mutations are the classic dominant form.**
- **Ocular associations: POSTERIOR SUBCAPSULAR CATARACT, CYSTOID MACULAR OEDEMA (treat with acetazolamide), open-angle glaucoma, keratoconus and myopia.**
- **Syndromic associations are heavily examined: USHER SYNDROME (RP + congenital SENSORINEURAL DEAFNESS — the commonest), BARDET–BIEDL (RP + obesity, polydactyly, hypogonadism, renal disease and learning difficulty), LAURENCE–MOON, REFSUM DISEASE (phytanic acid — treatable by dietary restriction and plasmapheresis!), KEARNS–SAYRE (mitochondrial — RP + ophthalmoplegia + heart block), ABETALIPOPROTEINAEMIA (Bassen–Kornzweig — treatable with vitamin E), and NEURONAL CEROID LIPOFUSCINOSIS.**
- **RETINITIS PIGMENTOSA SINE PIGMENTO lacks the pigment; ATYPICAL forms include sector RP and RP inversa (central).**
- **Management: no cure; vitamin A palmitate (controversial), sunglasses, treat cataract and CME, GENETIC COUNSELLING and low-vision aids. VORETIGENE NEPARVOVEC (RPE65 gene therapy) is approved for biallelic RPE65 mutations, and retinal prostheses are in use.**
- **DRIVING and occupational counselling matter — the field loss, not the acuity, is disabling.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not true about retinitis pigmentosa" → **night blindness is a late feature** (it is early).

**REMEMBER:** *Rods die first — so night vision and the mid-periphery go long before the acuity.*

## 35. Acute Anterior Uveitis

**PEARL:** **Acute pain, photophobia, blurred vision, CIRCUMCORNEAL (ciliary) CONGESTION, a SMALL IRREGULAR pupil (posterior synechiae), KERATIC PRECIPITATES on the corneal endothelium, and AQUEOUS FLARE AND CELLS = ACUTE ANTERIOR UVEITIS (IRIDOCYCLITIS).** Treatment is **TOPICAL STEROIDS plus a CYCLOPLEGIC (atropine/homatropine).**

**UNDERSTAND:** The **cycloplegic does three things at once**, which is why it is never optional: it **relieves pain by paralysing ciliary spasm**, it **prevents and breaks posterior synechiae by keeping the pupil mobile and dilated**, and it **reduces exudation by stabilising the blood–aqueous barrier.** Failing to dilate leads to a **fixed, festooned pupil, SECLUSIO PUPILLAE with IRIS BOMBÉ, and secondary angle-closure glaucoma.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **AQUEOUS FLARE (the Tyndall effect from leaked protein) is the earliest slit-lamp sign; CELLS indicate ACTIVE inflammation, while flare may persist after the inflammation settles. Grading of cells and flare guides therapy.**
- **KERATIC PRECIPITATES: fine/small and "stellate" in NON-GRANULOMATOUS disease (arranged in ARLT'S TRIANGLE inferiorly by convection currents); large "MUTTON-FAT" KPs in GRANULOMATOUS uveitis (sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, VKH, sympathetic ophthalmitis). Iris nodules: KOEPPE (at the pupillary margin) and BUSACCA (on the iris stroma).**
- **Causes: HLA-B27-associated (ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, IBD — typically ACUTE, unilateral, recurrent, alternating, often with HYPOPYON), JUVENILE IDIOPATHIC ARTHRITIS (CHRONIC, PAINLESS, WHITE eye — requires slit-lamp SCREENING because it is silent), sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, syphilis, herpes (with raised IOP and iris atrophy), Behçet's (a SHIFTING hypopyon), Fuchs' heterochromic iridocyclitis (heterochromia, stellate KPs, no synechiae, cataract).**
- **Complications: posterior synechiae and iris bombé, SECONDARY GLAUCOMA, COMPLICATED CATARACT (posterior subcapsular), cystoid macular oedema, band keratopathy and hypotony/phthisis.**
- **Investigate recurrent, bilateral, granulomatous or posterior uveitis: HLA-B27, chest X-ray, Mantoux/IGRA, VDRL/TPHA, ACE, and imaging as directed. Isolated first-episode unilateral acute anterior uveitis often needs no work-up.**
- **DISTINGUISH THE RED EYE: CONJUNCTIVITIS — diffuse peripheral redness, discharge, NORMAL pupil and vision. ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE — severe pain, haloes, HAZY cornea, MID-DILATED FIXED pupil, hard eye. KERATITIS — pain, photophobia, corneal opacity staining with fluorescein. EPISCLERITIS — mild, sectoral, blanches with phenylephrine, vision normal.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Circumcorneal congestion + irregular pupil + KPs → **acute anterior uveitis**.

**REMEMBER:** *Dilate the pupil and calm the ciliary body — steroids and atropine together, always.*

## 36. The Worth Four-Dot Test

**PEARL:** In **WORTH'S FOUR-DOT TEST** the patient wears a **RED lens over the RIGHT eye and a GREEN lens over the LEFT** and views **4 lights (1 red, 2 green, 1 white)**. **ALL 4 SEEN = normal binocular single vision (or abnormal retinal correspondence if a manifest squint is present). 2 RED LIGHTS = LEFT suppression. 3 GREEN LIGHTS = RIGHT suppression. 5 LIGHTS (2 red + 3 green) = DIPLOPIA.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The filters **dissociate the two eyes** — the red lens transmits only the red light (and the white light appears red to that eye), the green lens only the green lights. So the **number and colour of lights seen reports directly which eye's image the brain is using.** If the brain suppresses the right eye, only the **green-visible lights** remain — the two green dots plus the white one seen as green = 3 green. It is a test of **SENSORY fusion, not of alignment.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **SUPPRESSION is an active cortical mechanism to avoid diplopia in childhood squint — it prevents diplopia but causes AMBLYOPIA. Adults who develop a squint get DIPLOPIA instead, because the mature brain cannot suppress.**
- **The three grades of BINOCULAR SINGLE VISION (WORTH): (1) SIMULTANEOUS PERCEPTION, (2) FUSION, (3) STEREOPSIS — tested with the synoptophore, Worth four-dot/Bagolini glasses, and TITMUS FLY/TNO/Lang stereo tests respectively.**
- **ABNORMAL RETINAL CORRESPONDENCE — the fovea of one eye pairing with an extrafoveal point of the other — is a sensory adaptation to long-standing squint; it is the reason a patient with an obvious manifest deviation may still report all four lights.**
- **AMBLYOPIA ("lazy eye") is reduced best-corrected acuity with no organic cause: STRABISMIC, ANISOMETROPIC, STIMULUS-DEPRIVATION (congenital cataract, ptosis — the densest and most urgent) and MERIDIONAL. Management, in order: TREAT THE CAUSE (remove the cataract), CORRECT THE REFRACTIVE ERROR, then OCCLUSION (patching the good eye — the mainstay) or PENALISATION (atropine/optical). Levodopa–carbidopa and dichoptic VIDEO-GAME therapy are adjuncts.** ***SQUINT SURGERY IS NOT A TREATMENT FOR AMBLYOPIA*** — it is done for alignment AFTER amblyopia therapy.
- **Treatment works best under 7–8 years (the critical period) and is most effective under 3; the OLDER the child, the LONGER the treatment needed. Monitor the OCCLUDED eye for reverse amblyopia.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** 3 green lights → **right suppression** (the red lens is over the right eye).

**REMEMBER:** *Red lens right — if the reds vanish, the right eye is suppressed.*

## 37. Congenital Cataract and Leukocoria

**PEARL:** A **visually significant CONGENITAL/INFANTILE CATARACT requires SURGERY AS EARLY AS POSSIBLE — ideally by 6 WEEKS for a UNILATERAL and by 8–10 WEEKS for BILATERAL cataracts** — because delay causes **irreversible DEPRIVATION AMBLYOPIA and nystagmus.** **LEUKOCORIA must always be investigated to exclude RETINOBLASTOMA.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The visual system develops **only if the retina receives a formed image during the critical period** — cortical connections that are not stimulated are permanently lost. A dense unilateral cataract is the **most damaging visual insult in infancy** because the fellow eye competes and wins outright, which is why unilateral cases need earlier surgery AND aggressive postoperative patching. **Bilateral cataracts, though visually worse, cause symmetrical deprivation and therefore less amblyopia.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Causes of congenital cataract: IDIOPATHIC (one third), HEREDITARY (usually autosomal dominant, one third), INTRAUTERINE INFECTION (RUBELLA — a NUCLEAR PEARLY cataract, often with a "salt-and-pepper" retinopathy, PDA and deafness; also toxoplasmosis, CMV, herpes, varicella), METABOLIC (GALACTOSAEMIA — an "OIL-DROPLET" cataract that may REVERSE with dietary galactose exclusion; hypocalcaemia; diabetes mellitus — a "SNOWFLAKE" cataract), and syndromic (DOWN, Lowe, myotonic dystrophy).**
- **Work-up: red reflex screening at birth and at every well-baby visit, family history, TORCH serology, urine for reducing substances (galactosaemia), calcium and phosphate, and paediatric assessment.**
- **After lens removal in infancy, the eye is APHAKIC — correct IMMEDIATELY with CONTACT LENSES or spectacles (primary IOL implantation is usually deferred beyond ~1–2 years because of the changing axial length and high inflammatory response), and begin OCCLUSION THERAPY.** Long-term risks include **APHAKIC/SECONDARY GLAUCOMA** — lifelong follow-up is essential.
- **The leukocoria differential: RETINOBLASTOMA (the one that kills), congenital cataract, RETINOPATHY OF PREMATURITY, PERSISTENT FETAL VASCULATURE, COATS' DISEASE (unilateral, boys, telangiectasia with massive exudation), toxocariasis and coloboma.**
- **RETINOBLASTOMA: presents with LEUKOCORIA (60%) and STRABISMUS (20%) under 3 years; RB1 gene on 13q14 with the two-hit hypothesis; CALCIFICATION on ultrasound/CT is characteristic; FLEXNER–WINTERSTEINER ROSETTES on histology. Treatment is chemoreduction with focal therapy, and enucleation for advanced disease — BIOPSY IS CONTRAINDICATED. Bilateral/familial cases carry a risk of second malignancies (osteosarcoma, pinealoblastoma — "trilateral retinoblastoma").**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** 2-month-old with leukocoria from cataract → **immediate surgery**.

**REMEMBER:** *The visual cortex will not wait — a dense infantile cataract must come out within weeks.*

## 38. Post-Operative Endophthalmitis

**PEARL:** **Diminished vision with pain, ANTERIOR CHAMBER CELLS/HYPOPYON and VITREOUS HAZE obscuring the fundus, within DAYS TO 6 WEEKS of cataract surgery = ACUTE POSTOPERATIVE ENDOPHTHALMITIS**, most often caused by ***STAPHYLOCOCCUS EPIDERMIDIS* (coagulase-negative staphylococci — the commonest), *S. aureus*, streptococci and Gram-negatives** — organisms from the **patient's own lids and conjunctiva.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Because the source is the **ocular surface flora carried in at the time of surgery**, the single most effective preventive measure is **5% POVIDONE-IODINE to the conjunctiva and lid margins before surgery** — more effective than any antibiotic — supplemented by **INTRACAMERAL CEFUROXIME at the end of surgery (the ESCRS study showed a five-fold reduction).** Understanding the source also explains why **treating blepharitis and dacryocystitis BEFORE elective surgery is mandatory.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Management follows the ENDOPHTHALMITIS VITRECTOMY STUDY: perform a VITREOUS TAP AND INJECT INTRAVITREAL ANTIBIOTICS (VANCOMYCIN + CEFTAZIDIME/amikacin) — and if the presenting vision is PERCEPTION OF LIGHT ONLY, proceed to IMMEDIATE PARS PLANA VITRECTOMY. Systemic antibiotics alone are inadequate (poor intraocular penetration).**
- **DELAYED/CHRONIC endophthalmitis (>6 weeks, indolent, granulomatous, with a white capsular plaque and a partial response to steroids) is classically ***CUTIBACTERIUM (PROPIONIBACTERIUM) ACNES*** or fungal — treatment requires capsulectomy/IOL removal.**
- **BLEB-ASSOCIATED endophthalmitis after trabeculectomy is often STREPTOCOCCAL or *Haemophilus* and is aggressive; POST-INTRAVITREAL-INJECTION endophthalmitis is usually streptococcal.**
- **ENDOGENOUS endophthalmitis (haematogenous) occurs in IV drug users, diabetics, the immunosuppressed and those with indwelling lines — think ***CANDIDA*** (fluffy white "string-of-pearls" vitreous lesions) and *Klebsiella* (associated with LIVER ABSCESS in diabetics in Asia) — and requires a systemic infection work-up.**
- **The differential is TOXIC ANTERIOR SEGMENT SYNDROME (TASS): a STERILE inflammation appearing within 12–24 HOURS, LIMITED TO THE ANTERIOR SEGMENT with diffuse "limbus-to-limbus" corneal oedema, MINIMAL PAIN, no vitritis, and a good response to steroids — caused by contaminated instruments or solutions.**
- **Prognosis depends on the organism (coagulase-negative staphylococci do well; streptococci and Gram-negatives do badly) and on the SPEED of intravitreal therapy — this is a same-hour emergency.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two weeks post-cataract surgery with vitritis → **endophthalmitis, staphylococcal**.

**REMEMBER:** *The bugs come from the patient's own lids — povidone-iodine before, intravitreal antibiotics after.*

## 39. The Visual Pathway — Neuron Order

**PEARL:** In the visual pathway, **PHOTORECEPTORS (rods and cones) are the FIRST-order neurons, BIPOLAR cells the SECOND-order, and GANGLION CELLS the THIRD-order neurons** — whose axons form the nerve fibre layer, optic nerve, chiasma and tract, synapsing in the **LATERAL GENICULATE BODY (the fourth-order neuron)**, which projects via the optic radiation to the **primary visual cortex (area 17) in the occipital lobe.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The **retina is an outpouching of the brain**, so it contains an entire three-neuron chain before the signal ever leaves the eye — which is why the **optic "nerve" is really a CNS TRACT** (myelinated by oligodendrocytes, invested in meninges, incapable of regeneration, and subject to demyelination in MS). It also explains why **raised intracranial pressure is transmitted along its sheath to produce PAPILLOEDEMA.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Decussation rule: fibres from the NASAL retina (temporal field) CROSS at the chiasma; TEMPORAL retinal fibres stay ipsilateral. Hence CHIASMAL compression (PITUITARY ADENOMA from below, craniopharyngioma from above) causes BITEMPORAL HEMIANOPIA — starting SUPERIORLY with a pituitary tumour and INFERIORLY with a craniopharyngioma.**
- **Localise the lesion by the field defect: OPTIC NERVE — unilateral field loss with an RAPD; CHIASMA — bitemporal hemianopia; OPTIC TRACT — INCONGRUOUS homonymous hemianopia with a contralateral RAPD; TEMPORAL radiation (MEYER'S LOOP) — superior quadrantanopia ("PIE IN THE SKY"); PARIETAL radiation — inferior quadrantanopia; OCCIPITAL CORTEX — CONGRUOUS homonymous hemianopia WITH MACULAR SPARING (dual blood supply from the middle cerebral artery).**
- **The LGB has 6 layers: layers 1 and 2 MAGNOCELLULAR (motion, contrast, from the contralateral and ipsilateral eye respectively) and 3–6 PARVOCELLULAR (colour and fine detail).**
- **The PUPILLARY LIGHT REFLEX leaves the tract BEFORE the LGB (to the pretectal nucleus and thence bilaterally to the Edinger–Westphal nuclei) — which is why lesions BEHIND the LGB (radiation, cortex) cause field loss WITHOUT any pupillary abnormality, and why cortical blindness has NORMALLY REACTING PUPILS.**
- **The MACULA is represented at the OCCIPITAL POLE and occupies a disproportionately large cortical area (cortical magnification).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Ganglion cells are which order neuron" → **third**.

**REMEMBER:** *Three neurons inside the retina before the signal ever leaves the eye.*

## 40. Macular Dystrophies and the Electrodiagnostic Tests

**PEARL:** A young patient with **BILATERAL CENTRAL VISION LOSS, a family history, and NORMAL ERG AND EOG (early) = STARGARDT DISEASE (fundus flavimaculatus)** — autosomal recessive, **ABCA4 gene**, with **yellow "PISCIFORM" FLECKS at the level of the RPE, a "BEATEN-BRONZE" macula, and a "DARK/SILENT CHOROID" on fluorescein angiography.** Contrast **BEST'S VITELLIFORM DYSTROPHY: autosomal DOMINANT, BEST1 gene, an "EGG-YOLK" macular lesion, NORMAL ERG but a MARKEDLY ABNORMAL (reduced) EOG with an Arden ratio <1.5.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The two electrodiagnostic tests probe different layers, which is exactly why they separate these diseases. **The ERG tests the PHOTORECEPTORS AND INNER RETINA MASS (a-wave from photoreceptors, b-wave from bipolar/Müller cells)** — so it is normal in a purely macular disease (the macula is a tiny fraction of retinal area) and grossly abnormal in a WIDESPREAD disease like retinitis pigmentosa. **The EOG tests the RPE–photoreceptor complex globally** — so it is selectively abnormal in Best's disease, which is a primary RPE channelopathy.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **"A NORMAL ERG WITH AN ABNORMAL EOG IS BEST'S DISEASE" — the single most reliable exam discriminator, since the EOG is abnormal even in ASYMPTOMATIC CARRIERS.**
- **In Stargardt disease the DARK CHOROID results from LIPOFUSCIN (A2E) accumulation in the RPE masking the choroidal fluorescence — the visible consequence of ABCA4 failure to clear retinoid by-products. Patients should AVOID HIGH-DOSE VITAMIN A supplements, which accelerate the accumulation.**
- **The ERG is EXTINGUISHED in RETINITIS PIGMENTOSA (both a and b waves reduced) and in total retinal disease; a NEGATIVE ERG (a preserved a-wave with a reduced b-wave) indicates INNER retinal dysfunction — CONGENITAL STATIONARY NIGHT BLINDNESS, X-linked retinoschisis, or central retinal artery occlusion.**
- **The ERG is NORMAL in optic nerve disease (the ganglion cells contribute little); the VEP (visually evoked potential) is the test of OPTIC NERVE conduction — DELAYED LATENCY with preserved amplitude is the signature of DEMYELINATION, and the VEP remains abnormal for life after optic neuritis, which is why it can reveal a clinically silent second lesion in suspected MS.**
- **Other inherited maculopathies: cone dystrophy (photophobia, poor colour vision, a bull's-eye macula, abnormal PHOTOPIC ERG) and X-linked juvenile RETINOSCHISIS (a spoke-wheel foveal schisis with a negative ERG).**
- **There is no proven treatment for Stargardt disease — genetic counselling, sunglasses and low-vision aids; gene and stem-cell therapies are in trial.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Central loss, family history, normal ERG and EOG → **Stargardt disease**.

**REMEMBER:** *ERG reads the whole retina, EOG reads the pigment epithelium — Best's ruins only the EOG.*

## 41. Iris Nodules and Ocular Leprosy

**PEARL:** **IRIS PEARLS — small chalky-white glistening nodules on the iris — are PATHOGNOMONIC OF LEPROSY** (*Mycobacterium leprae*, lepromatous type). Distinguish them from **BRUSHFIELD SPOTS (white speckles in a concentric ring on the peripheral iris — DOWN SYNDROME, and normal in some blue irides), LISCH NODULES (melanocytic hamartomas — NEUROFIBROMATOSIS 1), and KOEPPE (pupillary margin) and BUSACCA (stromal) NODULES of GRANULOMATOUS UVEITIS.**

**UNDERSTAND:** *M. leprae* prefers **COOL tissue**, which is precisely why it attacks the **ANTERIOR segment of the eye (the coolest ocular tissue), the superficial nerves, the ear lobes and the nose** — and why the **posterior segment is characteristically spared.** Its neurotropism explains the rest of the ocular disease: **corneal nerve involvement causes anaesthesia; facial (VII) nerve damage causes LAGOPHTHALMOS; and autonomic damage causes miosis and iris atrophy.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Ocular leprosy features: THICKENED BEADED CORNEAL NERVES, superficial punctate keratitis with a pannus starting SUPERIORLY, reduced CORNEAL SENSATION, chronic low-grade ANTERIOR UVEITIS with iris atrophy and a MIOTIC pupil (sympathetic denervation), iris pearls, complicated cataract, and scleritis/episcleritis.**
- **The BLINDING complications are largely from the NERVE damage, not the bacterium: LAGOPHTHALMOS from facial nerve palsy → EXPOSURE KERATITIS, compounded by CORNEAL ANAESTHESIA (so the patient does not feel the injury) and reduced tear secretion — hence the necessity of lid surgery (tarsorrhaphy, temporalis transfer), lubricants and patient education.**
- **Leprosy remains a leading infectious cause of blindness in endemic regions, and eye examination is part of every leprosy assessment.**
- **The other iris and pupil signs worth carrying: KAYSER–FLEISCHER RING (Wilson's — copper in Descemet's membrane, best seen on GONIOSCOPY, with a SUNFLOWER CATARACT); iris HETEROCHROMIA (Fuchs' heterochromic iridocyclitis, Horner's syndrome — congenital, Waardenburg, siderosis); RUBEOSIS IRIDIS (new vessels — diabetes, CRVO); and iris NAEVUS/melanoma.**
- **Treatment is WHO multidrug therapy plus management of reactions — TYPE 1 (reversal) and TYPE 2 (erythema nodosum leprosum, which itself causes ACUTE IRIDOCYCLITIS and requires steroids or thalidomide).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Lepromatous leprosy + iris nodules → **iris pearls**.

**REMEMBER:** *The bacillus loves cool tissue — so leprosy takes the front of the eye and the nerves that protect it.*

## 42. Preseptal versus Orbital Cellulitis

**PEARL:** **PRESEPTAL (periorbital) CELLULITIS: lid swelling, erythema and fever, but NORMAL VISUAL ACUITY, NO PROPTOSIS, NO CHEMOSIS and FULL, PAINLESS EYE MOVEMENTS.** **ORBITAL CELLULITIS (posterior to the orbital septum): PROPTOSIS, OPHTHALMOPLEGIA WITH PAINFUL eye movements, CHEMOSIS, reduced acuity and an RAPD** — a sight- and life-threatening emergency.

**UNDERSTAND:** The **ORBITAL SEPTUM — a fibrous sheet from the periosteum of the orbital rim to the tarsal plates — is the anatomical barrier that separates the two.** Infection anterior to it can only swell the lid; infection behind it lies within a **rigid bony cone**, so it produces proptosis, compresses the optic nerve and restricts the muscles, and can track posteriorly through valveless veins into the CAVERNOUS SINUS and cranium.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The commonest source of ORBITAL cellulitis is ETHMOID SINUSITIS spreading through the paper-thin LAMINA PAPYRACEA (especially in children); preseptal cellulitis usually follows skin trauma, insect bite, a stye, dacryocystitis or conjunctivitis. Organisms: *S. aureus* (including MRSA), *Streptococcus* species, *H. influenzae* (now rare after Hib vaccination), and anaerobes.**
- **URGENT CT ORBITS AND SINUSES with contrast is mandatory whenever orbital cellulitis is suspected, to detect a SUBPERIOSTEAL ABSCESS; treat with ADMISSION and IV BROAD-SPECTRUM ANTIBIOTICS, with surgical drainage for an abscess or failure to improve in 24–48 hours. Preseptal cellulitis is usually treated with oral antibiotics as an outpatient (admit infants and the toxic).**
- **Complications of orbital cellulitis: optic neuropathy and blindness, orbital abscess, CAVERNOUS SINUS THROMBOSIS (BILATERAL signs, cranial nerve III, IV, V1, V2 and VI palsies, and systemic toxicity), meningitis and brain abscess.**
- **In a DIABETIC or IMMUNOCOMPROMISED patient with orbital signs and a BLACK NECROTIC ESCHAR on the palate or nasal mucosa, think RHINO-ORBITAL-CEREBRAL MUCORMYCOSIS — an emergency requiring urgent surgical debridement and LIPOSOMAL AMPHOTERICIN B.**
- **The clinical rule: ANY reduction of vision, proptosis, chemosis or restricted/painful movement moves the diagnosis behind the septum until imaging proves otherwise.**
- **CHANDLER'S CLASSIFICATION grades the spectrum: I preseptal, II orbital cellulitis, III subperiosteal abscess, IV orbital abscess, V cavernous sinus thrombosis.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Lid swelling with normal acuity, no proptosis and full movements → **preseptal cellulitis**.

**REMEMBER:** *The septum decides everything — in front is a swollen lid, behind is a threatened eye.*

## 43. Penetrating Ocular Injury

**PEARL:** In a **PENETRATING eye injury with a retained foreign body, DO NOT REMOVE THE FOREIGN BODY IN THE EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT.** The correct initial sequence is: **PRIMARY SURVEY of the patient, DOCUMENT VISUAL ACUITY in both eyes, SHIELD (never patch or pad) the eye, give TETANUS PROPHYLAXIS and PROPHYLACTIC ANTIBIOTICS, KEEP THE PATIENT NIL BY MOUTH, and obtain a CT SCAN to localise the foreign body** — removal is done under controlled conditions in theatre.

**UNDERSTAND:** A protruding foreign body may be **tamponading the wound**; pulling it out can cause **EXTRUSION OF INTRAOCULAR CONTENTS (vitreous, uvea, retina) and catastrophic haemorrhage.** Similarly, **any pressure on a ruptured globe — from a pad, from lid retraction, from tonometry or even from a crying, squeezing child — can expel the contents.** So the entire emergency management is designed around **protecting the globe from pressure until the surgeon is ready.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- ***MRI IS ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED WITH A SUSPECTED METALLIC INTRAOCULAR FOREIGN BODY*** — the magnet can move it and destroy the eye. CT is the imaging of choice; ultrasound is relatively contraindicated in an open globe (pressure risk).
- **Avoid: topical ointments (which can enter the eye), tonometry, gonioscopy, forceful lid opening, and any manipulation. Give ANTIEMETICS to prevent Valsalva.**
- **Intraocular foreign bodies retained long-term cause SIDEROSIS BULBI (iron — iris heterochromia, pupillary mydriasis, cataract and pigmentary retinopathy with an extinguished ERG) and CHALCOSIS (copper — a SUNFLOWER cataract and KAYSER–FLEISCHER-like ring); pure copper causes fulminant purulent endophthalmitis. Organic/vegetable matter carries the highest infection risk.**
- **The dreaded late complication is SYMPATHETIC OPHTHALMITIS — a bilateral granulomatous panuveitis in the uninjured "sympathising" eye, typically 2 weeks to 3 months after a penetrating injury involving UVEAL PROLAPSE (or intraocular surgery). Prevented by prompt meticulous repair, and by ENUCLEATION OF A BLIND INJURED EYE WITHIN 10–14 DAYS if it is beyond salvage. Histology shows DALEN–FUCHS NODULES.**
- **Prognostic signs after open-globe injury: presenting visual acuity (the strongest), afferent pupillary defect, wound location and size, and the presence of vitreous haemorrhage or retinal detachment (the Ocular Trauma Score).**
- **CHEMICAL injury is the one exception to "assess first": IRRIGATE IMMEDIATELY AND COPIOUSLY BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE, including before measuring acuity, and continue until the pH normalises.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not done initially" → **removal of the foreign body**.

**REMEMBER:** *Shield it, scan it, and never pull anything out at the bedside.*

## 44. Types of Optic Atrophy

**PEARL:** **GLAUCOMATOUS optic atrophy = deep CUPPING with thinning/notching of the neuroretinal rim, NASAL SHIFTING of vessels and bayonetting.** **PRIMARY optic atrophy — a chalky-WHITE disc with SHARPLY DEFINED margins and a visible lamina cribrosa (retrobulbar neuritis, optic nerve compression/tumour, trauma, tabes, toxic/nutritional).** **SECONDARY optic atrophy — a dirty-grey disc with BLURRED, indistinct margins and gliosis, following papilloedema or papillitis.** **CONSECUTIVE optic atrophy — a waxy pale disc with ATTENUATED ARTERIOLES, following RETINAL disease (retinitis pigmentosa, extensive photocoagulation, CRAO).**

**UNDERSTAND:** The disc appearance records **what happened upstream.** If the nerve simply died without swelling, the tissue clears away neatly — **sharp margins, primary atrophy.** If the disc was previously **SWOLLEN**, the resolving oedema leaves **glial proliferation and a blurred, dirty margin — secondary atrophy.** And if the disease began in the RETINA, the ganglion cells die from the far end and the **retinal vessels attenuate because there is no retina left to perfuse** — consecutive atrophy.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Optic atrophy is IRREVERSIBLE — the visual loss is permanent, so the value of recognising it lies in identifying and treating the CAUSE (a compressive lesion is the one that must never be missed: any unexplained optic atrophy needs NEUROIMAGING).**
- **Assess optic nerve function by FOUR parameters, not acuity alone: VISUAL ACUITY, COLOUR VISION, PUPILLARY REACTION (RAPD) and VISUAL FIELDS — colour vision and the RAPD are the most sensitive.**
- **Causes to run through: glaucoma, compressive (pituitary adenoma, meningioma, aneurysm, thyroid eye disease), inflammatory (optic neuritis, MS), ischaemic (AION, giant cell arteritis), toxic/nutritional (ETHAMBUTOL, methanol, tobacco–alcohol, B12 deficiency — typically with a CENTROCAECAL scotoma and BILATERAL symmetrical involvement), hereditary (LEBER'S HEREDITARY OPTIC NEUROPATHY — mitochondrial, young men, sequential bilateral loss with disc telangiectasia; dominant optic atrophy), traumatic, and raised intracranial pressure.**
- **CONSECUTIVE vs GLAUCOMATOUS: glaucoma cups; consecutive atrophy pales with thin arterioles and NO cupping.**
- **A pale disc with a normal-looking field and acuity should raise the possibility of a physiologically pale but healthy disc, myelinated fibres or optic disc drusen — do not over-diagnose.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Cupping + rim thinning + nasal vessel shift → **glaucomatous optic atrophy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Sharp and white came from behind; blurred and grey was swollen first; pale with thin vessels came from the retina.*

## 45. Grades of Corneal Opacity

**PEARL:** **NEBULAR corneal opacity — a faint haze; the IRIS DETAILS ARE STILL VISIBLE through it. MACULAR opacity — denser; iris details are OBSCURED but the PUPILLARY MARGIN IS STILL VISIBLE. LEUCOMATOUS opacity — dense white; NEITHER the iris NOR the pupillary margin can be seen.** **ADHERENT LEUCOMA** is a leucoma with iris incarcerated in it after perforation.

**UNDERSTAND:** The grading is simply a **measure of how much light still gets through**, and it maps directly onto both the depth of the original ulcer and the visual prognosis. A **NEBULAR opacity in the pupillary area can paradoxically cause MORE visual disturbance than a dense leucoma** — because irregular refraction and light scatter through a semi-transparent scar degrade the image, whereas a dense opacity simply blocks part of the pupil and light can still enter around it.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Corneal healing depends on depth: damage confined to the EPITHELIUM heals WITHOUT scarring; anything breaching BOWMAN'S MEMBRANE heals with a permanent scar (Bowman's layer does NOT regenerate). Descemet's membrane can regenerate; a bulge of intact Descemet's through a deep ulcer is a DESCEMETOCELE — an impending perforation.**
- **Management of visually significant opacity: OPTICAL IRIDECTOMY (for a central opacity with clear periphery), PENETRATING or LAMELLAR KERATOPLASTY, phototherapeutic keratectomy for superficial opacities, and CORNEAL TATTOOING for cosmesis in a blind eye.**
- **The cornea is avascular and immune-privileged, which is why KERATOPLASTY IS THE MOST SUCCESSFUL OF ALL TRANSPLANTS — but VASCULARISED corneas (chemical burns, trachoma, herpetic scarring) lose that privilege and reject.**
- **Corneal transparency depends on: regular lamellar collagen arrangement, RELATIVE DEHYDRATION maintained by the ENDOTHELIAL Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase PUMP and the epithelial barrier, avascularity, and an absence of myelinated nerves. Endothelial failure → stromal oedema → bullous keratopathy.**
- **Anatomical layers: epithelium, BOWMAN'S layer, stroma (90% of thickness), DUA'S layer, DESCEMET'S membrane, endothelium.**
- **Corneal blindness is a leading preventable cause of blindness in India — trauma, infective keratitis (often after agricultural injury), vitamin A deficiency and trachoma — and EYE DONATION/eye banking is the public-health answer.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Only the pupillary margin is visible" → **macular opacity**.

**REMEMBER:** *Nebula shows the iris, macula shows only the pupil edge, leucoma shows nothing.*

## 46. Asteroid Hyalosis

**PEARL:** **Numerous tiny yellow-white spherical opacities suspended in the vitreous that MOVE WITH EYE MOVEMENT BUT DO NOT SETTLE when the eye is still = ASTEROID HYALOSIS** — CALCIUM-CONTAINING (calcium pyrophosphate/soap) particles bound to the vitreous collagen framework. It is **UNILATERAL IN ~75%, RARELY SYMPTOMATIC, commoner in MEN and with AGE, DIABETES, hypertension and hyperlipidaemia — and it is NOT associated with MYOPIA.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The particles **do not sediment because they are BOUND TO THE VITREOUS COLLAGEN FIBRILS**, not free-floating — so they swing back to their original position when movement stops. That single observation distinguishes them from **SYNCHYSIS SCINTILLANS (cholesterolosis bulbi)**, where free **CHOLESTEROL crystals in a LIQUEFIED vitreous DO settle inferiorly with gravity**, are golden and refractile, are usually BILATERAL, and follow previous trauma or vitreous haemorrhage in a damaged eye.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **The striking clinical paradox: the fundus can be almost impossible for the examiner to see, yet the patient's vision is essentially NORMAL — reassurance is the treatment. Vitrectomy is reserved for the rare case where it obscures treatment of another retinal disease.**
- **It interferes with retinal imaging, laser photocoagulation and biometry, and can produce false readings on OCT and ultrasound.**
- **Contrast the causes of FLOATERS: POSTERIOR VITREOUS DETACHMENT (sudden floaters with FLASHES — must be examined to exclude a RETINAL TEAR), vitreous haemorrhage, vitritis (uveitis, endophthalmitis), asteroid hyalosis, and benign muscae volitantes.**
- **NEW-ONSET FLOATERS WITH FLASHES OF LIGHT, OR A "SHOWER" OF FLOATERS, OR A CURTAIN/SHADOW DEMAND SAME-DAY DILATED FUNDUS EXAMINATION** — 10–15% of acute symptomatic PVDs have a retinal tear, which is treatable by laser before it progresses to detachment.
- **Vitreous composition: 99% water, with type II collagen and hyaluronic acid; it liquefies (SYNERESIS) with age and myopia, which is why PVD occurs earlier in myopes.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All true EXCEPT" → **common in myopes** (it is not).

**REMEMBER:** *Asteroids are tethered and never fall; synchysis scintillans is free and sinks.*

## 47. Sclera — Anatomy, Scleritis and Staphyloma

**PEARL:** The sclera has three layers from outside in: **EPISCLERA → SCLERA PROPER → LAMINA FUSCA (the innermost, facing the choroid).** It is **THINNEST JUST BEHIND THE INSERTIONS OF THE RECTUS MUSCLES (~0.3 mm)** and thickest around the optic nerve (~1 mm) — which is why **traumatic globe rupture occurs at the limbus, at the rectus insertions, or at the equator OPPOSITE the site of impact, and rarely at the point of impact itself.** **SCLEROMALACIA PERFORANS is NECROTISING SCLERITIS WITHOUT INFLAMMATION, in elderly women with long-standing RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Blunt trauma **compresses the globe antero-posteriorly and expands it equatorially**, so it bursts where the coats are weakest — the thin sclera behind muscle insertions and the limbus — and the **shock wave propagates AWAY from the point of impact**, which is why the rupture is typically contrecoup. **Scleromalacia perforans is painless and quiet precisely because it is an OBLITERATIVE VASCULITIS of the deep episcleral vessels** — there is no inflammation left to hurt, only progressive melting with the blue-black uvea showing through.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **SCLERITIS vs EPISCLERITIS: SCLERITIS — severe BORING pain that WAKES the patient and radiates to the jaw/temple, a deep BLUISH-VIOLACEOUS hue, vessels that DO NOT BLANCH with PHENYLEPHRINE, tenderness, and a strong association with SYSTEMIC DISEASE (rheumatoid arthritis, GPA/Wegener's, polyarteritis nodosa, relapsing polychondritis, SLE, TB). EPISCLERITIS — mild discomfort, sectoral bright-red inflammation that BLANCHES with phenylephrine, benign and self-limiting.**
- **Anterior scleritis classification: NON-NECROTISING (DIFFUSE — the COMMONEST TYPE OVERALL; and NODULAR) and NECROTISING (with inflammation — the most destructive, with a high risk of visual loss and of underlying systemic vasculitis; and WITHOUT inflammation = scleromalacia perforans).** **POSTERIOR SCLERITIS is easily missed — pain, proptosis, disc oedema, exudative detachment, and the "T-SIGN" on B-scan ultrasound.**
- **Treatment: scleritis needs SYSTEMIC therapy — oral NSAIDs, then systemic STEROIDS and immunosuppressives/biologics for necrotising disease. TOPICAL STEROIDS ALONE ARE INADEQUATE, and PERIOCULAR STEROID INJECTIONS ARE AVOIDED in necrotising scleritis (risk of perforation). Necrotising scleritis mandates a full systemic work-up — it may be the first sign of a life-threatening vasculitis.**
- **STAPHYLOMA is an ectasia of cornea or sclera LINED BY UVEAL TISSUE: ANTERIOR (after a sloughed corneal ulcer, lined by iris), INTERCALARY (at the limbus, lined by iris root/anterior ciliary body), CILIARY (up to 8 mm behind the limbus, lined by ciliary body — classically in GLAUCOMA), EQUATORIAL, and POSTERIOR (pathological myopia).** **The common factors are RAISED IOP and a THINNED coat.**
- **BLUE SCLERA occurs when the sclera is abnormally thin so the uvea shows through: OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA, Ehlers–Danlos, Marfan syndrome, and in normal infants.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Rheumatoid arthritis with painless scleral melting → **scleromalacia perforans**; least likely rupture site → **the point of impact (lateral limbus)**.

**REMEMBER:** *Thinnest behind the muscle insertions — so that is where it bursts, opposite the blow.*

## 48. Ocular Toxoplasmosis

**PEARL:** A **focus of fluffy WHITE RETINITIS adjacent to an OLD PIGMENTED CHORIORETINAL SCAR, seen through a hazy vitreous — the "HEADLIGHT IN THE FOG" appearance — is OCULAR TOXOPLASMOSIS**, the commonest cause of **POSTERIOR UVEITIS in immunocompetent people (20–60%).** Reactivation at the edge of an old congenital scar ("satellite lesion") is the rule.

**UNDERSTAND:** The organism persists as **tissue CYSTS in the retina after congenital or acquired infection**; when cysts rupture, released bradyzoites provoke intense focal retinitis with **overlying vitritis — which is what produces the "fog"**, while the active white lesion is the "headlight". Because the disease is a **reactivation at an old scar**, the presence of a pigmented scar beside an active lesion is virtually diagnostic and explains its recurrent, unilateral pattern.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Treatment is indicated for lesions THREATENING THE MACULA, OPTIC DISC or a major vessel, for severe vitritis, or in the immunocompromised — small peripheral lesions in an immunocompetent patient are self-limiting over 6–8 weeks. Classic therapy: PYRIMETHAMINE + SULFADIAZINE + FOLINIC ACID, or cotrimoxazole; add ORAL PREDNISOLONE 24–48 HOURS AFTER starting antimicrobials. ***STEROIDS MUST NEVER BE GIVEN ALONE*** — unopposed, they cause fulminant necrotising retinitis.**
- **In HIV/immunosuppression the picture differs: MULTIFOCAL or BILATERAL lesions, often WITHOUT a pre-existing scar and with LESS vitritis — and lifelong suppressive therapy may be needed.**
- **CONGENITAL toxoplasmosis: the classic triad of CHORIORETINITIS (bilateral macular scars), DIFFUSE INTRACRANIAL CALCIFICATION and HYDROCEPHALUS. Transmission risk RISES with gestational age while SEVERITY FALLS.**
- **The differential of a white retinal lesion with vitritis: CMV retinitis ("PIZZA-PIE" haemorrhagic necrosis at CD4 <50), acute retinal necrosis (herpetic — peripheral confluent necrosis with occlusive vasculitis), toxocariasis (a granuloma in a child, with a positive ELISA), tuberculosis, syphilis, endophthalmitis and lymphoma.**
- **The cat is the DEFINITIVE host; humans acquire it from oocysts in cat faeces/soil or from tissue cysts in undercooked meat — and pregnant women should avoid both.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Headlight in the fog" → **toxoplasmosis**.

**REMEMBER:** *An active white lesion at the edge of an old scar, glowing through the haze.*

## 49. Retinoblastoma — Spread and Chemotherapy

**PEARL:** **DIRECT (contiguous) INVASION is the commonest route of spread of retinoblastoma** — into the vitreous (ENDOPHYTIC, with vitreous seeding), into the subretinal space (EXOPHYTIC, causing retinal detachment), and along the **OPTIC NERVE to the brain**, which is the most important route for mortality. The standard **CHEMOREDUCTION regimen is VINCRISTINE + CARBOPLATIN + ETOPOSIDE (VEC).**

**UNDERSTAND:** The eye has **NO LYMPHATICS**, so lymphatic spread does not occur unless the tumour breaches the conjunctiva or orbit. Growth is therefore **local and along anatomical planes** — and because the **optic nerve is a direct CNS tract, invasion beyond the lamina cribrosa carries the tumour into the subarachnoid space and brain.** This is exactly why **the length of optic nerve resected at enucleation matters, and why post-enucleation histology of the CUT END determines adjuvant therapy.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Presentation: LEUKOCORIA (~60%), STRABISMUS (~20%), and — in advanced disease — a red painful eye, glaucoma, proptosis or pseudo-hypopyon. Median age at diagnosis is under 2 years for bilateral and ~3 years for unilateral disease.**
- **Genetics: the RB1 tumour-suppressor gene on 13q14, KNUDSON'S TWO-HIT hypothesis. HEREDITARY (~40%) — germline mutation, usually BILATERAL and MULTIFOCAL, presenting EARLIER, autosomal dominant with ~90% penetrance, and carrying a lifelong risk of SECOND MALIGNANCIES (osteosarcoma above all, plus soft tissue sarcoma and melanoma) and of PINEALOBLASTOMA ("TRILATERAL retinoblastoma"). NON-HEREDITARY (~60%) — unilateral, unifocal, sporadic. Genetic counselling and sibling screening are mandatory.**
- ***BIOPSY IS ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED*** — it seeds tumour. Diagnosis is clinical (examination under anaesthesia with scleral indentation) plus ULTRASOUND/CT showing CALCIFICATION (highly characteristic) and MRI to assess optic nerve and pineal involvement.
- **Treatment: SYSTEMIC CHEMOREDUCTION (VEC) followed by focal consolidation (LASER photocoagulation, CRYOTHERAPY, transpupillary thermotherapy, plaque brachytherapy); INTRA-ARTERIAL (ophthalmic artery) and INTRAVITREAL chemotherapy for vitreous seeds; ENUCLEATION with a LONG (>10–15 mm) OPTIC NERVE STUMP for advanced disease; EXTERNAL BEAM RADIOTHERAPY IS AVOIDED where possible in hereditary cases because it dramatically increases second cancers.**
- **Histology: small round blue cells with FLEXNER–WINTERSTEINER ROSETTES (a true lumen), HOMER WRIGHT rosettes, and FLEURETTES (photoreceptor differentiation); necrosis with calcification is typical. RETINOCYTOMA is the benign variant.**
- **Prognosis in the developed world exceeds 95% survival — in India, late presentation with proptosis remains the main determinant of death, so LEUKOCORIA MUST NEVER BE DISMISSED.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Most common route of spread" → **direct invasion**; regimen → **vincristine, carboplatin, etoposide**.

**REMEMBER:** *No lymphatics in the eye — it spreads by growing, and the optic nerve is the road to the brain.*

## 50. Types of Myopia and "Second Sight"

**PEARL:** An elderly patient whose **DISTANCE vision worsens but who can suddenly read WITHOUT his reading glasses has INDEX MYOPIA from NUCLEAR SCLEROTIC CATARACT** — the phenomenon of **"SECOND SIGHT".** The types of myopia: **AXIAL (increased anteroposterior length — the commonest), CURVATURAL (steeper cornea or lens — keratoconus, keratoglobus), INDEX (increased refractive index of the lens — nuclear sclerosis, diabetes), and POSITIONAL (anterior displacement of the lens).**

**UNDERSTAND:** Nuclear sclerosis **raises the refractive index of the lens nucleus**, increasing the eye's total power — so parallel rays from distance focus in FRONT of the retina (myopia, blurred distance) while **near objects now focus correctly without an add.** The relief is temporary and deceptive: the cataract continues to opacify, and the "improvement" is a warning sign, not a reprieve. **A 1 mm increase in axial length equals roughly 3 dioptres of myopia** — which is why axial myopia progresses so rapidly in childhood.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **DEGENERATIVE (pathological) MYOPIA (>6 D, progressive, with an increasing axial length) carries serious risks: RETINAL DETACHMENT (lattice degeneration and breaks), POSTERIOR STAPHYLOMA, LACQUER CRACKS, FUCHS' SPOT (a pigmented macular scar after choroidal neovascularisation), CHOROIDAL NEOVASCULARISATION, early cataract, PRIMARY OPEN-ANGLE GLAUCOMA and vitreous liquefaction with early PVD. Such eyes need periodic dilated peripheral retinal examination.**
- **Myopia is CORRECTED WITH CONCAVE (MINUS) LENSES and hypermetropia with CONVEX (PLUS) lenses; the correcting lens is prescribed as the WEAKEST minus (or STRONGEST plus) that gives best acuity, to avoid over-stimulating accommodation.**
- **Childhood myopia progression can be slowed by ATROPINE 0.01% EYE DROPS, orthokeratology, outdoor time (≥2 hours daily) and specialised spectacle/contact lens designs — an important modern public-health point given the myopia epidemic.**
- **HYPERMETROPIA in children can cause ACCOMMODATIVE ESOTROPIA (excess accommodative convergence) — treated by FULL CYCLOPLEGIC REFRACTION AND SPECTACLES, not surgery.**
- **ASTIGMATISM: REGULAR (correctable with cylinders — with-the-rule, against-the-rule, oblique) and IRREGULAR (keratoconus, scars — needs rigid contact lenses). KERATOCONUS shows MUNSON'S SIGN, VOGT'S STRIAE, a FLEISCHER RING, oil-droplet reflex and scissoring on retinoscopy, and is managed with rigid lenses and CROSS-LINKING.**
- **A cataract also causes GLARE, MONOCULAR DIPLOPIA and reduced contrast — and vision may be better in dim light with a central lens opacity (day blindness) and better in bright light with peripheral cortical opacities.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Elderly, worse distance vision, no longer needs reading glasses → **index myopia**.

**REMEMBER:** *A hardening nucleus adds power — distance blurs and the reading glasses come off.*

## 51. Membranous and Angular Conjunctivitis

**PEARL:** A **GREYISH-WHITE MEMBRANE over the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva that BLEEDS ON PEELING, in an UNIMMUNISED child, is MEMBRANOUS CONJUNCTIVITIS — classically *CORYNEBACTERIUM DIPHTHERIAE*** (also beta-haemolytic streptococci, *S. pneumoniae* and gonococcus). **ANGULAR CONJUNCTIVITIS — redness confined to the INTERMARGINAL STRIP at the CANTHI with EXCORIATION of the adjacent skin and foamy discharge — is caused by *MORAXELLA LACUNATA*, a Gram-negative DIPLOBACILLUS, and IS associated with BLEPHARITIS in chronic cases.**

**UNDERSTAND:** A **TRUE MEMBRANE bleeds on removal because the fibrinous exudate has permeated INTO the substance of the conjunctival epithelium**, so peeling it strips the epithelium with it; a **PSEUDOMEMBRANE sits loosely ON the surface and peels away without bleeding** (adenoviral, chlamydial, vernal, chemical burns, Stevens–Johnson syndrome). *Moraxella*'s angular distribution is equally mechanistic — it **secretes a PROTEOLYTIC ENZYME that macerates the epithelium**, and it thrives where discharge collects at the canthi.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Diphtheritic conjunctivitis is a systemic emergency: give ANTITOXIN and systemic penicillin/erythromycin, isolate the child, and beware corneal ulceration and symblepharon. It is a strong argument for immunisation.**
- **Angular conjunctivitis is treated with ZINC (oxide/sulphate) drops or ointment, which inhibits the proteolytic enzyme, plus oxytetracycline for the associated blepharitis.**
- **Classify conjunctivitis by discharge: PURULENT/mucopurulent → BACTERIAL (gonococcal is hyperacute); WATERY with FOLLICLES, preauricular lymphadenopathy and often a pseudomembrane → VIRAL (ADENOVIRUS — epidemic keratoconjunctivitis, highly contagious, treat supportively and emphasise hand hygiene); ROPY/stringy discharge with itching and giant COBBLESTONE PAPILLAE → ALLERGIC/VERNAL keratoconjunctivitis (with HORNER–TRANTAS DOTS at the limbus and SHIELD ULCERS); scanty with FOLLICLES in a chronic course → CHLAMYDIAL (trachoma, adult inclusion conjunctivitis).**
- **FOLLICLES (lymphoid, pale, avascular centre, with vessels at the periphery) mean VIRAL, CHLAMYDIAL or drug-induced; PAPILLAE (vascular core, cobblestone) mean ALLERGIC or bacterial/chronic irritation.**
- **NEVER PATCH an infected eye, and NEVER give topical steroids for an undiagnosed red eye — they convert a herpetic dendritic ulcer into a sight-destroying geographic one.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Membrane that bleeds on peeling in an unvaccinated child → ***C. diphtheriae***; canthal redness with excoriation → ***Moraxella***.

**REMEMBER:** *A true membrane bleeds because it grew into the epithelium; Moraxella eats the skin at the corners.*

## 52. The Photostress Test

**PEARL:** In the **PHOTOSTRESS (macular dazzle) TEST**, the patient reads the smallest legible line, is dazzled with a bright light for 10–15 seconds, and the **PHOTOSTRESS RECOVERY TIME** to read that line again is measured (normal <50 seconds, and within 1.5× the fellow eye). **RECOVERY IS PROLONGED IN MACULAR DISEASE and NORMAL IN OPTIC NERVE DISEASE.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Dazzling **BLEACHES the visual pigment in the photoreceptors**, and recovery depends on **REGENERATION of that pigment through the VISUAL CYCLE between the photoreceptors and the RPE.** So the test measures **PHOTORECEPTOR–RPE function**, which is precisely what is damaged in maculopathy and entirely intact in an optic neuropathy (where the fault lies downstream in the ganglion cell axons). **It is the mirror image of the RAPD**, which is prolonged/present in optic nerve disease and absent in most macular disease.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **This is the classic bedside pair: MACULAR disease → prolonged photostress recovery, METAMORPHOPSIA on the AMSLER GRID, a central scotoma, normal colour vision until late and NO significant RAPD. OPTIC NERVE disease → NORMAL photostress recovery, an RAPD, DISPROPORTIONATE loss of COLOUR VISION and contrast, and a field defect.**
- **It is particularly useful in EARLY CENTRAL SEROUS CHORIORETINOPATHY or subtle maculopathy where the fundus looks nearly normal and acuity is only mildly reduced.**
- **CENTRAL SEROUS CHORIORETINOPATHY: a young man, type-A personality, STEROID use (any route) or high endogenous cortisol; a serous neurosensory detachment at the macula with micropsia, metamorphopsia and a HYPERMETROPIC SHIFT; FFA shows a "SMOKE-STACK" or "INKBLOT" leak; most resolve spontaneously in 3 months — STOP THE STEROIDS; persistent cases need focal laser, photodynamic therapy or eplerenone.**
- **Other useful bedside/office tests: AMSLER GRID (metamorphopsia and central scotoma), pinhole (refractive versus organic), colour vision plates (ISHIHARA — optic nerve disease and congenital colour defects), and contrast sensitivity.**
- **Objective confirmation now comes from OCT, which shows the subretinal fluid or macular architecture directly.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Recovery time is prolonged in" → **macular disease**.

**REMEMBER:** *Bleaching tests the pigment — only the macula is slow to recover.*

## 53. Trachoma

**PEARL:** **HERBERT'S PITS — shallow depressions at the LIMBUS, the scars of resolved limbal follicles — are PATHOGNOMONIC of TRACHOMA** (*Chlamydia trachomatis* serovars **A, B, Ba and C**). Other signs: **conjunctival FOLLICLES containing LEBER CELLS (multinucleated macrophages), PANNUS beginning SUPERIORLY, ARLT'S LINE (a horizontal linear scar in the upper tarsal conjunctiva), and CONCRETIONS.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Trachoma blinds in **two stages separated by decades**: the **ACTIVE inflammatory stage in childhood** (follicles and papillae, mild symptoms, highly transmissible) and the **CICATRICIAL stage in middle age**, where scarring contracts the tarsus into **ENTROPION and TRICHIASIS**, so that **the lashes abrade the cornea with every blink** — producing corneal opacity and blindness. The blindness is therefore MECHANICAL, long after the infection has gone, which is why **surgery for trichiasis (the "S" of SAFE) saves sight even when no organism remains.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **WHO SIMPLIFIED GRADING (FISTO): TF (Trachomatous inflammation–Follicular: ≥5 follicles on the upper tarsus), TI (Intense inflammation: obscured tarsal vessels), TS (Scarring), TT (Trichiasis), CO (Corneal Opacity).**
- **The WHO "SAFE" STRATEGY: SURGERY for trichiasis (bilamellar tarsal rotation), ANTIBIOTICS (mass drug administration with a SINGLE ORAL DOSE OF AZITHROMYCIN — 1 g in adults, 20 mg/kg in children — which has replaced 6 weeks of topical tetracycline), FACIAL CLEANLINESS and ENVIRONMENTAL improvement (water, sanitation, latrines to control the *MUSCA SORBENS* fly).**
- **Transmission is by the "FIVE Fs": Fingers, Fomites, Flies, Fluids (ocular discharge) and Family (crowded households) — which is why the F and E of SAFE matter as much as the antibiotic.**
- **Trachoma is the leading INFECTIOUS cause of blindness worldwide, is a WHO elimination target, and INDIA WAS DECLARED FREE OF INFECTIVE TRACHOMA IN 2017 (though trichiasis-related disease persists).**
- **Distinguish ADULT INCLUSION CONJUNCTIVITIS (serovars D–K, sexually transmitted, follicles in the LOWER fornix, associated genital infection — treat the patient and partner with oral azithromycin/doxycycline) from trachoma (A–C, upper tarsus, endemic, non-sexual).**
- **Complications: corneal opacity, entropion/trichiasis, dry eye (goblet cell and ductule destruction), ptosis, and superimposed bacterial keratitis.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Limbal pits → **Herbert's pits, trachoma**; drug of choice under SAFE → **azithromycin**.

**REMEMBER:** *The infection ends in childhood; the scarring blinds decades later by turning the lashes inward.*

## 54. Ocular Hypertension

**PEARL:** **Raised IOP (>21 mmHg) with a NORMAL optic disc, NORMAL visual fields and an OPEN angle = OCULAR HYPERTENSION, not glaucoma.** Risk factors for CONVERSION to POAG (from the OHTS study): **higher IOP, INCREASING AGE (older people are MORE at risk, not younger), a larger vertical CUP-DISC RATIO, THIN CENTRAL CORNEA, greater pattern standard deviation on fields, African ancestry and a positive family history.** **Lowering the IOP by 20% roughly HALVES the rate of conversion.**

**UNDERSTAND:** **Central corneal thickness matters twice over.** A thin cornea causes **GOLDMANN APPLANATION TONOMETRY TO UNDER-READ the true pressure** (so the real IOP is higher than measured), and it also appears to be an **INDEPENDENT structural risk factor** for optic nerve susceptibility. That is why **PACHYMETRY is mandatory** in assessing anyone with borderline pressures — and why a thick cornea (as after uncorrected hyperopic LASIK) can make a genuinely normal eye look hypertensive.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Only about 10% of ocular hypertensives convert to glaucoma over 5 years, so not everyone is treated — treatment is offered to those with multiple risk factors, and observation with 6-monthly disc and field assessment to the rest.**
- **NORMAL-TENSION GLAUCOMA is the converse: typical glaucomatous disc and field damage with IOP consistently ≤21 mmHg — associated with MIGRAINE, RAYNAUD'S phenomenon, nocturnal hypotension, sleep apnoea and disc haemorrhages. It still responds to IOP lowering (the CNTGS showed a 30% reduction slows progression), but always exclude a COMPRESSIVE lesion and previous shock/blood loss.**
- **Factors that alter measured IOP: corneal thickness and curvature, previous refractive surgery, diurnal variation (highest in the early morning), posture, Valsalva, and tight collars. Goldmann applanation (based on the IMBERT–FICK principle) remains the gold standard; non-contact and rebound tonometers screen; SCHIØTZ indentation tonometry is affected by scleral rigidity.**
- **Normal IOP is 10–21 mmHg (mean ~16, two standard deviations), with a diurnal variation of <5 mmHg. Aqueous is produced by the CILIARY EPITHELIUM (mostly by ACTIVE SECRETION via carbonic anhydrase and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) at ~2–2.5 µL/min and drains 80–90% through the TRABECULAR MESHWORK into SCHLEMM'S CANAL and 10–20% by the UVEOSCLERAL route.**
- **The single message: IOP IS A RISK FACTOR, NOT A DIAGNOSIS — the diagnosis is made from the DISC AND FIELD.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False statement" → **younger people are more at risk of progression** (older are).

**REMEMBER:** *Thin cornea under-reads the pressure and marks a weaker nerve — measure the thickness before you reassure.*

## 55. Ptosis and Pseudoptosis

**PEARL:** **PSEUDOPTOSIS is APPARENT drooping with a NORMAL levator: BROW PTOSIS (from excess brow skin or a seventh nerve palsy — diagnosed by manually elevating the eyebrow), LACK OF GLOBE SUPPORT (enophthalmos, microphthalmos, phthisis bulbi, an artificial eye), IPSILATERAL HYPOTROPIA, and CONTRALATERAL LID RETRACTION** (as in thyroid eye disease, where the "ptotic" lid is actually the normal one). **TRUE ptosis is >2 mm covering of the superior cornea.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The distinction matters entirely because of **treatment**: operating on the levator in pseudoptosis achieves nothing and may cause exposure. The systematic check is therefore anatomical — **is the levator working (measure LEVATOR FUNCTION and the MARGIN–REFLEX DISTANCE), is the globe in the right place, and is the fellow lid normal?** In **contralateral lid retraction, HERING'S LAW** explains the trap: covering the retracted eye lets the "ptotic" lid rise.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Classification of TRUE ptosis: MYOGENIC — CONGENITAL (the commonest overall, from levator dysgenesis, with POOR levator function and LID LAG ON DOWNGAZE — the reverse of acquired ptosis), BLEPHAROPHIMOSIS syndrome, myasthenia gravis, Lambert–Eaton, chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia and myotonic dystrophy. APONEUROTIC — the commonest ACQUIRED type (involutional/senile, post-surgical, contact lens wearers) with GOOD levator function, a HIGH lid crease and a thinned lid. NEUROGENIC — third nerve palsy, HORNER'S syndrome (mild ptosis with miosis, from Müller's muscle), and MARCUS GUNN JAW-WINKING synkinesis. MECHANICAL — a large chalazion, lid tumour, oedema or scarring.**
- **Assessment: MARGIN–REFLEX DISTANCE 1 (normal 4–5 mm), PALPEBRAL FISSURE HEIGHT, LEVATOR FUNCTION (normal >12 mm; poor <5 mm), lid crease position, BELL'S PHENOMENON (essential — poor Bell's risks postoperative exposure keratopathy), corneal sensation and tear film, and a FATIGUE/ICE-PACK TEST for myasthenia.**
- **Surgery is chosen by LEVATOR FUNCTION: GOOD function → levator resection or MÜLLER'S MUSCLE–CONJUNCTIVAL RESECTION; POOR function (<4–5 mm) → FRONTALIS SLING. Congenital ptosis is repaired at 3–5 years unless it is severe enough to cause AMBLYOPIA, in which case it is done EARLY.**
- **ALWAYS exclude myasthenia gravis before operating — variable, fatiguable ptosis with diurnal variation and diplopia is medical, not surgical.**
- **Third nerve palsy with ptosis and a dilated pupil is an aneurysm until imaged; Horner's syndrome demands a search along the sympathetic chain (PANCOAST TUMOUR, carotid dissection).**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Cause of pseudoptosis" → **brow ptosis**.

**REMEMBER:** *Lift the brow, straighten the globe, cover the other eye — if the lid comes up, it was never ptosis.*

## 56. Fortified Antibiotics in Bacterial Keratitis

**PEARL:** For **bacterial corneal ulcer not responding to standard drops, use FORTIFIED antibiotic eye drops: CEPHAZOLIN 5% (50 mg/mL) and VANCOMYCIN 5% for GRAM-POSITIVES, with TOBRAMYCIN/GENTAMICIN 1.3% (13.6 mg/mL) or fortified ceftazidime for GRAM-NEGATIVES** — plus a **CYCLOPLEGIC (1% atropine)** and oral analgesia.

**UNDERSTAND:** "Fortified" simply means **concentrations far above commercial preparations**, prepared by adding the intravenous drug to an ophthalmic vehicle — necessary because the **cornea is avascular, so the ONLY route to therapeutic tissue levels is topical**, and the tear film dilutes and drains what is instilled. This is why the drops must also be given **HOURLY (even every 30 minutes initially), around the clock** — frequency matters as much as concentration.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **SCRAPE FIRST, TREAT SECOND: corneal scrapings for Gram and Giemsa stain, KOH mount, and culture (blood agar, chocolate agar, Sabouraud's, and non-nutrient agar with *E. coli* overlay for *Acanthamoeba*) — a small ulcer may be treated empirically, but any large, central, vision-threatening or atypical ulcer must be scraped before antibiotics.**
- **Modern practice often uses monotherapy with a FOURTH-GENERATION FLUOROQUINOLONE (moxifloxacin, gatifloxacin) for uncomplicated ulcers, reserving fortified combinations for severe or unresponsive cases.**
- **The cycloplegic is not optional — it relieves the intense ciliary spasm that causes the pain and photophobia, and prevents posterior synechiae.**
- ***TOPICAL STEROIDS ARE CONTRAINDICATED until the organism is known and controlled*** — disastrous in fungal, herpetic and *Acanthamoeba* keratitis (the SCUT trial showed no overall benefit in bacterial ulcers).
- **Non-healing ulcers: debridement, chemical cautery, bandage contact lens, conjunctival flap or amniotic membrane; IMPENDING PERFORATION: lower IOP, CYANOACRYLATE TISSUE ADHESIVE with a bandage lens, and therapeutic keratoplasty. Never pad an infected eye.**
- **Vitamin A and C support epithelial healing, and NUTRITIONAL/vitamin A deficiency (KERATOMALACIA) must be treated in malnourished children.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Fortified cephazolin concentration → **5%**.

**REMEMBER:** *An avascular cornea can only be reached from the surface — so make the drops strong and give them hourly.*

## 57. Goldenhar Syndrome

**PEARL:** **GOLDENHAR (oculo-auriculo-vertebral) SYNDROME — a first- and second-BRANCHIAL-ARCH developmental disorder: facial asymmetry with HYPOPLASIA of the malar, maxillary and mandibular regions, MACROSTOMIA, MICROTIA with PREAURICULAR SKIN TAGS, HEMIVERTEBRAE (usually cervical), and CARDIAC, RENAL and CNS anomalies.** Ocular features: **LIMBAL (epibulbar) DERMOID, upper lid COLOBOMA/notching, MICROPHTHALMOS and DISC COLOBOMA.** **LUNG HYPOPLASIA is NOT a feature.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Every feature is explained by **failure of development of the FIRST AND SECOND BRANCHIAL ARCHES and the surrounding neural crest**, which together form the **mandible, maxilla, malar bone, external and middle ear, and part of the eyelids and orbit.** The **vertebral anomalies reflect the same somitic/neural-crest disturbance**, which is why an "eye and ear" syndrome also involves the spine — and why cardiac and renal anomalies (also neural-crest dependent) must be actively sought.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **A LIMBAL DERMOID is a benign CHORISTOMA (normal tissue in an abnormal place — containing skin, hair follicles and sebaceous glands), classically at the INFEROTEMPORAL limbus, causing ASTIGMATISM and AMBLYOPIA rather than pain. Excise it for astigmatism, amblyopia or cosmesis — with the caution that deep excision may perforate.**
- **Any child with a limbal dermoid or preauricular tags needs assessment for HEARING, cardiac and RENAL anomalies and spinal imaging.**
- **The wider group of "eye + systemic" syndromes worth carrying: TREACHER COLLINS (also first-arch — downslanting palpebral fissures, lower lid COLOBOMA, malar hypoplasia, but SYMMETRICAL and autosomal dominant), CHARGE syndrome (Coloboma, Heart defects, Atresia choanae, Retardation, Genital and Ear anomalies), and Waardenburg syndrome (dystopia canthorum, heterochromia, white forelock, deafness).**
- **COLOBOMA results from failure of the EMBRYONIC FISSURE to close, so it is characteristically INFERONASAL — of the iris ("keyhole pupil"), ciliary body, choroid, retina, optic disc or lid.**
- **Management is multidisciplinary: ophthalmology, ENT/audiology, cardiology, orthopaedics, plastic/craniofacial surgery and genetics.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are components EXCEPT" → **lung hypoplasia**.

**REMEMBER:** *First and second arches — face, ear, eye and cervical spine, but not the lungs.*

## 58. Blunt Ocular Trauma — The Named Signs

**PEARL:** Blunt trauma produces **Vossius ring** (a ring of pigment imprinted on the anterior lens capsule by the contracted pupillary margin), **rosette cataract** (the typical concussion cataract), and **commotio retinae / Berlin's oedema** (milky white posterior pole with a **cherry-red fovea**).

**UNDERSTAND:** A blunt blow compresses the globe antero-posteriorly and forces it to expand equatorially in a few milliseconds. That single mechanism explains the whole list — the iris slaps the lens (Vossius), lens fibres are disrupted (rosette), the retina is contused (commotio), and the angle can be torn (recession).

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Soemmering's ring is NOT trauma** — it is an **after-cataract (secondary cataract)**, a doughnut of retained lens matter after extracapsular extraction. **Elschnig pearls** are the other after-cataract.
- **Commotio retinae** is **self-limiting and needs no treatment**; the cherry-red spot here is an important mimic of **CRAO** (where the retina is pale from ischaemia and vision is catastrophic).
- Other blunt trauma sequelae to look for: **hyphaema, traumatic mydriasis, iridodialysis, angle recession glaucoma (may present years later — always re-examine the angle), lens subluxation, retinal dialysis and choroidal rupture**.
- **Sphincter tears + Vossius ring + rosette cataract** together = a textbook blunt injury.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are seen after blunt trauma EXCEPT" → **Soemmering's ring**.

**REMEMBER:** *Vossius, rosette and Berlin's belong to the blow; Soemmering's belongs to the surgeon.*

## 59. Orbital Blowout Fracture

**PEARL:** A blowout fracture **protects the globe — severe ocular damage is RARE**. Expect **infraorbital nerve anaesthesia**, **double diplopia** (in both up- and down-gaze), and **enophthalmos appearing only after ~10 days** as the oedema settles.

**UNDERSTAND:** When an object larger than the orbital rim strikes the eye, pressure is transmitted to the orbital contents and the **thin floor and medial wall give way** — decompressing the orbit and sparing the globe. Nature's pressure-relief valve.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Floor is the commonest site** (maxillary sinus below), and it carries the **infraorbital nerve** → numbness of the **lower lid, cheek, side of the nose, upper lip and upper teeth**.
- **Lid emphysema (crepitus on blowing the nose) points to a MEDIAL wall fracture** into the ethmoid.
- **Proptosis initially** (oedema and haemorrhage) → **enophthalmos and mechanical ptosis at ~10 days**.
- **Diplopia** is from **entrapment of the inferior rectus/inferior oblique and orbital fat** — confirmed by a **forced duction test**.
- **The "white-eyed blowout fracture" in children is an emergency** — a trapdoor fracture with minimal bruising but **severe restriction and an oculocardiac reflex (bradycardia, nausea)** — needs release within 24–48 hours.
- Warn the patient **not to blow the nose**; give antibiotics if a sinus is breached.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False statement regarding blowout fracture" → **"severe ocular damage in most cases"**.

**REMEMBER:** *The floor breaks so the eye doesn't; numb cheek now, sunken eye in ten days.*

## 60. Optic Chiasma — Blood Supply

**PEARL:** Supplied by the **anterior cerebral (dorsal surface), anterior communicating, internal carotid and superior hypophyseal arteries**. The **middle cerebral artery does NOT supply the chiasma**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The chiasma sits in the middle of the **circle of Willis anteriorly** — so it is fed by the vessels that surround it, all of which are ICA/ACA-derived. The MCA runs laterally into the Sylvian fissure, well away from it.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Bitemporal hemianopia** = chiasmal compression, because the **decussating nasal fibres** are hit. Causes: **pituitary adenoma (from below — UPPER quadrants first)**, **craniopharyngioma (from above — LOWER quadrants first)**, meningioma, and **anterior communicating artery aneurysm**.
- **Junctional scotoma** = lesion at the junction of the optic nerve and chiasma → **ipsilateral central scotoma + contralateral superotemporal defect** (Wilbrand's knee) — localises very precisely.
- **Optic tract lesion** → **incongruous homonymous hemianopia** with a **relative afferent pupillary defect**; **occipital lesion** → **congruous hemianopia with macular sparing** (dual PCA/MCA supply of the occipital pole).
- The chiasma lies **above the sella and the diaphragma sellae**, ~10 mm above the pituitary — hence a pituitary tumour must be sizeable before it produces field loss.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Perimetry showing bitemporal hemianopia + "which vessel does NOT supply this site?" → **MCA**.

**REMEMBER:** *Everything around the chiasma is ICA/ACA territory — the MCA never reaches it.*

## 61. Ciliary Body — The Numbers That Get Asked

**PEARL:** **60–70 ciliary processes**, all located on the **PARS PLICATA**. The **pars plana lies 3.5–4.5 mm behind the limbus** — the safe surgical entry into the vitreous cavity.

**UNDERSTAND:** The pars plana is the only part of the anterior segment with **no retina in front of it and no lens behind it** — so a needle or trocar can pass without touching either. That is why every intravitreal injection and vitrectomy port goes there.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Entry distances:** **3.5 mm from the limbus in pseudophakes/aphakes, 4.0 mm in phakic adults** (and ~3 mm in children) — the standard for **intravitreal anti-VEGF injection** and **pars plana vitrectomy**.
- The ciliary body is **triangular in cross-section**, and its **epithelium is derived from the two layers of the optic cup (neuroectoderm)** — outer pigmented, inner non-pigmented; the **non-pigmented epithelium secretes aqueous**.
- **Ciliary MUSCLE is from neural crest** (mesenchyme), unlike the epithelium.
- **Aqueous production** — mostly by **active secretion (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and carbonic anhydrase)**, the target of **acetazolamide and topical dorzolamide**; **β-blockers** reduce production, **prostaglandin analogues** increase uveoscleral outflow.
- **Cycloplegics (tropicamide, cyclopentolate, atropine)** paralyse the ciliary muscle for refraction; **atropine is the longest-acting (up to 2 weeks)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "False statement about the structure this drug acts on" → the number of processes (60–70, not 30–40) or their location (pars plicata, not pars plana).

**REMEMBER:** *Processes on the plicata; needles through the plana, 4 mm behind the limbus.*

## 62. Herpes Simplex Keratitis

**PEARL:** A painful red eye with an **irregular (dendritic/geographic) ulcer on
fluorescein staining** and **markedly REDUCED corneal sensation** = **herpes simplex
keratitis** → **topical acyclovir 3% ointment five times daily**. **Topical steroids are
contraindicated in epithelial disease** — they cause a geographic ulcer and corneal
melt.

**UNDERSTAND:** Reduced corneal sensation is the diagnostic key: HSV is a **neurotropic**
virus that reactivates from the **trigeminal ganglion** and travels down the nerve,
damaging the corneal nerves themselves. No other infective keratitis blunts sensation
this way — bacterial and fungal ulcers are exquisitely painful with intact sensation. The
dendrite's shape is simply the anatomy of the epithelial spread along those nerves, with
**terminal end-bulbs** that distinguish it from the pseudodendrite of herpes zoster.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Epithelial (dendritic/geographic) keratitis:** antiviral (topical acyclovir/ganciclovir gel ± oral acyclovir 400 mg 5×/day), **cycloplegic**, **NO steroids**; debridement is an option.
- **Stromal (disciform) keratitis** is immune-mediated → **topical steroid IS indicated, always under antiviral cover**. Knowing which layer is involved decides whether steroids help or harm.
- **Herpes zoster ophthalmicus:** **Hutchinson sign** (vesicles on the nose tip = nasociliary involvement, predicts ocular disease), **pseudodendrites** (raised mucous plaques, no end-bulbs), post-herpetic neuralgia; treat with **oral acyclovir 800 mg 5×/day within 72 hours**.
- Other ulcers by clue: **fungal** — feathery margins, satellite lesions, hypopyon, history of **vegetative trauma**, treat **natamycin 5%** (filamentous) or voriconazole; **Acanthamoeba** — contact lens/water exposure, **pain out of proportion**, ring infiltrate, radial keratoneuritis, treat PHMB/chlorhexidine; **bacterial** — dense suppurative infiltrate, hypopyon, treat fortified/fourth-generation fluoroquinolone (moxifloxacin).
- **Neurotrophic keratopathy** is the late consequence of repeated HSV — an anaesthetic cornea that ulcers painlessly.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Corneal sensation decreased" is the single word that fixes the
diagnosis. The distractors are always **natamycin** (fungal), **moxifloxacin** (bacterial)
and **atropine** (adjunct only).

**REMEMBER:** *Dendrite plus a numb cornea equals herpes — acyclovir, never steroids on the
epithelium.*

## 63. Toxoplasma Chorioretinitis

**PEARL:** **Dense vitritis obscuring the fundus, with a focal creamy-white retinitis
adjacent to an old pigmented chorioretinal scar** = **toxoplasma chorioretinitis** — the
classic **"headlight in the fog"** appearance.

**UNDERSTAND:** The picture is one of **reactivation, not new infection**: cysts persist at
the edge of a congenital or old scar and rupture, so the fresh lesion is **satellite to
the scar**. The intense vitreous inflammation happens because *Toxoplasma* is a live
replicating organism in an immunocompetent host — hence "fog". CMV retinitis, by
contrast, occurs in profound immunosuppression (**CD4 <50**), where there are too few
cells to make vitritis at all — giving a **quiet vitreous** with a "pizza-pie"
haemorrhagic necrotising retinitis along the vessels.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Toxoplasma** is the **commonest cause of posterior uveitis/infectious retinitis worldwide**; diagnosis is **clinical**, supported by serology (IgG positivity confirms exposure; a negative IgG effectively excludes it) or **aqueous PCR/Goldmann–Witmer coefficient** in atypical cases.
- **Treat when:** the lesion threatens the **macula or optic disc**, there is severe vitritis, or the patient is **immunocompromised or pregnant**. Peripheral lesions in a healthy person are self-limiting in 6–8 weeks.
- **Regimen: pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid**, or **co-trimoxazole** (widely used in India); **systemic steroid added 24–48 hours AFTER starting antimicrobials** — never alone, and **never** in the immunocompromised.
- **Congenital toxoplasmosis triad: chorioretinitis + hydrocephalus + intracranial (diffuse) calcification** (contrast **CMV: periventricular calcification, microcephaly, sensorineural deafness**).
- Differentials given in the stem: **pars planitis** (intermediate uveitis, **snowballs and snowbanking** at the pars plana, associated with multiple sclerosis); **CMV retinitis** (minimal vitritis, CD4 <50, treat ganciclovir/valganciclovir); **sarcoidosis** (granulomatous panuveitis, **candle-wax drippings** along veins, raised ACE, hilar lymphadenopathy).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Fundus image plus "dense vitritis" — the fog is the answer. If the
vitreous is described as **clear** in an HIV patient, switch to **CMV**.

**REMEMBER:** *Headlight in the fog, beside an old scar.*

## 64. Congenital Cataract Morphology

**PEARL:** **Bilateral leukocoria with a zone of opacity surrounding the fetal nucleus and
radial "spokes of a wheel" (RIDERS) extending toward the periphery = LAMELLAR (zonular)
cataract** — the **commonest visually significant congenital cataract**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Lens fibres are laid down in concentric layers throughout life, so an
insult during a defined window (intrauterine or early infantile — **hypocalcaemia,
galactosaemia, rubella, idiopathic**) opacifies only the **lamella being formed at that
time**. Clear lens is laid down before and after, which is why the opacity is a **shell**
sandwiched between clear cortex and clear nucleus. The "riders" are extensions of that
affected zone along the sutures.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Cataracta centralis pulverulenta:** a small, dust-like **powdery opacity of the embryonic nucleus**, autosomal dominant, **non-progressive and visually insignificant**.
- **Coronary cataract:** club-shaped opacities arranged like a **crown in the deep cortex, peripheral** — appears at puberty, does **not affect vision**.
- **Blue-dot (cerulean) cataract:** tiny bluish dots, visually harmless.
- **Posterior polar cataract:** at the posterior pole, **visually significant** (close to the nodal point) and notorious for **posterior capsular dehiscence during surgery**.
- **Sutural/Y-shaped, anterior polar (pyramidal), membranous, total** are the other named forms.
- **Leukocoria differential — the one that must never be missed is RETINOBLASTOMA**; also **congenital cataract, ROP, Coats disease, persistent fetal vasculature (PHPV), toxocariasis**.
- **Treatment of a visually significant congenital cataract is EARLY SURGERY** — bilateral by **8–10 weeks**, unilateral even earlier — because the **critical period for visual development** means delay causes irreversible **deprivation amblyopia and nystagmus**. Aphakia is corrected with contact lenses/glasses in infants, IOL usually after 1–2 years, plus **aggressive amblyopia therapy**.
- Work-up: TORCH serology, **urine for reducing substances (galactosaemia)**, calcium/phosphate, karyotype if dysmorphic.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Morphology-to-name matching. **Riders/spokes around the fetal nucleus
= lamellar**; **powdery central = pulverulenta**; **crown-like peripheral = coronary**.

**REMEMBER:** *A shell of opacity with spokes — lamellar, and it needs surgery.*

## 65. Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension

**PEARL:** Headache and transient visual obscurations in a young **obese woman** with
**bilateral papilloedema**, a **normal neurological examination except a VI nerve palsy**,
**normal imaging** and a **raised opening pressure (>25 cm H₂O) with normal CSF
composition** = **idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumour cerebri)**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The syndrome is defined by exclusion, and each diagnostic criterion exists
to remove a mimic — normal imaging excludes a mass and **venous sinus thrombosis**, normal
CSF constituents exclude meningitis and carcinomatosis, and the normal examination excludes
focal disease. The **sixth nerve palsy is a FALSE localising sign**, produced by the long
intracranial course of CN VI being stretched over the petrous ridge by the raised pressure
— so it does not indicate a brainstem lesion.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Modified Dandy criteria:** symptoms and signs of raised ICP only, **no localising signs except CN VI**, **normal CSF**, **normal neuroimaging (MRI + MR VENOGRAPHY to exclude sinus thrombosis)**, raised opening pressure, alert patient, no other cause.
- Risk factors: **obesity and recent weight gain**, women of childbearing age, **vitamin A/retinoids (isotretinoin), tetracyclines, nalidixic acid, steroid withdrawal, oral contraceptives, thyroxine in children**.
- **Vision, not headache, is what is at stake** — untreated papilloedema causes **progressive optic atrophy and permanent blindness**. Monitor with **serial VISUAL FIELDS (perimetry)**, which show an **enlarged blind spot and inferonasal loss**; acuity is preserved until late and is therefore a poor monitor.
- Treatment: **weight loss** (the definitive intervention), **acetazolamide** (reduces CSF production), topiramate, furosemide; **serial LPs** as a temporising measure; surgery for failing vision — **optic nerve sheath fenestration** (protects vision) or **CSF shunt/venous sinus stenting** (better for headache).
- MRI signs (supportive, not diagnostic): **empty sella**, flattened posterior globes, distended perioptic subarachnoid space, transverse sinus stenosis.
- **Papilloedema is bilateral disc swelling from raised ICP** — distinguish from **papillitis** (unilateral, painful eye movements, marked acuity loss, RAPD) and **anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy** (sudden painless loss, **altitudinal field defect**, elderly, check ESR/CRP for giant cell arteritis).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** A fundus photograph of bilateral swollen discs plus "headache and
blurred vision" in a young woman. The management question is almost always **weight loss +
acetazolamide**, with **visual fields** as the monitoring test.

**REMEMBER:** *High pressure, normal scan, normal fluid — save the optic nerve, not just
the headache.*

## 66. Pan-Retinal Photocoagulation: Why It Works

**PEARL:** Pan-retinal photocoagulation in **proliferative diabetic retinopathy** works by
**destroying areas of ISCHAEMIC PERIPHERAL RETINA** — removing the source of VEGF — not by
sealing leaking vessels or ablating the new vessels themselves.

**UNDERSTAND:** The new vessels are a **response**, not the disease. Ischaemic peripheral
retina releases **VEGF**, which drives neovascularisation on the disc and retina. PRP
sacrifices the oxygen-starved, low-value peripheral retina, which does two things: it
**abolishes the VEGF stimulus**, and it **reduces total retinal oxygen demand** so that the
remaining retina — including the macula — is better perfused. The neovessels then regress.
This is why the treatment is deliberately **peripheral and spares the macula**, and why the
cost is **peripheral field and night vision**, not central acuity.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Indications: proliferative diabetic retinopathy** (especially high-risk characteristics — new vessels on the disc >1/3 disc area, or any NVD/NVE with vitreous haemorrhage), **rubeosis iridis/neovascular glaucoma**, ischaemic **central retinal vein occlusion**, sickle cell retinopathy, Eales disease.
- Technique: **1200–1600 burns**, 500 µm spots, scattered from outside the arcades to the periphery, in 2–4 sittings. Side effects: **loss of peripheral field and night vision**, macular oedema worsening transiently, reduced accommodation.
- **Focal/grid laser and intravitreal ANTI-VEGF (ranibizumab, bevacizumab, aflibercept)** are for **diabetic MACULAR OEDEMA** — anti-VEGF is now first-line for centre-involving oedema. Anti-VEGF can also be used as an adjunct to PRP or before vitrectomy.
- **Vitrectomy** for non-clearing vitreous haemorrhage or **tractional retinal detachment involving the macula**.
- **Diabetic retinopathy staging:** **NPDR** — microaneurysms (earliest), dot-and-blot haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, **venous beading and IRMA** (severe, "4-2-1 rule"); **PDR** — neovascularisation, vitreous haemorrhage, tractional detachment.
- Screening: **type 1 — 5 years after diagnosis; type 2 — at diagnosis**; then annually. **Glycaemic and blood pressure control is the primary prevention** (DCCT/UKPDS) — but **rapid** tightening of control can transiently worsen retinopathy.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** All four options sound plausible; the mechanism question is testing
whether you know the laser targets the **ischaemic retina**, not the vessels.

**REMEMBER:** *Sacrifice the starving periphery to save the macula.*

## 67. Refractive Surgery: Why Not RK in a Boxer

**PEARL:** **Radial keratotomy (RK) is contraindicated in anyone at risk of blunt ocular
trauma — boxers, contact-sport athletes, military personnel** — because the deep radial
incisions (**90% of corneal thickness**) permanently weaken the globe and predispose to
**rupture along the scars**. RK is in any case now **obsolete**, superseded by PRK, LASIK
and SMILE.

**UNDERSTAND:** Every refractive procedure flattens the central cornea, but they differ in
**how much structural integrity they sacrifice**. RK does it by **cutting nearly through the
stroma**, leaving radial lines of permanent weakness. Laser procedures instead **ablate or
remove tissue from within the stroma**, leaving no full-thickness incision — and **SMILE**,
which extracts a lenticule through a small side-cut, preserves the most biomechanical
strength of all, which is why it is preferred in contact-sport athletes.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **RK complications:** globe rupture with trauma, **irregular astigmatism from uneven healing, diurnal fluctuation of vision, night glare and starbursts, and a progressive HYPEROPIC SHIFT over years**.
- **PRK** — epithelium removed, laser applied to the surface; **no flap, so no flap complications and the best choice after RK or in thin corneas** — but painful for days and risks **corneal haze**.
- **LASIK** — flap + stromal ablation; **fastest visual recovery** but **flap dislocation with trauma** and **post-LASIK ectasia** are the risks; requires an adequate **residual stromal bed (≥250–300 µm)**.
- **SMILE (small-incision lenticule extraction)** — flapless, **best corneal biomechanics, less dry eye** (fewer corneal nerves cut); for myopia and myopic astigmatism.
- **Screen every candidate with corneal TOPOGRAPHY and PACHYMETRY** — **keratoconus and forme fruste keratoconus are absolute contraindications** to any corneal refractive surgery. Others: age <18, unstable refraction, pregnancy, autoimmune disease, severe dry eye.
- Alternatives for high myopia or thin corneas: **phakic intraocular lens (ICL)** and **refractive lens exchange**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem names a high-impact occupation or sport — the answer is the
procedure that **structurally weakens the cornea most**.

**REMEMBER:** *Deep radial cuts never heal strong — never in a fighter's eye.*

## 68. With-the-Rule vs Against-the-Rule Astigmatism

**PEARL:** **With-the-rule astigmatism = the VERTICAL meridian is more curved/more powerful**
(corrected by a **minus cylinder at 180°**, or a plus cylinder at 90°). **Against-the-rule =
the HORIZONTAL meridian is more powerful** (minus cylinder at 90°).

**UNDERSTAND:** Work it out on a cross. A **minus cylinder at axis 180° places its power in
the meridian 90° AWAY — i.e. vertically**. So a prescription of **−1.00 DS / −2.00 DC × 180**
means the horizontal meridian carries −1.00 D and the vertical meridian carries −3.00 D:
the **vertical is the more myopic and therefore more curved meridian → with-the-rule**. The
axis notation always refers to the meridian **without** the cylinder's power, which is the
single most confusing convention in refraction.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **With-the-rule is normal in the young** — the upper eyelid presses on the cornea, steepening it vertically. Ageing and lid laxity shift the cornea toward **against-the-rule**, which is why elderly patients tend to have it.
- **Types of regular astigmatism (by where the two focal lines fall):** **simple myopic/hyperopic** (one meridian emmetropic), **compound myopic/hyperopic** (both meridians in front of/behind the retina — as here, both minus), **mixed** (one in front, one behind — a plus and a minus).
- **Oblique astigmatism** — the principal meridians lie near 45° and 135°; causes the greatest reduction in vision. **Irregular astigmatism** — the meridians are not perpendicular; from **keratoconus, corneal scars and pterygium** — correctable only by **rigid gas-permeable contact lenses**, not spectacles.
- Tests: **keratometry, corneal topography, retinoscopy, Placido disc, Jackson cross-cylinder** for refining the axis and power.
- **Astigmatism causes asthenopia (eye strain and headache), head tilt and blurring at all distances**; children need early correction to prevent **meridional amblyopia**.
- Transposition rule (plus ↔ minus cylinder): **add the cylinder to the sphere, change the cylinder's sign, change the axis by 90°.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Draw the cross. Put the sphere in both meridians, add the cylinder's
power **90° from its stated axis**, then compare. **Vertical more minus → with the rule.**

**REMEMBER:** *The cylinder acts 90° from its axis — axis 180 means the vertical is steep,
and that's with the rule.*

## 69. Measuring Proptosis

**PEARL:** **Hertel's exophthalmometer** is the standard instrument for measuring proptosis.
**Proptosis = absolute protrusion >20 mm, or a difference of >2 mm between the two eyes.**

**UNDERSTAND:** The measurement is of the **corneal apex relative to the LATERAL ORBITAL
MARGIN**, which is used as the reference because it is a **fixed bony landmark unaffected by
soft-tissue swelling**. Hertel's design rests on **both lateral orbital rims simultaneously**,
so the two eyes are measured against the same baseline in one reading — which is what makes
a 2 mm asymmetry meaningful.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Instruments: Hertel's (commonest, measures both eyes together), Luedde's (simplest, a transparent ruler against one rim), Naugle's (rests on the superior and inferior rims — useful when the LATERAL rim is fractured or has been surgically altered).**
- **Commonest cause of proptosis — unilateral AND bilateral — is THYROID EYE DISEASE** in adults. In children the commonest cause is **orbital cellulitis**; the commonest malignant orbital tumour of childhood is **rhabdomyosarcoma**.
- **Direction of displacement localises the lesion:** axial proptosis → intraconal (optic nerve glioma, meningioma, cavernous haemangioma); **down and out → lacrimal gland tumour**; **down and in → frontal/ethmoidal mucocele**; pulsatile → **carotico-cavernous fistula or neurofibromatosis type 1 with a sphenoid wing defect**; **proptosis increasing on bending forward or Valsalva → orbital VARIX**.
- **Pseudoproptosis** — the eye only looks proptotic: **high axial myopia (large globe), buphthalmos, ipsilateral lid retraction, contralateral enophthalmos or ptosis, facial asymmetry**.
- **Sight-threatening complications: exposure keratopathy and compressive optic neuropathy** — check acuity, colour vision and pupil reactions in every proptotic patient.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** An instrument photograph. Recognise **Hertel's** and know the two
numbers — **>20 mm absolute, >2 mm asymmetry**.

**REMEMBER:** *Measure the cornea from the lateral orbital rim — twenty is the limit.*

## 70. Branch Retinal Vein Occlusion

**PEARL:** **Loss of only a SECTOR of the visual field** (here, the superior field) is a
**BRANCH** occlusion — the deficit maps to one vascular territory. Because the retinal image
is inverted, a **SUPERIOR field defect corresponds to an INFERIOR retinal branch occlusion**.
Sudden **total** monocular loss would indicate a **central** occlusion.

**UNDERSTAND:** Retinal vessels supply discrete wedge-shaped territories, so a branch
occlusion produces an **altitudinal or sectoral scotoma respecting the horizontal midline**,
while a central occlusion affects the whole retina. **Vein occlusions are usually
thrombotic and gradual over hours to days**, and characteristically occur at
**arteriovenous crossings**, where a thickened arteriole (in hypertension or
atherosclerosis) compresses the vein sharing its adventitial sheath. **Artery occlusions
are usually embolic and cause instantaneous, profound, painless loss.**

**MUST KNOW:**
- **BRVO fundus: flame-shaped haemorrhages, dilated tortuous veins, cotton-wool spots and oedema CONFINED TO ONE SECTOR, wedge-shaped with its apex at the AV crossing.** **CRVO: "BLOOD AND THUNDER" fundus** — haemorrhages in all four quadrants, dilated tortuous veins, disc swelling.
- **Risk factors for vein occlusion: HYPERTENSION (the commonest), diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, glaucoma, hyperviscosity states, thrombophilia and the oral contraceptive pill in the young.**
- **Complications: MACULAR OEDEMA (the commonest cause of visual loss — treat with intravitreal ANTI-VEGF ± laser) and NEOVASCULARISATION** (of the disc, retina, or **iris → NEOVASCULAR GLAUCOMA**, especially after ischaemic CRVO — the "90-day glaucoma"). Ischaemic areas are defined by **fluorescein angiography** and treated with **sectoral or pan-retinal photocoagulation**.
- **CRAO: sudden painless complete loss, a pale oedematous retina with a CHERRY-RED SPOT at the fovea (choroidal blush showing through the thin foveal retina), a RELATIVE AFFERENT PUPILLARY DEFECT, and boxcarring of the vessels.** It is a **stroke equivalent** — an ocular emergency; attempt ocular massage, anterior chamber paracentesis and IOP-lowering within 90 minutes, and **investigate urgently for carotid disease, atrial fibrillation, cardiac source of embolus and GIANT CELL ARTERITIS (check ESR/CRP in anyone over 50)**.
- **Rheumatic heart disease** (as in the stem) is a source of **cardiac emboli and of thrombotic risk** — always look for **atrial fibrillation and valvular vegetations**, and examine the fundus for **Roth spots** if endocarditis is suspected.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Match the **extent of visual loss** to the **calibre of vessel**:
sectoral field defect = branch; total loss = central. Sudden and complete = artery; gradual
and partial = vein.

**REMEMBER:** *A slice of vision lost means a branch, not the trunk.*

## 71. Superior Oblique and Third Nerve Palsy

**PEARL:** The **SUPERIOR OBLIQUE (CN IV) INTORTS, DEPRESSES and ABDUCTS** the eye — it does
**NOT adduct**. In a complete **THIRD nerve palsy** the eye rests **"DOWN AND OUT"** with
**ptosis and a fixed dilated pupil**, because the only surviving muscles are the **lateral
rectus (VI) and superior oblique (IV)** — so **"lateral and upward gaze" is impossible** and
is the false statement.

**UNDERSTAND:** Each oblique muscle's action follows from its **line of pull relative to the
visual axis**. The superior oblique approaches the globe from the trochlea **anteromedially
and inserts posterolaterally on the superior surface**, so its pull rotates the top of the
eye inward (**intorsion**), pulls the back of the eye up (hence the front goes **down**),
and swings it **outward**. Its depressing action is **greatest when the eye is ADDUCTED**
(when its line of pull aligns with the visual axis) — which is why superior oblique palsy
causes diplopia **looking down and in**, as when reading or descending stairs.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Muscle actions: superior rectus — elevation (maximal in ABduction), adduction, intorsion; INFERIOR OBLIQUE — elevation (maximal in ADduction), abduction, EXTORSION; inferior rectus — depression (maximal in abduction), adduction, extorsion; SUPERIOR OBLIQUE — depression (maximal in ADduction), abduction, INTORSION.** ("The obliques abduct and are named for their torsion — superior intorts.")
- **Fourth nerve palsy: vertical diplopia worse on looking DOWN AND IN, a compensatory HEAD TILT AWAY from the affected side, and a POSITIVE BIELSCHOWSKY head-tilt test.** CN IV is the **only cranial nerve that decussates and emerges DORSALLY**, and it is the **thinnest and longest**, hence commonly injured in **closed head trauma**.
- **Third nerve palsy: ptosis (levator), "down and out" eye, and pupil involvement. PUPIL-INVOLVING third nerve palsy is a SURGICAL emergency — a POSTERIOR COMMUNICATING ARTERY ANEURYSM or uncal herniation compressing the superficially placed parasympathetic fibres. PUPIL-SPARING palsy suggests a MEDICAL/microvascular cause (diabetes, hypertension) — the vasa nervorum supply the core, sparing the surface.**
- **Sixth nerve palsy: failure of ABduction with horizontal diplopia; a FALSE LOCALISING SIGN in raised intracranial pressure.**
- **Diplopia rule: the image furthest from the midline in the direction of maximal separation belongs to the paretic eye.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "All are functions EXCEPT" — the odd one out for the superior oblique is
**adduction**. For third nerve palsy, the false statement is any that permits **elevation or
medial movement**.

**REMEMBER:** *Superior oblique takes the eye down, out and rolls it in — it never adducts.*

## 72. IOL Power Calculation After Refractive Surgery

**PEARL:** After **LASIK/PRK for MYOPIA**, standard IOL formulas overestimate corneal power
and leave the patient **HYPEROPIC**. Use a formula designed for post-refractive eyes —
**HAIGIS-L** (or the Barrett True-K, Shammas, or the ASCRS online calculator).

**UNDERSTAND:** Keratometers do not measure corneal power directly — they measure the
**anterior corneal curvature and assume a fixed anterior:posterior ratio** (the "keratometric
index", 1.3375). **Myopic LASIK flattens the ANTERIOR surface while leaving the POSTERIOR
surface unchanged**, so that assumption fails and the cornea's true power is **overestimated**
— which produces an under-powered IOL and a **hyperopic surprise**. There is also an
**effective lens position error**, because a flat cornea makes the formulas predict the IOL
will sit more anteriorly than it does. Post-refractive formulas correct for both.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **SRK (P = A − 2.5L − 0.9K) and SRK-II are older regression formulas; SRK/T, Hoffer Q, Holladay and HAIGIS are theoretical formulas; BARRETT UNIVERSAL II is the current general-purpose standard.** **Hoffer Q performs best in SHORT eyes; SRK/T in LONG eyes.**
- **Biometry: OPTICAL biometry (IOL Master, Lenstar — partial coherence interferometry) is more accurate than ultrasound A-scan;** with contact ultrasound, corneal compression causes a falsely short axial length and an over-powered lens (immersion is better).
- **A 1 mm error in axial length causes about a 2.5–3 D error in IOL power** — axial length is the single greatest source of error.
- Post-hyperopic LASIK the error runs the other way (a **myopic surprise**) if uncorrected.
- Always **ask about previous refractive surgery** before cataract surgery, and try to obtain the **pre-LASIK keratometry and the refractive change** — historical data improve accuracy (clinical history method, double-K method).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Post-LASIK cataract surgery → the answer is the **special formula**.
SRK I/II are always the wrong choice in this stem.

**REMEMBER:** *LASIK flattens the front but not the back — standard formulas guess wrong.*

## 73. WHO Simplified Grading of Trachoma

**PEARL:** **ARLT's LINE (a linear scar in the sulcus subtarsalis of the upper tarsal
conjunctiva) and HERBERT's PITS (pitted scars at the superior limbus, left by healed limbal
follicles) are CICATRICIAL signs = TS (Trachomatous Scarring)** — even when there are no
follicles, no trichiasis and no corneal opacity.

**UNDERSTAND:** The WHO grading was built for **field workers with a torch and a loupe**, so
each grade corresponds to a stage of the natural history and to a specific arm of the
**SAFE strategy**. **Active inflammatory grades (TF, TI) occur in children and drive
transmission — they need antibiotics.** **Cicatricial grades (TS, TT, CO) are the legacy of
years of infection in adults — antibiotics no longer help; surgery prevents blindness.**
Grading therefore decides **who gets azithromycin and who gets an operation**.

**MUST KNOW — the five WHO grades:**
- **TF — Trachomatous inflammation, FOLLICULAR: ≥5 follicles (each ≥0.5 mm) on the upper tarsal conjunctiva.**
- **TI — Trachomatous inflammation, INTENSE: pronounced inflammatory thickening obscuring more than half the deep tarsal vessels.**
- **TS — Trachomatous SCARRING: scarring of the tarsal conjunctiva (Arlt's line).**
- **TT — Trachomatous TRICHIASIS: at least one lash touching the globe, or evidence of epilation.**
- **CO — Corneal OPACITY over the pupil, blurring the pupillary margin.**
- **SAFE strategy: SURGERY for trichiasis (bilamellar tarsal rotation), ANTIBIOTICS (single-dose azithromycin 20 mg/kg, mass drug administration), FACIAL cleanliness, ENVIRONMENTAL improvement (water and sanitation, fly control).**
- **Causative agent: *Chlamydia trachomatis* serovars A, B, Ba and C** (serovars D–K cause genital infection and inclusion conjunctivitis; L1–L3 cause LGV). Transmission by **eye-seeking flies, fingers and fomites**.
- **Pathology: mixed follicular and papillary conjunctivitis with LEBER cells (large macrophages) in the follicles; intracytoplasmic HALBERSTAEDTER–PROWAZEK inclusion bodies on Giemsa.**
- **Blindness results from the cicatricial sequence: scarring → entropion and trichiasis → corneal abrasion → opacity.** Trachoma is a leading **infectious** cause of blindness worldwide.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Read which signs are **present and absent**. Scars without follicles or
trichiasis = **TS**.

**REMEMBER:** *Follicles are active, Arlt's line is a scar, a lash on the cornea is TT.*

## 74. Aberrant Regeneration of the Third Nerve

**PEARL:** **Involuntary elevation of the eyelid on attempted adduction or downgaze, months
after a traumatic or compressive third nerve palsy, is the PSEUDO-VON GRAEFE SIGN** — a
feature of **aberrant regeneration (oculomotor synkinesis)**.

**UNDERSTAND:** After axonal disruption, regrowing fibres do not reliably find their original
endoneurial tubes (a Sunderland grade 3 or worse injury). Fibres destined for the **medial or
inferior rectus** are misdirected into the **levator palpebrae superioris**, so every attempt
to adduct or depress the eye also fires the lid — a **cross-wiring**, not a recovery. The
same misdirection into the **pupillary constrictor** produces **pupillary constriction on
adduction (the "pseudo-Argyll Robertson" pupil of aberrant regeneration)**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Aberrant regeneration follows TRAUMATIC and COMPRESSIVE (aneurysm, tumour) third nerve palsies. It essentially NEVER follows an ISCHAEMIC/microvascular (diabetic) palsy** — so **its appearance mandates imaging to exclude a compressive lesion** (an aneurysm or a cavernous sinus/skull-base tumour). "Primary" aberrant regeneration without a preceding palsy is especially suspicious.
- **True VON GRAEFE sign is entirely different: LID LAG on downgaze in THYROID eye disease** — the upper lid fails to follow the globe down.
- **Contrast the other synkineses: MARCUS GUNN JAW-WINKING — congenital, the lid elevates when the jaw moves (trigeminal–oculomotor misconnection, present from birth with congenital ptosis); Duane retraction syndrome — globe retraction and narrowing of the palpebral fissure on adduction (abducens nucleus aplasia with aberrant III innervation of the lateral rectus).**
- **ARGYLL ROBERTSON pupil: small, irregular pupils that ACCOMMODATE but do not REACT to light ("prostitute's pupil"), classically NEUROSYPHILIS.** **ADIE (tonic) pupil: a large pupil with a slow, tonic near response, LIGHT–NEAR DISSOCIATION, vermiform iris movements and SUPERSENSITIVITY to dilute (0.125%) pilocarpine; with absent tendon reflexes = HOLMES–ADIE syndrome.**
- **Third nerve palsy rules: PUPIL-INVOLVING = surgical/compressive (posterior communicating artery aneurysm) until proven otherwise; PUPIL-SPARING = microvascular.**

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives a **latency of months after a third nerve palsy** plus a
lid movement linked to gaze — that combination is aberrant regeneration.

**REMEMBER:** *The lid rises when the eye tries to move — the nerve rewired itself wrongly.*

## 75. Yoke Muscles

**PEARL:** Yoke muscles are **contralateral synergists** — one from each eye, contracting together in a version movement. **Right superior oblique pairs with left inferior rectus** (levodepression).

**UNDERSTAND:** Both eyes must move **conjugately** to the same point in space, but each eye reaches a given gaze position using different muscles. In **left-and-down** gaze, the **left eye depresses in abduction — the inferior rectus job**, while the **right eye depresses in adduction — the superior oblique job**. The pairing is dictated by which muscle depresses each eye *in that particular horizontal position*.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Quick rule: **flip every word** — Left→Right, Superior→Inferior, Oblique→Rectus. So **Left SO ↔ Right IR**.
- The six pairs: **RMR–LLR (levoversion)**; **RLR–LMR (dextroversion)**; **RSR–LIO (dextroelevation)**; **RIO–LSR (levoelevation)**; **RIR–LSO (dextrodepression)**; **RSO–LIR (levodepression)**.
- **Hering's law** — equal and simultaneous innervation to yoke muscles — explains why a paretic muscle causes **secondary deviation greater than primary deviation** when the paretic eye fixes.
- **Sherrington's law** — reciprocal innervation: a muscle relaxes as its ipsilateral antagonist contracts.
- Muscle actions to keep straight: **obliques ABduct; superior muscles INtort; inferior muscles EXtort**. The **obliques act as elevators/depressors in ADduction**; the **vertical recti in ABduction** — the basis of the H-test.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Options mix ipsilateral pairs (**RMR + RLR** — antagonists, not yoke) and same-name pairs. Apply the flip rule mechanically.

**REMEMBER:** *Swap the side, swap the level, swap the muscle type.*

## 76. Vogt's Triad After Acute Angle-Closure

**PEARL:** **Vogt's triad = glaukomflecken + patchy iris atrophy + a fixed, slightly dilated pupil** — the residual signs of a **past acute angle-closure attack**.

**UNDERSTAND:** All three are **ischaemic scars**. The extreme pressure rise (often 50–80 mmHg) shuts down perfusion of the anterior segment: the **lens epithelium infarcts** leaving anterior subcapsular opacities (glaukomflecken), the **iris stroma and sphincter necrose** giving patchy atrophy and a pupil that can no longer constrict. Their presence proves an attack occurred even after the pressure has normalised.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Acute attack presentation:** severe pain, **haloes around lights**, nausea and vomiting (may be mistaken for an abdominal emergency), **stony-hard eye, circumcorneal congestion, hazy cornea, shallow anterior chamber, mid-dilated vertically oval non-reacting pupil**.
- **Anatomical risk factors: hypermetropia, short axial length, shallow anterior chamber, thick anteriorly-placed lens, elderly female, Asian ethnicity**; precipitated by **dim light, mydriatics, and prone position**.
- Treatment: **IV mannitol/oral glycerol + acetazolamide + topical beta-blocker + steroid**, then **pilocarpine once the pressure falls** (the ischaemic sphincter cannot respond at very high pressure), followed by **YAG laser peripheral iridotomy in both eyes**.
- **Acetazolamide is contraindicated in sickle cell disease** — it promotes **sickling** through acidosis; use **IV mannitol** instead.
- **Prostaglandin analogues should be avoided in uveitic glaucoma** — they worsen inflammation and can cause **cystoid macular oedema** and reactivation of **herpes simplex keratitis**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Eponym recall (Vogt), plus the two drug-avoidance scenarios (**sickle cell → not acetazolamide; uveitis → not latanoprost**), which are the more clinically useful points.

**REMEMBER:** *A past attack leaves scars on the lens, the iris and the pupil.*

## 77. Antiglaucoma Drugs — Mechanism Groups

**PEARL:** **Latanoprost and the prostaglandin analogues increase UVEOSCLERAL outflow.** **Pilocarpine increases TRABECULAR outflow.** **Beta-blockers and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors REDUCE aqueous production.**

**UNDERSTAND:** There are only three ways to lower intraocular pressure — **make less aqueous, drain more through the trabecular meshwork, or drain more through the uveoscleral route**. Prostaglandins work by triggering **matrix metalloproteinase release in the ciliary muscle**, remodelling the extracellular matrix and opening the uveoscleral pathway — which is why they are potent, once-daily, and free of systemic effects.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Increase trabecular outflow:** **pilocarpine (miotics)**, epinephrine/dipivefrine, **bimatoprost**, **netarsudil (Rho-kinase inhibitor)**.
- **Increase uveoscleral outflow:** **prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost, travoprost, tafluprost)**, brimonidine, apraclonidine, epinephrine.
- **Decrease aqueous production:** **beta-blockers (timolol)**, **carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, dorzolamide, brinzolamide)**, **alpha-2 agonists**.
- **Hyperosmotic agents (mannitol, glycerol, urea)** dehydrate the vitreous — used only acutely.
- **Prostaglandins are first line** for POAG. Side effects: **conjunctival hyperaemia, eyelash lengthening, iris and periocular hyperpigmentation, periorbital fat atrophy (bimatoprost), and CME/uveitis reactivation**.
- Contraindications: **timolol in asthma, COPD and heart block**; **acetazolamide in sulpha allergy, sickle cell, renal stones, hypokalaemia**; **pilocarpine in uveitis and in a retinal detachment risk**.
- **Normal cup-to-disc ratio is up to 0.6**; **> 0.6, asymmetry > 0.2 between eyes, notching, or disc haemorrhage** suggests glaucoma. Large discs and myopia give physiologically large cups.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Mechanism matching is asked directly. Anchor **prostaglandin = uveoscleral**, **pilocarpine = trabecular**.

**REMEMBER:** *Prostaglandins open the back door; miotics open the front one.*

## 78. Pupillary Light Reflex Pathway

**PEARL:** The pupillary light reflex **bypasses the lateral geniculate body**: retinal ganglion cells → **optic nerve/tract → pretectal nucleus → BOTH Edinger–Westphal nuclei → ciliary ganglion → short ciliary nerves → sphincter pupillae**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Pupillomotor fibres **leave the optic tract just before the LGB**, travelling via the brachium of the superior colliculus to the pretectum. That anatomical detour is why **cortical blindness spares the pupillary reflex** (the fibres never reach the cortex) and why an **optic tract lesion can produce both a field defect and a pupillary defect**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The **bilateral projection from each pretectal nucleus to both EW nuclei** is what produces the **consensual response**.
- **Wernicke's hemianopic pupil** — an **optic tract** lesion: light on the **intact hemiretina gives a normal response**, light on the **affected hemiretina gives none**. Hard to elicit clinically but conceptually important.
- **RAPD (Marcus Gunn pupil)** indicates **optic nerve or extensive retinal disease**, not media opacity — a dense cataract never causes an RAPD.
- **Argyll Robertson pupil** (neurosyphilis): **light–near dissociation**, small irregular pupils. **Adie's tonic pupil**: large pupil, poor light response, slow tonic near response, **supersensitive to dilute pilocarpine**.
- The **near reflex** (accommodation–convergence–miosis) **does involve the cortex and LGB** — hence it survives in Argyll Robertson while the light reflex does not.
- **Oculocephalic (doll's-eye) reflex** tests cranial nerves **VIII (afferent), III and VI (efferent)** and the intervening brainstem — used to assess brainstem integrity in coma.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Not part of the light reflex pathway" → **lateral geniculate body**. It is the deliberate intruder from the visual pathway.

**REMEMBER:** *Light reflex leaves the tract before the geniculate.*

## 79. Localising Visual Field Defects

**PEARL:** **Right homonymous hemianopia = LEFT-sided lesion** behind the chiasma. "Loss of the right half of both visual fields" means loss of the **left eye's nasal and right eye's temporal** fields → **left optic tract, radiation or cortex**.

**UNDERSTAND:** After the chiasma, each side of the brain carries the **contralateral half of the visual world** — because nasal retinal fibres (which see the temporal field) have crossed. So a post-chiasmal lesion always produces a field defect on the **opposite** side, in **both** eyes.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Optic nerve** → unilateral total field loss with **RAPD**. **Chiasma** → **bitemporal hemianopia** (pituitary adenoma pressing from below affects **upper** quadrants first). **Optic tract** → **incongruous** homonymous hemianopia ± Wernicke's pupil. **Temporal (Meyer's) loop** → **superior** quadrantanopia ("**pie in the sky**"). **Parietal radiation** → inferior quadrantanopia ("pie on the floor"). **Occipital cortex** → **congruous** homonymous hemianopia with **MACULAR SPARING** (dual blood supply from PCA and MCA).
- **Congruity increases the further back the lesion** — a useful localising rule.
- Causes of tract lesions: **pituitary adenoma (commonest), craniopharyngioma, aneurysms, demyelination, infarction**.
- **Bilateral occipital infarction → cortical blindness with normal pupils**; add denial of blindness and it is **Anton's syndrome**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Translate "right halves of both eyes" into hemiretinas before choosing. The side of the lesion is always **opposite** to the side of field loss.

**REMEMBER:** *Field loss on the right means damage on the left.*

## 80. Hypertensive vs Diabetic Retinopathy

**PEARL:** **IRMA (intraretinal microvascular abnormalities) belong to DIABETIC retinopathy, not hypertensive retinopathy.** Hypertension gives **arteriolar narrowing, AV nicking, cotton-wool spots, flame haemorrhages, hard exudates and papilloedema**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The two diseases damage different parts of the circulation. Hypertension attacks the **arterioles** — vasoconstriction, sclerosis and fibrinoid necrosis — so its signs are **arteriolar and ischaemic**. Diabetes attacks the **capillaries and venules** — pericyte loss, microaneurysms, capillary closure — so its signs include **microaneurysms, venous beading and IRMA (shunt vessels bypassing closed capillary beds)**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Keith–Wagener/modified grading:** **Grade 1** generalised arteriolar narrowing; **Grade 2** focal narrowing + **AV nicking (Salus' sign)**; **Grade 3** copper-wiring, **cotton-wool spots, haemorrhages, hard exudates**; **Grade 4** all the above + **papilloedema** (= malignant hypertension).
- Other hypertensive signs: **Gunn's sign** (venous tapering at crossings), **Bonnet's sign** (banking), **silver-wiring**, **macular star**, **Elschnig spots** and **Siegrist streaks** (choroidopathy).
- **ETDRS diabetic grading — the 4-2-1 rule for SEVERE NPDR:** haemorrhages/microaneurysms in **4** quadrants, **OR** venous beading in **2** quadrants, **OR** IRMA in **1** quadrant. **Very severe NPDR** = any two of these.
- The **ETDRS trial** established **focal laser photocoagulation for clinically significant macular oedema**; anti-VEGF is now first line for centre-involving DMO.
- **Cotton-wool spots are nerve fibre layer infarcts** and are common to both diseases (plus HIV, anaemia and vasculitis) — so they never discriminate.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Least likely on fundoscopy" in hypertension → **IRMA**. The 4-2-1 rule is asked separately as a numbers question.

**REMEMBER:** *Hypertension narrows the arteries; diabetes remodels the capillaries.*

## 81. Sympathetic Ophthalmia

**PEARL:** **Sympathetic ophthalmia follows PENETRATING trauma** (or intraocular surgery) — a **bilateral granulomatous panuveitis** presenting **2 weeks to 3 months** after the injury.

**UNDERSTAND:** Uveal antigens are normally **sequestered behind the blood–ocular barrier** and were never presented to the immune system during tolerance induction. A penetrating wound — especially one involving the **uveal tissue and the wound edges** — exposes them, and the resulting autoimmune response attacks **both eyes**, including the healthy "sympathising" one. Blunt trauma does not breach the globe, so it cannot expose these antigens.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Earliest symptom: blurring of NEAR vision in the sympathising (uninjured) eye**; **earliest sign: retrolental flare**.
- Fundus shows **multifocal choroidal infiltrates** and **Dalen–Fuchs nodules** (granulomas between Bruch's membrane and the RPE).
- Treatment: **high-dose systemic corticosteroids for months**, with **azathioprine, ciclosporin or methotrexate** as steroid-sparing agents.
- **Prophylaxis: enucleation of a severely injured eye with no visual potential WITHIN 2 WEEKS** of injury. Once sympathetic ophthalmia has developed, enucleation no longer helps (and the injured eye may end up the better-seeing one).
- Histology is **identical to Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada syndrome** — the difference is the history of trauma.
- Other trauma signs to keep separate: **D-shaped pupil = iridodialysis** (dis-insertion of the iris root, with a peripheral red reflex); **vossius ring, angle recession, commotio retinae, sphincter tears** in blunt injury.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The word **penetrating** is the answer. The two-week enucleation window is the high-yield management point.

**REMEMBER:** *An open globe exposes the uvea and both eyes pay for it.*

## 82. Thyroid Eye Disease — Motility and Treatment

**PEARL:** The commonest ocular motility defect in Graves' disease is **restriction of ELEVATION**, caused by **fibrosis of the INFERIOR rectus**. **Beta-blockers do nothing for the orbitopathy** — they treat the systemic thyrotoxicosis only.

**UNDERSTAND:** Thyroid eye disease is a **restrictive, not paralytic**, myopathy: the enlarged and fibrosed muscle **cannot relax**. Since a muscle limits movement in the direction *away* from its own action, a fibrosed **inferior rectus limits UPGAZE**. The **muscle belly is involved with sparing of the tendon** — the imaging feature that distinguishes it from orbital myositis.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Order of involvement (**"I'M SLow"**): **Inferior rectus > Medial rectus > Superior rectus > Lateral rectus** — hence elevation defect, then abduction defect, then depression, then adduction.
- **NO SPECS** classification: **N**o signs, **O**nly signs (lid retraction), **S**oft tissue involvement, **P**roptosis, **E**xtraocular muscle involvement, **C**orneal involvement, **S**ight loss (optic neuropathy).
- Treatment: **systemic corticosteroids are the mainstay** (IV methylprednisolone pulses for active moderate–severe disease), **orbital triamcinolone**, **orbital radiotherapy**, **teprotumumab (IGF-1R inhibitor)** for steroid-resistant disease, and **orbital decompression** for **compressive optic neuropathy or corneal exposure**.
- Order of surgery when needed: **decompression → squint surgery → lid surgery** — never the reverse.
- **Smoking is the strongest modifiable risk factor**; **radioiodine can worsen** the orbitopathy (cover with steroids).
- **Lid signs:** von Graefe (lid lag), Dalrymple (lid retraction), Stellwag (infrequent blinking), Mobius (convergence failure), Joffroy (absent forehead wrinkling on upgaze).

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Two versions: "commonest motility defect" (**elevation restriction**) and "not a treatment" (**beta-blockers**).

**REMEMBER:** *A tethered inferior rectus stops the eye looking up.*

## 83. Ptosis in Myasthenia Gravis

**PEARL:** **Ptosis is the commonest ocular finding in myasthenia gravis** — more common than diplopia — and it is **insidious, bilateral and asymmetrical**, worsening through the day.

**UNDERSTAND:** The **levator palpebrae superioris and extraocular muscles have the highest firing frequencies and the smallest safety margin** at the neuromuscular junction, so they fatigue first when acetylcholine receptors are reduced. Sustained tonic activity — as in holding the eyelid open all day — is exactly the pattern that exhausts a compromised junction.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Ocular myasthenia** is the presenting form in ~50–60% and remains purely ocular in about 15%.
- Bedside tests: **ice-pack test** (2 minutes of cold improves ptosis — cold inhibits acetylcholinesterase); **sustained upgaze fatigue test**; **Cogan's lid twitch**; **enhanced ptosis** (lifting one lid worsens the other, by Hering's law).
- Confirmatory: **anti-AChR antibodies (most specific)**, **anti-MuSK** in seronegative cases, **repetitive nerve stimulation showing a decremental response**, and **single-fibre EMG (most sensitive)**. **Tensilon (edrophonium)** test is largely historical.
- **Always image the mediastinum** — **thymoma in ~10–15%**, thymic hyperplasia in many more; **thymectomy** benefits selected patients.
- Treatment: **pyridostigmine**, steroids, azathioprine/mycophenolate; **IVIG or plasma exchange for crisis**.
- Drugs that worsen myasthenia: **aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, beta-blockers, magnesium, penicillamine**.
- **Pupils are ALWAYS spared** in myasthenia — the discriminator from a third nerve palsy.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Most common ocular finding" → **ptosis**, with diplopia as the near-miss distractor.

**REMEMBER:** *The lid tires before the eye moves wrong.*

## 84. Ocular Sarcoidosis

**PEARL:** **"Candle-wax dripping" (taches de bougie) — segmental perivenous sheathing on fundoscopy — is characteristic of sarcoid posterior uveitis.** **Anterior uveitis is the commonest ocular manifestation overall.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Sarcoid granulomas form **around retinal venules**, and the perivascular exudate seen through the retina resembles wax running down a candle. Because sarcoidosis is a **granulomatous** disease, its uveitis is granulomatous throughout — hence **mutton-fat keratic precipitates and iris nodules** rather than the fine precipitates of non-granulomatous inflammation.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Anterior:** **mutton-fat KPs**, **Koeppe (pupillary margin) and Busacca (iris stroma) nodules**, trabecular meshwork nodules, **tent-shaped peripheral anterior synechiae**.
- **Intermediate:** **snowballs and snowbanking**, "string of pearls" vitreous opacities.
- **Posterior:** **candle-wax dripping periphlebitis**, **Landers' sign** (pre-retinal nodules), choroidal granulomas.
- Other: **lacrimal gland enlargement and dry eye**, conjunctival granulomas (easily biopsied), band keratopathy from hypercalcaemia. **Heerfordt syndrome** = uveitis + parotid enlargement + fever + facial nerve palsy.
- Investigations: **serum ACE, chest imaging, serum calcium**; **steroids are the mainstay**.
- **Keratic precipitates sit on the corneal ENDOTHELIUM**, classically in **Arlt's triangle** (inferior central cornea). **Mutton-fat = granulomatous; fine/granular = non-granulomatous; stellate = Fuchs heterochromic iridocyclitis**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Image-based — recognise candle-wax dripping. The KP question tests the **layer (endothelium)**, not the disease.

**REMEMBER:** *Wax runs down the veins in sarcoid.*

## 85. Keratoconus

**PEARL:** **Intacs (intrastromal corneal ring segments) are placed for KERATOCONUS** — a non-inflammatory **axial thinning with apical protrusion** of the cornea.

**UNDERSTAND:** The cone is a **biomechanical failure** — defective collagen and proteoglycan cross-linking lets the central cornea bulge forward under normal intraocular pressure. Ring segments work purely mechanically: inserted in the peripheral stroma, they **shorten the arc length of the central cornea and flatten the cone**, without removing tissue.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Clinical signs: **progressive myopia with irregular astigmatism**, **Fleischer ring** (iron deposit at the cone base), **Vogt's striae** (vertical stromal lines that disappear on pressure), **Munson's sign** (V-shaped lower lid indentation on downgaze), **Rizzuti sign**, **oil-droplet/scissoring reflex on retinoscopy**.
- **Corneal topography is the most sensitive diagnostic tool** and detects subclinical disease.
- **Acute hydrops** — sudden pain, photophobia and clouding from a **rupture of Descemet's membrane** letting aqueous into the stroma.
- Management ladder by severity: **spectacles → rigid gas-permeable/scleral contact lenses → corneal collagen CROSS-LINKING (halts progression) → Intacs → deep anterior lamellar or penetrating keratoplasty**.
- Associations: **atopy and eye rubbing, Down syndrome, Leber congenital amaurosis, Ehlers–Danlos, Marfan, osteogenesis imperfecta**.
- **Cross-linking is the only treatment that stops progression** — everything else is optical rehabilitation.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Image of ring segments in the peripheral cornea → **keratoconus**. Keratoglobus (whole-cornea thinning) is the plausible distractor.

**REMEMBER:** *Rings in the periphery flatten the cone in the centre.*

## 86. Orbital Rhabdomyosarcoma

**PEARL:** **Rapidly progressive proptosis in a child + small round blue cells + DESMIN positivity = rhabdomyosarcoma** — the **commonest primary orbital malignancy of childhood**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Desmin is a **muscle-specific intermediate filament**, so its presence in a small round blue cell tumour points to **skeletal muscle origin**. The panel of small round blue cell tumours (neuroblastoma, Ewing, lymphoma, retinoblastoma, rhabdomyosarcoma) can only be separated by **immunohistochemistry**, and desmin/myogenin/MyoD1 are the rhabdomyosarcoma markers.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Presents at **7–8 years** with **rapid, painless, non-axial proptosis**, often **superonasal**; may follow trivial trauma (a red herring in the history). **No fever** — which separates it from orbital cellulitis.
- CT shows a **well-defined mass with adjacent bone destruction**.
- **Cross-striations in tumour cells are pathognomonic**; **myoglobin is the most specific marker**, desmin the practical one.
- Subtypes: **embryonal (commonest, best prognosis in the orbit)**, **alveolar (worst, PAX3/7-FOXO1 fusion)**, botryoid, pleomorphic.
- Treatment is **chemotherapy plus radiotherapy** after biopsy — **exenteration is rarely needed**; orbital disease has a good prognosis (> 90% survival).
- Differentials of childhood proptosis: **orbital cellulitis (fever, painful), capillary haemangioma (increases on crying, involutes), dermoid cyst (superotemporal, slow), metastatic neuroblastoma (bilateral, ecchymotic lids), Langerhans cell histiocytosis, leukaemic deposit (chloroma)**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** The stem gives **no fever** (excludes cellulitis) plus **desmin** (excludes retinoblastoma and lymphoma). Both clauses are deliberate.

**REMEMBER:** *Desmin means muscle; muscle in the orbit means rhabdomyosarcoma.*

## 87. Managing Squint in a Child

**PEARL:** The order is **ROOP: Refractive correction → Orthoptics/Occlusion (amblyopia therapy) → Operative correction → Prisms**. **Never operate before treating amblyopia.**

**UNDERSTAND:** Much of childhood esotropia is **accommodative** — driven by uncorrected hypermetropia, so **glasses alone can eliminate the deviation**. Operating first would over-correct a deviation that spectacles would have fixed, and would leave the **amblyopia untreated during the critical period** — the one deficit that becomes permanent.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Amblyopia treatment works best **before 7–8 years**; **occlusion (patching) of the better eye** or **atropine penalisation** are the options.
- Surgery addresses only the **non-accommodative residual** deviation, and is performed **after** vision has been equalised.
- **Accommodative esotropia** presents around **2–3 years**, with a **high AC/A ratio** in the convergence-excess type (treated with bifocals or miotics).
- **Pseudosquint** (epicanthal folds, negative angle kappa) must be excluded with the **Hirschberg corneal reflex and cover tests**.
- Sensory adaptations in childhood squint: **suppression and amblyopia** (child), vs **diplopia** (adult) — the eye tests to know are the **Worth four-dot test** (fusion/suppression) and the **Titmus fly stereotest** (stereopsis, using polarised glasses at 16 inches).
- **Aniseikonia** — a difference in **image SIZE** between the two eyes; **up to 5–8% is tolerated**; treat with **contact lenses (phakia)** or **IOL implantation (aphakia)** rather than spectacles, which magnify.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Sequence question — the answer is always **glasses and amblyopia therapy before surgery**.

**REMEMBER:** *Correct the refraction, save the vision, then straighten the eye.*

## 88. Punched-Out Chorioretinal Lesions

**PEARL:** **Bilateral healed "punched-out" macular chorioretinal scars = TOXOPLASMOSIS**, usually congenital.

**UNDERSTAND:** *Toxoplasma* has a particular tropism for the **retina and CNS**, and the encysted parasite causes a **necrotising retinitis** that heals with **sharply demarcated atrophy and pigment clumping** — the punched-out appearance. Recurrences arise at the **edge of an old scar** ("satellite lesion"), because that is where dormant cysts persist.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Active lesion: a **fluffy white focus of retinitis adjacent to a pigmented scar** with overlying vitritis — the classic **"headlight in the fog."**
- **Congenital toxoplasmosis** — transplacental; infants often born with **inactive bilateral macular scars** and profound visual loss; part of the triad with **hydrocephalus and intracranial calcification**.
- Treat sight-threatening lesions (macula, optic nerve, dense vitritis) with **pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid** (± steroids, never steroids alone), or cotrimoxazole.
- Distinguish the look-alikes: **posterior staphyloma** — excavated scleral bulge with vessels dipping in; **fundal coloboma** — comet-shaped inferonasal defect from failed embryonic fissure closure; **congenital rubella** — diffuse **"salt-and-pepper" retinopathy**; **CMV retinitis** — **"pizza-pie"** haemorrhagic necrotising retinitis in HIV with CD4 < 50.
- **Photostress test** distinguishes **macular from optic nerve** disease — recovery time is **prolonged in macular disease** (photoreceptor pigment regeneration is impaired) and **normal in optic neuropathy**.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** "Punched-out lesions" is a fixed phrase mapping to toxoplasmosis; the distractors each have their own distinctive descriptive phrase.

**REMEMBER:** *Punched-out and pigmented, next to a fluffy new lesion — toxoplasma.*

## 89. Zonular Spaces and the Anterior Vitreous

**PEARL:** The **canal of Petit** is the **capsulohyaloid space** — between the anterior hyaloid membrane behind and the ciliary body/zonule in front.

**UNDERSTAND:** The zonule is not a single sheet but **two layers of fibres running to different points on the lens capsule**, and the potential spaces between them and the vitreous face have separate names. Knowing which structure bounds which space is what the question tests — and it matters clinically because these are the routes along which **blood tracks after posterior capsule rupture**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Canal of Petit** — capsulohyaloid space, in front of the anterior hyaloid, behind the ciliary body and zonule; a site of **retrolental haemorrhage**.
- **Canal of Hannover** — between the **orbiculo-anterocapsular and orbiculo-posterocapsular** parts of the zonular fibres.
- **Berger's space** — the potential space between the **posterior lens capsule and the anterior hyaloid face**.
- **Weiger's ligament (hyaloideocapsular ligament)** — the circular adhesion between them; its circular attachment is **Egger's line**.
- Cataract surgery relevance: **an intraocular lens implanted uneventfully is NEVER routinely replaced.** IOL exchange is indicated only for **wrong power, dislocation/decentration, asymmetric haptic placement, sunset syndrome or persistent hyphaema**.
- **Posterior capsular opacification ("secondary cataract")** is treated by **Nd:YAG capsulotomy**, not lens exchange; **presbyopia** is corrected with glasses.
- **Eye drops go in FIRST, ointment 10–15 minutes later** — the ointment's oily base would otherwise block drop absorption, and ointments blur vision so are best at bedtime.

**EXAM CONNECTION:** Labelled anatomical diagram. Learn the four eponymous spaces as a set, since the options are always drawn from within it.

**REMEMBER:** *Petit sits in front of the hyaloid, Berger behind the lens.*
