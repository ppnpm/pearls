# A. Hernia

## 1. Named Hernia Repairs

**PEARL:**
- **Bassini** — sutures the **conjoint tendon to the inguinal ligament**; no double-breasting.
- **Shouldice** — **double-breasting of the transversalis fascia** creating a **4-layered** posterior wall (the best pure tissue repair).
- **Lichtenstein** — **tension-free synthetic mesh** over the defect; the current gold standard.
- **Desarda** — an **undetached strip of external oblique aponeurosis** reinforces the posterior wall; **no mesh**.
- **McVay (Cooper's ligament)** — conjoint tendon to **Cooper's ligament**; repairs **both inguinal and femoral** hernias.
- **Mayo** — **"double-breasting" overlap of fascia** for a small **umbilical/paraumbilical** hernia.
- **McEvedy** — **high (preperitoneal) approach for femoral hernia** through a transverse incision over the lower rectus; allows bowel resection if strangulated.

**MUST KNOW:** **Shouldice and Mayo both use "double-breasting"** — Shouldice for the inguinal canal, Mayo for the umbilicus (the classic confusion). **McVay is the tissue repair of choice for femoral hernia**; **McEvedy is the approach of choice when strangulation is suspected**.

**REMEMBER:** *Shouldice = 4 layers of transversalis; Mayo = umbilical overlap; McVay = Cooper's; McEvedy = high femoral.*

---

## 2. Taxis

**PEARL:** **Taxis = manual reduction of an incarcerated hernia** in the emergency setting.

**MUST KNOW:** **Contraindicated if strangulation is suspected** (tenderness, toxicity, >6 h) — risk of **reduction en masse** (reducing the sac with the still-obstructed bowel inside) and of returning gangrenous bowel to the abdomen. Do not confuse with **Kegel's exercises** (pelvic floor strengthening) — a common distractor.

**REMEMBER:** *Taxis pushes it back — never if it might be dead.*

---

## 3. Richter's Hernia & Strangulation Physiology

**PEARL:** In strangulation, **venous outflow obstruction precedes arterial inflow obstruction**. **Richter's hernia can strangulate without complete bowel obstruction** (only part of the circumference is trapped), and **closed-loop obstruction strangulates rapidly**.

**UNDERSTAND:** Thin-walled veins collapse under a pressure that arteries resist → blood keeps arriving but cannot leave → congestion, oedema, rising pressure, and finally arterial cut-off and infarction.

**MUST KNOW:** So a patient can have **strangulation without vomiting or distension** — pain and tenderness out of proportion is the clue. Femoral hernias have the **highest strangulation rate**; Richter's is classically femoral or at a port site.

**REMEMBER:** *Veins go first; Richter's kills bowel without obstructing it.*

# B. Colorectal & Small Bowel

## 4. The 3-6-9 Rule

**PEARL:** Normal bowel calibre — **small bowel <3 cm, large bowel <6 cm, caecum <9 cm** (and **appendix <6 mm**).

**MUST KNOW:** Caecum >9–12 cm risks perforation. Use with the plain-film findings below.

**REMEMBER:** *3 small, 6 large, 9 caecum, 6 mm appendix.*

---

## 5. Small Bowel Obstruction — X-ray Triad

**PEARL:** The most specific plain-film findings: **(1) dilated small bowel loops >3 cm, (2) air–fluid levels on erect film, (3) paucity of gas in the colon**.

**MUST KNOW:** Small bowel is identified by **central position and valvulae conniventes crossing the full width**; large bowel is **peripheral with haustra that do not cross fully**. Commonest cause of SBO: **adhesions** (then hernia, then malignancy). **CT is the investigation of choice** — it shows the transition point, cause and ischaemia.

**REMEMBER:** *Dilated small bowel + air-fluid levels + empty colon = SBO.*

---

## 6. Caecum in Large Bowel Obstruction

**PEARL:** In large bowel obstruction, the **caecum** — **not the splenic flexure** — is the segment most prone to ischaemia and perforation.

**UNDERSTAND:** **Laplace's law** — wall tension is proportional to radius, and the caecum has the largest diameter, so it takes the greatest wall stress and its blood supply fails first.

**MUST KNOW:** This applies with a **competent ileocaecal valve** (closed-loop obstruction) — the more dangerous situation. The **splenic flexure** is the classic **watershed** area for *ischaemic colitis*, which is a different question.

**REMEMBER:** *Biggest radius, highest tension — the caecum blows first.*

---

## 7. Colonic Pseudo-obstruction (Ogilvie Syndrome)

**PEARL:** **IV neostigmine** is used to decompress the colon.

**UNDERSTAND:** It is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor → boosts parasympathetic drive to the colon → propulsive activity resumes.

**MUST KNOW:** Give with **cardiac monitoring and atropine at hand** (bradycardia/asystole risk); contraindicated in **mechanical obstruction** — which must be excluded by CT first. Escalation: conservative (correct electrolytes, stop opioids/anticholinergics, NG and flatus tube) → neostigmine → **colonoscopic decompression** → surgery if caecum >12 cm or perforation.

**REMEMBER:** *Ogilvie = neostigmine, but only after excluding a real blockage.*

---

## 8. Colonic Diverticula

**PEARL:** Colonic diverticula are **acquired FALSE diverticula** (mucosa and submucosa herniating through the muscle), commonest in the **sigmoid and descending colon**. **The rectum is spared** because of its **complete, thick outer muscle coat**.

**MUST KNOW:** They herniate where the **vasa recta penetrate** the circular muscle — which is why bleeding can be brisk. **In Asians, right-sided (caecal) diverticula are commoner** and are often **true, congenital** diverticula. **Meckel's diverticulum is the classic TRUE diverticulum.**

**REMEMBER:** *False, acquired, sigmoid, rectum spared (taeniae fuse there).*

---

## 9. Meckel's Diverticulum

**PEARL:** A **true** diverticulum; **~20% contain ectopic gastric mucosa**, which secretes acid → **ileal ulceration and painless rectal bleeding in children**. Treatment: **diverticulectomy or wedge/segmental resection**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **Rule of 2s:** 2% of population, 2 feet from the ileocaecal valve, 2 inches long, 2 types of ectopic mucosa (gastric and pancreatic), presents before age 2, twice as common in males.
- Diagnosis: **technetium-99m pertechnetate (Meckel's) scan** — it is taken up by gastric mucosa.
- Other presentations: **intussusception (commonest lead point in children), obstruction, diverticulitis mimicking appendicitis, perforation**.

**REMEMBER:** *Painless bleeding + Meckel's scan + rule of 2s.*

---

## 10. Rectal Cancer — Lymphatic Spread

**PEARL:** Rectal malignancy **does not usually spread to inguinal nodes** — its lymphatic drainage runs **upwards along the superior rectal vessels to the inferior mesenteric/mesenteric nodes**.

**MUST KNOW:** **Inguinal nodes are involved only in tumours BELOW the dentate line** (anal canal carcinoma) or with very low rectal tumours invading the anal canal — following the embryological watershed at the dentate line.

**REMEMBER:** *Above the dentate line drains up; below it drains to the groin.*

---

## 11. Anorectal Abscess

**PEARL:** **Perianal abscess is the commonest (60%)**, followed by **ischiorectal (30%)**. Drain **under anaesthesia through a cruciate skin incision**.

**MUST KNOW:** Origin: infected **anal (cryptoglandular) glands** at the dentate line. Do **not** delay for antibiotics or "ripening" — **drainage is the treatment**. Look for diabetes/immunosuppression; the feared progression is **Fournier's gangrene**. Around one-third later develop a **fistula-in-ano** (Goodsall's rule guides tract prediction).

**REMEMBER:** *Perianal > ischiorectal; cut it open, don't wait.*

---

## 12. Post-haemorrhoidectomy Complication

**PEARL:** **Urinary retention is the commonest complication (up to 30%)** — managed with catheterization and **good pain control**.

**MUST KNOW:** Contributing factors: pain, pelvic floor spasm, spinal anaesthesia and excess perioperative IV fluids. Other complications: pain (universal), reactionary/secondary haemorrhage (day 7–10), anal stenosis, incontinence.

**REMEMBER:** *After haemorrhoidectomy, the bladder is the first thing to stop working.*

---

## 13. Ulcerative Colitis — Extraintestinal Manifestations

**PEARL:** **Arthropathy is the commonest extraintestinal manifestation (~15%)**.

**MUST KNOW:** Manifestations **parallel to disease activity**: peripheral arthritis, erythema nodosum, episcleritis, oral aphthae. **Independent of activity**: **primary sclerosing cholangitis** (strongly UC-associated, with cholangiocarcinoma risk), ankylosing spondylitis/sacroiliitis (**HLA-B27**), uveitis, **pyoderma gangrenosum**.

**REMEMBER:** *Joints most often; PSC and ankylosing spondylitis don't care about the colitis activity.*

---

## 14. Familial Adenomatous Polyposis — Screening

**PEARL:** **Genetic testing for the APC mutation is the recommended initial screening step** for at-risk children.

**MUST KNOW:** Test from **age 10–12**; if the mutation is present, begin **annual sigmoidoscopy/colonoscopy** — colectomy is essentially inevitable (**100% cancer risk by 40**). Autosomal dominant, chromosome **5q21**. Variants: **Gardner** (osteomas, desmoids, epidermoid cysts) and **Turcot** (CNS tumours). **CHRPE** (congenital hypertrophy of retinal pigment epithelium) is an early clue.

**REMEMBER:** *Test the gene first at 10–12; scope only those who carry it.*

---

## 15. Peutz–Jeghers Polyps

**PEARL:** The polyps are **hamartomatous, not neoplastic** — so they themselves rarely turn malignant; they cause **intussusception and bleeding**.

**MUST KNOW:** Despite benign polyps, **overall cancer risk is very high** (pancreas highest, then breast, colon, stomach) — from the **STK11/LKB1** germline mutation, not from the polyps. Perioral/buccal **melanotic pigmentation** is the visual clue.

**REMEMBER:** *Benign polyps, dangerous patient.*

---

## 16. Murphy's Triad

**PEARL:** In acute appendicitis: **periumbilical pain migrating to the right iliac fossa + vomiting + low-grade fever**.

**MUST KNOW:** The **order matters** — pain comes **before** vomiting in appendicitis (**Murphy's sequence**); vomiting first suggests gastroenteritis. Pain is initially **visceral referred to T10 (periumbilical)** and localizes to **McBurney's point** once the parietal peritoneum is involved. **Alvarado score** aids diagnosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Pain → vomiting → fever, in that order.*

---

## 17. Ladd's Procedure

**PEARL:** For **malrotation with midgut volvulus**: **detorsion (anticlockwise) + division of Ladd's bands + broadening of the mesentery + incidental appendicectomy + placement of small bowel on the RIGHT and colon on the LEFT**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The bowel is not returned to normal anatomy — it is deliberately left in a **wide-based non-rotated position**, because a broad mesentery cannot twist again.

**MUST KNOW:** Presentation: **bilious vomiting in a neonate = malrotation with volvulus until proven otherwise** — a surgical emergency. Upper GI contrast study is the gold standard (**abnormal DJ flexure position**); ultrasound shows the **whirlpool sign** and SMA/SMV inversion. **Appendicectomy is done because the appendix ends up in the left side of the abdomen**, which would confuse any future diagnosis.

**REMEMBER:** *Bilious vomiting in a newborn → Ladd's: untwist, divide bands, widen mesentery, take the appendix.*

# C. Hepatobiliary & Pancreas

## 18. Gallstones — Asian Pattern

**PEARL:** In **Asia**, the commonest gallstones are **pigment stones**; in the West, cholesterol/mixed stones predominate.

**MUST KNOW:** **Brown pigment stones** are associated with **biliary infection/stasis and parasites (*Clonorchis*, *Ascaris*)** and are typically found in the **bile ducts**; **black pigment stones** occur with **chronic haemolysis and cirrhosis** and stay in the gallbladder. Only ~10–15% of gallstones are **radio-opaque** (pigment stones more so than cholesterol).

**REMEMBER:** *Asia = pigment; brown = infection in the duct, black = haemolysis in the gallbladder.*

---

## 19. Gallbladder Cancer Risk Factors

**PEARL:** Recognized risk factors warranting prophylactic cholecystectomy: **polyps >1 cm, gallstones >3 cm, choledochal cysts** (also porcelain gallbladder, anomalous pancreaticobiliary duct junction, chronic *Salmonella typhi* carriage). **Adenomyomatosis is NOT a recognized risk factor.**

**MUST KNOW:** Gallbladder cancer is common in **North India (Gangetic belt)**, in **women**, and spreads early to **segments IVb/V of the liver**. It is frequently an incidental finding after cholecystectomy.

**REMEMBER:** *Polyp >1 cm, stone >3 cm, choledochal cyst = take it out. Adenomyomatosis = leave it.*

---

## 20. IPMN

**PEARL:** **Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm** — cyst fluid shows **positive mucin staining, raised CEA, and raised amylase**.

**UNDERSTAND:** High **amylase** = the cyst **communicates with the pancreatic duct** (IPMN or pseudocyst); high **CEA + mucin** = a **mucinous** (premalignant) lesion.

**MUST KNOW:** **Main-duct IPMN has a high malignant potential → resect**; branch-duct IPMN may be surveilled. Contrast: **serous cystadenoma** — low CEA, low amylase, benign, central scar; **pseudocyst** — high amylase but **low CEA**, no mucin.

**REMEMBER:** *Mucin + high CEA = mucinous and dangerous; high amylase = talks to the duct.*

---

## 21. TIPSS — Contraindication

**PEARL:** **Portal vein thrombosis is a contraindication to TIPSS** — without a patent portal vein, the shunt cannot be created.

**MUST KNOW:** Other contraindications: **severe hepatic encephalopathy** (TIPSS worsens it by bypassing the liver), **right heart failure/severe pulmonary hypertension**, polycystic liver disease, sepsis. Main indications: refractory variceal bleeding and refractory ascites.

**REMEMBER:** *No portal vein, no shunt; and never in encephalopathy.*

---

## 22. Rockall vs Glasgow–Blatchford Score

**PEARL:** **Haemoglobin is NOT part of the Rockall score.** Rockall = **age, shock (BP and pulse), comorbidity, endoscopic diagnosis, and stigmata of recent haemorrhage**. **Glasgow–Blatchford includes haemoglobin and blood urea** but uses **no endoscopic findings**.

**UNDERSTAND:** That difference decides their use — **Blatchford is a pre-endoscopy triage tool** (a score of 0 allows outpatient management), while **Rockall predicts rebleeding and mortality after endoscopy**.

**REMEMBER:** *Blatchford before the scope (bloods); Rockall after it (findings).*

# D. Breast, Urology & Miscellaneous

## 23. Fibroadenoma

**PEARL:** Fibroadenoma is an **aberration of normal lobular development (ANDI)** — not a true neoplasm.

**MUST KNOW:** In the **ANDI** classification it is a disorder of the **early reproductive period (15–25 years)**; it is hormone-responsive (grows in pregnancy, regresses after menopause). Clinically: **firm, smooth, highly mobile "breast mouse"**. Small ones in young women may be observed after triple assessment; excise if >3–4 cm, growing, or symptomatic.

**REMEMBER:** *Fibroadenoma = aberration of lobular development, not a tumour.*

---

## 24. Breast Conservation Surgery — Contraindications

**PEARL:** BCS is best avoided with: **multicentric tumour, diffuse microcalcifications on mammography, a large tumour-to-breast ratio, persistently positive margins after re-excision, previous breast/chest wall radiation, SLE or other collagen vascular disease (severe radiation reaction), ankylosing spondylitis, and severe orthopnoea** (cannot lie flat for radiotherapy).

**UNDERSTAND:** Almost every contraindication is really a contraindication to the **radiotherapy** that must follow BCS, or to achieving clear margins.

**MUST KNOW:** **Pregnancy in the first/second trimester** is a contraindication (radiotherapy must be deferred); **multifocal** disease (same quadrant) is acceptable, **multicentric** (different quadrants) is not. BCS + radiotherapy gives **survival equal to mastectomy**.

**REMEMBER:** *If she can't have the radiotherapy or clear margins, she can't have BCS.*

---

## 25. Plummer–Vinson Syndrome

**PEARL:** Oesophageal webs are treated by **dilation with bougies/dilators — NOT myotomy**; correcting the **iron deficiency** alone improves symptoms.

**MUST KNOW:** Triad: **dysphagia + iron deficiency anaemia + oesophageal web** (post-cricoid), classically in middle-aged women; also glossitis, angular stomatitis, koilonychia. It is **premalignant — post-cricoid squamous cell carcinoma**, so these patients need follow-up. **Myotomy is for achalasia** (a motility disorder), not for a web.

**REMEMBER:** *Web = dilate + iron; achalasia = myotomy. And watch for post-cricoid cancer.*

---

## 26. Retrograde Urethrogram

**PEARL:** In RGU, contrast is instilled **retrogradely** to visualize the **anterior urethra (penile and bulbar)** — the best study for **anterior urethral** abnormalities.

**MUST KNOW:** The **posterior (membranous and prostatic) urethra** is better seen on a **micturating cystourethrogram (MCU)**, done antegrade during voiding. **Pelvic fracture** typically injures the **posterior/membranous** urethra; **straddle injury** damages the **bulbar (anterior)** urethra. **Blood at the meatus = do an RGU before catheterizing.**

**REMEMBER:** *RGU for the front, MCU for the back; blood at the meatus means no catheter until imaged.*

---

## 27. Gossypiboma

**PEARL:** A **retained surgical sponge/swab** causing a foreign body reaction.

**MUST KNOW:** Two responses: an **exudative** reaction (early abscess/fistula) or a **fibrinous** reaction (late mass mimicking a tumour). On imaging: a **whorled mass with a radio-opaque marker**, "spongiform" gas pattern. Prevention = swab and instrument counts; it is a classic **medicolegal (negligence res ipsa loquitur)** case.

**REMEMBER:** *Gossypiboma = the sponge you left behind — count them.*
