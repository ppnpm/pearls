## 1. ATRA and Acute Promyelocytic Leukaemia

**PEARL:** **All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA)** treats **APML (AML-M3)**, which carries the **PML-RARA fusion gene** from **t(15;17)**.

**UNDERSTAND:** The fusion protein blocks myeloid differentiation at the promyelocyte stage; **pharmacological doses of ATRA overcome the block and force the promyelocytes to mature** — it is *differentiation* therapy, not cytotoxic chemotherapy.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **DIC is the main cause of early death** (promyelocyte granules release procoagulants) → **start ATRA on clinical suspicion, before cytogenetics return**, and support with fibrinogen/platelets.
- **ATRA differentiation syndrome** — fever, hypoxia, infiltrates, weight gain → **dexamethasone immediately**.
- **Arsenic trioxide + ATRA** now cures most low-risk APML **without chemotherapy**; APML has become the **best-prognosis AML**.
- Morphology: hypergranular promyelocytes with **faggot cells (bundles of Auer rods)**.

**REMEMBER:** *t(15;17) → PML-RARA → ATRA differentiates it; DIC kills early.*

## 2. ALL — Immunophenotype and Age

**PEARL:**
- **B-ALL:** **TdT (intracellular) + CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, CD10** — presents as **early childhood leukaemia**.
- **T-ALL:** **TdT + CD1, CD2, CD3, CD5, CD7** — presents in **adolescents as a thymic (mediastinal) lymphoma/mass**.
- **TdT marks the cell as a BLAST**; the CD cluster marks the **lineage**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- **CD10 (CALLA) positivity in B-ALL carries a GOOD prognosis**; T-ALL classically arises in an adolescent **boy** with a mediastinal mass ± superior vena cava obstruction and pleural effusion.
- TdT is **absent in AML** — where **myeloperoxidase and Auer rods** appear instead.
- Peak age for B-ALL is **2–5 years**; CNS and testes are **sanctuary sites**.

**REMEMBER:** *TdT = blast. 19/20/22/10 = B (child). 1/2/3/5/7 = T (teenage mediastinum).*

## 3. White Blood Cell Casts

**PEARL:** **WBC casts are specific for PYELONEPHRITIS** — they prove the leucocytes came from the **renal tubules**, not the lower tract.

**MUST KNOW:** Cast catalogue: **RBC casts → glomerulonephritis**; **WBC casts → pyelonephritis (also acute interstitial nephritis)**; **muddy-brown granular casts → acute tubular necrosis**; **fatty casts/"Maltese cross" → nephrotic syndrome**; **broad waxy casts → chronic kidney disease**; **hyaline casts → normal/dehydration**. All casts form in the tubule on a **Tamm–Horsfall protein** matrix — hence any cast localizes the problem to the kidney.

**REMEMBER:** *WBC cast = infection is INSIDE the kidney; a cast always means renal origin.*

## 4. Folate Deficiency in Chronic Alcoholics

**PEARL:** Alcoholics become folate deficient by four mechanisms: **inadequate dietary intake, trapping of folate within the liver, excessive urinary loss, and disordered folate metabolism**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- Result: **megaloblastic anaemia with hypersegmented neutrophils** — though alcohol also causes macrocytosis **without** megaloblastic change (a direct marrow toxic effect) and **B12 deficiency must be excluded before giving folate**, since folate alone precipitates **subacute combined degeneration**.
- Folate stores last only **3–4 months** (versus **3–5 years for B12**) — which is why deficiency appears fast.
- Alcoholics also lose **thiamine, magnesium, pyridoxine and zinc**; give **thiamine before glucose**.

**REMEMBER:** *Folate runs out in months — alcohol attacks it from four directions at once.*

## 5. Colorectal Carcinoma — Adenoma–Carcinoma Sequence

**PEARL:** **p53 mutation is associated with the INVASIVE phase**, distinguishing it from **APC and KRAS**, which occur in the **pre-invasive** (adenoma) stages.

**UNDERSTAND:** Sequence: **APC loss ("gatekeeper", the first hit → hyperproliferative epithelium) → KRAS activation (adenoma growth) → SMAD4/DCC loss → p53 loss (the final step permitting invasion)**. p53 is the **"guardian of the genome"** — while it works, genetically damaged cells are arrested or killed; once it goes, invasion follows.

**MUST KNOW:** This is the **chromosomal instability pathway (~80%)**. The alternative is the **microsatellite instability pathway (MMR genes — Lynch syndrome)**: right-sided, mucinous/poorly differentiated, better prognosis, and **responsive to checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab)**.

**REMEMBER:** *APC starts it, KRAS grows it, p53 lets it invade.*

## 6. Dysplastic Nodules and Hepatocellular Carcinoma

**PEARL:** **High-grade dysplastic nodules** may progress to **hepatocellular carcinoma** in **viral hepatitis and alcoholic liver disease**.

**MUST KNOW:**
- The pathway is **chronic injury → cirrhosis → regenerative nodule → low-grade dysplastic nodule → high-grade dysplastic nodule → HCC**; a high-grade nodule is the recognized **precancerous lesion**.
- Risk factors: **HBV (can cause HCC WITHOUT cirrhosis, via viral DNA integration)**, HCV, alcohol, NAFLD, **aflatoxin (p53 codon 249 mutation)**, haemochromatosis.
- Surveillance in cirrhosis: **6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP**. On imaging, HCC shows **arterial-phase hyperenhancement with portal venous washout** — enough to diagnose without biopsy.

**REMEMBER:** *Regenerative → dysplastic → high-grade → HCC; HBV can skip the cirrhosis step.*
